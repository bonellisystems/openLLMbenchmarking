## 10 -- Frontier

Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

# ACCEPTABLE USE POLICY (AUP)
**Corporate Laptops & Shop-Floor Tablets**
**Effective Date: [Date] | Version: 1.0**

## Section 1: Scope and Purpose
This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) applies to all corporate-owned laptops, shop-floor tablets, and any peripheral devices issued by [Company Name] ("the Company"). This policy governs how these devices may be used on both the general corporate network and the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control-System (ICS) network.

## Section 2: Reasonable Personal Use and Monitoring Disclosure
**2.1 Permitted Personal Use.** Employees are permitted to use Company devices for reasonable personal purposes during non-work hours, provided such use does not interfere with work performance, violate security protocols, or incur excessive data costs. This includes limited use of social media, email, and web browsing for personal matters.

**2.2 Monitoring of Device and Network Activity.** The Company reserves the right to monitor all activity on Company devices and networks, including but not limited to email, internet browsing, file transfers, and device connectivity logs. This monitoring is conducted for legitimate business purposes, including network security, asset protection, and operational efficiency.

**2.3 Protected Concerted Activity.** Notwithstanding Section 2.2, the Company acknowledges that under Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), employees retain the right to engage in **protected concerted activity**. Monitoring activity shall **not** be used to identify, target, or penalize employees for discussing wages, hours of employment, benefits, or working conditions with each other, or for organizing or engaging in lawful collective bargaining. The Company will not use monitoring tools to collect data specifically intended to surveil protected conversations, and all monitoring logs shall be reviewed with the understanding that protected NLRA activity is exempt from disciplinary action.

**2.4 Acknowledgment and Issuance.** No employee shall be issued a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet until they have received a clear explanation of this AUP, specifically including the monitoring provisions described in Sections 2.2 and 2.3. The employee must sign an **AUP Acknowledgment Form** confirming that they have read, understood, and agree to comply with this policy. Unsigned devices will not be released.

## Section 3: Prohibited Uses
**3.1 OT/ICS Network Restrictions.** The Operational-Technology (OT) and Industrial-Control-System (ICS) networks control critical manufacturing equipment, assembly line robotics, and production diagnostics. These networks are strictly segmented from the general corporate network. The following are strictly prohibited on devices connected to the OT/ICS network:
*   **Personal Email Attachments:** Employees shall not open, download, or execute personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes email received on a personal account accessed via the OT network, or any attachment not originating from an approved Company distribution list.
*   **External Media:** The execution or import of files from external sources onto OT/ICS connected devices is prohibited unless explicitly authorized by the IT Security and OT Engineering teams.

**3.2 Production-Line USB Restrictions.**
*   **Unapproved USB Drives:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, memory sticks, or external storage devices to production-line PCs or shop-floor tablets is strictly prohibited.
*   **Approved Media:** Only approved, Company-issued external storage media (e.g., encrypted drives issued by IT) may be connected to production-line systems.
*   **Rationale:** Unauthorized media connections pose a significant risk of introducing malware, ransomware, or data exfiltration that could halt production lines or compromise safety-critical systems.

## Section 4: Disciplinary Consequences
**4.1 General Violations.** Violations of this AUP that do not impact safety-critical systems may result in a verbal warning, written reprimand, performance improvement plan (PIP), or suspension, at the Company's sole discretion.

**4.2 Safety-Critical OT Violations.** Violations of Section 3.1 (OT/ICS Network Restrictions) or Section 3.2 (Production-Line USB Restrictions) that are determined to have the potential to impact safety-critical systems, production integrity, or data security are treated as serious offenses.
*   **Termination:** The Company reserves the right to terminate an employee's employment **immediately and without notice** for violations involving the OT/ICS network or production-line PCs.
*   **No-Recourse:** Employees who violate these provisions shall not be eligible for severance, unemployment benefits related to the violation, or other post-employment benefits associated with the termination.
*   **Legal Action:** In cases where a violation results in physical injury, equipment damage, or significant data breach, the Company reserves the right to pursue legal action against the individual and their employer.

## Section 5: Policy Review and Amendments
The Company reserves the right to modify this AUP at any time. Revised policies will be communicated to all employees via [Company Intranet / Email] and require re-acknowledgment upon issuance of a new device.

## Pre-Rollout Recommendations

Before deploying this AUP, the following steps should be taken to ensure legal compliance, operational readiness, and employee buy-in:

1.  **Legal Counsel Review:** Have employment counsel in relevant jurisdictions review the "Protected Concerted Activity" carve-out (Section 2.3) to ensure it is not inadvertently overbroad or creates a liability for the company. Ensure the termination language (Section 4.2) complies with at-will employment statutes in the state(s) where facilities operate.
2.  **IT/OT Security Audit:** Verify that the technical controls match the policy language. 4.  **NLRB Consultation:** Confirm that the monitoring carve-out for Section 7 activity is sufficient to withstand scrutiny. If the company uses monitoring tools (e.g., keystroke logging, network packet inspection) that inherently capture protected activity, ensure the data retention policy is set to purge such data immediately to minimize risk.
5.  **Sign-Off Logistics:** Establish a process for tracking signed acknowledgments. If a device is issued without a signed form, that device should not be activated until compliance is documented.

### 1. Permitted Personal Use and Company Monitoring Rights
**Reasonable Personal Use:**
Employees are permitted to use company-issued corporate laptops and shop-floor tablets for reasonable personal use, provided such use does not interfere with job duties, violate other company policies, or compromise network security. Personal use may occur during breaks or non-working hours. "Reasonable use" excludes excessive bandwidth consumption, access to inappropriate content, or any activity that degrades device performance.

**Monitoring Rights:**
The Company reserves the explicit right to monitor, access, record, and review all activity on corporate devices and across all company networks. This includes, but is not limited to, network traffic, email communications, file transfers, application usage, location data (where applicable), and keystrokes. Employees should have no expectation of privacy regarding any data stored on, transmitted through, or accessed via company devices and networks.

### 2. Strict Prohibitions: OT/ICS and Production Networks
Due to the safety-critical nature of agricultural equipment manufacturing and the integrity of our automation infrastructure, the following actions are **strictly prohibited** on devices connected to Operational Technology (OT), Industrial Control Systems (ICS), or production-line networks:

*   **Personal Email Attachments:** Opening, downloading, or executing attachments from personal email accounts on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes shop-floor tablets, HMIs, engineering workstations, and any laptop docked to production systems.
*   **Unapproved External Media:** Connecting personal USB drives, external hard drives, smartphones, or other unapproved storage media to production-line PCs, CNC controllers, robotics controllers, or any OT-connected device. Only Company-approved, sanitized, and encrypted media may be used for data transfer to production systems, and only via designated secure protocols.

### 3. Monitoring Disclosure and Pre-Issuance Acknowledgment
**Disclosure:**
This policy serves as clear disclosure that all device and network activity is subject to monitoring by IT Security and authorized management personnel. Monitoring is conducted to ensure security, protect intellectual property, maintain operational reliability, and ensure compliance with safety standards.

**Acknowledgment Requirement:**
No corporate laptop, shop-floor tablet, or access to OT/ICS networks will be issued or enabled until the employee has signed the **AUP Acknowledgment Form**. This signature confirms that the employee has read, understood, and agrees to abide by this policy. IT will not provision devices for employees with unsigned acknowledgments on file.

### 4. Disciplinary Consequences and OT Safety Criticality
Violations of this AUP may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination, depending on the severity of the infraction.

**Safety-Critical OT Violations:**
Violations involving safety-critical OT/ICS systems pose direct risks to personnel safety, heavy machinery operation, and production integrity. Therefore, prohibited actions on OT networks are treated as **Gross Misconduct**.
*   Connecting unapproved personal USB drives to production-line PCs or OT devices.
*   Opening personal email attachments on OT-connected devices.
*   Attempting to bypass OT security controls.

**Consequence:**
Violations of OT/ICS prohibitions will typically result in **immediate termination**, regardless of intent, due to the potential for catastrophic safety incidents, equipment damage, or prolonged production shutdown. The Company may also pursue legal action if violations result in significant liability or harm.

### 5. Protected Concerted Activity Carve-Out
**Preservation of Labor Rights:**
Nothing in this AUP, including its monitoring provisions or disciplinary consequences, shall be construed to prohibit, interfere with, or limit employees' rights to engage in protected concerted activity under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) or applicable state labor laws.

**Explicit Carve-Out:**
Employees are expressly permitted to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, terms of employment, and other labor-related topics with coworkers using company devices and networks. The Company will not discipline employees for such discussions, nor will monitoring be used to penalize employees for engaging in these protected communications.

**Clarification of Safety vs. Rights:**
While discussions regarding wages and working conditions are protected, these rights do not authorize the use of prohibited methods that endanger safety-critical systems. For example, employees may discuss working conditions, but they must not do so by connecting personal USB drives to production-line PCs or opening personal email attachments on OT devices, as the safety risk to the operation remains prohibited regardless of the content of the discussion.

The Company recognizes that limited, reasonable personal use of these devices may occur (e.g., personal web browsing during breaks, non-business communication), provided such use does not interfere with job performance, compromise security, or violate Company policy.

However, all devices issued by the Company are Company property. The Company reserves the right to monitor, inspect, and record all activity on corporate devices and the corporate network, including internet usage, file transfers, and communications. By accepting a device, employees acknowledge that there is no expectation of privacy regarding Company data, device usage, or network activity.

**Labor Law Protection:** Notwithstanding the monitoring provisions above, this Policy is not intended to, and shall not be interpreted to, restrict or prohibit employees from engaging in protected concerted activity under federal labor law (including but not limited to Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act). Employees retain the right to discuss wages, hours, and working conditions with one another. The Company does not monitor communications between employees regarding protected concerted activity.

**II. Prohibited Activities: OT and Production Security**

Due to the safety-critical nature of our manufacturing operations, specific prohibitions apply to devices connected to the Operational Technology (OT) / Industrial Control Systems (ICS) network.

1.  **Personal Email Attachments:** Employees are strictly prohibited from opening, downloading, or executing personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes, but is not limited to, shop-floor tablets used for equipment diagnostics or laptops assigned to production supervisors.
2.  **USB Devices:** Only Company-approved USB drives (e.g., encrypted drives issued by IT) may be connected to production-line PCs or OT interfaces. Connecting unapproved personal USB drives to these systems is a violation of this Policy due to the risk of introducing malware or unauthorized data transfers.

**III. Disclosure and Acknowledgment**

The Company provides clear disclosure to all employees that the use of Company devices and networks is subject to monitoring. Before a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet is issued, the employee must:

1.  Read this Acceptable Use Policy in its entirety.
2.  Complete a brief training module covering OT security and the monitoring provisions.
3.  Sign a digital or physical Acknowledgment Form confirming they understand the Policy, the monitoring rights, and the prohibited activities.

Failure to sign the Acknowledgment Form prior to device issuance will result in the device being withheld.

**IV. Disciplinary Action and Consequences**

Violations of this Policy will be addressed in accordance with the Company’s Disciplinary Action Framework. The severity of the response will depend on the nature of the violation and the potential risk to operations.

*   **General Violations:** Minor violations (e.g., excessive personal browsing) may result in a verbal or written warning, suspension of device privileges, or retraining.
*   **Safety-Critical OT Violations:** Violations involving the OT/ICS environment (e.g., connecting unauthorized USB drives to production PCs or introducing malware via email on OT networks) pose a direct threat to worker safety and operational continuity.
    *   **Consequence:** Violations of this nature will result in immediate disciplinary action, **up to and including termination of employment.**
    *   **Investigation:** The Company reserves the right to conduct a forensic investigation of the device to determine the scope of the violation and potential impact on the production environment.

### 5. Protected Concerted Activity & Employee Rights
Nothing in this Acceptable Use Policy is intended to restrict, prohibit, or penalize employees from engaging in activity protected under federal labor law, including but not limited to the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Employees retain the full right to freely discuss and exchange information regarding wages, hours, benefits, working conditions, workplace safety, scheduling, and other terms and conditions of employment with fellow employees, regardless of union affiliation or collective bargaining status.

The monitoring, logging, and disciplinary provisions in this AUP apply exclusively to unauthorized device usage, security violations, and actions that compromise system integrity or safety. They are not intended to, and shall not be construed to, monitor, interfere with, or discipline employees for protected communications about wages, hours, or working conditions. The company will not take adverse action against any employee for exercising rights protected by federal, state, or local labor laws.

### Pre-Rollout Implementation Notes
Before deploying this AUP across the shop floor, the manufacturer should complete the following steps:

1. **Legal & Compliance Review:** Have employment counsel and OT/ICS security leadership review the final draft to ensure alignment with state privacy notice requirements, NLRB guidance on monitoring policies, OSHA communication standards, and IEC 62443/ISA-62443 OT security frameworks.
2. **Technical Enforcement Verification:** Confirm with IT/OT engineering that USB-port controls, network segmentation, email attachment filtering on OT endpoints, and endpoint logging are actively enforced *before* policy rollout. Policy language alone cannot secure a safety-critical network.
3. **Supervisor & Workforce Communication:** Schedule briefings for both office and floor supervisors. Distribute the AUP in plain language, with translations as needed, and establish a dedicated channel for employee questions prior to acknowledgment collection.
4. **Acknowledgment Collection Process:** Implement a secure, auditable workflow for signing and storing acknowledgments (digital or physical). Devices should remain locked in asset control until signed forms are returned and verified.
5. **Pilot & Feedback Loop:** Run a controlled pilot on a single production line or department. Gather input from shop-floor users, safety personnel, and OT engineers, then adjust monitoring tooling, policy wording, or issuance procedures for clarity and practicality before enterprise-wide deployment.
