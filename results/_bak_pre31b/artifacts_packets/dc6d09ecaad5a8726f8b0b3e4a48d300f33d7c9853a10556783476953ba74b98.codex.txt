### Phase 2: Why VIP Pressure Must NOT Override Standard Process

**The "Reimage to Fix Fast" Trap**
The most dangerous instinct in this scenario is: *"Just wipe and reinstall Windows so she can work."* **Do not do this.** Here is why:

1.  **Data Destruction:** The final presentation was saved **locally** and never synced. Reimaging the disk (via SCCM, Intune, or a manual wipe) will destroy that local file unless it was backed up to an external server *first*. If you reimage, you lose the presentation permanently.
2.  **Time Budget:** A clean reimage, application deployment, and configuration takes **hours**, not minutes. You have 40 minutes. Reimaging is a guarantee of failure on the timeline.
3.  **Security Precedent:** As an elected official, the Commissioner is the public face of the county's security posture. Bypassing BitLocker (or reimaging a locked device) sets a precedent that security controls are "optional for VIPs." 4.  **TPM Integrity:** The BIOS update changed the TPM PCR values. A reimage will reset the TPM or require a new TPM enrollment. This does not fix the "drift"; it just resets the lockout mechanism. It is a **treat** rather than a **cure**.
5.  **Compliance & Audit:** Wiping a county asset without proper authorization triggers a compliance incident in the county's IT governance. This distracts the helpdesk from fixing the actual issue and creates a paper trail that could embarrass the administration during the budget hearing.

**The Non-Disruptive-First Principle:**
The goal is to restore the *session*, not the *device*. BitLocker is working exactly as designed; it is protecting the data from being read without the key. By retrieving the key, you are validating the device's integrity, not bypassing it.
