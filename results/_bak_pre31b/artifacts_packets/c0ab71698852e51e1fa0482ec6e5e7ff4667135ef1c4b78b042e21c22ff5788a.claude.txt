## 🔍 GAP ANALYSIS

### 📉 RPO Gaps
1. **Backup Frequency Mismatch:** Nightly full backups yield a maximum theoretical data loss of ~24 hours. This exceeds the 4-hour policy RPO.
2. **Missing Transaction Log Backups:** No incremental or log backup strategy exists. SQL Server cannot guarantee point-in-time recovery or meet a 4-hour RPO without continuous log backups.
3. **No Backup Window Control:** Without differential/log strategy, backup duration may exceed the 4-hour window, increasing risk of backup overlap or failure during peak litigation hours.

### ⏱️ RTO Gaps
1. **Unvalidated Recovery Process:** 18 months without a test restore means actual recovery time is unknown and highly likely to exceed 8 business hours.
2. **No Documented Runbook/Recovery Steps:** Manual restoration from a single NAS requires VM rebuild, SQL restore, dependency verification, and client communication. Without automation or pre-tested steps, 8-hour RTO is not achievable.
3. **No Isolated Recovery Environment:** Restoring directly to production creates conflict risk. Lack of a DR/standby path increases downtime and validation time.

### 🛡️ Ransomware Resilience Gaps
1. **Co-Location = Physical & Logical SPOF:** Backup NAS in the same server closet shares power, HVAC, networking, and physical access with production. A single event (power surge, fire, misconfigured switch, hardware failure) destroys both source and backup.
2. **Domain-Joined NAS + Domain Service Account = Credential Lateral Movement Vector:** Ransomware that compromises production or a user endpoint can harvest domain credentials and authenticate to the NAS. The domain service account provides persistent, privileged access, making the backup store a trivial target for encryption or deletion.
3. **No Air Gap / Immutable Storage:** Backups are continuously online and network-attached. Zero protection against silent corruption or rapid encryption propagation.
4. **No Offsite or Cloud Copy:** Entire data lifecycle resides on a single network segment. No geographic or logical separation.
5. **Single Point of Failure for Backup Infrastructure:** The NAS is both the repository and authentication endpoint. If it fails, becomes unresponsive, or is corrupted, recovery is impossible.
6. **14-Day Retention Without Integrity Validation:** Silent corruption (bit rot, failed logs, partial writes) can persist for two weeks undetected, resulting in catastrophic data loss when a restore is finally attempted.
