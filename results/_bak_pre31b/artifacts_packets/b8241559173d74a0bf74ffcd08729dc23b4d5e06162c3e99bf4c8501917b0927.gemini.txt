## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this

# Helpdesk -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Triage priority matches the actual business impact described, and the response tone is consistent with the urgency that priority implies. Troubleshooting steps are ordered from least to most disruptive, a clear escalation path is given for when the fix is out of the agent's scope, and the answer is calibrated about what genuinely needs remote hands or an on-site visit.
Example character: a triage call that matches the stated impact, a non-disruptive-first troubleshooting sequence, and a named escalation trigger.

## 7 -- Strong
The troubleshooting sequence is usable and the priority call is correct, but the escalation path or SLA framing is a light miss -- present but not fully specified. Nothing here jumps to a disruptive step too early.
Example character: a correct non-disruptive troubleshooting sequence with no stated escalation trigger if it fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is a generic troubleshooting checklist (restart, check cables, reinstall) not tied to the specific symptom or business impact described in the ticket.
Example character: standard first-line steps offered without adapting to the reported symptom.

## 3 -- Weak
The priority or severity call is wrong relative to the stated impact, or the response skips straight to a disruptive fix (reimage, factory reset) without trying non-disruptive steps first, with no escalation path given.
Example character: recommends reimaging a device as the first step for a minor, non-blocking symptom.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends a data-destructive action (wipe, reformat, delete profile) with no backup step or warning, or fabricates vendor or product behavior presented as known fact. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Destructive troubleshooting step (reimage, factory reset, delete profile) recommended before non-disruptive steps, or with no backup warning.
- Priority/SLA call that does not match the stated business impact.
- No escalation path given when the issue is clearly out of scope.
- Fabricated product or vendor behavior presented as known fact.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Triage priority matches the stated business impact.
- Troubleshooting steps ordered least-to-most disruptive.
- Explicit escalation trigger and path given.
- SLA/urgency framing consistent with the ticket's actual severity.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

A contract research organization's remote clinical research associates (CRAs) upload case
report form data to a sponsor's clinical-trial portal over the company VPN. Over the last 6
weeks, 11 separate tickets from 7 different CRAs all report the same thing: "VPN disconnects
mid-upload, I have to restart the upload." Past fixes, one per ticket, each treated as
unrelated: told a user to reconnect and retry (worked temporarily, every time); once
increased the VPN client's idle-timeout setting for one specific user; once told a different
user to switch from WiFi to a wired connection at home, which seemed to help that one user.
No common cause was ever identified across the 11 tickets. Here is the pattern nobody had
connected yet: in all 11 cases the disconnect happened between 35 and 50 minutes into the VPN
session -- never earlier, never much later. The affected CRAs are on different ISPs,
different home networks, and different laptop hardware. The VPN concentrator's own connection
logs show these sessions ending with a code unrelated to idle-timeout, and the timing lines up
closely with one fact from the concentrator's configuration: its max-session /
re-authentication interval is set to 45 minutes. State the actual root cause (not the
per-ticket fixes already tried), explain why the idle-timeout change and the wired-connection
suggestion each appeared to help their one user even though neither addressed the real cause,
give the actual fix, and say how this should have been caught after the 2nd or 3rd similar
ticket instead of the 11th.

**Root‑cause**

The VPN concentrator is configured with a **hard‑coded max‑session / re‑authentication timer of 45 minutes**.  
When a client’s session reaches exactly 45 minutes the concentrator forces a re‑authentication and terminates the tunnel, regardless of how much data has been transferred or whether the user is still actively uploading.  

The 45‑minute “dead‑spot” is what all 11 tickets have in common – every disconnect occurs between 35 and 50 minutes, never earlier or later, even though the users have different ISPs, hardware, home‑network setups, and different VPN client versions. The concentrator’s own logs show the session ending with a *session‑expire* code (the same code that is emitted when a max‑session timer fires), not an idle‑timeout or network‑error code.

| Fix that was tried | What it actually changed | Why it *appeared* to help that one user |

3. **Communicate the change** to all CRA teams and roll it out globally (or at least to all VPN profiles used for data uploads).  

After this change the tunnel will stay up for the entire duration of an upload, and users will no longer see a hard stop at the 45‑minute mark. If a brief network glitch does occur, the VPN client will automatically reconnect and the upload can resume from where it left off (most modern clients support session‑resume or checkpointing).
