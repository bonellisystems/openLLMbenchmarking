# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

This investigation plan is designed to address two distinct legal issues: **Pregnancy Discrimination (Harassment)** and **Retaliation (Adverse Employment Action).**

### 1. Addressing the Confidentiality Request
Before the investigation begins, the HR representative must meet with the complainant to address her request for 100% confidentiality.

**The Response:**
"I hear your concerns, and I want to assure you that we take your privacy very seriously. We will handle this investigation with as much discretion as possible, sharing information only with those who have a strict 'need-to-know' to complete the investigation. 

However, I cannot promise 100% confidentiality. To ensure a fair and thorough investigation, we are required to inform the supervisor of the specific allegations so they have the opportunity to respond. If we do not tell them what is being alleged, the process cannot be legally or procedurally sound. What I *can* promise is that we have a strict zero-tolerance policy regarding retaliation, and we will take immediate steps to protect you from any further adverse actions while we investigate."

**Why this is necessary:**
*   **Due Process:** In an employment context, the accused has a right to know the nature of the allegations against them to provide a defense.
*   **Legal Integrity:** If an employer fails to investigate because they cannot guarantee secrecy, they may be seen as failing to take "immediate and appropriate corrective action," which increases the county's liability.

---

### 2. Investigator Selection
To ensure impartiality and avoid "conflict of interest" or "perception of bias," the following criteria will be used:
*   **Selection:** An investigator from a different department (e.g., a County HR Director who does not oversee the employee’s department) or an external third-party investigator.
*   **Qualifications:** The investigator must be trained in Title VII and the Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA) to understand the nuances of "disparate treatment" and "retaliation."
*   **Avoidance:** The investigator must not have a direct or indirect reporting relationship with the supervisor or the complainant.

---

### 3. Interview Sequence
The order of interviews is designed to prevent the "contamination" of witnesses.

1.  **The Complainant:** To establish the scope. We need specific dates, times, exact quotes of the comments, locations where they occurred, and any potential witnesses to the comments or the project reassignment.
2.  **Witnesses:** Anyone identified by the complainant who may have overheard the comments or witnessed the "handover" of the project. We will ask them specifically about the supervisor's comments and the timing of the project change.
3.  **The Respondent (Supervisor):** Once the evidence is gathered, the supervisor will be interviewed. They will be presented with the specific allegations (e.g., "It is alleged that on [Date], you said [Quote]") and given the opportunity to respond to each point.
4.  **Follow-up (if necessary):** To clarify discrepancies between the supervisor's statement and the complainant's or witnesses' accounts.

---

### 4. Evidence to Gather
The investigator will collect both testimonial and documentary evidence to look for a "causal link" between the pregnancy disclosure and the project reassignment.

*   **Communication Logs:** Emails, Slack/Teams messages, or text messages from the supervisor to the complainant regarding her pregnancy or her workload.
*   **Project Documentation:** The timeline of the vendor-logistics project. When was the reassignment discussed? Was there a written order? Was the reassignment documented as a performance-based decision or a departmental shift?
*   **Comparative Data:** Did other employees recently lose projects? Is the complainant's performance history (prior to pregnancy) consistent with the decision to move the project?
*   **Personnel Files:** The complainant’s performance reviews and the supervisor’s past disciplinary history regarding similar complaints.

---

### 5. Interim Measures
To protect the complainant from retaliation *during* the investigation, the following measures may be implemented:

*   **Reporting Structure Change:** If the relationship has become untenable, the complainant could report to a different manager (not a demotion) temporarily.
*   **Communication Protocol:** Instructing the supervisor to keep all communications regarding work tasks strictly to email or involving a third party, to prevent further verbal harassment.
*   **Anti-Retaliation Warning:** A formal, written directive sent to the supervisor explicitly stating that any change in the employee's work duties, schedule, or environment during the investigation will be viewed as retaliation and may result in immediate termination.
*   **Monitoring:** HR will schedule brief check-ins with the complainant every week during the investigation to ensure no further adverse actions have occurred.

# Investigation Plan: Alleged Pregnancy Discrimination & Retaliation

## I. Acknowledgment and Immediate Actions
Upon receipt of this written complaint, HR will issue a written acknowledgment to the employee within **48 hours**. This letter will confirm receipt, state the investigation timeline, and explicitly detail the interim measures being activated to ensure safety (see Section IV).

## II. Impartial Investigator Selection
To ensure the integrity of the process and protect the employee's rights, the investigator must meet strict neutrality criteria.

**Selection Criteria:**
*   **No Reporting Relationship:** The investigator must not have a direct or indirect reporting relationship with the complainant (the employee) or the respondent (the supervisor).
*   **Seniority & Training:** The investigator should be a senior HR business partner, the HR Director, or an external consultant with specialized training in employment law, Title VII, the Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA), and conflict resolution.
*   **Conflict of Interest Disclosure:** Any staff member assigned must sign a conflict-of-interest waiver acknowledging that the complaint involves their unit or chain of command.
*   **External vs. Internal:** Given that the complaint involves a direct supervisor and a high-stakes pregnancy claim, **it is strongly recommended to assign an external consultant or a second-level HR review** rather than the immediate supervisor's peer. This reduces the risk of institutional bias or collusion.

## III. Addressing the Confidentiality Request
*This is the most critical communication point.* The investigator must have a candid conversation with the employee regarding their request for "100% confidentiality."

### What HR Can Promise
1.  **Anti-Retaliation Assurance:** HR can promise that the employee is legally protected from retaliation. If retaliation occurs, it will be treated as a separate, serious violation.
2.  **Need-to-Know Access:** HR can promise that the investigation file will be restricted. Only individuals with a specific "need to know" to conduct the investigation (the investigator, legal counsel, and the HR Director) will have access to the full written file.
3.  **General Privacy:** HR can promise that the employee will not be publicly singled out during the process.
4.  **No Unnecessary Disclosure:** HR will not volunteer the existence of the investigation to anyone outside the investigation team unless legally required.

### What HR Cannot Promise
1.  **Total Secrecy:** HR **cannot** promise that the employee's identity will never be shared.
    *   **Why:** Due process requires that the accused supervisor be notified of the general nature of the allegations so they can prepare a defense. In a government context, the accused has a right to respond to the specific claims made against them.
    *   **The Consequence:** If the investigation proceeds without the supervisor knowing what they are being accused of, the findings may be legally indefensible, and the county will lose in any subsequent administrative or judicial review.
2.  **Preventing Rumor:** HR cannot control how other employees discuss the investigation internally.
3.  **Outcome Control:** HR cannot guarantee the outcome of the investigation.

### Recommended Script for the Employee
> *"I hear your concern, and I understand why you want this to be private. I want to be honest with you: I cannot promise that your name will never appear in a file that the accused supervisor has access to, because a fair investigation requires both sides to be heard. However, I can promise you the following: your identity will not be shared with anyone outside this investigation team unless legally required; you are protected from retaliation under Title VII and our internal anti-retaliation policy; and I will do everything I can to minimize disruption to your work while this is active. We will proceed with the investigation, but I need your agreement to participate so that the findings are legally sound."*

## IV. Interim Measures to Protect the Employee
While the investigation is pending, the county must take steps to prevent further harm, ensure safety, and protect the employee's job security.

1.  **Immediate Separation of Duties:** If the supervisor is the one making the comments, HR must immediately ensure the supervisor does not have direct, unsupervised contact with the employee (e.g., requiring all interactions to be documented or in writing, or changing the workspace).
2.  **Project Reassignment (Protective, Not Punitive):** The project reassignment is noted but not assumed to be retaliatory without investigation. However, the employee should not be *punished* for the reassignment. HR should ensure the workload is not increased to punish the employee while the investigation runs.
3.  **Pregnancy Accommodation Review:** HR should immediately consult with legal counsel regarding the employee's eligibility for pregnancy accommodation (e.g., reduced hours, remote work, light duty) under the PDA and the ADA. These are available regardless of the investigation's outcome.
4.  **No Disciplinary Action Pending:** HR should not discipline the supervisor based solely on the written complaint until the investigation is complete, to avoid "pre-emptive punishment" which could be seen as an admission of guilt or bias.
5.  **Retaliation Monitoring:** HR should monitor for any subtle changes in the employee's treatment (e.g., exclusion from meetings, changed schedule) and document them as potential retaliation if they occur.
6.  **FMLA/Leave Options:** Inform the employee of their rights to Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) or unpaid leave if the work environment becomes hostile, ensuring they are not penalized for taking medical leave.

## V. Evidence Gathering
The investigator must collect all relevant documents before conducting interviews to prevent witnesses from being influenced.

**Documentary Evidence:**
1.  **The Written Complaint:** The employee's letter, dated and signed.
2.  **Email/Slack/Calendar Records:** Search for all correspondence between the employee and supervisor, specifically around the date the employee disclosed her pregnancy and the date the project was reassigned.
3.  **Performance Reviews:** Pull the employee's last 2-3 performance evaluations. Look for changes in rating, language, or criteria after the pregnancy disclosure.
4.  **Attendance Records:** Check for any changes in the employee's attendance or schedule immediately following the disclosure.
5.  **Project Logs:** Compare the project timeline before and after the reassignment to see if the work stalled or if the transition was abrupt.
6.  **Prior Complaints:** Check if the employee or other staff have filed prior complaints against the supervisor.
7.  **Vendor Logs:** Check if the project reassignment was a standard procedure or if there is a legitimate, documented business reason for the change.

**Physical/Digital Evidence:**
1.  **Record of the Reassignment:** Get the email or memo where the project was transferred. Note who authorized it.
2.  **Witness Communication Logs:** Identify if anyone in the office has reported hearing the comments.

## VI. Interview Sequence
The order of interviews is critical to prevent witness tampering and to preserve the integrity of the evidence.

**Phase 1: Preparation (Before Interviews)**
*   Review all documentary evidence gathered.
*   Draft interview questions based on the facts found.
*   Brief the investigator on the legal standards (preponderance of evidence).

**Phase 2: The Complainant (Employee)**
*   **Why First:** To establish the timeline and baseline facts before the supervisor or witnesses can alter their accounts or influence them.
*   **Scope:** Confirm dates, specific quotes, the context of the comments, the timeline of the project reassignment, and any other instances of differential treatment.
*   **Documentation:** Take detailed notes. Record the employee's understanding of the supervisor's behavior.

**Phase 3: The Respondent (Supervisor)**
*   **Timing:** Immediately after the complainant interview.
*   **Procedure:** Present the *general* nature of the allegations (without revealing the complainant's identity if the employee consents, or presenting it as a protected complaint if required by policy).
*   **Opportunity to Respond:** Allow the supervisor to explain their actions, provide context for the reassignment (e.g., "I reassigned it because of X reason"), and offer their side of the comments made.
*   **Why:** Due process requires the accused to have a chance to respond.

**Phase 4: Witnesses**
*   **Who:** Colleagues who worked on the project, peers in the department, and anyone who may have overheard the comments.
*   **Timing:** After the initial interviews (complainant and respondent).
*   **Why:** Witnesses often change their stories after hearing the accused's defense. Interviewing them before the accused has had a chance to speak preserves the independent account.
*   **Scope:** Ask about the employee's performance, treatment, and any overheard comments.

**Phase 5: Supervisor's Chain of Command**
*   **Who:** The supervisor's manager or the HR Director.
*   **Purpose:** To confirm if the project reassignment was approved by the manager, and to check if any prior concerns were raised.

## VII. Findings and Reporting
*   **Timeline:** The investigation should conclude within **30 days** of receipt of the complaint.
*   **Report:** A written report will be prepared for the HR Director.
*   **Standards:** Findings will be based on the "preponderance of the evidence" (whether it is more likely than not that the conduct occurred).
*   **Outcome:**
    *   If discrimination is found: Corrective action for the supervisor (counseling, retraining, disciplinary action, or removal).
    *   If not found: A clear explanation will be provided to the employee.
*   **Feedback:** The employee will be informed of the general outcome (without disclosing confidential details of other witnesses) and informed of their right to file a charge with the EEOC or relevant state agency if they disagree with the findings.

## VIII. Why This Plan Matters
This plan balances the employee's safety and privacy with the county's legal obligation to conduct a fair, thorough, and legally defensible investigation. By acknowledging the confidentiality request honestly, HR builds trust with the employee while maintaining the procedural integrity required to hold the county harmless from liability.

## 7. Confidentiality – What Can Be Promised & What Cannot  

### 7.1. Legal & Policy Framework  

| Requirement | Impact on Confidentiality |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Title VII / PDA** | The employer must **investigate** the complaint; the investigation process inherently involves informing certain parties (the accused supervisor, witnesses, and possibly senior management). |
| **County Confidentiality Policy** | Limits disclosure to “need‑to‑know” personnel; however, absolute secrecy is not feasible when an investigation is conducted. |
| **Retaliation‑protection statutes** | Require that the employer **inform the accused of the allegations** and give them an opportunity to respond. |
| **Document‑retention & discovery** | All investigation records become part of the agency’s official file and may be disclosed in a subsequent EEOC, civil rights, or court proceeding. |

### 7.2. What We Can Promise  

| Promise | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|

    *   **Credibility:** If the complaint escalates to litigation or a government audit, an external report carries higher evidentiary weight.
    *   **Conflict Check:** The selected firm must confirm no prior representation of the County, the Supervisor, or the Employee.
    *   *Alternative:* If external resources are unavailable, the investigation must be assigned to an HR Director or Employment Law Advisor from a **different department/county office** who has no reporting relationship with the Supervisor and no prior involvement with the Employee.

### 2. Interim Measures (Protection During Investigation)

Interim measures must be non-punitive, preserve the status quo, and prevent retaliation without prematurely adjudicating the claims.

*   **Anti-Retaliation Reinforcement:**

*   **Workplace Access & Workload:**
    *   Monitor the Employee's access to work, schedules, and opportunities. Ensure no further reassignments or exclusion occurs.
    *   **No "No-Contact" Order at this stage:** A no-contact order may signal guilt and escalate tensions. It is reserved for cases involving harassment where contact is ongoing and hostile. Here, standard professional boundaries should be maintained.
*   **Reporting Structure:**
    *   *Do not* immediately remove the Supervisor or move the Employee, as this may be viewed as punitive or an admission of liability.
    *   If the Employee requests a temporary change due to anxiety or inability to work effectively, this can be discussed as a voluntary accommodation, but it should not be imposed by the investigator.
*   **Documentation Preservation:**
    *   Issue a **litigation hold** notice to IT and HR to preserve all relevant emails, calendar data, performance files, and communications involving the Employee and Supervisor.

Before interviews, the investigator will collect the following to establish context and identify witnesses:

*   **Employment Records:** Employee's and Supervisor's job descriptions, organizational charts, and tenure history.
*   **Performance Data:** Recent performance evaluations for the Employee to assess if workload capacity was ever an issue prior to pregnancy.
*   **Project Documentation:**
    *   Scope and status of the "county-fair vendor-logistics project."
    *   Emails/memos regarding the reassignment of this project.
    *   Criteria used for assigning the project to the colleague.
    *   Career trajectory data: Is the Employee up for promotion? Is this project critical for advancement? (To assess if reassignment is an "adverse action.")

### 4. Interview Sequence

The sequence is designed to preserve evidence, assess credibility, and prevent coaching.

1.  **Interview 1: The Complainant (Employee)**
    *   *Purpose:* Formal intake, scope the allegations, obtain specific dates/quotes, identify witnesses, and discuss the impact of the reassignment.
    *   *Key Topics:* Exact wording of comments; dates of comments; details of the pregnancy announcement; rationale provided by Supervisor for reassignment; whether the colleague requested the project; how the reassignment affects her career goals.
2.  **Interview 2: The Colleague (Recipient of Project)**
    *   *Purpose:* Corroborate the timeline and understand the business rationale for the reassignment.
    *   *Key Topics:* Why were you assigned the project? Did the Supervisor mention the Employee's pregnancy? Did you volunteer for the project? Were there performance issues with the Employee?
3.  **Interview 3: The Subject (Supervisor)**
    *   *Purpose:* Confront allegations, obtain the defense, and assess credibility.
    *   *Key Topics:* Response to specific comments ("handle workload," "pop"); justification for reassigning the project; business necessity; awareness of County pregnancy policies; timeline of decision-making.

### 5. Response to Confidentiality Request

**This requires a delicate, honest, and policy-based conversation.** The investigator must manage expectations immediately to maintain trust while adhering to legal obligations.

#### What Can Be Promised:
*   **"Need-to-Know" Standard:** The County will limit disclosure to individuals who are essential to the investigation.
*   **No Gossip:** The investigator will not discuss the complaint with peers, other supervisors, or general HR staff.
*   **Retaliation Prohibition:** The County will strictly enforce anti-retaliation policies. If the Supervisor or others discover the complaint and take adverse action, that will be treated as a serious violation.
*   **Limited Disclosure to Subject:** The investigator will not share the *specifics* of the evidence or the Complainant's emotional state with the Supervisor, only the allegations necessary for the Supervisor to respond.

#### What Cannot Be Promised (and Why):
*   **Absolute Confidentiality:** "I cannot promise 100% confidentiality. An effective investigation requires interviewing your supervisor to give him a fair opportunity to respond. To do that, he will need to know that a formal concern regarding his conduct has been raised. While I can ask him to keep it confidential, I cannot control his reaction or guarantee he won't infer you were the complainant."
*   **Interviewing Witnesses:** "We may need to interview the colleague who received the project. While we will not disclose your identity, the context of the interview may reveal that the complaint relates to the project reassignment you were involved in."
*   **Corrective Action:** "If we find merit to the complaint, corrective action will likely be taken. This may include disciplinary action against the supervisor or changes to reporting structures. These actions might be visible to you or discussed in management meetings, which could indirectly reveal that a complaint was filed."
*   **Legal Requirements:** "If this matter leads to litigation or a government inquiry, certain details may be subject to discovery or disclosure under public records laws, depending on our jurisdiction's statutes regarding internal investigations."

#### Recommended Script for the Employee:
> "I want to respect your wish for privacy, and we will work hard to protect you. We will keep this strictly limited to the investigation team.
