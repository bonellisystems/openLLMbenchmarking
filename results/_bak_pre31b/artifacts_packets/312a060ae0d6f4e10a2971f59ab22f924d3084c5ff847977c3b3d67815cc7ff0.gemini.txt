## 2. WARN Notice Timing Flag & Gap Analysis

**⚠️ CRITICAL LEGAL FLAG ⚠️**

### The Problem
*   **WARN Act Applicability:** The closure triggers the **WARN Act** (and likely state WARN laws) because:
    1.  **Site Threshold:** 32 employees affected at a single site (Threshold: 30+).
    2.  **Company Threshold:** 210 total employees (Threshold: 100+).
    3.  **Event Type:** Plant closing/Layoff resulting from loss of business.
*   **Notice Requirement:** The Federal WARN Act requires **60 days** of written notice. Many state laws require the same or longer notice.
*   **The Gap:** You cannot legally close in **45 days** if you issue WARN notice today. The effective date of the layoff must be at least **60 days** after the date of notice. Closing in 45 days constitutes a **shortening of notice**, which incurs significant penalties (back pay, benefits, and liquidated damages) for the employees laid off.

### Immediate Actions to Manage the Gap

**Option A: Extend Effective Date (Recommended)**
*   **Action:** Delay the physical closure and layoff effective date to **60 days** from the date of notice issuance.
*   **Rationale:** This is the safest path to avoid liability. The business can operate in a "wind-down" mode for the extra 15 days.
*   **Risk:** Slight operational cost increase for 15 days; potential delay in re-deploying assets.

**Option B: "Shortening of Notice" Exception (High Risk/Fact-Intensive)**
*   **Analysis:** WARN allows shortening notice by 10 days (bringing it to 50 days) or more under strict conditions (29 CFR § 253.23), such as:
    *   The employer can prove the decision is *not* based on economic conditions (hard to prove here given the lost contract).
    *   The employer offers a severance/economic incentive package to the employees.
*   **Recommendation:** This path is **complex**. You cannot close in 45 days; you can potentially close in 50 days if a structured severance package is offered. Closing in 45 days is likely still a violation. **Consult counsel immediately** if you pursue this.

**Option C: Voluntary Separation Incentive (VSI) / Restructuring**
*   **Action:** Instead of a mandatory layoff, offer voluntary buyouts or transfer opportunities.
*   **Risk:** If the company forces the closure and employees are laid off anyway, VSI does not necessarily remove WARN notice requirements. However, it can sometimes allow the company to argue the closing was not necessary if enough employees leave voluntarily. **Legal review required.**

### 🚨 Immediate Directive
1.  **Pause:** Do not issue final notice or schedule the 45-day closure until counsel confirms the path.
2.  **Consult:** Engage employment counsel to review state-specific WARN laws (some states have "60-day + 10 days" or immediate notice triggers).
3.  **Draft:** Prepare WARN notice to be issued on **[Date: Today]**, setting the new effective closure date to **[Date: 60 days out]**.

## 3. Standard Layoff Elements

These elements should be included in the "Separation Packet" distributed on the day of the meeting.

### A. Severance Package
*   **Structure:** Based on tenure and contract value.
*   **Recommended Formula:**
    *   *Base:* 1 week of pay per year of service.
    *   *Multiplier:* 1.5x for employees with >5 years tenure.
    *   *Example:* Employee with 2 years receives 2 weeks; Employee with 10 years receives 15 weeks.
*   **Conditions:** Severance is contingent upon signing a **Release of Claims** waiving further liability, and participation in an outplacement program (if required).
*   **Timing:** First payment upon separation; remaining installments over 30-60 days to encourage retention of data/confidentiality.

### B. Benefits Continuation
*   **COBRA:** Inform employees of their right to continue health insurance at their own expense under COBRA for 18 months.
*   **401(k) / Retirement:**
    *   Option to rollover to new account or leave in plan.
    *   Company match contribution is forfeited after separation (unless vested).
    *   Provide statements immediately.
*   **Unemployment:** Information on how to file for state unemployment benefits. *Note: Severance payments may impact eligibility; advise employees to consult their state agency.*

### C. References
*   **Policy:** Company will provide a professional reference upon request for the duration of the notice period and up to 12 months post-separation.
*   **Mechanism:** HR will be the sole point of contact for references to ensure consistency.
*   **Statement:** "We value our former employees and encourage our other leaders to provide positive references where warranted."

### D. Outplacement Support
*   **Purpose:** Assist with job search and reduce future liability.
*   **Vendor:** Engage a third-party outplacement provider (or internal HR team) to offer:
    *   Resume/LinkedIn optimization workshops.
    *   Mock interviews.
    *   Job search coaching.
    *   Access to a job board.
*   **Commitment:** Offer this for a minimum of 6 months post-separation.

## 2. WARN‑Notice Timing Flag & Immediate Gap‑Closure Actions

### 2.1 Why 45 days is a red flag

| **Regulation** | **Threshold** | **Notice Period Required** | **Our Situation** |
|----------------|---------------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Federal WARN Act** | Plant closing affecting **≥ 50** employees (or 25 % of workforce) at a single site, **or** a mass layoff of **≥ 500** employees total | **60 days** written notice | 32 employees → **Below federal threshold** (no statutory WARN liability). |
| **State WARN‑type statutes** (examples) | • California: ≥ 75 employees <br>• New York: ≥ 50 employees <br>• Illinois: ≥ 50 employees <br>• Texas: No state WARN (uses federal only) | 60 days (CA, NY) or 45‑60 days (varies) | **Potential exposure** if the office is in a state with a lower threshold (e.g., ≥ 50 in NY/IL). 45‑day notice may be **non‑compliant** under those statutes. |
| **Local ordinances / collective bargaining agreements** | May impose even shorter thresholds (e.g., 30 days) | Varies | Must be checked. |

**Bottom line:** Even if the federal WARN does **not** apply, **state‑level WARN‑type laws may**. The 45‑day notice window is **shorter than the typical 60‑day period** most employers use to stay safely on the right side of the law.

### 2.2 Immediate actions to mitigate the gap

| **Step** | **What to do** | **Who leads** | **Timing** | **Why it matters** |
|----------|----------------|---------------|------------|--------------------|
| **1️⃣ Confirm jurisdiction** | Verify the exact state (and any city/county) where the office is located; pull the state‑WARN statute, any applicable local ordinances, and any collective‑bargaining provisions. | Corporate Counsel + HR Compliance Lead | **Today** (within 8 h) | Determines legal exposure; if the state threshold is ≤ 32, you must issue a 60‑day notice or risk penalties. |
| **2️⃣ Issue a “Supplemental Notice”** | Draft a **voluntary 60‑day notice** (or the longest period allowed under the applicable law) and **deliver it today**, even though the actual closure date is 45 days away. | HR + Legal | **Today** (same day as announcement) | Provides the “extra” 15 days the law expects; reduces risk of penalties and demonstrates good‑faith effort. |

#### The Gap: 45 Days vs. 60 Days

**Federal WARN Requirement:**
Under the federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act (29 U.S.C. § 2101), covered employers generally must provide **60 calendar days** written notice before a qualifying "plant closing" or "mass layoff."

*   **Coverage Threshold:** Federal WARN applies to employers with 100+ full-time employees (the company has 210). However, a "partial plant closing" typically requires a loss of **50+ employees** within a 90-day period, OR 33% of the workforce at a single site (if the workforce exceeds 100).
    *   *Note:* With 32 affected employees out of 210 (15%), the company may technically fall below the strict federal WARN threshold for a partial plant closing (which usually requires 50 employees at a site).
    *   *However,* many **state WARN laws** have different thresholds. Some states (e.g., California, Colorado) have lower triggers or broader definitions of "plant closing" that may apply even to smaller headcount reductions.

**The Risk:**
If state WARN laws apply, or if the company voluntarily elects to follow the federal 60-day standard as a best practice, closing in **45 days is insufficient**. Failure to provide adequate notice can result in:
1.  **Liquidated Damages:** Backpay equal to 60 days of wages for each affected employee.
2.  **Attorney's Fees:** The company pays the employees' legal costs.
3.  **Reputational Risk:** Negative press or regulatory scrutiny.

#### Immediate Actions Required (Next 24-48 Hours)

1.  **Engage Employment Counsel Immediately:** Do not finalize the closure date without legal review. Counsel must determine:
    *   Does state WARN law apply (thresholds, notice period, penalties)?

    *   Is there a "faltering company" exception?
2.  **Assess the "Partial Plant Closing" Definition:** Verify if the 32 employees constitute a "plant closing" under local law. If state law requires notice for fewer than 50 employees, the 45-day timeline is likely non-compliant.
3.  **Extend the Timeline (If Possible):** If the contract loss cannot be reversed, can the closure be delayed? Even a 15-day extension could bring the timeline to 60 days.
4.  **Prepare for WARN Penalties:** If the closure cannot be delayed, the company should be prepared to pay WARN liquidated damages (60 days of wages) to affected employees to cover the shortfall, potentially reducing the need for a separate severance negotiation.
5.  **Notify State Dislocated Worker Unit:** If state WARN applies, the company is legally required to notify the state agency *before* the employees receive notice.

### 2. WARN-Notice Timing Flag & Immediate Actions
**FLAG: NOTICE GAP DETECTED.** The planned closure is in 45 days; standard notice is 60 days. The company is short by 15 days.

#### A. Threshold Analysis (Crucial Step)
*   **Federal WARN:** Requires 60 days notice for a "Plant Closing" (50+ employees at one site) or "Mass Layoff" (50+ employees AND 33% of workforce, or 500+ total).
    *   *Assessment:* With 32 employees, this likely falls **below** the federal numerical threshold for a Plant Closing. However, if the site's total workforce is small (e.g., 40 employees), 32/40 = 80%, which triggers the Mass Layoff percentage test **only if** the numerical count is met. Since 32 < 50, Federal WARN may not apply.
    *   *Action:* **Do not assume Federal WARN is irrelevant.** State laws differ drastically.
*   **State WARN/Mini-Warns:** Many states have lower thresholds (e.g., California: 50+ in 30 days; New York: 75+; New Jersey: 50+; Illinois: varies; others have no numerical threshold for closures).
    *   *Risk:* If the state threshold is met (e.g., threshold is 20 employees), notice is required. If the state requires 60 days and you have 45, the gap exists.
    *   *Action:* Counsel must confirm the jurisdictional trigger immediately.

#### B. Addressing the 15-Day Gap
If notice is required (Federal or State), the company has two primary options to mitigate risk:
1.  **Pay in Lieu of Notice:** The safest course is to pay all affected employees wages and benefits for the 15-day gap. This converts the notice requirement to a payment obligation and eliminates the risk of penalties/litigation.
2.  **Unforeseen Circumstances Defense:** WARN allows a 60-day reduction if the closing is due to "unforeseen business circumstances."
    *   *Test:* The company must show that the contract loss was unforeseeable and that giving notice was impracticable.
    *   *Risk:* If the contract expiration was known >60 days ago, or if the company delayed the decision, this defense fails. If the defense is weak, the company risks paying **back pay, benefits, and civil penalties** for the full 60 days (or the gap, depending on jurisdiction), plus attorney fees.

#### C. Immediate Action Items
1.  **Legal Review:** Employment counsel to analyze the specific state/federal thresholds and the timeline of the contract loss notification.
2.  **Calculate Liability:** Quantify the cost of paying 15 days of wages/benefits for all 32 employees. Compare this cost to the potential risk of litigation.
3.  **Decision:** Recommend paying the 15-day difference to "cure" the notice gap, unless legal counsel confirms the "Unforeseen Circumstances" exception applies with high certainty.
4.  **State-Specific Checks:** Verify if state laws require notice of **termination** (not just WARN) with shorter windows or different rules. Some states require immediate written notice of termination regardless of WARN.
5.  **Draft Notice:** Prepare the WARN notice (if applicable) to be distributed to the affected employees and state/disaster relief agencies immediately upon legal sign-off.

5. **Recordkeeping** – Keep copies of all filings, employee notices, and correspondence for at least 3 years.
