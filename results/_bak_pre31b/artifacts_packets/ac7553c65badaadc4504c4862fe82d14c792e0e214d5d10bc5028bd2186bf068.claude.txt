## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.

- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers

**Incident ID:** TTX-IR-2023-0914

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This incident presents a four-dimensional convergence of a critical vulnerability exploitation (CVE-2023-4966), active ransomware encryption of privileged litigation data, a time-bound financial demand, and restrictive cyber insurance terms.

The response sequence is dictated not by speed alone, but by the interplay between **preservation of privilege**, **insurance coverage validity**, and **containment efficacy**. The following walk-through prioritizes operations to ensure the firm survives financially and legally.

## PHASE 1: TECHNICAL CONTAINMENT (Priority: Immediate)
**Objective:** Stop lateral movement and encryption spread without relying on identity controls that are already compromised.

**The MFA Reality:**
Per the injected facts, MFA is functionally useless in this scenario. CVE-2023-4966 ("Citrix Bleed") allows an attacker to replay a valid session token to access the NetScaler Gateway. Because the token represents an *already-authenticated* session, the MFA challenge is never triggered. Therefore, any containment step that relies on "lockout" or "re-enrollment" is a false sense of security.

**Ordered Containment Steps:**

1.  **Network Segmentation (Do NOT Power Off FS-DOCS02):**
    *   Immediately isolate FS-DOCS02 from the corporate VLAN at the switch level (VLAN shutdown or ACL drop). This stops the ransomware from propagating to other file servers or the email gateway.
    *   *Why:* Powering off FS-DOCS02 destroys volatile RAM state (encryption keys, active processes). We need the live memory image for forensic analysis (see Phase 2).
2.  **NetScaler ADC Session Termination & Patching:**
    *   Force all active Gateway sessions to terminate via the NetScaler management console.
    *   Revoke all session tokens in the database.
    *   **Do NOT patch the NetScaler ADC yet.** Patching changes the environment and may alter logs or memory state relevant to the initial entry vector. Patch after evidence preservation is complete.
3.  **Outbound Traffic Block:**
    *   Block all outbound traffic from FS-DOCS02 at the perimeter firewall.
    *   Block inbound connections to FS-DOCS02.
    *   *Goal:* Prevent C2 (Command & Control) communication and prevent the ransomware from attempting to spread to backup systems or external storage.
4.  **Credential Rotation:**
    *   Rotate all NetScaler Administrator credentials.
    *   Reset passwords for all accounts that accessed the Gateway in the last 48 hours.
    *   *Note:* This does not invalidate the stolen token, but it ensures the attacker cannot use new session tokens to move laterally from the Gateway to other systems.
5.  **EDR Isolation:**
    *   Quarantine FS-DOCS02 from the EDR management server to prevent the EDR agent from potentially alerting the attacker (if they have admin access) or to ensure the EDR agent does not interfere with memory capture.

## PHASE 2: EVIDENCE PRESERVATION ON FS-DOCS02
**Objective:** Capture forensic evidence without violating the active litigation hold or waiving attorney-client privilege.

**The Legal Constraint:**
The firm holds an active litigation hold on FS-DOCS02 (three active matters, attorney work product). Standard IR playbooks often recommend "powering off" the host to stop encryption. **In this scenario, powering off FS-DOCS02 is a spoliation risk.** If the firm destroys data in violation of the litigation hold, opposing counsel can argue spoliation of evidence, potentially resulting in adverse inferences or sanctions.

**Ordered Preservation Steps:**

1.  **Forensic Image Capture (Bit-for-Bit):**
    *   Deploy a forensic imaging tool (e.g., FTK Imager, EnCase) from a *clean, non-compromised* system to FS-DOCS02.
    *   Capture a full disk image (including unallocated space) to preserve encryption artifacts, file deletion markers, and ransom notes.
    *   Capture a memory dump (RAM image) to identify the ransomware payload hash, encryption keys, and lateral movement indicators.
2.  **Litigation Hold Compliance Documentation:**
    *   Issue a formal Litigation Hold Notice to all relevant attorneys and staff.
    *   Document that FS-DOCS02 has been placed under "forensic hold" to comply with the litigation hold.
    *   Ensure that no one (including the IR team) deletes temporary files, logs, or encryption artifacts on the host.
3.  **Privilege Marking:**
    *   All evidence collected from FS-DOCS02 must be marked as **Privileged and Confidential**.
    *   Chain of custody logs must be maintained by the firm's outside counsel or legal team, not solely by the IT team.
4.  **No Remediation Before Imaging:**
    *   Do not run antivirus, do not run decryption tools, and do not restore from backups until the forensic image is complete.
    *   Remediation actions could overwrite the encryption state, destroying evidence of the attack chain.
5.  **Network Logs Preservation:**
    *   Export all NetScaler ADC logs, firewall logs, and EDR logs from the last 7 days.
    *   Store these logs on a separate, offline server to prevent tampering.

## Table‑top Exercise – Incident Response for a Mid‑Size Law Firm

| **Day 1 15:00** | Ransom note demands 12 BTC (~$780k) within 96 hrs. | Cyber‑insurance policy is claims‑made with a consent‑to‑settle clause that requires the carrier’s pre‑approval of any incident‑response vendor engagement or costs before the firm can spend against the policy. |

The exercise walks through the response in **priority order**, addressing the four specific questions:

1. **Technical containment given MFA does not help.**  
2. **Preserving evidence on FS‑DOCS02 without violating litigation hold.**  
3. **Stakeholders to engage before remediation spending and why the consent‑to‑settle clause matters.**  
4. **Approach to the ransom demand without unconditionally recommending payment.**

---

## 1. Immediate Technical Containment (08:14–09:30)

| Step | Action | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **1.1. Isolate the compromised NetScaler** | • Disable or disconnect the NetScaler from the production network.<br>• If isolation is not feasible, block all inbound traffic to the ADC and redirect it to a quarantine segment. | Stops further session‑token theft and protects other services that rely on the ADC. |
| **1.2. Revoke all active session tokens** | • In the NetScaler admin console, force‑logout all users and revoke all active session tokens.<br>• Force re‑authentication for all users. | Removes the stolen token from circulation; MFA re‑prompting is no longer needed. |
| **1.3. Apply the latest Citrix patch** | • Immediately apply the vendor‑issued hot‑fix for CVE‑2023‑4966 (e.g., Citrix NetScaler 12.0.2.6 or later).<br>• Verify the patch integrity using checksums. | Fixes the root vulnerability that allows token theft. |
| **1.4. Harden the NetScaler** | • Disable legacy authentication protocols (e.g., NTLM).<br>• Enable session time‑outs and IP‑based restrictions.<br>• Enable logging of all authentication attempts. | Reduces attack surface for future exploitation attempts. |
| **1.5. Network segmentation** | • Place the NetScaler, FS‑DOCS02, and other critical servers on a separate VLAN with strict egress/ingress controls.<br>• Use micro‑segmentation to isolate FS‑DOCS02 from other workstations. | Limits lateral movement and protects the file server from further compromise. |

**Why MFA doesn’t help** – The attacker steals an authenticated session token and replays it, so the MFA challenge is never triggered. Re‑prompting for MFA is ineffective; the only reliable defense is to revoke stolen tokens, patch the vulnerability, and isolate the appliance.

### Why Acting First Could Jeopardize Coverage
1. **Breach of “Consent‑to‑Settle”** – If the firm pays a vendor or initiates a ransom negotiation **without the insurer’s written approval**, the expense is deemed “unapproved” and the insurer can refuse to reimburse, even if the loss would otherwise be covered.
2. **Potential Policy Exclusion** – Some cyber policies exclude “ransom payments” unless a specific rider is in place. Early payment without insurer consent may trigger an exclusion, turning the payment into an **uninsured loss**.
3. **Audit Trail** – The insurer will later audit all expenditures. Documented, pre‑approved spendings provide the audit evidence needed to prove that the firm acted in good faith and complied with policy conditions.
4. **Attorney‑Client Privilege** – If legal counsel is bypassed and the firm directly engages a “ransom negotiation” firm, communications may not be privileged, exposing the firm to discovery in litigation or regulatory investigations.

> **Bottom‑Line:** **All remediation‑related spend** (vendor contracts, third‑party forensic services, any ransom‑payment facilitation) **must be cleared by the insurer’s claims team** *before* any purchase order or payment is issued.

### **1. TECHNICAL CONTAINMENT: Addressing the MFA Bypass**
**Inject Analysis:** The attacker exploited CVE-2023-4966 ("Citrix Bleed") to steal authenticated session tokens. MFA is bypassed because the stolen token represents a session that has already passed authentication. Resetting MFA credentials or re-enrolling users is **ineffective** as it does not invalidate the stolen token currently in the attacker's possession.

**Containment Actions (Priority Order):**

*   **A. Invalidate NetScaler Sessions Globally or Targeted:**
    *   *Action:* The ADC administrator must immediately execute commands to clear all active sessions on the NetScaler ADC/Gateway.
    *   *Technical Detail:* Use `set ns session -clearAll` or revoke specific session tokens identified by the SOC. This forces re-authentication. Since the attacker holds the token, they will be dropped. When they attempt to re-authenticate, they will trigger MFA (assuming MFA is properly configured for re-authentication and not just initial login), at which point the compromised credentials can be flagged.
    *   *Note:* Do not rely on token expiration; force immediate revocation.

*   **B. Isolate FS-DOCS02 and Segregate Subnets:**
    *   *Action:* Network isolation of FS-DOCS02 immediately to prevent further encryption or lateral movement from this pivot point.
    *   *Technical Detail:* Apply firewall rules to block all traffic to/from FS-DOCS02 except for authorized forensic imaging channels. Do **not** power off the server, as this destroys RAM-resident artifacts (encryption keys, active connections) needed for forensics.

*   **C. Kill Encrypted Processes and Block Lateral Movement:**
    *   *Action:* Use EDR to kill the ransomware processes. Search for the attacker's session tokens in other endpoints.
    *   *Technical Detail:* Since the attacker has valid ADC tokens, they may have accessed other resources. Hunt for the stolen token values or associated session IDs across the environment. Block internal traffic between workstations and sensitive servers (Active Directory, other file shares) to limit blast radius.

*   **D. Patch NetScaler Immediately:**
    *   *Action:* Once sessions are cleared, apply the vendor patch for CVE-2023-4966 to close the vulnerability.
    *   *Risk:* Ensure configuration backup before patching to prevent downtime extending the incident.
