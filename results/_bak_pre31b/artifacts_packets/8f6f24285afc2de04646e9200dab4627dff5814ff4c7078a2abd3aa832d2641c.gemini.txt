## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

# IT Onboarding Document: Critical Operations & Security Protocols

**Department:** Information Technology
**Audience:** New IT Support Staff
**Context:** Transferred knowledge from outgoing Senior IT Admin

---

### Protocol 1: Matter Folder Creation in iManage

**The Rule**
Do not create a new matter folder in iManage until the Records team has explicitly confirmed that the conflict check is clear.

**Why This Matters**
Creating a matter folder prematurely grants file access to anyone added to that folder immediately. If a conflict check later comes back positive, you will have already distributed access to the wrong personnel. In a law firm, this is not just an administrative error; it is an ethics violation involving unauthorized exposure of confidential client data.

---

### Protocol 2: Ethical Walls & Client Restrictions

**The Rule**
When a request appears to involve a client on the firm's restricted list, or when a matter name is ambiguous, pause and verify with Records immediately. Do not guess based on the matter name alone.

**Why This Matters**
Attorney-client privilege and ethical obligations create "walls" that separate certain clients from each other. If you grant access based on your own judgment rather than the restricted list, you risk inadvertently breaching an active ethical wall. Records maintains the authoritative list; guessing is never a valid security decision.

---

### Protocol 3: iManage Access Authorization

**The Rule**
iManage access for a new matter must follow the matter team list defined on the official intake form. It does not follow ad-hoc email requests from individuals claiming to be on the team.

**Why This Matters**
Email requests are not a valid authorization source. Without this rule, anyone with access to a lawyer's email could request access to sensitive files. The intake form is the only approved record of who is authorized to work on a matter, ensuring that access controls remain strictly tied to the firm's approved workflow rather than individual convenience.

---

### Protocol 4: Partner Account & Password Resets

**The Rule**
All password reset requests for partners must be routed through the partner's Executive Assistant (EA). Do not reset partner accounts directly upon receiving a request from the partner's email address.

**Why This Matters**
A common social engineering vector is a fraudster who knows a partner's email address and sends a password reset request from that address to IT. The partner's EA serves as a trusted identity gatekeeper who can verbally confirm the request is genuine. This protects the firm's highest-value accounts from unauthorized resets.

---

### Escalation Path

If you are ever unsure whether a request is authorized or whether a conflict exists, **stop and ask Records.** In this firm, a delay is preferable to a breach. Do not proceed if you are uncertain.

**IT Support On‑Boarding – Core Policies for Managing iManage & Partner Security**  
*Purpose: These rules protect client confidentiality, keep the firm compliant with ethical obligations, and prevent avoidable “clean‑up” work later. Each step explains **what** you must do **and why** it matters.*

---  

### 1. Verify Conflict‑of‑Interest Clearance Before Creating a Matter Folder  
**What to do:**  
1.1 When a new matter request arrives, **do not** create the iManage folder until the Records department has returned a “conflict‑check cleared” confirmation.  
1.2 If the request comes via email, ticket, or intake form, log the request, forward it to Records, and wait for their reply before proceeding.  

**Why:**  
- A premature folder gives the requesting users immediate access to client files.  
- If the conflict check later flags a conflict, those users would have already seen privileged information—an ethics violation that can expose the firm to sanctions and malpractice claims.  
- Waiting eliminates the need for later forensic removal of access and protects the firm’s reputation.

---  

### 2. Treat “Restricted‑Client” Requests as Ethical Walls Until Confirmed  
**What to do:**  
2.1 Maintain the master list of **restricted clients** (provided by Records).  
2.2 Whenever a matter name, client name, or request *appears* to involve a restricted client, **immediately** route the request to Records for verification.  
2.3 Do **not** make a judgment based on the matter title alone; always get explicit confirmation.  

**Why:**  
- Restricted clients often require “ethical walls” that prevent certain attorneys or staff from accessing the matter.  
- Mis‑identifying a matter can inadvertently breach a court‑imposed or client‑imposed confidentiality order, leading to disciplinary action.  
- Using the official verification process ensures the wall is built correctly the first time.

---  

### 3. Assign iManage Permissions Only From the Official Matter‑Team Intake Form  
**What to do:**  
3.1 When a matter is approved, locate the **Intake Form** (usually a PDF or SharePoint list) that lists the authorized team members and their roles.  
3.2 Create the iManage folder and grant access **exclusively** to the individuals listed on that form.  
3.3 Ignore any ad‑hoc “please add me” emails, even if they appear to come from a senior attorney.  

**Why:**  
- The intake form is the firm‑wide, auditable source for who may view the matter; it has been vetted by Records and the managing partner.  
- Granting access based on informal requests bypasses the conflict‑check and ethical‑wall safeguards, creating security gaps.  
- Consistent, documented permission sets simplify audits and reduce the chance of accidental data exposure.

---  

### 4. Route All Partner Password Reset Requests Through Their Executive Assistant (EA)  
**What to do:**  
4.1 When a ticket or email arrives requesting a partner’s password reset, verify that the request originated **only** from the partner’s designated EA.  
4.2 If the request comes directly from the partner, the EA, or any other source, **reject** the ticket and instruct the requester to have the EA submit the request through the proper channel.  
4.3 Document the EA’s name and the date of the request in the ticket for audit purposes.  

**Why:**  
- Partners are high‑value targets for phishing and social‑engineering attacks.  
- The EA is a pre‑approved, vetted point of contact; routing through them adds a verification layer that dramatically reduces the risk of unauthorized password changes.  
- Maintaining a clear audit trail helps demonstrate compliance with the firm’s security policy during internal or external reviews.

---  

### 5. Document Every Access‑Change Action in the Ticketing System  
**What to do:**  
5.1 For every folder creation, permission grant/revoke, or password reset, create a ticket (or update the existing one) with:  
   - Date & time  
   - Requestor (and EA, if applicable)  
   - Reason (conflict‑check cleared, intake form reference, ethical‑wall verification, etc.)  
   - Action taken (folder created, user added, password reset)  
5.2 Attach supporting evidence (e.g., conflict‑check email, intake form screenshot, EA approval).  

**Why:**  
- Provides an auditable trail that satisfies Records, the firm’s compliance officer, and any external regulators.  
- Enables quick rollback if a mistake is discovered and helps new hires understand the context of past actions.  

---  

### 6. Escalate Unclear or Suspicious Requests Immediately  
**What to do:**  
6.1 If any request deviates from the standard workflow (e.g., missing intake form, unknown EA, ambiguous client name), **escalate** to the senior IT manager or Records without making changes.  
6.2 Record the escalation in the ticket and note the reason for escalation.  

**Why:**  
- Prompt escalation prevents accidental breaches and shows due diligence.  
- It reinforces a culture of “better safe than sorry,” which is critical in a law‑firm environment where confidentiality is paramount.

---  

### 7. Periodic Review & Refresh (Quarterly)  
**What to do:**  
7.1 Every quarter, review a random sample of matter folders created in the past three months to confirm:  
   - Conflict‑check clearance was documented before folder creation.  
   - Permissions match the intake form.  
   - No restricted‑client matter was opened without Records’ sign‑off.  
7.2 Report any discrepancies to the IT manager and Records for corrective action.  

**Why:**  
- Regular audits catch any drift from the procedures early, reducing long‑term risk.  
- Demonstrates proactive compliance to firm leadership and external auditors.

# IT SUPPORT ONBOARDING: CORE PROCEDURES & ETHICS RULES
**Purpose:** This document captures essential operational and compliance rules for IT support at this firm. Every rule includes a reason so you understand the risk behind the process, not just the mechanical step. When in doubt, follow the rule and document your actions.

### 1. Conflicts Check Clearance Required Before iManage Folder Creation
**Step:** Do not create any new matter folder in iManage until Records has provided written confirmation that the conflicts check has cleared. If Records has not responded, hold the ticket and follow up; do not proceed on assumption.
**Why:** If you create a folder first and the conflicts check later returns positive, you will have already granted the wrong individuals file access. In a law firm, unauthorized access to a restricted matter is a serious ethics violation that can trigger firm disqualification, client notifications, and potential malpractice exposure. It is an ethics issue, not a routine IT cleanup.

### 2. Ethical Walls and Restricted Client Verification
**Step:** Keep a current copy of the restricted/ethical-wall client list from Records in your reference materials. When any access request involves a matter that could be tangentially related to a restricted client, pause and verify with Records before provisioning. Never determine whether an ethical wall applies by reading the matter name in iManage.
**Why:** Matter names in iManage are inconsistent and rarely reflect actual client relationships, opposing parties, or litigation positioning. Guessing based on naming conventions can result in granting access to restricted matters, forcing breach protocols, and potentially disqualifying the firm from active cases.

### 3. Access Provisioning Follows the Official Intake Team List
**Step:** When granting iManage access to a new matter, use only the matter team list documented on the signed intake form. Ignore unsolicited access requests from individuals not listed on that form. Route any out-of-turn access requests to the matter coordinator or managing partner for formal approval before action.
**Why:** The intake form reflects formally approved team assignments and attorney accountability. Granting access based on email requests bypasses proper authorization, creates orphaned permissions when assignments change, and increases the risk of unauthorized access to confidential files.

### 4. Partner Password Resets Verified Through Executive Assistants
**Step:** For any partner password reset, verify the request through the partner’s designated Executive Assistant. Do not process resets based on a direct call, email, or chat from the partner. Document the EA confirmation (name, time, method) in the ticket before proceeding.
**Why:** Partner accounts hold sensitive firm and client data and are frequent targets for social engineering and account takeover attacks. Routing resets through the EA adds a necessary human verification layer to prevent unauthorized access that could compromise confidential matters or trigger data breach protocols.
