## TL;DR  
**Replace the “‑75 °C > P1” rule with a **state‑machine / time‑window** alert that:**

1. **Knows when a defrost cycle is *supposed* to happen** (per‑freezer schedule).  
2. **Only raises a P1 if the temperature stays **above –70 °C for a *continuous* period that is longer than the longest possible defrost overshoot** (≈ 20 min) **and is still rising** at a rate that cannot be explained by a normal defrost ramp.  
3. **Requires a second, independent signal** (e.g., “compressor‑run‑flag” or a *trend‑slope* check) before the page is actually sent.  
4. **Imposes a “cool‑down” lock‑out** after any P1‑worthy event so that the same freezer cannot generate another page for at least **30 min** (or until the temperature has dropped back below –78 °C).  

When these three pieces are combined, a normal 12‑minute defrost excursion is ignored, but a genuine compressor stall (which will keep the temperature climbing past –70 °C **and** keep rising for > 20 min) will still generate a P1.

Below is a detailed design that can be implemented in any RMM/monitoring platform that accepts custom rule scripts (e.g., LogicMonitor, Splunk‑On‑Call, PagerDuty Rules, etc.).

## 1. Background – Why the Simple Threshold Fails  

| Symptom | Normal defrost | Real compressor failure |
|---------|----------------|--------------------------|
| **Temperature trace** | Rapid rise from –80 °C → –70 °C over ~1 min, then a **plateau** at –70 °C for ~12 min while the heater runs, then a rapid fall back to –80 °C. | Slow, monotonic rise (or very shallow slope) that **does not plateau**; temperature may stall near –70 °C for > 15 min and continue to climb if the compressor is completely off. |
| **Duration of “warm” state** | ≤ 12 min (often 8–12 min). | Typically > 30 min, often > 1 h before the freezer finally trips. |
| **Contextual clues** | Defrost is scheduled (e.g., every 6 h) and the unit’s **compressor power drops to 0 %** during that window. | Compressor power stays at 0 % *and* the unit never enters its scheduled defrost window. |

The current rule fires on any reading > –75 °C, which catches the tail‑end of every defrost cycle (≈ 4–5 false P1s per freezer per day). Engineers are forced to silence them, creating a dangerous blind‑spot.

---

## 2. High‑Level Alert Design  

### 2.1 “Defrost‑Aware” State Machine  

| State | Trigger | Exit Condition | Action |
|-------|---------|----------------|--------|
| **NORMAL** | Temperature ≤ –78 °C **or** temperature is falling. | **Enter “WARM”** when `T > –70 °C`. | Log “enter warm”. |
| **WARM** | `T > –70 °C` for **≥ WARM_MIN** continuous minutes (default 20 min). | Either **temperature drops back ≤ –78 °C** *or* **temperature has been rising for ≥ RAMP_MIN** minutes with a slope > SLOPE_LIMIT. | If exit due to *drop*, go back to **NORMAL**. If exit due to *rising slope*, go to **RISING**. |
| **RISING** | `dT/dt > SLOPE_LIMIT` **and** `T > –70 °C` for **≥ RAMP_MIN** minutes. | **Temperature crosses –68 °C** *or* **temperature begins to fall** (i.e., slope ≤ 0). | **Generate P1** *only if* a secondary verification passes (see 2.3). |
| **P1_SENT** | After a P1 is dispatched. | **Lock‑out** until `T ≤ –78 °C` for at least **LOCKOUT_COOLDOWN** minutes (default 30 min) **or** until a full “cool‑down” cycle is observed. | Prevent repeated paging for the same incident. |

**Key Parameters (tunable per‑freezer):**

| Parameter | Typical Value | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| `WARM_MIN` | 20 min | Longer than the longest documented defrost overshoot (≈ 12 min). |
| `RAMP_MIN` | 10 min | Guarantees we’re not reacting to a brief bounce. |
| `SLOPE_LIMIT` | 0.15 °C/min (rising) | Defrost ramp is *steady*; a real failure often shows a slower, more constant slope. |
| `LOCKOUT_COOLDOWN` | 30 min | Gives engineers time to investigate without being bombarded. |

> **Why 20 min?**  
> Empirical data from the lab shows that a *full* defrost overshoot (including heater‑on plateau) never exceeds 12 min. Adding a safety margin of ~8 min (to account for sensor lag, door‑open cycles, or slightly longer defrost in older units) gives us 20 min. Anything that stays > –70 °C for longer than that *must* be something other than a normal defrost.

### 2.2 “Schedule‑Based Suppression”  

Most freezers have a **predictable defrost schedule** (e.g., every 6 h starting at 02:00, 08:00, 14:00, 20:00). The alert engine can:

1. **Mark a freezer as “in‑defrost window”** for the next `DEFROST_DURATION` (≈ 15 min) each time the schedule fires.  
2. **During that window, automatically downgrade any temperature‑exceeds‑–70 °C event to “INFO”** (no page).  

If a freezer *fails to follow its schedule* (i.e., a defrost never occurs when expected), the system treats any “warm” event as suspicious and raises a **pre‑alert** (non‑P1) that escalates only after the state‑machine conditions above are met.

> **Implementation tip:** Store the last‑known defrost start‑time per freezer in a small KV table (e.g., Redis, DynamoDB, or a simple CSV). The RMM can query it every 5 min and auto‑clear after `DEFROST_DURATION + 5 min`.

### 2.3 Secondary Verification (the “compensating control”)  

A P1 page should never be sent on temperature alone. We add **one more independent check** before the page is emitted:

| Verification | How to compute | Pass condition |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Compressor‑Run flag** | Pull the freezer’s power/compressor‑run telemetry (most IoT sensors expose a “compressor ON” binary or a current‑draw value). | **FAIL** (i.e., compressor is OFF) **AND** temperature is in **RISING** state. |
| **Trend‑Slope + Acceleration** | Compute a 5‑point linear regression over the last 5 data points (≈ 5 × sample‑interval). | Slope > `SLOPE_LIMIT` **AND** acceleration (second derivative) > 0.02 °C/min² (i.e., still curving upward). |
| **Cross‑Freezer Correlation** (optional, if you have many units) | If ≥ 2 freezers in the same rack/room exceed –70 °C **simultaneously**, treat as a shared‑plant fault and auto‑page. | Not required for a single freezer but can be used as an extra confidence boost. |

The **final rule** becomes:

```
IF (State == RISING) 
   AND (CompressorRun == OFF) 
   AND (Slope > SLOPE_LIMIT) 
   AND (Acceleration > ACCEL_LIMIT)
THEN 
   Send P1 page to on‑call
   Enter P1_SENT state (lock‑out)
```

If any of the above checks fails, the system stays in **RISING** but does **not** fire a page; instead it logs a **WARNING** (non‑escalating) that can be reviewed later.

## 3. Full Pseudocode (Python‑ish) – Plug‑and‑Play

```python
# Constants (tune per freezer)
WARM_MIN        = 20      # minutes temperature must stay > -70C
RAMP_MIN        = 10      # minutes of rising slope before we consider failure
SLOPE_LIMIT     = 0.15    # °C per minute minimum rise
ACCEL_LIMIT     = 0.02    # °C per minute squared minimum curvature
LOCKOUT_MIN     = 30      # minutes after a P1 before we can page again
DEFROST_WINDOW  = 15      # minutes after each scheduled defrost we suppress alerts

# State dict per freezer (persisted between cycles)
state = {
    'prev_temp': -80.0,
    'in_warm': False,
    'warm_start': None,
    'rising_start': None,
    'p1_sent': False,
    'last_p1_ts': None,
    'defrost_suppress_until': 0,
    'compressor_on': True   # assume ON until telemetry says otherwise
}

def update(freezer_id, temp_c, compressor_status, timestamp):
    cfg = get_config(freezer_id)                 # retrieve per‑freezer params
    now = timestamp

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Defrost‑window suppression
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    if now < freezer_state['defrost_suppress_until']:
        # we are inside a scheduled defrost window → ignore warm alerts
        return  # (nothing to do, suppress)

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. Basic temperature check – enter/exit WARM state
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    if temp_c > -70.0:                     # threshold for “warm”
        if not freezer_state['in_warm']:
            freezer_state['in_warm'] = True
            freezer_state['warm_start'] = now
        else:
            # already warm – just keep tracking duration
            warm_elapsed = now - freezer_state['warm_start']
            if warm_elapsed >= cfg['WARM_MIN'] * 60:   # convert to seconds
                # Move to RISING state if compressor is OFF and still rising
                if not compressor_status and rising_trend_ok(freezer_id, now):
                    promote_to_rising(now)
    else:
        # temperature fell back below -70°C → reset warm tracking
        freezer_state['in_warm'] = False
        freezer_state['warm_start'] = None
        freezer_state['rising_start'] = None

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Rising‑state detection (temperature still > -70C and climbing)
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
def rising_trend_ok(fid, now):
    # Grab last N samples (e.g., 5) from the TSDB
    samples = tsdb.query(fid, now-5*sample_interval, now, interval='1m')
    temps   = [s['value'] for s in samples if s['value'] is not None]
    if len(temps) < 5:
        return False
    # Linear regression (simplified)
    xs = np.arange(len(temps))
    slope, _ = np.polyfit(xs, temps, 1)
    # Acceleration approx = second diff of slope over last 2 windows
    accel = (slope - prev_slope) / sample_interval
    return (slope > SLOPE_LIMIT) and (accel > ACCEL_LIMIT)

def promote_to_rising(now):
    freezer_state['rising_start'] = now
    freezer_state['p1_sent'] = False   # allow first P1 if conditions later met

def maybe_send_p1(now):
    if (freezer_state.get('rising_start') is None or
        not freezer_state.get('compressor_on') == False):
        return False   # not yet in a valid RISING scenario

    # How long have we been rising?
    rise_elapsed = now - freezer_state['rising_start']
    if rise_elapsed < cfg['RAMP_MIN'] * 60:
        return False   # still too early

    # Final verification (compressor OFF + slope still positive)
    if (freezer_state['compressor_on'] == False and
        verify_slope(now) and verify_accel(now)):
        # Send the page
        paginator.pagerduty.trigger(
            service_key=freezer_service_key(fid),
            summary=f"Freezer {fid} temperature > -70°C for {int(rise_elapsed/60)} min",
            severity='P1',
            source=f"Freezer {fid} alarm"
        )
        freezer_state['p1_sent'] = True
        freezer_state['last_p1_ts'] = now
        freezer_state['lockout_until'] = now + LOCKOUT_MIN * 60
        return True
    return False

# -----------------------------------------------------------------
# Periodic housekeeping (run every 5 min)
# -----------------------------------------------------------------
def housekeeping():
    now = utc_now()
    for fid, state in all_freezers():
        # Reset defrost window flag after its duration expires
        if now > state['defrost_suppress_until']:
            # look ahead to next scheduled defrost start
            nxt = next_scheduled_defrost(fid)
            state['defrost_suppress_until'] = nxt.start + DEFROST_WINDOW

        # If we are in LOCKOUT period and temperature is still warm,
        # keep suppressing pages but allow logging.
        if freezer_state['p1_sent'] and now < freezer_state['lockout_until']:
            continue   # nothing to do, stay silent
```

> **What the code does in plain English**  
> 1. **Suppresses every alert** while the freezer is inside its known defrost window.  
> 2. **Tracks how long the temperature stays above –70 °C**. Only when that duration exceeds 20 min do we consider it a possible fault.  
> 3. **Requires the compressor to be OFF** (i.e., the freezer isn’t deliberately heating) **and that the temperature has been rising steadily for at least 10 min with a slope > 0.15 °C/min and a positive curvature**.  
> 4. **Only then does it fire a P1** and starts a 30‑minute lock‑out so the same incident can’t repeatedly re‑page.

## 6. What If the Lab Adds More Freezers or Changes Defrost Frequency?  

* **Scale‑out:** The same rule can be applied per freezer; the only freezer‑specific values are the constants (`WARM_MIN`, `RAMP_MIN`, etc.) which can be overridden per‑unit.  
* **Dynamic schedules:** If a freezer’s defrost interval changes (e.g., from every 6 h to every 8 h due to a firmware upgrade), simply update the schedule table – the suppression window automatically expands/contracts.  
* **New failure modes:** If a new failure mode produces a long‑lasting “warm” state but still has the compressor ON (e.g., a refrigerant leak that causes the heater to stay on), the secondary verification will still *prevent* a P1 because `compressor_on` will be **True**. That is desirable – the engineer will see a *warning* but not an urgent page, giving them time to investigate without being over‑alerted.

### Proposal: Retuned Alerting Strategy & Compensating Controls

**Executive Summary:**
The current P1 alerting strategy is failing due to alert fatigue, causing on-call engineers to habitually silence pages. This creates a critical risk where genuine compressor failures are dismissed as defrost cycles. The proposed strategy replaces the static threshold with **conditional duration/recovery logic** to eliminate false positives from defrost cycles. Crucially, it implements a **compensating control** that bypasses the on-call engineer's complacency by introducing independent failure detection and direct escalation to the asset owner.

---

### 1. Retuned Alerting Logic: Stabilization Window
**Goal:** Differentiate between a transient defrost cycle and a sustained compressor failure using time and recovery behavior, rather than a static threshold.

**New Rule Configuration:**
*   **Metric:** `Freezer_Temp`
*   **Condition:** `Freezer_Temp > -75°C`
*   **Duration:** `>= 15 Minutes`
*   **Recovery Clause:** If `Freezer_Temp` returns to `<= -80°C` (or baseline) before the 15-minute window expires, the alert is **auto-resolved/canceled**.

**Rationale:**
*   **Defrost Filter:** Defrost cycles rise to ~-70°C but recover within ~12 minutes. A 15-minute stabilization window ensures the freezer has passed the defrost phase and stabilized. Since normal defrost is shorter than the window, no page is fired.
*   **Failure Detection:** A compressor failure results in a continuous temperature rise or a plateau well above -80°C. The temperature will not recover within the window, causing the alert to fire.
*   **Sample Safety:** Ultra-low temperature freezers have significant thermal mass. A 15-minute excursion to -70°C is generally within the safety margin for -80°C samples. The risk of alert fatigue causing missed failures far outweighs the negligible risk of a brief, monitored excursion.

### Proposed Alerting Strategy: "Recovery-Validated Breach"

**Core Problem:** A genuine compressor failure looks identical to a defrost cycle in the first few minutes. A static temperature threshold (e.g., > -75°C) cannot distinguish between a transient defrost and a runaway failure.

**New Alert Rule:**
*   **Threshold:** **>-72°C** (Defrost peaks at -70°C, so this provides a 2°C safety margin).
*   **Sustained Window:** **15 minutes.** (Defrost lasts ~12 minutes, so this ensures the event is not a transient blip).
*   **Recovery Validation (Suppression Logic):** **If the temperature returns to < -78°C within 12 minutes of the breach start, suppress the alert.**
*   **Action:** **Page P1** only if the temperature remains above -72°C for >15 minutes *and* does not recover to -78°C within 12 minutes.

**Why This Works:**
*   **Defrost Cycle:** Temperature rises to -70°C, stays there for ~12 minutes, then drops back to -80°C. The "Recovery Validation" logic detects the return to baseline and suppresses the alert.
*   **Genuine Failure:** Temperature rises to -70°C, stays there, and continues climbing. It never recovers to -78°C. The "Sustained Window" ensures the alert fires after the defrost has already ended.
*   **First Few Minutes:** The strategy does *not* page on the initial spike. It waits for the *absence of recovery*. This is the critical differentiator. A defrost *must* recover; a failure *will not*.

### Compensating Control: "Silence-to-Escalation Watchdog"

**Core Problem:** On-call engineers are acknowledging/silencing P1 pages without investigating, relying on the "first few minutes" of the failure to look like a defrost. Even if the retuned strategy catches the failure, the engineer's inaction means the samples are at risk.

**New Escalation Rule:**
*   **Trigger:** **If the alert is acknowledged/suppressed AND the temperature remains above -72°C for > 24 hours, escalate to the Lab Director + SMS to the Lab Safety Officer.**
*   **Bypass:** This escalation is **independent of the on-call engineer's acknowledgement.** It triggers based solely on the *duration of the breach*, not the *status of the alert*.

**Why This Works:**
*   **Defrost Cycle:** Resolves in 12 minutes. Even if the engineer ignores the alert, the temperature drops below -72°C within 12 minutes, and the escalation timer resets. No escalation occurs.
