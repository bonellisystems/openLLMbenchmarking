# Cybersecurity -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS identifiers presented as real.
- Eradication or patching recommended before containment on an active incident.
- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.
- Ignoring implied breach-notification or regulatory timelines when sensitive data is involved.
- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Severity correctly triaged and prioritized against the described impact.
- Containment-before-eradication-before-recovery ordering respected.
- Real, correctly-applied CVE/CWE/ATT&CK references where relevant.
- Evidence-preservation explicitly called out.
- Notification or compliance timeline awareness when data exposure is implied.

# Tabletop Exercise Response: Law Firm Ransomware Incident
**Client:** Mid-Size Law Firm
**Incident ID:** LFR-2023-001
**Status:** Active – Day 1 (T+0)

## Executive Summary
The firm is under active ransomware attack leveraging Citrix Bleed (CVE-2023-4966). The threat actor has compromised the NetScaler ADC, bypassed MFA via session token replay, and initiated mass encryption on a critical file server hosting litigation hold data. Immediate containment is required, but financial and legal constraints (insurance consent-to-settle clause, active litigation holds) dictate a specific sequence of actions to avoid coverage denial or spoliation of evidence.

The following walkthrough prioritizes **containment**, **legal alignment**, **evidence preservation**, and **ransom strategy**.

---

## 1. Technical Containment: Bypassing the MFA Limitation
**Constraint:** MFA does not stop this attack vector because CVE-2023-4966 allows session token theft.
**Priority:** Immediate (Day 1, 14:35)

Since the attacker is replaying valid session tokens, simply enforcing MFA or resetting passwords will not terminate the active hijacked sessions. The containment strategy must focus on session invalidation and network isolation.

*   **Action A: Isolate the Citrix ADC/Gateway.**
    *   Immediately disconnect the NetScaler ADC from the corporate network (VLAN isolation). This cuts off the attacker’s command-and-control and prevents further lateral movement from the gateway to other systems.
    *   *Why:* This is the only way to stop the "replay" capability at the source.
*   **Action B: Revoke Active Session Tokens.**
    *   Administer the `Citrix ADC` commands to flush all existing sessions (`clear session all`).
    *   *Why:* This forces all users (including the attacker's hijacked sessions) to re-authenticate. Since the attacker is using stolen tokens, forcing a token refresh invalidates the stolen credentials.
    *   *Note:* This will cause a service outage for legitimate users. This is acceptable given the ransomware is actively encrypting data.
*   **Action C: Patch the Vulnerability.**
    *   Apply the vendor-supplied patch for CVE-2023-4966 immediately after isolation.
    *   *Why:* Patching alone is insufficient for containment but is required to prevent re-infection once the system is brought back online.

---

## 2. Evidence Preservation & Litigation Hold Protection
**Constraint:** FS-DOCS02 hosts active litigation holds and attorney work product.
**Priority:** Concurrent with Containment (Day 1, 14:35)

Disabling FS-DOCS02 to stop encryption creates a risk of spoliation if evidence is destroyed or if privileged data is lost. The preservation process must be conducted under the supervision of outside counsel to maintain Attorney-Client Privilege.

*   **Action A: Engage Legal Counsel Immediately.**
    *   Notify the firm's General Counsel and the external counsel overseeing the active litigation matters.
    *   *Why:* They must authorize the forensic imaging to ensure the chain of custody protects privilege and complies with the litigation hold order.
*   **Action B: Create Forensic Images (Do Not Shut Down Yet).**
    *   If the server is still running, connect a forensic write-blocker to the storage subsystem (or use a bootable forensic USB) to create a bit-for-bit image of FS-DOCS02 *before* pulling the power.
    *   *Why:* Powering down may terminate encryption processes (good) but may also lose volatile data or trigger anti-forensic scripts (bad). Imaging allows analysis without altering the live state.
*   **Action C: Suspend Automated Deletion Policies.**
    *   Instruct the IT team to pause any snapshot rotation, backup deletion, or log purging policies on FS-DOCS02.
    *   *Why:* Automated processes may violate the litigation hold by destroying potential evidence before the forensic image is secured.
*   **Action D: Contain the File Server.**
    *   Once imaging is complete, isolate FS-DOCS02 from the network.
    *   *Why:* This stops the mass file encryption.

---

## 3. Insurance Engagement: The Consent-to-Settle Clause
**Constraint:** Claims-made policy with a consent-to-settle clause requiring carrier pre-approval for vendor engagement.
**Priority:** Day 1, 14:45 (Immediately after Initial Containment)

The policy stipulates that the carrier must approve *any* incident-response vendor engagement or cost before spending. Ignoring this clause is the highest risk for coverage denial.

*   **Action A: Issue "Notice of Claim" to Carrier.**
    *   Contact the cyber insurance broker/carrier immediately. Provide the date, time, and nature of the incident (Ransomware, Citrix Bleed).
    *   *Why:* Most policies have a "prompt notice" requirement. Delaying notice can trigger a coverage defense.
*   **Action B: Request Specific Authorization for Vendors.**
    *   Submit a written request for approval of specific vendors (e.g., Forensic Imaging Firm, Cyber-Ransom Negotiation Specialist, Legal Counsel) *before* they are engaged.
    *   *Why:* The "consent-to-settle" clause allows the insurer to control the defense. If we hire a vendor without approval, the insurer may refuse to reimburse, leaving the firm with a $780k exposure.
*   **Action C: Reserve the Right to Decline.**
    *   Acknowledge to the carrier that the firm retains the right to take immediate containment actions using internal staff (e.g., pulling network cables) to mitigate loss, as this is standard duty to mitigate.
    *   *Why:* Clarifying that *immediate containment* does not require approval, but *paid remediation* does, avoids confusion.

## 4. Ransom Demand Strategy
**Constraint:** 12 BTC ($780k) demand within 96 hours. No unconditional recommendation to pay.
**Priority:** Day 1, 15:00 (After Insurance Authorization)

Payment decisions must be data-driven and legally vetted. The firm should not negotiate directly with the threat actor.

*   **Action A: Engage a Specialized Ransom Negotiation Firm (Pending Insurance Approval).**

    *   *Why:* Law firms should not negotiate directly; this risks exposing legal strategy or triggering criminal liability.
*   **Action B: Validate Backup Integrity.**
    *   Verify if the firm's off-site backups are clean (not encrypted).
    *   *Decision Point:* If backups are clean, **DO NOT PAY.** Restore from backup is the standard recovery path. Paying provides no guarantee of decryption and funds criminal activity.

    *   *Why:* Law enforcement often requires notification before payment to track funding chains. Some jurisdictions mandate reporting.

    *   *Why:* 96 hours is insufficient for a full forensic investigation, insurance approval, and backup validation. The negotiation firm can stall while the firm assesses the true impact.

    *   **Recommendation:** **Do not pay** unless:
        1.  Backups are completely unrecoverable.
        2.  The data is critical to business continuity (not just litigation matters).
        3.  Legal counsel and the insurer have approved the payment.
        4.  A decryption key is guaranteed (ideally, payment is for a "key," not just a "promise").

### Tabletop Exercise: Incident Response Walkthrough
**Client:** Mid-Size Law Firm
**Incident:** CVE-2023-4966 (Citrix Bleed) → Session Hijacking → Ransomware Encryption (FS-DOCS02)
**Exercise Lead:** Incident Commander (IC)

The following is the prioritized response walkthrough based on the injected facts. This walkthrough assumes the firm has activated its Incident Response Plan (IRP) and the IC is directing the response.

### Priority 1: Technical Containment
**Constraint:** CVE-2023-4966 is a session-token theft vulnerability. The prompt explicitly notes that MFA does not mitigate this vector because the attacker replays an already-authenticated session token. MFA re-enrollment is a recovery control, not a containment control.

**IC Directive:**
1.  **NetScaler Session Termination (Immediate):**
    *   **Action:** Admin must force immediate termination of **all** active sessions on the compromised Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway.
    *   **Technical Detail:** The attack exploits the `nscookie` (session token). The IC directs the NetScaler admin to purge session state and rotate session secret keys. This invalidates the stolen tokens.
    *   **MFA Clarification:** The team must **not** waste time re-enrolling MFA as a containment measure. The stolen tokens bypass MFA entirely; the attacker can use the replayed token to access resources as a legit user until the token is killed or expires. Re-enrollment only protects *future* authentications.
2.  **Network Isolation of NetScaler:**
    *   **Action:** Disable public-facing access to the NetScaler immediately. Quarantine the NetScaler VLAN to prevent lateral movement from the ADC to internal servers (including FS-DOCS02, which may have been accessed via a hijacked session).
    *   **Rationale:** While session termination kills the token, we must assume the attacker has moved laterally. Isolating the ADC stops further exploitation attempts and blocks command-and-control if the ADC was used for egress.
3.  **FS-DOCS02 Isolation:**
    *   **Action:** Based on the EDR flag at 14:30, FS-DOCS02 is actively encrypting files. The IC orders the Network Admin to **shutdown the switch port** or quarantine the FS-DOCS02 VLAN immediately to halt I/O and encryption processes.
    *   **Critical Nuance:** Do **not** power off FS-DOCS02. We must preserve volatile data (RAM) for forensic analysis and maintain the file system state for the litigation holds. Powering off may trigger full-disk encryption mechanisms or destroy evidence.
4.  **Account Assessment:**
    *   **Action:** Identify the user accounts associated with the hijacked session tokens. Force password resets and revoke all refresh tokens for these accounts. Since the tokens bypassed MFA, the accounts were compromised via credential theft prior to the Citrix Bleed exploitation. Investigate whether these accounts have local admin rights, which may have facilitated the ransomware payload execution on FS-DOCS02.

---

### Priority 2: Evidence Preservation & Litigation Holds
**Constraint:** FS-DOCS02 hosts files for three active litigation matters under litigation hold, including attorney work product. Actions must preserve evidence, prevent spoliation, and maintain privilege.

**IC Directive:**
1.  **Litigation Hold Activation:**
    *   **Action:** The General Counsel (GC) must formally activate the litigation hold protocol for FS-DOCS02 immediately. All custodians with access to the affected matters must be notified to cease any deletion or modification of files.
    *   **Risk:** Shutting down or imaging the server must be documented as a preservation measure, not a destruction measure. The IC ensures the hold is suspended only insofar as necessary for forensic imaging under counsel's direction, and immediately re-activated upon completion.
2.  **Forensic Vendor Engagement (Privilege Strategy):**
    *   **Action:** The firm must **not** engage a forensic vendor directly. Any engagement must be made by **outside legal counsel** under the direction of the GC to ensure the forensic work product is protected by the **Attorney-Client Privilege (ACP)** and the **Work Product Doctrine (WPD)**.
    *   **Rationale:** If the firm hires a forensics vendor directly, this disclosure may waive privilege, exposing the firm's investigation to discovery by opposing counsel in the active matters. The forensic report and the data extracted must remain under counsel's control.
3.  **FS-DOCS02 Preservation Steps:**
    *   **Action:**
        *   **Forensic Image:** Counsel directs the forensic vendor to create a bit-for-bit forensic image of FS-DOCS02 while the server remains powered on and isolated. This captures the current encrypted state, metadata, and artifacts.
        *   **Work Product Segregation:** The forensic vendor must identify and isolate the specific files belonging to the three litigation matters. These files are high-priority. The firm must determine if the encryption has rendered them unreadable or if shadow copies/VSS are intact.
        *   **No Third-Party Disclosure:** Under no circumstances should encrypted files, the ransom note, or decryption capabilities be shared with the ransomware actors or third-party negotiators until authorized by counsel and the insurer. Doing so could compromise privilege and violate the litigation hold.
4.  **Backup Verification (Privileged Scope):**
    *   **Action:** Counsel directs IT to check backup integrity for the specific litigation-matter files. This verification must be done under privilege. If backups exist for the hold-matter files, the firm has a path to restoration that avoids paying the ransom. If backups are corrupt or absent for these files, the risk profile changes significantly.

### Priority 3: Insurance & Pre-Engagement
**Constraint:** Cyber insurance is claims-made with a **consent-to-settle clause**. The carrier requires pre-approval for **all** IR vendor engagements and costs **before** the firm spends. Acting first jeopardizes coverage.

**IC Directive:**
1.  **Carrier Notification:**
    *   **Action:** The GC and Risk Management must notify the cyber insurance carrier immediately. Under a claims-made policy, the incident is the trigger. Notification must be made within the timeframe specified in the policy (typically "as soon as practicable"). Late notification could result in coverage denial.
    *   **Detail:** Provide the SOC confirmation of CVE-2023-4966, the EDR encryption flags, and the ransom note. Do not admit liability or make settlement offers to the carrier yet; provide facts.
2.  **Pre-Approval for All Vendors/Costs:**
    *   **Action:** The IC must **pause all vendor contracting** until the carrier provides written pre-approval.
    *   **Consent-to-Settle Risk:** The consent-to-settle clause, combined with the pre-approval requirement, means that if the firm engages a forensics vendor, an IR firm, or a ransomware negotiator without carrier consent, **those costs will be out-of-pocket**. The carrier can deny reimbursement for any expense incurred without prior written approval.
    *   **Vendor Panel:** The IC contacts the carrier to ask if the policy requires the use of a panel vendor for IR or forensics. If so, the firm must request a referral or approval for their preferred vendor.
    *   **Scope of Work:** Submit a scope of work to the carrier for approval. This includes:
        *   Forensic investigation of CVE-2023-4966 and FS-DOCS02.
        *   Litigation hold preservation support.
        *   Ransomware negotiation (if considered).
        *   Legal counsel costs related to the breach and holds.
    *   **Budget Cap:** Request a budget cap approval. If the carrier approves $150k for forensics, spending $200k without further approval risks the excess being uncovered.
3.  **Ransom Payment Authorization:**
    *   **Action:** The consent-to-settle clause almost certainly requires carrier pre-approval for ransom payments. The IC informs the firm that **no payment can be made** (even to preserve litigation data) without carrier written consent. The carrier will likely require a board resolution and law enforcement consultation before approving payment.

### Priority 4: Ransom Demand Approach
**Constraint:** Demand is 12 BTC (~$780k) within 96 hours. Approach must not unconditionally recommend payment.

**IC Directive:**
1.  **Initial Recommendation: No Payment:**
    *   **Recommendation:** The IC recommends **against** paying the ransom at this time.
    *   **Justification:**
        *   **Backups:** If forensic verification confirms clean backups for the litigation-matter files, restoration is the superior path. Payment is unnecessary.
        *   **Litigation Hold Risk:** Paying the ransom introduces a risk of data destruction. If the attackers delete the encryption keys or purge the files after payment, the firm is in violation of the litigation hold. The 96-hour deadline creates pressure, but the hold obligations are perpetual and non-negotiable.
        *   **Ethical/Legal Duties:** As a law firm, paying ransom may conflict with professional conduct rules regarding client confidentiality and data protection obligations. There is also a risk that paying funds criminal enterprises and may violate OFAC sanctions if the ransomware group is listed (verify via sanctions check).
        *   **Attacker Reliability:** There is no guarantee the attackers will provide working decryption keys. Paying funds the operation and may mark the firm as a "paying customer" for future targeting.
2.  **Conditional Consideration of Payment:**
    *   **Scenario:** If forensic analysis reveals that backups are unavailable for the litigation-matter files and the data is critical to the firm's ability to meet ethical duties to clients, the IC recommends escalating to the **Board of Management** and **Outside Counsel** to evaluate payment as a last resort.
    *   **Conditions for Payment Discussion:**
        *   Payment is only considered if the loss of litigation-matter data would cause irreparable harm to clients or result in disbarment/damages exceeding the ransom.
        *   Payment must be approved by the Board, Counsel, and **Insurance Carrier**.
        *   Payment must be reported to Law Enforcement (FBI/IC3) as required by law and insurer policy.
        *   Sanctions screening must clear.
        *   Negotiation is required. The first ask of 12 BTC is likely inflated; negotiation could reduce the amount, but this must be done by a specialized negotiator approved by the carrier.
3.  **Response to Threat:**
    *   **Action:** The firm does not respond to the attackers directly. All communication with the ransomware group must be handled by a specialized negotiation firm (if approved by insurer) or Law Enforcement.
    *   **Internal Communication:** Inform the affected attorneys and clients regarding the incident. The GC drafts notifications to clients based on the hold status and data availability. Clients have a right to know about the breach affecting their matter, but the IC ensures notifications do not disclose sensitive investigation details that could prejudice the matters.

## 1. Technical Containment: Why MFA Fails and What Actually Works

**The Misconception:**
Management and SOC teams often default to "Force MFA re-enrollment" as the first response. **This is ineffective for this specific attack vector.** CVE-2023-4966 is a memory corruption vulnerability in the NetScaler ADC that allows an attacker to steal a user's session token (cookie). Once the token is stolen, the attacker replays it to hijack the session. Because the session is *already authenticated*, the system does not trigger a new MFA challenge. Re-enrolling the user's MFA credentials creates a new credential set but does not invalidate the stolen session token currently active in the attacker's hands.

**Correct Containment Steps (Priority Order):**

1.  **Invalidate All Active Sessions:**
    *   Log into the NetScaler ADC console directly (over a trusted, out-of-band channel if possible).
    *   Execute the command to invalidate all current session tokens: `disable session`.
    *   *Verification:* Confirm that no active sessions remain on the ADC. This cuts off the attacker's immediate access to the ADC.

2.  **Network Isolation of the ADC:**
    *   Remove the ADC from the load balancer pool or physically disconnect the appliance from the production network.
    *   Block all ingress traffic to the ADC's management IP from the internal network to prevent re-infection or further exploitation of the ADC management plane.

3.  **Patch the Vulnerability (CVE-2023-4966):**
    *   Apply the vendor patch immediately to the NetScaler ADC firmware.
    *   *Note:* Patching stops future exploitation but does not fix the current compromised state. Therefore, patching must happen *concurrently* with session invalidation.

4.  **Segment FS-DOCS02:**
    *   The ransomware on FS-DOCS02 suggests lateral movement from the compromised ADC sessions.
    *   **Air-gap FS-DOCS02 immediately.** Disconnect the file server from the network (pull the network cable or disable the NIC). Do not attempt to run anti-malware scans on the live host if it risks triggering encryption processes or altering system state.

5.  **Credential Reset (Secondary):**
    *   After the ADC is patched and the network is segmented, reset all NetScaler administrator credentials.
    *   Reset all user credentials that accessed the ADC (as they may have been compromised during the session hijack).
    *   *Only after* these steps are complete should MFA be re-enrolled to prevent future attacks via valid login paths.

---

## 2. Evidence Preservation on FS-DOCS02 (Litigation Hold Compliance)

**The Constraint:**
The firm is under a **litigation hold** for three active matters. This creates a dual obligation:
1.  **Preserve** all evidence of the cyber incident (for insurance and law enforcement).
2.  **Do not destroy** or alter any client files, attorney work product, or litigation strategy (to avoid spoliation sanctions).

**Evidence Preservation Protocol:**

1.  **Do Not Boot from External Media:**
    *   Booting FS-DOCS02 from a USB drive or external DVD to run forensics tools can alter the Master Boot Record (MBR) and partition tables. This constitutes spoliation of the original system state.

## 1.  Correct Technical Containment Steps (MFA does not stop the attack)

**Why MFA is irrelevant:**  
CVE‑2023‑4966 (“Citrix Bleed”) exploits a *session‑token* weakness in the ADC. Once an attacker obtains a valid token, the session is already authenticated; subsequent MFA challenges are bypassed. Revoking MFA or forcing re‑enrollment will not invalidate the stolen token.

### 1.1 Immediate “Network‑Level” Isolation (T + 0‑5 min)

| Action | Owner | Rationale / Detail |
|--------|-------|---------------------|
| **a. Block all external traffic to the NetScaler ADC** – deny inbound on ports 443/80/22 from the Internet; allow only internal management IPs. | NetScaler Admin (or SOC) | Prevents further token‑stealing sessions from external hosts. |
| **b. Disable *External* Virtual Servers / Gateways** that expose the ADC to the Internet. | NetScaler Admin | If the ADC is only used for internal services, consider a *temporary* “maintenance mode” that shuts down all external virtual servers. |
| **c. Force a **session‑token purge** on the ADC – invalidate all active tokens by restarting the authentication service (or by clearing the session‑cache). | NetScaler Admin | A controlled reboot of the authentication module drops all authenticated sessions, forcing re‑login with MFA. |
| **d. Deploy a *temporary* Web‑Application‑Firewall (WAF) rule** to drop requests containing the vulnerable endpoint’s signature (e.g., `POST /nsrpc/…` with malformed length). | SOC / WAF Team | Provides a “stop‑gap” while the ADC firmware is patched. |
| **e. Issue an **out‑of‑band MFA re‑enrollment** only for accounts that will be re‑authenticated after the service restart. | IAM / Security Ops | Re‑enrollment is not a containment measure, but a *pre‑condition* for safe restoration of access. |

### 1.2 Patch / Firmware Update (T + 15‑30 min)

| Action | Owner | Why now? |
|--------|-------|----------|
| Apply the **official Citrix security patch** for CVE‑2023‑4966 (or upgrade to a version that removes the vulnerable feature). | NetScaler Engineer + Change‑Management | The patch eliminates the token‑theft vector permanently. |
| Verify patch integrity with a **hash check** and **configuration backup**. | Change‑Management | Guarantees audit trail; required for insurance evidence of “reasonable security measures.” |

### 1.3 Post‑Containment Validation

| Action | Owner | Success Criteria |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Run a **SOC “token‑theft replay test”** – simulate a legitimate login, capture the session‑ID, and attempt to reuse it without MFA. | SOC | No successful reuse → vector closed. |
| Confirm **no new external sessions** to the ADC in NetScaler logs for the past 2 hours. | SOC | Logs clean → containment effective. |

### Why Acting First Could Jeopardize Coverage

1. **Consent‑to‑Settle Clause** – Most cyber‑insurance policies for law firms contain a clause requiring the insured to **obtain written consent** before incurring any “loss‑mitigation expenses.”  
2. **Retroactive Application** – Even if the expense is later deemed “necessary,” the insurer can **deny the claim** on the basis that the firm **did not seek pre‑approval**.  
3. **Potential Conflict of Interest** – Engaging a vendor without insurer sign‑off may be viewed as a “self‑service” remedy, which could be interpreted as an *unreasonable* escalation, again voiding coverage.  
4. **Audit Trail** – The insurer will request documentation of *all* invoices, contracts, and purchase orders. If these were not generated under their oversight, they may deem the expense “uninsurable.”  

**Bottom‑Line:** The **first formal step** after technical containment is to **notify the carrier** (via the designated claims officer) and request **pre‑approval for any vendor engagement or direct cost** related to the incident.
