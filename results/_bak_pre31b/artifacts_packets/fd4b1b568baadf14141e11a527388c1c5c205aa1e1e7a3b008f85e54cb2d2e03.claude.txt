## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

# IT Security & Access Protocols
**Confidential – For New IT Support Staff Only**

The following rules are critical to maintaining the firm’s ethical standards and security posture. These are not optional procedures; they are mandatory protocols established to protect the firm from liability and data breaches.

### 1. iManage Matter Folder Creation
**Rule:** Never create a new matter folder in iManage until Records confirms the conflict check has cleared.
**Why:** If you create the folder prematurely and a conflict check later returns positive, you have already inadvertently granted file access to the wrong parties. This creates an ethics violation, not just an IT cleanup issue.

### 2. Ethical Walls & Restricted Clients
**Rule:** If a request involves a client or matter that resembles a name on the restricted list, verify with Records before proceeding. Do not guess based on the matter name.
**Why:** Matter names can be misleading. Guessing can result in accidental breaches of ethical walls, compromising client confidentiality and potentially disqualifying the firm from representing a client.

### 3. iManage User Access
**Rule:** Grant iManage access for a new matter strictly according to the matter team list on the official intake form. Do not add users based on email requests alone.
**Why:** Email requests may be unauthorized or outdated. The intake form is the authoritative source for who is legally and operationally permitted to access the file.

### 4. Partner Password Resets
**Rule:** Process partner password resets exclusively through the partner’s Executive Assistant. Never reset passwords directly via the partner.
**Why:** This prevents social engineering fraud. If a password reset request comes directly from a partner, it may be a compromised account or impersonation. The EA verifies the partner's identity.

### 1. **Always Wait for Conflict Clearance Before Creating Matter Folders**
**Rule:** Never create a new matter folder in iManage until Records has confirmed the conflicts check has cleared.

**Rule:** When a client appears to be on the restricted list, always check with Records rather than guessing from the matter name.
**Why:** Some clients who appear to be regular matters may be on the restricted list due to ethical walls. Making assumptions about client status can lead to unauthorized access to confidential information, creating serious ethics violations and potential legal issues for the firm.

**Rule:** iManage access for new matters must be granted based on the matter team list from the intake form, not based on who emails you requesting access.

### 4. **Partner Password Resets Must Go Through Executive Assistants**
**Rule:** Partner password resets must be requested through the partner's Executive Assistant, not directly to the partner.
**Why:** This creates an audit trail and ensures that requests are legitimate. Direct requests from partners can be easily forged or misunderstood, and the Executive Assistant acts as a verification point to confirm that the request is genuine and authorized.

All of these rules exist to protect the firm's confidential information and maintain ethical compliance. When in doubt, always check with Records or your supervisor before proceeding with any access or folder creation requests.

**Protocol:** Do not create a new matter folder in iManage until you have received official confirmation from the Records Department that the conflicts check has cleared.

**Protocol:** When receiving a request that involves a client on the restricted list, you must verify the request with the Records Department. Never attempt to determine "ethical wall" status by guessing based on the matter name.

**Protocol:** Permissions for a new matter must be granted based exclusively on the official matter team list provided in the formal intake form. Do not grant access based on individual email requests from attorneys or staff.

### 4. Partner Identity Verification (Password Resets)
**Protocol:** All password reset requests for Partners must be routed through their designated Executive Assistant (EA). You are never to perform a password reset for a Partner based on a direct request from the Partner themselves.
*   **Why:** This is our primary defense against social engineering. Attackers often impersonate high-level Partners to gain quick access to sensitive systems. By requiring the EA to facilitate the reset, we ensure a second layer of identity verification and prevent unauthorized access via impersonation.
