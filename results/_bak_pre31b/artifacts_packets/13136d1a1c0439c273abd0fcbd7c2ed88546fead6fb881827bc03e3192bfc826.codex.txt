# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Legal & Compliance -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The answer correctly distinguishes general legal information from jurisdiction-specific legal advice, and flags when licensed counsel review is genuinely required. Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated statute, regulation, or case citation presented as real.
- Definitive legal conclusion stated with no jurisdiction or consult-counsel caveat on a matter that clearly needs one.
- General information presented as a substitute for licensed advice on a high-stakes ask.
- No privilege or confidentiality flag when the scenario implies sensitive written communication.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Clear line drawn between general information and advice requiring licensed counsel.
- Only real, correctly-applied citations used -- never invented ones.
- Privilege or confidentiality caution raised when the scenario implies it.
- Jurisdictional caveat stated explicitly rather than assumed.

### **Detailed Remediation Actions**

#### **1. Finding 2: Unauthorized Destruction of Public Records**
*   **Risk:** **Critical**. Unauthorized destruction may violate state public records acts, breach legal holds (if litigation is pending), and constitute spoliation of evidence. It also undermines agency transparency and accountability.
*   **Owner:** Records Manager / Compliance Officer.
*   **Remediation Actions:**
    1.  **Immediate Halt:** Immediately suspend all destruction activities for the affected categories.
    2.  **Recovery Assessment:** Engage IT and Forensics to determine if records are recoverable from backups or shadow drives.
    3.  **Legal Hold Check:** Verify if any of the destroyed records fall under active or potential legal holds.
    4.  **Control Implementation:** Configure digital rights management and workflow tools to block destruction without mandatory system-enforced approvals.
    5.  **Process Update:** Revise the destruction workflow to require mandatory sign-off from Records Management and Legal/Compliance prior to execution.
*   **Target Closure Date:** **30 Days.** (Immediate halt required Day 0; closure upon proof of controls implementation and recovery report).
*   **⚖️ LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED:** **YES.**
    *   **Why:** Legal must assess potential spoliation liabilities, verify compliance with public records retention schedules, determine if a "legal hold" exists for the destroyed categories, and ensure the revised workflow meets regulatory and court-mandated requirements.

#### **2. Finding 1: Local Administrator Rights on Workstations**
*   **Risk:** **High**. Excessive privileges allow malicious actors (via phishing or malware) to bypass security controls, exfiltrate PII, and alter system configurations. This is a common vector for ransomware and data breaches.
*   **Owner:** IT Security Lead / System Administrator.
*   **Remediation Actions:**
    1.  **Privilege Reduction:** Immediately remove local administrator rights from all 14 affected workstations; reassign users to standard user roles.
    2.  **Audit & Verification:** Run a scan to verify removal of privileges and check for persistence mechanisms or unauthorized admin accounts created during the audit period.
    3.  **Access Justification:** Require IT staff to review the original justification for these rights; if none exist, enforce least privilege permanently.
    4.  **Policy Enforcement:** Update endpoint management policies to automatically enforce standard user profiles unless exception approval is granted via a formal change request.
    5.  **User Communication:** Notify agency staff regarding the change and provide alternatives for approved administrative tasks.
*   **Target Closure Date:** **20 Days.**
*   **⚖️ LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED:** **NO.**
    *   **Explanation:** This is an internal security control failure violating agency policy. While it involves risk, it does not require legal counsel to close the finding unless specific regulatory reporting thresholds (e.g., HIPAA/PCI-DSS) are triggered, which is not indicated by the finding.

#### **3. Finding 3: Cloud Backup Vendor Contract Lacks Data-Residency Clause**
*   **Risk:** **High**. Lack of a data-residency clause may result in PII being processed or stored in jurisdictions outside agency-mandated boundaries. This can violate privacy regulations (e.g., state privacy laws, GDPR equivalents) and agency privacy policies.
*   **Owner:** Procurement Director / Privacy Officer.
*   **Remediation Actions:**
    1.  **Legal Requirement Definition:** Define exact data-residency and data-processing location requirements based on the specific types of PII stored.
    2.  **Vendor Negotiation:** Initiate contract amendment discussions with the vendor to insert required data-residency and restriction clauses.
    3.  **Risk Acceptance or Migration:** If the vendor cannot agree to residency requirements within a reasonable timeframe, evaluate the risk of data migration to a compliant vendor or acceptance of risk via a formal risk register (if permitted).
    4.  **Validation:** Verify that the vendor's current data centers comply with the residency requirements during the contract negotiation period.
*   **Target Closure Date:** **60 Days.** (Closure depends on vendor negotiation cycles; if migration is required, timeline may extend).
*   **⚖️ LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED:** **YES.**
    *   **Why:** Data residency is intrinsically tied to privacy regulations and data sovereignty laws. Legal must define the regulatory requirements based on data classification, review the contractual language for enforceability, and ensure the final clause satisfies all applicable privacy mandates.

#### **4. Finding 4: Incident-Response Plan Not Tabletop-Tested**
*   **Risk:** **Medium**. An untested IRP indicates unvalidated procedures. In a real incident, this could lead to communication failures, delayed response times, and improper containment, impacting business continuity and data integrity.
*   **Owner:** Incident Response Lead / CISO.
*   **Remediation Actions:**
    1.  **Schedule Exercise:** Schedule a tabletop exercise involving key stakeholders (IT, Legal, Communications, Operations) within 30 days.
    2.  **Scenario Development:** Develop scenarios relevant to the agency's risk profile (e.g., ransomware, data breach, insider threat).
    3.  **Execution & Documentation:** Conduct the exercise, record decisions and communication flows, and document gaps.
    4.  **Plan Update:** Update the IRP based on lessons learned and close any identified operational gaps.
    5.  **Reporting:** Submit execution report and updated IRP to compliance committee.
*   **Target Closure Date:** **30 Days.** (Closure upon completion and submission of updated IRP).
*   **⚖️ LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED:** **NO.**
    *   **Explanation:** This is an operational readiness gap. While Legal may participate as a stakeholder in the tabletop test, the finding itself relates to policy compliance and operational procedure, not legal liability or contract law.
