## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

### 4. Protected Leave and Accommodations
This section addresses the intersection of the PIP with employment law, FMLA, ADA, and state/local regulations.

#### A. Unrelated Protected Leave (e.g., FMLA, Family Leave)
*   **Policy:** If the employee takes protected leave for reasons *unrelated* to their performance (e.g., personal/family medical reasons), the PIP **pauses** during the absence.
*   **Action:**
    *   The start date of the PIP will be adjusted by the duration of the leave.
    *   Goals related to time-sensitive tasks (e.g., RFI response) will be recalculated based on the employee's active work days.
    *   No penalty is applied for non-performance occurring during the protected leave.

#### B. Reasonable Accommodation (ADA/Disability)
*   **Policy:** If a request for reasonable accommodation is made, the PIP must be evaluated for reasonableness. The PIP cannot be a pretext for discrimination.
*   **Action:**
    *   **HR/Legal Consultation:** The Manager must immediately consult HR and Legal to determine if the performance issues are related to the disability.
    *   **Modification:** If the PIP goals are not feasible due to the disability, the PIP may be modified (e.g., extended timeline, adjusted metrics, additional tools) to ensure the employee can succeed.
    *   **Leave for Treatment:** If the leave is related to the condition, the PIP pauses, similar to unrelated leave, until the accommodation plan is implemented.
    *   **Documentation:** Any modification to the PIP due to accommodation must be documented in the PIP file and signed by HR.

**Manager's Duty:** In all cases involving leave or accommodation, the Manager must document the action taken and ensure the PIP process remains consistent, non-retaliatory, and compliant with federal, state, and local laws.

## 4. CHECKPOINT PROTOCOL

### 📅 30-DAY CHECKPOINT
**Action:** PM and employee meet formally with HR notified. Review metrics against targets.
- **If ≥2 of 3 goals consistently met:** Continue to 60-day checkpoint with no changes.
- **If <2 of 3 goals met or partial compliance:** PM documents specific gaps, adjusts support (e.g., additional QA review, workload redistribution), and extends PIP by 30 days. Employee signs acknowledgment.

### 📅 60-DAY CHECKPOINT
**Action:** Second formal review. Compare performance to baseline and 30-day trajectory.
- **If all 3 goals consistently met for ≥30 consecutive days:** Prepare for successful completion at Day 90. Continue standard workload and review cycle.
- **If 1 goal remains below target:** Document pattern, provide final support package, and clearly communicate that Day 90 will be the final evaluation.
- **Documentation:** Progress report, metric trends, and decision memo signed by PM and HR.

### 📅 90-DAY FINAL EVALUATION
**Action:** conclusive performance review.
- **Pass:** PIP closed. Employee returns to standard annual/semi-annual review cycle. No further disciplinary notation beyond PIP record.
- **Fail:** One or more goals not sustainably met. PM, in consultation with HR, initiates next steps per company policy (reassignment, final written warning, or separation). All documentation retained per records policy.
- **Documentation:** Final evaluation report, outcome decision, and employee acknowledgment filed.

## 5. PROTECTED LEAVE & ACCOMMODATION PROTOCOL
This section governs any interruption to the PIP due to legally protected circumstances.

1. **Immediate Pause:** If the employee requests or is placed on protected leave (e.g., FMLA, ADA/interactive accommodation, workers’ compensation, parental leave, jury duty, military leave), the PIP **automatically pauses** on the start date of the leave. All metrics, deadlines, and checkpoint schedules are frozen.
2. **No Adverse Inference:** Performance is evaluated only on work completed before leave. Time on protected leave cannot be counted against the 30-60-90 timeline or used to justify PIP failure.
3. **HR Consultation:** The PM must notify HR **within 24 business hours** of receiving a leave or accommodation request. HR will:
   - Confirm eligibility/paperwork requirements
   - Issue a formal PIP pause letter with resumption date
   - Coordinate with the employee on return-to-work transition
4. **Reactivation:** Upon return, the PIP resumes from the day before leave began. Checkpoints shift proportionally. The employee is not required to “make up” missed days.
5. **Accommodation Requests During PIP:** If the employee requests a workplace or workflow accommodation (e.g., ergonomic setup, adjusted hours for medical appointments, assistive CAD tools), the PM does not unilaterally approve/deny. The request is routed to HR/ADA coordinator within 48 hours. The PIP continues alongside approved accommodations.

   - **Notify HR** within 24 hours of receiving the request, providing the request details (type of leave/accommodation, expected duration).

2. **Accommodation Process**  
   - HR conducts an **interactive assessment** (ADA/FMLA interactive process) to determine a reasonable accommodation (e.g., flexible hours, modified work‑load, assistive technology).
   - The agreed‑upon accommodation is **documented** and communicated to all parties. The PIP schedule is **re‑aligned** to reflect any additional time needed because of the accommodation.

3. **Performance Evaluation Adjustments**  
   - The **goal‑completion deadlines** are extended proportionally to the amount of protected time taken, **provided the employee is making good‑faith efforts** toward the accommodations.
   - The evaluation criteria remain the same, but the **timeline** for meeting them is adjusted. For example, if a two‑week medical leave occurs during the 60‑day phase, the 60‑day deadline becomes an 80‑day deadline, with the same targets to achieve.

4. **Protected‑Leave Non‑Retaliation**  
   - Any adverse employment action (e.g., termination, demotion) taken **because** the employee took protected leave or requested accommodation is prohibited. All decisions must be **documented independently** of the PIP outcome and approved by HR and legal counsel.

5. **Communication to the Employee**  
   - The manager must convey, in writing, that the PIP will be **temporarily suspended** and that the revised schedule will be provided once HR’s accommodation determination is finalized.
