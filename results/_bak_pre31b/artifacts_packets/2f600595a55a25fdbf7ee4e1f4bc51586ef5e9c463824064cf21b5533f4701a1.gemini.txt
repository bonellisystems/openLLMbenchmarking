## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

**Title:** Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B Across All Client Environments  
**Date:** [Current Date]

### **Context**

Our Managed Service Provider (MSP) currently uses RMM-A to monitor and manage 40 client environments. Two critical issues have emerged with RMM-A:

1. **Cost Escalation:** RMM-A uses a per-endpoint pricing model that has become increasingly expensive as our client base and device count grow.
2. **Functional Gaps:** RMM-A lacks essential patch-approval workflows, a feature repeatedly requested by clients and required for compliance and operational control.

RMM-B, the proposed replacement, addresses both concerns: it offers predictable pricing and built-in patch management workflows that align with our operational needs.

However, timing constraints complicate the transition:

- The current RMM-A license auto-renews annually.
- To prevent auto-renewal, written cancellation must be submitted **at least 30 days before** the renewal date.
- The renewal date is **60 days from today**, meaning the cancellation deadline is **30 days from today**.
- The planned migration to RMM-B is a **90-day phased rollout**, starting with the smallest clients.

This creates a 60-day gap between the cancellation of RMM-A and the completion of the migration. If RMM-B fails during the migration, some clients may be left without active RMM coverage — a serious operational and reputational risk.

---

### **Decision**

We will proceed with the migration from RMM-A to RMM-B under the following conditions:

1. **Immediate Action:**  
   The written cancellation notice for RMM-A will be sent within the next 30 days (by the cancellation deadline), ensuring we avoid an unwanted renewal.

2. **Phased Migration Plan:**  
   Migrate client environments in four phases over 90 days, prioritizing smaller, lower-risk clients first. Each phase includes:
   - Pre-migration assessment
   - Agent deployment and configuration
   - Validation of monitoring, alerting, and patch workflows
   - Decommissioning of RMM-A agents (post-validation)

3. **Contingency for RMM-B Failure:**  
   If a critical defect, service outage, or showstopper issue is discovered in RMM-B **after** RMM-A has been cancelled, the following actions will be taken:
   - Pause the migration immediately.
   - Re-engage RMM-A vendor support to negotiate a temporary license extension or pay-as-you-go coverage (even post-cancellation, some vendors offer short-term reinstatement under emergency terms).
   - For clients already migrated to RMM-B: attempt remediation; if unsuccessful, revert monitoring to RMM-A if technically feasible (agent reinstallation, configuration recovery).
   - For clients not yet migrated: delay migration and maintain RMM-A via emergency license or temporary agent reinstatement where possible.
   - Communicate transparently with affected clients, detailing the issue and recovery plan.

4. **Fallback Ownership:**  
   The Director of Operations is accountable for executing the fallback plan and maintaining a documented rollback procedure for each client environment during the migration window.

5. **Monitoring During Transition:**  
   All client environments will remain under active monitoring during migration. Dual-agent presence (RMM-A and RMM-B) is permitted temporarily to ensure no coverage gaps.

---

### **Consequences**

**Positive Outcomes:**
- Long-term cost savings due to RMM-B’s favorable pricing model.
- Improved service quality with integrated patch-approval workflows.
- Enhanced client satisfaction and compliance posture.

**Risks and Mitigations:**
- **Risk:** RMM-B fails after RMM-A is cancelled, leaving clients unprotected.  
  **Mitigation:** Pre-negotiate emergency support terms with RMM-A vendor. Maintain migration rollback playbooks and test reinstallation procedures in advance.

- **Risk:** Migration delays prevent completion within 90 days.  
  **Mitigation:** Schedule buffer time in the plan; prioritize clients based on complexity and criticality.

- **Risk:** Data or configuration loss during migration.  
  **Mitigation:** Perform full backups of RMM-A configurations and client policies prior to decommissioning.

- **Risk:** Client disruption due to agent conflicts or monitoring gaps.  
  **Mitigation:** Conduct migration during maintenance windows; validate alerting and monitoring before deactivating RMM-A agents.

2. What is the fallback plan for clients that have not yet been migrated if RMM‑B encounters a critical failure after we have already cancelled RMM‑A?

## 2. Decision  

1. **Cancellation Timing**  
   - **Send the written cancellation notice to RMM‑A today** (Day 0). This satisfies the “≥ 30 days before renewal” requirement because the renewal date is 60 days from now.  
   - The cancellation will become effective **30 days from today** (i.e., on Day 30).  

2. **Phased Migration Schedule**  
   - **Phase 1 – Pilot (Days 0‑15):** Migrate the *smallest* 5 client environments (≈ 5 % of total endpoints).  
   - **Phase 2 – Small‑Client Wave (Days 16‑45):** Migrate all *small* clients (≈ 15 % of total endpoints).  
   - **Phase 3 – Medium‑Client Wave (Days 46‑75):** Migrate *medium* clients (≈ 35 % of total endpoints).  
   - **Phase 4 – Large‑Client Wave (Days 76‑90):** Migrate the remaining *large* clients (≈ 45 % of total endpoints).  

   *Each phase must complete with ≥ 99.9 % uptime, zero data loss, and validated patch‑approval workflow before moving to the next.*

3. **Contingency & Rollback**  
   - **If a critical failure occurs in RMM‑B after Day 30 (when RMM‑A has already been cancelled):**  
     - Immediately **re‑enable RMM‑A** for any client still on the legacy platform. This is achieved by restoring the RMM‑A agent from the last known good backup and re‑establishing the connection before the 30‑day cancellation window expires.  
     - The **rollback window** is defined as **“≤ 5 days after failure detection”**; if rollback cannot be completed within this window, the migration for that client is paused, and an incident is declared.  
   - **Clients not yet migrated when RMM‑A is cancelled** will continue to be monitored **only** by RMM‑A until they are successfully moved to RMM‑B. If RMM‑A is forced offline (e.g., provider terminates service), the migration team must execute the *Emergency Migration Protocol* (see Consequences).  

4. **Communication & Governance**  
   - All client‑facing notifications (migration start dates, expected downtime, rollback notices) will be sent at least **7 days in advance**.  
   - A **Migration Review Board** (MRB) comprising Ops, Security, and Client Success will sign‑off each phase before proceeding to the next.

### 3.3 What Happens to Clients Not Yet Migrated If RMM‑B Fails After Cancellation?  

1. **Immediate Activation of RMM‑A Restoration**  
   - The **Rollback Playbook** is executed:  
     - Retrieve the most recent **agent backup** from the centralized repository.  
     - Re‑install the RMM‑A client on the affected endpoints.  
     - Verify connectivity to RMM‑A within **2 hours** of failure detection.  

2. **If Restoration Fails**  
   - **Escalate to Incident Management** (Severity 1).  
   - Engage the **RMM‑A vendor’s emergency support** (pre‑negotiated 24‑hour response SLA).  
   - If RMM‑A cannot be restored within the **5‑day rollback window**, the client is placed in **“Exception Status”**:  
     - The client remains **unmonitored** until a manual interim solution (e.g., third‑party monitoring) is provisioned.  
     - Client Success contacts the client to explain the situation and negotiate a temporary service extension or alternative monitoring arrangement.  

3. **Post‑Recovery**  
   - Once RMM‑A is back online, the migration team re‑evaluates the failed client’s environment, documents the root cause, and either retries migration after fixes or **re‑queues** the client for a later phase.  
   - All incidents are logged in the **Migration Incident Register** for future risk assessment and process improvement.

Status: Accepted (with contingency conditions below), 2026-07-17.

patch-approval workflows. RMM-A's current term auto-renews annually unless we send written
cancellation at least 30 days before the renewal date, which is 60 days from now -- meaning
the cancellation notice must go out within the next 30 days. The planned migration covers all

Decision: Proceed with the migration to RMM-B, phased over 90 days as planned. However, do
NOT send RMM-A's final, unconditional cancellation notice at day 30 as a blind default. By

validated on RMM-B -- most of the client base, including our largest and highest-risk
environments, will still be on RMM-A. Before sending the cancellation notice, contact the
RMM-A vendor to negotiate one of: (a) a short paid month-to-month bridge period past the
official renewal date instead of a hard cancel-by-day-30, or (b) confirmation that a
cancellation notice can be rescinded or converted to a partial/reduced-seat renewal if we
need to extend coverage for not-yet-migrated clients. Only send the full, final cancellation
once at least 75% of clients are validated and stable on RMM-B, even if that means paying for
a short overlap period between the two platforms.

Consequences: If we had sent the cancellation notice as originally planned at day 30 with no
bridge negotiated, and RMM-B then had a significant issue (e.g., a monitoring gap or failed
patch-approval rollout) after RMM-A access lapsed, the ~25+ not-yet-migrated clients would
have zero RMM coverage with no fallback -- an unacceptable, avoidable outage risk purely from
contract-timing mismanagement rather than a technical failure. The bridge/negotiated-extension
approach costs some additional short-term spend (dual-platform overlap) but removes that
single point of failure. If RMM-B proves unstable during the phased rollout, the negotiated
bridge is also what allows a full rollback to RMM-A for any client without an active-coverage
gap.

# Architecture Decision Record: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

## Context

The MSP's current RMM platform (RMM-A) has become cost-prohibitive due to its per-endpoint pricing model and lacks critical patch approval workflows that are essential for our clients' security posture. The new platform (RMM-B) addresses both of these concerns with competitive pricing and built-in patch approval capabilities.

The leadership team has determined that migrating all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B is necessary for operational efficiency and client service quality. The migration will be conducted in phases over a 90-day period, beginning with the smallest client environments.

## Decision

We will proceed with the migration of all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B using a phased approach over 90 days, beginning with the smallest clients. To facilitate this transition, we will send the written cancellation notice to RMM-A 30 days before the annual renewal date (60 days from now), which will result in RMM-A auto-renewal being canceled effective immediately upon cancellation.

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- **Cost Savings**: Elimination of expensive per-endpoint pricing from RMM-A
- **Enhanced Functionality**: Implementation of patch approval workflows in RMM-B
- **Risk Mitigation**: Phased migration reduces impact on client services
- **Improved Client Service**: Better security posture through patch management workflows

### Negative Consequences and Risks

**Timing Conflict**: The 90-day migration timeline conflicts with the 30-day cancellation deadline for RMM-A. If migration fails to complete within the 30-day window before RMM-A renewal, clients will be left without remote management capabilities.

**Critical Failure Scenario**: If RMM-B experiences critical issues (system outages, functionality gaps, or integration problems) after RMM-A has been canceled but before all clients have been migrated, the following consequences will occur:

1. **Immediate Service Disruption**: All clients not yet migrated will lose remote management capabilities
2. **Client Impact**: Clients will be without monitoring, patch management, and remote support capabilities
3. **Recovery Complexity**: Reverting to RMM-A will require significant effort to restore services
4. **Client Relationship Damage**: Potential loss of client confidence and trust

### Recovery Plan

In the event of migration failure or RMM-B system issues after RMM-A cancellation:

1. **Immediate Activation**: Revert to RMM-A within 48 hours of detection
2. **Client Notification**: Notify affected clients within 24 hours of service disruption
3. **Emergency Resources**: Deploy emergency support personnel to restore RMM-A services
4. **Compensation**: Provide appropriate compensation or service credits to affected clients
5. **Post-Incident Review**: Conduct comprehensive post-mortem analysis to prevent recurrence

### Success Criteria

Migration success will be measured by:
- Complete migration of all 40 client environments within 90 days
- No service interruption during transition period
- All clients operational on RMM-B with full functionality
- Zero client impact or disruption during migration process

This decision requires close monitoring of the migration timeline and readiness of RMM-B systems to ensure no client service interruption occurs due to timing conflicts.

- **SLA/contractual risk:** Breaches of our own monitoring/patch SLAs with clients become likely if RMM-B fails and no parallel coverage is in place.

### Required mitigations & fallback actions
1. **Pilot validation before cancellation:** Complete a successful pilot with 2–3 smallest clients. Confirm agent stability, alert routing, and patch-approval workflow before issuing the cancellation notice.
2. **Hard rollback triggers:** Define and document go/no-go thresholds (e.g., >15% agent failure rate, missed critical alerts for 24+ hours, patch-approval workflow breakdown). If triggered, halt migration immediately and escalate to leadership.
3. **Vendor re-engagement clause:** Upon rollback trigger, contact RMM-A vendor to negotiate short-term reactivation, extended termination notice, or migration-support partnership. Standard contract penalties may apply, but business continuity takes precedence.
4. **Parallel coverage plan:** Maintain a lightweight temporary monitoring solution (e.g., extended RMM-B evaluation license, open-source agent fleet, or manual runbook escalation matrix) to cover unmigrated clients during any RMM-B instability or final migration phase.
5. **Contract flexibility fallback:** If pilot data shows unacceptable risk, negotiate with RMM-A for month-to-month conversion, a pro-rated early termination, or a delayed cancellation that aligns with migration completion.
6. **Client communication:** Proactively notify affected clients of the transition timeline, expected downtime windows, and new support contacts. Document SLA impacts and obtain written acknowledgment where required.

### Tracking & Ownership
- **Migration Lead:** Owns phase sequencing, quality gates, daily status reporting.
- **Contract Owner:** Owns cancellation notice delivery, vendor re-engagement, and renewal deadline tracking.
- **Decision Authority:** MSP Leadership (unanimous or designated executive) controls rollback and parallel-coverage activation.
- **Review Cadence:** Weekly migration status until pilot sign-off; daily thereafter. Immediate escalation on any rollback trigger.

# ADR 001: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

## Context
Our Managed Services Provider (MSP) currently utilizes RMM-A as the Remote Monitoring and Management platform for all 40 client environments. Over time, several challenges have emerged:

1. **Cost**: The per-endpoint pricing model of RMM-A has become increasingly expensive, impacting our profitability margins.
2. **Functionality**: RMM-A lacks essential patch-approval workflows that are frequently required for our clients, leading to manual workarounds and potential delays in security updates.

An alternative platform, RMM-B, has been identified. RMM-B offers a more cost-effective pricing structure and includes the patch-approval workflows we need. Given these factors, the leadership team has decided to migrate all client environments to RMM-B.

However, there are timing constraints to consider:
- **RMM-A's Auto-Renewal**: RMM-A auto-renews annually. The current term is set to renew in 60 days, with a requirement for written cancellation at least 30 days before the renewal date to avoid an additional annual commitment.
- **Migration Timeline**: The proposed migration plan is to transition client environments in phases over the next 90 days, starting with the smallest clients to minimize disruption.

## Decision
The leadership team has decided to proceed with the migration from RMM-A to RMM-B, acknowledging the timing conflict between the 90-day migration period and the 30-day cancellation deadline for RMM-A. The following steps will be taken:

1. **Immediate Action**: Send the written cancellation notice for RMM-A **today**, ensuring it is at least 30 days before the renewal date to avoid automatic renewal.
2. **Phased Migration**: Begin the migration of client environments to RMM-B immediately, prioritizing the smallest clients first to facilitate a smoother transition and to quickly realize benefits from RMM-B's features.
3. **Contingency Planning**: In the event that issues with RMM-B arise after the cancellation of RMM-A, especially for clients not yet migrated within the 90-day window, the following contingency measures will be implemented:
   - **Temporary Workarounds**: Utilize manual processes or alternative tools to manage and monitor the unmigrated clients' environments until RMM-B is fully operational for them.
   - **Client Communication**: Proactively communicate with affected clients about the situation, the steps being taken to resolve any issues, and the expected timeline for full migration to RMM-B.
   - **Financial Adjustments**: Consider temporary adjustments to service fees or contracts for clients significantly impacted by the transition issues, to maintain goodwill and client satisfaction.

## Consequences
### Positive
- **Cost Savings**: Transitioning to RMM-B is expected to reduce costs due to its more favorable pricing model.
- **Enhanced Functionality**: The availability of patch-approval workflows in RMM-B will streamline operations and improve service delivery to clients.
- **Future-Proofing**: Adopting a platform that meets current and anticipated needs positions the MSP for scalable growth and improved service offerings.

### Negative
- **Risk of Service Disruption**: The tight timeline between cancelling RMM-A and fully migrating all clients to RMM-B poses a risk of service disruption, especially if RMM-B encounters unforeseen issues.
- **Operational Strain**: The initial phase of the migration may require additional resources and effort, potentially straining operational capacities.
- **Client Impact**: Should RMM-B fail to meet expectations or encounter significant issues, clients not yet migrated could experience service quality degradation, risking client satisfaction and retention.

### Mitigation Strategies
- **Parallel Monitoring**: During the migration period, maintain parallel monitoring capabilities using both RMM-A (until its cancellation takes effect) and RMM-B to ensure continuous oversight of all client environments.
- **Stakeholder Engagement**: Keep open lines of communication with clients, providing regular updates on migration progress and any potential impacts.
- **Performance Benchmarks**: Establish clear performance benchmarks for RMM-B early in the migration process to quickly identify and address any shortcomings.

This ADR documents the decision to migrate from RMM-A to RMM-B, the rationale behind it, the planned approach, and the potential consequences, including strategies to mitigate risks associated with the transition.

## Consequences

### 1. Positive Consequences
*   **Cost Reduction:** Monthly spend decreases once RMM-A contracts lapse and RMM-B scales in.
*   **Operational Capability:** Team gains automated patch-approval workflows, reducing manual ticketing and human error.
*   **Contract Security:** We avoid the annual auto-renewal cost hike.

*   **RMM-A Dies:** Day 60 (after cancellation notice sent on Day 30).
*   **Migration Complete:** Day 90.

1.  **Total Management Loss:** There is no RMM-A fallback. These clients are completely "blind" to the operations team. No patching, no inventory visibility, no remote access.
2.  **SLA Breach Exposure:** If any of these clients have SLAs for uptime monitoring or proactive maintenance, we are in violation immediately.
3.  **Emergency Recovery Delay:** If a client on RMM-B fails, and the issue is related to infrastructure shared by unmigrated clients, response time is degraded because the team is split between debugging RMM-B and manually logging into unmigrated hosts.
4.  **Data Inconsistency:** Patch status and inventory data for the unmigrated portion will become stale, meaning the client's "health picture" is inaccurate until they are migrated.

### 3. Mitigation Required (For Future Hires)
If you find yourself on this task in the future, verify the following before assuming the current plan is safe:

*   **Verify the "Failover" Gap:** Does RMM-B offer a "dual-connection" or "legacy agent" mode that allows unmigrated clients to stay on RMM-A's management plane? If not, the gap is a hard constraint.
*   **Push the Deadline:** If RMM-B has not been live-tested with 100% of clients, the cancellation notice should be pushed back. Sending the notice today assumes RMM-B will be bug-free by Day 60.
*   **Emergency Access Plan:** Ensure the team has "break-glass" credentials for the unmigrated clients (e.g., SSH keys, VNC, or RDP) in the event RMM-B fails and the 30-day cancellation window has already passed.
*   **Client Communication:** Clients must be informed that during the 90-day window, *some* of their endpoints will briefly be unmanaged during the cutover.
