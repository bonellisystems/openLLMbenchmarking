### Backup/DR Gap Analysis

#### 1. **RPO (Recovery Point Objective) and RTO (Recovery Time Objective) Gaps:**
   - **Nightly Full Backup Only:** The current configuration performs a nightly full backup, which does not meet the RPO of at most 4 hours. In the event of a failure, up to 24 hours of data could be lost.
   - **No Offsite or Cloud Backup:** The lack of an offsite or cloud backup means that the RTO of 8 business hours cannot be guaranteed, especially if the physical site is compromised (e.g., fire, flood, or ransomware that encrypts local backups).
   - **Untested Restores:** The last successful restore test was conducted 18 months ago. Without recent testing, there's no assurance that backups are viable, directly impacting both RPO and RTO compliance.

#### 2. **Ransomware Resilience Gaps:**
   - **Co-located Backup NAS:** The backup NAS is in the same server closet as the production VM host and is domain-joined. This makes it a single point of failure, especially vulnerable to ransomware that can traverse the network using domain credentials, as evidenced by the peer firm's incident.
   - **No Air-Gapped or Immutable Backups:** The current setup lacks any form of air-gapped or immutable backups that could prevent alteration or encryption by ransomware.

### Remediation Plan (Priority Order)

#### **Priority 1: Implement Incremental Backups with a Shorter RPO**
   - **Action:** Transition from nightly full backups to a combination of daily full backups and more frequent (e.g., hourly) incremental backups. This ensures data loss does not exceed 4 hours.
   - **Tools/Methods:** Utilize SQL Server's built-in backup capabilities or third-party solutions that support incremental backups and can handle transaction log backups for near real-time protection.

#### **Priority 2: Establish Offsite/Cloud Backup with Air-Gap or Immutability**
   - **Action:** Set up an offsite or cloud-based backup solution that is not directly accessible from the production network. This could involve cloud storage with immutability features (e.g., AWS S3 Object Lock, Azure Immutable Blob Storage) or a physically separate backup site.
   - **Ransomware Protection:** Ensure the offsite/cloud backups are not domain-joined or directly reachable from the production network to mitigate ransomware risks.

#### **Priority 3: Regularly Test Restore Procedures**
   - **Action:** Conduct quarterly restore tests from both on-prem and offsite/cloud backups to ensure data integrity and to validate that the RPO and RTO objectives can be met.
   - **Documentation:** Document the restore procedures and results, adjusting the backup strategy as needed based on test outcomes.

#### **Priority 4: Decommission or Secure the Current Backup NAS**
   - **Action:** Either secure the current NAS with network segmentation and access controls that prevent it from being a ransomware vector or decommission it in favor of the new offsite/cloud solution.
   - **Security Enhancements:** If retained, apply strict access controls, use non-domain credentials for backup jobs, and consider network segmentation to isolate the NAS.

### Quantifying the Blast Radius if Ransomware Hits Before Fixes

- **Data Loss:** Up to 24 hours of data could be lost due to the nightly backup window, violating the RPO.
- **Downtime:** Without viable offsite backups, restoring the case-management system could take significantly longer than 8 business hours, potentially days, depending on the extent of data corruption and the availability of clean backup sources.
- **Operational Impact:** Missing court filing deadlines could lead to malpractice claims, client dissatisfaction, and potential financial penalties.
- **Reputation Damage:** A prolonged outage or data loss could damage the firm's reputation, both among clients and within the legal community.

### Pre-Change Requirement: Validation of Current Backup Integrity

**Before any changes are made to the current backup regime, the following must occur:**

1. **Immediate Restore Test:** Perform a full restore test from the existing NAS to a separate, isolated environment to confirm that the backups are intact and can be successfully restored. This step is critical to ensure that there is at least one known good backup before transitioning to a new system.
   
2. **Documentation and Sign-Off:** Document the restore test results and obtain sign-off from key stakeholders (IT, legal, compliance) confirming the viability of the current backups.

3. **Parallel Backup Implementation:** During the transition to the new backup strategy, run both the old and new backup methods in parallel for a defined period (e.g., 30 days) to ensure the new system is capturing and retaining data as expected without affecting the safety net provided by the existing backups.

By meticulously following these steps, the firm can mitigate the risks associated with both data loss and ransomware attacks, ensuring compliance with its malpractice insurance requirements and safeguarding its operations against potential disruptions.

#### **Gaps Against Stated RPO/RTO and Ransomware Resilience**

1. **RPO Violation (4-hour requirement)**  
   - **Gap**: Current nightly full backups result in a 24-hour RPO, exceeding the 4-hour requirement.  
   - **Impact**: Up to 24 hours of case data, billing records, and client communications could be lost after a failure, violating insurance terms and risking court deadlines.  

2. **RTO Violation (8-hour requirement)**  
   - **Gap**: No restore testing in 18 months means the RTO is unverified. Restoring a 24-hour full backup (plus potential manual recovery) could exceed 8 hours, especially if backups are corrupt or inaccessible.  
   - **Impact**: Failure to meet RTO risks missed court deadlines, malpractice penalties, and client trust erosion.  

3. **Ransomware Resilience Failure**  
   - **Gap 1**: Co-located backup NAS in the same server closet as production creates a **single point of failure**. Ransomware can spread via network, encrypting both production and backups simultaneously.  
   - **Gap 2**: Domain-joined backup NAS uses domain credentials, allowing ransomware to access and delete backups using compromised accounts.  
   - **Gap 3**: No immutable or air-gapped backups. Backups are vulnerable to deletion/modification by attackers.  
   - **Impact**: Ransomware would encrypt both production and backups, leading to **complete data loss** and no recovery option.  

4. **No Offsite/Cloud Backup**  
   - **Gap**: All backups are on-prem and physically co-located. Physical disasters (fire, theft) or ransomware targeting the entire facility would destroy all data.  
   - **Impact**: Permanent loss of case files, billing records, and privileged communications, violating confidentiality and regulatory requirements (e.g., bar association rules).  

5. **Backup Testing Deficiency**  
   - **Gap**: No restore testing in 18 months invalidates backup integrity and RTO feasibility.  
   - **Impact**: Unknown if backups are restorable within 8 hours. Corrupt backups could force costly manual data reconstruction.  

6. **Backup Type Limitation**  
   - **Gap**: Full backups only (no transaction logs or incremental backups) extend recovery time and increase RPO.  
   - **Impact**: Slow restores risk exceeding RTO; larger backup windows increase vulnerability window.  

---

### **Remediation Plan (Priority Order)**

#### **Priority 1: Address RPO/RTO and Ransomware Resilience (Immediate)**  
- **Action 1**: Implement **frequent transaction log backups** every 15–30 minutes to achieve a 4-hour RPO.  
- **Action 2**: Adopt the **3-2-1 backup rule**:  
  - 3 copies: Production + local backup + offsite/cloud.  
  - 2 media: NAS + immutable cloud storage (e.g., Azure Blob Storage with versioning).  
  - 1 offsite: Use a cloud provider (e.g., AWS, Azure) with **immutable backups** and network isolation.  
- **Action 3**: **Decouple backup access** from domain credentials. Use:  
  - Dedicated backup accounts with minimal permissions.  
  - Certificate-based authentication for NAS/cloud.  
  - Isolated backup network segment (no direct access from production).  

#### **Priority 2: Restore Validation and Testing**  
- **Action 4**: **Test restores quarterly** to verify RTO compliance. Simulate scenarios:  
  - Full system restore from cloud.  
  - Point-in-time recovery using transaction logs.  
  - Document recovery steps and timelines.  
- **Action 5**: **Retain backups for 30+ days** to support longer recovery windows and audits.  

#### **Priority 3: Hardening Against Ransomware**  
- **Action 6**: Enable **immutable backups** in the cloud (e.g., Azure Blob Storage with soft-delete + legal hold).  
- **Action 7**: **Isolate the backup NAS** from production:  
  - Move to a separate subnet with firewall rules blocking inbound production traffic.  
  - Disable domain-join; use local service accounts.  
- **Action 8**: Implement **read-only backups** (e.g., Veeam Immutable Backup) to prevent deletion/encryption.  

#### **Priority 4: Offsite and Cloud Integration**  
- **Action 9**: **Automate offsite replication** to cloud storage with encryption (AES-256) and multi-factor authentication.  
- **Action 10**: Use **backup-as-a-service** (e.g., Druva, Cohesity) for automated, immutable cloud retention.  

---

### **Blast Radius if Ransomware Hits Before Fixes**  
- **Data Loss**: 24 hours of case files, billing, and client communications (RPO violation).  
- **System Downtime**: 8–72+ hours to recover (RTO violation), risking:  
  - Missed court deadlines → Case dismissal/sanctions.  
  - Loss of billable hours → Financial impact.  
  - Breach of attorney-client privilege → Regulatory fines and malpractice claims.  
- **Reputational Damage**: Client trust loss, negative reviews, and potential bar association sanctions.  
- **Operational Impact**: Manual data reconstruction costing $50k–$200k and weeks of attorney downtime.  

---

### **Critical Pre-Change Requirement**  
**Before decommissioning the current backup system**, the following **must** occur:  
1. **Validate New Backup Integrity**:  
   - Perform a **full restore test** of the new backup system in a staging environment.  
   - Confirm backups are **restorable within 8 hours** and meet RPO.  
   - Verify **immutability** and **offsite isolation** via penetration testing.  
2. **Parallel Run**:  
   - Maintain **both old and new backups** for 30 days.  
   - **Disable old backups only after** new backups pass testing.  
3. **Documentation**:  
   - Document the new restore process, RPO/RTO validation, and disaster recovery plan.  

**Failure to do this risks discovering new backup flaws after old backups are gone, leaving the firm with no recovery options.**  

---  
**Summary**: The current setup is critically vulnerable to ransomware and non-compliant with RPO/RTO. Immediate remediation focusing on immutable cloud backups, access hardening, and testing is essential to avoid catastrophic data loss and regulatory penalties.
