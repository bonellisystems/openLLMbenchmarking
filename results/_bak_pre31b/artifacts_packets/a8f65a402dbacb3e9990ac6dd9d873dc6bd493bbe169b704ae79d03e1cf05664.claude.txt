## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

**Title:** Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

#### 1. Context
The MSP currently manages 40 client environments using **RMM-A**. Leadership has identified two critical deficiencies with the current platform:
1.  **Cost Scaling:** RMM-A's per-endpoint pricing model is becoming cost-prohibitive as client environments grow.
2.  **Feature Gap:** RMM-A lacks native patch-approval workflows, which are increasingly requested by clients and required for our compliance processes.

**RMM-B** has been evaluated and satisfies both requirements: it offers a more favorable pricing structure and includes the necessary patch-approval workflows.

**Contractual Constraints & Timing Conflict:**
*   **RMM-A Renewal:** The current RMM-A contract auto-renews annually. The renewal date is in **60 days**.
*   **Cancellation Policy:** RMM-A requires written cancellation notice at least **30 days** prior to renewal to avoid charges.
*   **Migration Timeline:** The technical plan requires a phased migration over **90 days**, starting with the smallest clients and working up to larger environments.
*   **Conflict:** The cancellation deadline (30 days) occurs well before the migration can be completed (90 days). Cancelling RMM-A on time creates a 60-day window where RMM-A access will be terminated for clients that are not yet migrated to RMM-B.

#### 2. Decision
The leadership team unanimously agreed to the following actions:

1.  **Migrate to RMM-B:** All 40 client environments will be migrated from RMM-A to RMM-B.
2.  **Immediate Cancellation:** Written cancellation notice for RMM-A will be sent **immediately** to ensure it is received within the 30-day window, avoiding the upcoming auto-renewal.
3.  **Phased Execution:** Migration will proceed in phases over the next 90 days, prioritizing smallest client environments first.
4.  **Risk Acceptance:** The team explicitly accepts the risk that RMM-A will be cancelled while a significant portion of clients remain unmigrated. There will be no ability to retain RMM-A for "straggler" clients once the cancellation is processed.

#### 3. Consequences

**Positive Outcomes:**
*   **Cost Reduction:** Elimination of RMM-A's per-endpoint pricing structure will reduce recurring expenses.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Implementation of patch-approval workflows in RMM-B will improve client compliance and reduce manual engineering overhead.
*   **Platform Unification:** All clients will operate on a single RMM platform, simplifying support and training.

**Negative Outcomes & Risks:**
*   **Loss of Rollback Capability:** Once the cancellation notice is sent, **RMM-A cannot be reactivated** for clients not yet migrated. There is no "safety net" to return unmigrated clients to the previous platform.
*   **Migration Risk during Cancellation Window:** For the 60-day period following cancellation but preceding full migration, the organization is dependent entirely on RMM-B for both migrated and pending clients.
*   **Scenario: RMM-B Failure Post-Cancellation:**
    *   *Impact:* If RMM-B experiences critical failures, security vulnerabilities, or is deemed unsuitable *after* RMM-A has been cancelled, **clients not yet migrated cannot be moved back to RMM-A.**
    *   *Remediation:* In this event, leadership and operations would be forced to either:
        1.  Procure and deploy a third-party RMM solution ("RMM-C") under duress to cover unmigrated clients.
        2.  Accelerate the migration to RMM-B immediately to minimize exposure, even if the migration plan is not technically ready.
        3.  Accept a period where unmigrated clients have no RMM coverage until a new solution is onboarded.
*   **Operational Burden:** The phased migration requires careful tracking to ensure no client is left in an unmanaged state. The "smallest first" approach mitigates blast radius but delays the migration of larger, potentially more complex environments.

**Future Reference:**
Any future hire or engineer reviewing this record should note that **RMM-A is a closed option.** If the RMM-B migration encounters show-stopping issues, the team cannot revert to the legacy tool. Mitigation strategies must focus on RMM-B stability or the rapid procurement of an alternative vendor. The 30-day cancellation deadline was prioritized over migration safety; this trade-off was a conscious decision to save costs and remove feature constraints, accepting the vendor-lock-in risk during the transition period.

# Architecture Decision Record: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

## Context

The MSP's current RMM platform (RMM-A) has become cost-prohibitive due to its per-endpoint pricing model and lacks critical patch approval workflows that are essential for our clients' security posture. The new platform (RMM-B) addresses both of these concerns with competitive pricing and built-in patch approval capabilities.

The leadership team has determined that migrating all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B is necessary for operational efficiency and client service quality. The migration will be conducted in phases over a 90-day period, beginning with the smallest client environments.

## Decision

We will proceed with the migration of all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B using a phased approach over 90 days, beginning with the smallest clients. To facilitate this transition, we will send the written cancellation notice to RMM-A 30 days before the annual renewal date (60 days from now), which will result in RMM-A auto-renewal being canceled effective immediately upon cancellation.

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- **Cost Savings**: Elimination of expensive per-endpoint pricing from RMM-A
- **Enhanced Functionality**: Implementation of patch approval workflows in RMM-B
- **Risk Mitigation**: Phased migration reduces impact on client services
- **Improved Client Service**: Better security posture through patch management workflows

### Negative Consequences and Risks

**Timing Conflict**: The 90-day migration timeline conflicts with the 30-day cancellation deadline for RMM-A. If migration fails to complete within the 30-day window before RMM-A renewal, clients will be left without remote management capabilities.

**Critical Failure Scenario**: If RMM-B experiences critical issues (system outages, functionality gaps, or integration problems) after RMM-A has been canceled but before all clients have been migrated, the following consequences will occur:

1. **Immediate Service Disruption**: All clients not yet migrated will lose remote management capabilities
2. **Client Impact**: Clients will be without monitoring, patch management, and remote support capabilities
3. **Recovery Complexity**: Reverting to RMM-A will require significant effort to restore services
4. **Client Relationship Damage**: Potential loss of client confidence and trust

### Recovery Plan

In the event of migration failure or RMM-B system issues after RMM-A cancellation:

1. **Immediate Activation**: Revert to RMM-A within 48 hours of detection
2. **Client Notification**: Notify affected clients within 24 hours of service disruption
3. **Emergency Resources**: Deploy emergency support personnel to restore RMM-A services
4. **Compensation**: Provide appropriate compensation or service credits to affected clients
5. **Post-Incident Review**: Conduct comprehensive post-mortem analysis to prevent recurrence

### Success Criteria

Migration success will be measured by:
- Complete migration of all 40 client environments within 90 days
- No service interruption during transition period
- All clients operational on RMM-B with full functionality
- Zero client impact or disruption during migration process

This decision requires close monitoring of the migration timeline and readiness of RMM-B systems to ensure no client service interruption occurs due to timing conflicts.

## 2. Decision  

1. **Cancellation Timing**

# ADR 001: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

## Context
Our Managed Services Provider (MSP) currently utilizes RMM-A as the Remote Monitoring and Management platform for all 40 client environments. Over time, several challenges have emerged:

1. **Cost**: The per-endpoint pricing model of RMM-A has become increasingly expensive, impacting our profitability margins.
2. **Functionality**: RMM-A lacks essential patch-approval workflows that are frequently required for our clients, leading to manual workarounds and potential delays in security updates.

An alternative platform, RMM-B, has been identified. RMM-B offers a more cost-effective pricing structure and includes the patch-approval workflows we need. Given these factors, the leadership team has decided to migrate all client environments to RMM-B.

However, there are timing constraints to consider:
- **RMM-A's Auto-Renewal**: RMM-A auto-renews annually. The current term is set to renew in 60 days, with a requirement for written cancellation at least 30 days before the renewal date to avoid an additional annual commitment.
- **Migration Timeline**: The proposed migration plan is to transition client environments in phases over the next 90 days, starting with the smallest clients to minimize disruption.

## Decision
The leadership team has decided to proceed with the migration from RMM-A to RMM-B, acknowledging the timing conflict between the 90-day migration period and the 30-day cancellation deadline for RMM-A. The following steps will be taken:

1. **Immediate Action**: Send the written cancellation notice for RMM-A **today**, ensuring it is at least 30 days before the renewal date to avoid automatic renewal.
2. **Phased Migration**: Begin the migration of client environments to RMM-B immediately, prioritizing the smallest clients first to facilitate a smoother transition and to quickly realize benefits from RMM-B's features.
3. **Contingency Planning**: In the event that issues with RMM-B arise after the cancellation of RMM-A, especially for clients not yet migrated within the 90-day window, the following contingency measures will be implemented:
   - **Temporary Workarounds**: Utilize manual processes or alternative tools to manage and monitor the unmigrated clients' environments until RMM-B is fully operational for them.
   - **Client Communication**: Proactively communicate with affected clients about the situation, the steps being taken to resolve any issues, and the expected timeline for full migration to RMM-B.
   - **Financial Adjustments**: Consider temporary adjustments to service fees or contracts for clients significantly impacted by the transition issues, to maintain goodwill and client satisfaction.

## Consequences
### Positive
- **Cost Savings**: Transitioning to RMM-B is expected to reduce costs due to its more favorable pricing model.
- **Enhanced Functionality**: The availability of patch-approval workflows in RMM-B will streamline operations and improve service delivery to clients.
- **Future-Proofing**: Adopting a platform that meets current and anticipated needs positions the MSP for scalable growth and improved service offerings.

### Negative
- **Risk of Service Disruption**: The tight timeline between cancelling RMM-A and fully migrating all clients to RMM-B poses a risk of service disruption, especially if RMM-B encounters unforeseen issues.
- **Operational Strain**: The initial phase of the migration may require additional resources and effort, potentially straining operational capacities.
- **Client Impact**: Should RMM-B fail to meet expectations or encounter significant issues, clients not yet migrated could experience service quality degradation, risking client satisfaction and retention.

### Mitigation Strategies
- **Parallel Monitoring**: During the migration period, maintain parallel monitoring capabilities using both RMM-A (until its cancellation takes effect) and RMM-B to ensure continuous oversight of all client environments.
- **Stakeholder Engagement**: Keep open lines of communication with clients, providing regular updates on migration progress and any potential impacts.
- **Performance Benchmarks**: Establish clear performance benchmarks for RMM-B early in the migration process to quickly identify and address any shortcomings.

This ADR documents the decision to migrate from RMM-A to RMM-B, the rationale behind it, the planned approach, and the potential consequences, including strategies to mitigate risks associated with the transition.

contract-timing mismanagement rather than a technical failure. The bridge/negotiated-extension
approach costs some additional short-term spend (dual-platform overlap) but removes that
single point of failure. If RMM-B proves unstable during the phased rollout, the negotiated
bridge is also what allows a full rollback to RMM-A for any client without an active-coverage
