### 1.1 Keyword Cannibalisation  

* **Exact‑match overlap** – All three URLs contain the phrase *hipaa training* (or a close variant). [model] treats them as competing for the same head‑term.  
* **Diluted link equity** – The 45 referring domains point to Page 1, but only a handful of those links use the exact anchor text “hipaa training.” The other two pages receive a trickle of links (8 + 3 RDs) and most of those anchors are longer, more specific phrases (“compliance training for employees”, “online HIPAA courses”).  
* **Similar search intent** – All three pages answer the same user query: “how do I get HIPAA training?”  The only distinguishing factor is the depth of coverage (overview vs. employee‑focused vs. course catalog). Because the intent is virtually identical, [model] cannot decide which one best satisfies the query, so it shows them alternately.  

### 1.2 Why the Rankings Bounce 6 ↔ 14  

* **Limited topical depth on each page** – Each page only scratches the surface of the broader “HIPAA training” topic. None has a clear, comprehensive authority signal (e.g., a dedicated pillar page, substantial unique content, or many high‑quality backlinks).  
* **Signal competition** – With three pages vying for the same keyword, [model]’s algorithm continuously re‑evaluates which page has the *slightly* strongest signals at any moment (e.g., a recent backlink, a fresh social mention, or a minor on‑page tweak). That results in the 6‑14 dance.  
* **Low CTR** – The SERP snippet for each URL looks very similar (title tags, meta descriptions, and H1s are all “HIPAA Training”). Users cannot differentiate quickly, so they either skip the result or click only one of them. The combined 95 clicks (≈2.3 % CTR) are the net result of three weak listings fighting for the same click.
