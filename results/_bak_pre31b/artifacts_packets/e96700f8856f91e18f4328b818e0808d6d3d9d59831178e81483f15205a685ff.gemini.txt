## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Finance -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Numbers reconcile and are internally consistent, and the correct concept is applied to the work shape at hand -- accurate AP/AR aging or reconciliation logic for transactional asks, or sound forecast/valuation methodology for strategic asks. Assumptions behind any calculation are stated explicitly, work is shown rather than a bare final number, and the answer is calibrated about when a licensed CPA or controller must sign off.
Example character: a reconciliation or forecast with the formula and inputs shown, an explicit assumption flagged, and a concrete next action (a specific entry, adjustment, or budget line).

## 7 -- Strong
The methodology is correct and the numbers are usable, but one assumption is left unstated, or a reconciliation step is skipped without affecting the bottom line. The work shape (transactional versus strategic) is correctly matched to the ask.
Example character: a correct forecast or reconciliation that does not show its underlying assumptions explicitly.

## 5 -- Adequate
The direction is correct but the numbers are rounded or generic without shown work, or don't fully reconcile against the figures given in the prompt.
Example character: a plausible-looking total that cannot be traced back to the specific inputs provided.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer applies the wrong treatment (conflates cash and accrual basis, misapplies an AP/AR concept), contains a math error, or ignores the specific figures given in the prompt in favor of generic placeholders.
Example character: a calculation that silently mixes cash and accrual figures without flagging the inconsistency.

## 0 -- Unusable
States fabricated financial figures as if sourced or verified, is internally inconsistent to the point of being unusable (totals do not sum, signs are wrong), or gives advice that would misstate a regulated filing with no caveat. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Math that does not reconcile -- totals that do not sum, or an obviously wrong sign.
- Fabricated financial statistics or rates presented as real sourced data.
- Conflating cash and accrual basis without flagging it.
- Advice that would misstate a regulated filing (tax, GAAP) with no caveat that a professional should verify.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Work or formula shown, not just a final number.
- Assumptions behind any calculation stated explicitly.
- Correct AP/AR or forecast-methodology reasoning matched to the stated work shape.
- Explicit flag of when CPA or controller sign-off is needed.

**3. Trust‑to‑Fee Transfer – What Must Happen Before Any Trust Balance Can Be Applied to Fees**

1. **Client‑Authorized Direction**  
   * The client must provide a **written, signed instruction** expressly authorising the firm to apply a portion of the IOLTA advance toward fees that have become **earned** under the engagement letter.  The instruction must reference the specific fee invoice(s) and the amount to be transferred.

2. **Earned‑Fee Determination**  
   * The firm must **document** that the fees are earned (e.g., completion of a milestone, entry of a court order, or the passage of a billing cycle as defined in the retainer).  Only the portion of the advance that corresponds to earned fees may be transferred.

3. **Compliance Review & Approval**  
   * The **Trust Officer/Legal Compliance** must review the transfer request to confirm:  
     - The advance is *client‑owned* and not restricted for any other purpose.  
     - The requested transfer does not exceed the earned fee amount.  
     - All required internal approvals (partner sign‑off, finance controller) are in place.  

4. **Ledger Adjustment Procedure**  
   * Once approved, an **internal journal entry** is made:  
     - **Debit** “IOLTA – Client Advance” for the transferred amount.  
     - **Credit** “Operating Fees Revenue” (or “Earned Fees”) for the same amount.  
   * The adjusted ledger must be reconciled **before any disbursement** is made.

5. **Notification to Client**  
   * Provide the client with a **reconciliation statement** showing:  
     - Original advance balance.  
     - Amount applied to fees.  
     - Remaining advance (if any).  
   * This transparency satisfies the ethical duty of “full accounting” of client funds.

6. **Document Retention**  
   * All authorizations, earned‑fee calculations, compliance approvals, and journal entries must be retained for at least **seven (7) years** in accordance with ABA Model Rules and state bar regulations.

> **Bottom line:**  The $22,000 IOLTA advance **cannot** be swept into the overdue invoice automatically.  It may only be used **after** (i) the client expressly directs the transfer, (ii) the fees are proven to be earned, and (iii) the firm’s trust‑account officer approves the adjustment in writing.  Until those steps are completed, the advance remains a **separate liability** on the IOLTA ledger and must not be offset against any operating‑fee balances.

### ⚖️ TRUST-TO-FEE TRANSFER REQUIREMENTS (Client A)
**Client A's IOLTA balance ($22,000) is client-owned, held specifically for future expert-witness costs, and may NOT be applied against the $18,500 fee invoice without strict compliance.** Before any trust funds could be legally and ethically transferred to the operating account to cover fees, the following must be satisfied:

### 3. Trust-to-Fee Transfer Compliance Statement (Client A)

**Requirement:** The $22,000 held in IOLTA trust for Client A **cannot** be swept to cover the $18,500 fee invoice.

**Proper Trust-to-Fee Transfer Requirements:**
Before any portion of the trust balance can be moved to the firm's operating account, the following conditions must be met to satisfy ethical rules (ABA Model Rules / State Bar Standards):

1.  **Funds Must Be Earned:** The firm must have provided the service or incurred the cost that justifies the transfer. In this specific case, the $22,000 was designated as an **advance for future expert-witness costs**, not a general retainer.
2.  **Engagement Letter Provisions:** The transfer must be authorized by the engagement letter (which currently specifies the funds are for *expert costs*).
3.  **Billing and Notice:** The firm must issue a billing statement showing the expert costs have been incurred and billed to the client. A "Notice of Conversion" or "Notice of Intent to Transfer" must be sent to the client.
4.  **Client Authorization:** Unless the engagement letter explicitly waives this, the client must authorize the conversion of the cost advance into a fee payment.

**Conclusion:** The $22,000 remains a client liability on the IOLTA ledger until specific expert costs are billed and approved. Attempting to apply this balance against the fee invoice immediately would constitute **misappropriation of client funds** (conversion), creating potential disciplinary liability for the firm and the attorneys involved.
