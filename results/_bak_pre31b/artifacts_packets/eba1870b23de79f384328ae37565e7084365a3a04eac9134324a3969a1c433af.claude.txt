# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Marketing -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics presented as real.
- Generic copy with no connection to the specific brand voice or audience given.
- No success metric or measurement plan anywhere in the response.
- Ignoring a stated budget or resource constraint.
- Recommending tactics that misrepresent the product or service.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Concrete, measurable KPI tied directly to the stated goal.
- Channel choice explicitly justified against the stated audience.
- Honest acknowledgment of what needs testing or data before scaling spend.
- Messaging reflects the specific brand voice and constraints given, not a generic template.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

You handle marketing for Hearth & Grain, a small-batch artisan bakery moving from
farmers-market sales into wholesale accounts with independent grocery stores. You surveyed
40 independent grocery buyers who currently stock at least one local bakery brand, asking
what matters most when deciding whether to add a new local bakery vendor to their shelves.

Results: 68% ranked "consistency of supply" (reliably delivering the same quantity, on
schedule, every week) as their #1 concern. 22% ranked price as #1. The remaining 10% ranked
packaging/branding as #1. One buyer commented: "We had a great bakery drop off our shelf
twice last year when they couldn't keep up during the holidays -- we won't take that risk
again." Hearth & Grain's stated goal for Q3 is to sign 15 new wholesale grocery accounts.

Write a positioning statement for pitching Hearth & Grain to independent grocery buyers,
built on what the research actually shows matters most to this audience -- not a generic
"handmade, artisan quality" pitch. State the Q3 account goal the positioning should support.

## Positioning Statement for Hearth & Grain Wholesale Pitch

**For Independent Grocery Buyers seeking a dependable local bakery partner,**

**Hearth & Grain is the small-batch artisan bakery that guarantees consistent supply** – reliably delivering the same high-quality quantity on schedule every week, even during peak demand.  
**Unlike vendors who risk stockouts and lost sales, we provide the operational reliability** your shelves require to build customer trust and maintain category strength.  
**Our commitment to artisanal quality is matched by an unwavering dedication to predictable delivery,** ensuring your customers never find their favorite Hearth & Grain items missing.  
**This positioning directly supports Hearth & Grain's Q3 goal of securing 15 new wholesale grocery accounts by demonstrating we solve the #1 concern of independent buyers: supply chain reliability.**

---

### Why this works based on the research

1.  **Addresses the Top Concern (68%):** Immediately centers on "consistency of supply" ("reliably delivering the same quantity, on schedule, every week," "guarantees consistent supply," "operational reliability," "predictable delivery"). This is the *primary* pain point identified.
2.  **Directly References the Pain Point:** Uses the language and sentiment from the buyer comment ("risk stockouts and lost sales," "ensure your customers never find... missing"). This builds empathy and shows we understand their experience.
3.  **Downplays Generic Artisan Pitch:** While "small-batch artisan bakery" is included (essential for credibility), it's framed *as part of the solution* ("Our commitment to artisanal quality is matched by an unwavering dedication to predictable delivery"), not the *primary* selling point. The focus is squarely on reliability *enabled* by their artisanal approach (implying care and process control).
4.  **Acknowledges Secondary Concerns Implicitly:** By emphasizing reliability *first*, it inherently addresses the risk of price volatility (22%) and poor branding (10%) often linked to inconsistent suppliers. A reliable partner *is* a better value proposition overall.
5.  **States the Q3 Goal Clearly:** Explicitly states the goal ("This positioning directly supports Hearth & Grain's Q3 goal of securing 15 new wholesale grocery accounts...") and links it directly to solving the core buyer problem.
6.  **Action-Oriented & Benefit-Focused:** Uses strong verbs ("guarantees," "provides," "ensuring") and highlights clear benefits for the buyer ("solve the #1 concern," "build customer trust," "maintain category strength").

### Key Phrases Reflecting Research

*   **"operational reliability"**
*   **"predictable delivery"**
*   **"risk stockouts and lost sales"**
*   **"ensure your customers never find... missing"**
*   **"solve the #1 concern"**

This positioning moves beyond generic artisanal claims to directly address the critical operational barrier identified by the target audience, making Hearth & Grain the obvious choice for buyers who value shelf stability and customer satisfaction above all else.
