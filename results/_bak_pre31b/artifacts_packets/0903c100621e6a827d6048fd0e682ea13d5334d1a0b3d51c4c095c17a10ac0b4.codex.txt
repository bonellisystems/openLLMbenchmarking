# Cybersecurity -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS identifiers presented as real.
- Eradication or patching recommended before containment on an active incident.
- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.
- Ignoring implied breach-notification or regulatory timelines when sensitive data is involved.
- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Severity correctly triaged and prioritized against the described impact.
- Containment-before-eradication-before-recovery ordering respected.
- Real, correctly-applied CVE/CWE/ATT&CK references where relevant.
- Evidence-preservation explicitly called out.
- Notification or compliance timeline awareness when data exposure is implied.

**1. What is your organization's regulatory timeline for notifying the covered entity after discovering a breach of unsecured PHI?**  
Our notification timeline is defined in our Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and aligns with HIPAA’s Breach Notification Rule. We are contractually required to notify your practice **within five (5) business days** of discovering a suspected or confirmed breach of unsecured PHI. In practice, our incident response runbook mandates an **initial security alert within 24–48 hours** of confirmation, followed by a preliminary incident report within 72 hours and a detailed forensic summary as evidence is gathered. This accelerated internal timeline ensures you remain well within HIPAA’s 60-calendar-day requirement to notify affected individuals and the HHS Office for Civil Rights (when applicable), and allows your compliance team adequate time to activate your own breach response protocols.

**2. Do you enforce multi-factor authentication for all remote administrative access to systems that touch PHI?**  
Yes. We enforce mandatory, phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all remote administrative access to any infrastructure, application, or cloud service that stores, processes, or transmits PHI. This policy is non-exceptionable and applies to all MSP engineers, contractors, and authorized third parties. MFA is enforced through our identity provider and integrated with a Privileged Access Management (PAM) solution that requires MFA at login, session re-authentication for elevated tasks, and automatic credential rotation. Where technically feasible, we prioritize FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware tokens or certificate-based authentication over SMS or TOTP. Access is continuously audited, and any MFA bypass or degradation triggers immediate access revocation and security review.

**3. Describe your process for verifying whether a third-party software component you rely on, such as a managed file transfer tool, has a known CVE before you deploy it in the practice's environment.**  
All third-party software undergoes a formal security intake and vulnerability verification process before deployment into PHI-handling environments. Our workflow includes:

- **Pre-Deployment CVE & Threat Intel Validation:** We query the NVD, vendor security advisories, CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, and commercial threat intelligence feeds for the exact version/intended build. We cross-reference component-level dependencies using Software Composition Analysis (SCA) tools.
- **Vendor Supply Chain Assessment:** We require vendors to provide a current Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), documented patch cadence, and evidence of secure development practices. Products lacking transparent vulnerability disclosure or patch commitments are flagged for risk review or exclusion.
- **Remediation Gate:** If any CVEs are identified, deployment is blocked until the vendor provides a verified patch or an architecturally validated compensating control. Critical/High-severity CVEs with no available fix or workaround cannot be deployed until resolved.
- **Staging & Configuration Hardening:** The software is first deployed in an isolated, non-production staging environment. We run targeted vulnerability scans, validate that default accounts/ports are disabled, enforce least-privilege network segmentation, and confirm that logging/auditing capabilities are enabled.
- **Documentation & Approval:** Findings, scan reports, and remediation evidence are logged in our GRC platform. Deployment requires sign-off from our Security Lead and your practice’s IT/compliance liaison. Post-deployment, the component is added to our continuous vulnerability management program with automated scanning and patch tracking aligned to our SLAs (Critical: ≤30 days, High: ≤60 days, Medium/Low: ≤90 days or per vendor release cycle).

This process is designed to prevent the deployment of components with known exploitable vulnerabilities and to maintain ongoing visibility into third-party risk throughout the software lifecycle.

**3. Process for verifying whether a third‑party software component (e.g., a managed file‑transfer tool) has a known CVE before deployment**  
1. **Vulnerability Intelligence Feed Integration**  
   - We subscribe to and ingest feeds from the **National Vulnerability Database (NVD)**, **CVE Details**, ** vendor security advisories**, and **commercial threat‑intel platforms** (e.g., Rapid7, Qualys, Recorded Future).  
   - A scheduled nightly job parses these feeds for any newly disclosed CVEs affecting the component’s version or prior versions.  

2. **Version‑Specific CVE Mapping**  
   - Our **Software Composition Analysis (SCA)** tool (e.g., Snyk, Black Duck, or an internal scanner) automatically correlates the component’s version against a curated CVE database, generating a “vulnerability impact report.”  
   - The report flags any **known CVEs with a CVSS‑v3 score ≥ 7.0** that are exploitable in the context of the component (e.g., remote code execution, privilege escalation).  

3. **Pre‑Deployment Review Workflow**  
   - **Step A – Automated Check:** The CI/CD pipeline runs the SCA scan on the proposed component version. If any qualifying CVE is found, the pipeline is halted.  
   - **Step B – Manual Review:** The security engineering team reviews the CVE details (exploit availability, mitigation patches, vendor’s response).  
   - **Step C – Patch/Upgrade Decision:** If a vulnerability exists, we either:  
     - **Apply the vendor’s security patch** (if released and vetted), or  
     - **Upgrade to a newer, patched version** that is free of known high‑severity CVEs.  
   - **Step D – Regression Testing:** The patched/upgraded component undergoes functional and security regression testing in a staging environment that mirrors the production data flow.  
   - **Step E – Sign‑off:** The security lead signs off only after the component meets our **“no high‑severity CVE”** criterion and passes all compliance checkpoints (e.g., data‑handling controls, audit‑log requirements).  

4. **Ongoing Post‑Deployment Monitoring**  
   - After deployment, the component is continuously monitored via **runtime vulnerability scanning** and **log‑based anomaly detection** for any emergent threats.  
   - We subscribe to the vendor’s **security bulletin feed** and automatically trigger a re‑assessment whenever a new advisory is released for that component.  

5. **Documentation & Audit Trail**  
   - All CVE‑verification activities—including feed sources, scan results, remediation actions, and final sign‑off—are recorded in our **Secure Change Management System** and retained for at least three years to satisfy audit and compliance requirements.  

By following this structured, automated‑plus‑manual workflow, we ensure that any third‑party component introduced into the practice’s environment has been vetted for known CVEs, patched or upgraded as necessary, and only then authorized for production use.

2.  **Automated CVE Scanning:** We integrate the product and its SBOM into our automated vulnerability scanning pipeline. 3.  **Vendor Vetting & Advisory Monitoring:** We subscribe to vendor security advisories and threat intelligence feeds. 4.  **Patch Verification:** Before deployment, we verify that the vendor has released and we have applied all available security patches for the specific version being deployed. If a critical CVE exists and a patch is not yet available, we halt deployment until the vendor provides a fix or a temporary mitigating control is in place (e.g., network segmentation or application allow-listing).
5.  **Continuous Monitoring:** Post-deployment, the software is continuously monitored against updated vulnerability databases to detect newly disclosed CVEs, ensuring immediate remediation if a new vulnerability is reported.

### 2. MFA Enforcement for Remote Administrative Access to PHI Systems

- **Policy:** All remote administrative access (VPN, RDP, SSH, cloud console, privileged session management) to any system that stores, processes, or transmits PHI is **MFA‑protected.**
- **Implementation:**
  - **Two‑factor minimum** – something you know (password/PIN) + something you have (hardware token, OTP app, U2F key).  
  - **Zero‑trust network access (ZTNA):** MFA is required for every session, regardless of location or device.
  - **Privileged Access Management (PAM):** Session recording, just‑in‑time (JIT) access, and least‑privilege enforcement.
- **Verification:** Quarterly audit of MFA logs and configuration checks ensure MFA is enforced on all relevant endpoints.

### 3. Third‑Party Software Component CVE Verification Process
