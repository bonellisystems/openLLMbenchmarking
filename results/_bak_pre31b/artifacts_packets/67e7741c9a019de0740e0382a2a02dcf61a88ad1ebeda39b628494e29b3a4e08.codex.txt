## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

# ADR-007: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** [Insert Date]  
**Authors:** MSP Leadership Team  
**Relevant Parties:** Delivery, Security/Compliance, Finance, Client Success

---

## Context

- **Current State:** 40 client environments are managed by RMM-A. Per-endpoint pricing is rising, compressing margin across the portfolio. RMM-A also lacks patch-approval workflows required for client compliance and internal SLAs.
- **Target State:** RMM-B provides a lower per-endpoint cost structure and native patch-approval workflows that satisfy current compliance requirements.
- **Contract Constraint:** RMM-A auto-renews annually. The current term renews in 60 days. Written cancellation notice must be delivered ≥30 days prior to the renewal date to prevent auto-renewal.
- **Execution Plan:** Migrate all 40 environments in phases over 90 days, beginning with the smallest clients.
- **Leadership Consensus:** Approve migration, submit the RMM-A cancellation notice, and begin phased rollout.

---

## Decision

1. **Approve phased migration** of all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B across a 90-day window, sequenced smallest-to-largest.
2. **Submit written cancellation notice** to RMM-A within the 30-day window to satisfy contractual terms.
3. **Resolve timing conflict:** The 90-day migration extends 30 days past RMM-A’s 60-day renewal. If RMM-A service terminates at Day 60, unmigrated clients would operate without endpoint monitoring, remote access, or patching during Days 60–90. To eliminate this gap, **actual service termination is deferred** until all 40 environments are validated on RMM-B. The account will either be placed on a 90-day vendor service hold or allowed to auto-renew for this cycle only. The cancellation of the *subsequent* contract term remains in effect.
4. **Enforce phase gates** at Days 15, 30, 60, and 90. Migration proceeds to the next phase only when stability, patch workflow, and licensing thresholds are met.

---

## Consequences

### Positive
- Long-term reduction in per-endpoint licensing costs.
- Standardized patch-approval workflows across all client environments.
- Single-vendor tooling simplifies training, runbooks, and vendor management.

### Negative / Failure Scenario (Explicit)
If RMM-B experiences a critical failure **after RMM-A has already been cancelled/terminated**, the following occurs for clients not yet migrated:
- **Coverage vacuum:** Unmigrated clients lose real-time endpoint visibility, remote session capability, and automated patching. Security posture degrades; vulnerability remediation windows close.
- **SLA & contractual exposure:** Missed patch windows trigger compliance violations. Client-facing SLAs are breached, potentially invoking penalty clauses or termination rights.
- **Client trust & churn risk:** Newly migrated clients observe tooling instability; pending clients may question the MSP’s operational maturity. Risk of attrition increases.
- **Operational drag:** Engineers must revert to manual triage, vendor escalation queues, and temporary workarounds while RMM-B is restored or alternative coverage is sourced. Delivery capacity is consumed by firefighting rather than proactive management.

### Mitigation & Rollback Protocol (Mandatory)
- **RMM-A remains fully operational through Day 90.** No client operates unmonitored during migration. The deferred termination acts as a safety net.
- **Rollback Trigger:** If RMM-B fails at any phase, migration halts immediately. Affected clients are reverted to RMM-A in reverse phase order within a maximum 48-hour window.
- **Parallel Run Requirement:** Both platforms must monitor simultaneously during the first 30 days. Zero-blind-spot validation is required before any RMM-A agent is decommissioned.
- **Go/No-Go Enforcement:** If RMM-B stability, patch workflow reliability, or licensing compliance falls below defined thresholds, the next phase is paused. Cancellation of the following term is deferred until a stable baseline is proven.

- [ ] Engineering to publish the 48-hour rollback runbook and test it in a non-production environment before Day 1.
- [ ] Client Success to draft client communications for the migration window and rollback contingency.

---

# ADR-001: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

**Status:** Accepted  
**Authors:** MSP Leadership Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Tags:** `infrastructure`, `rmm`, `migration`, `contractual`, `risk`

---

## Context

Our current Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) platform (RMM-A) is approaching the end of its annual contract term. The leadership team identified two critical pain points with RMM-A that justify a replacement:

1.  **Cost:** Per-endpoint pricing is scaling unsustainably as our client portfolio grows.
2.  **Functionality:** RMM-A lacks native patch-approval workflows, requiring manual processes that create compliance risk.

We have evaluated RMM-B, which offers a lower cost structure and includes the required patch-approval workflows out of the box. The proposed plan is to migrate all 40 client environments in phases over a 90-day window, prioritizing the smallest, lowest-risk clients first.

There is, however, a critical contractual constraint on RMM-A that interacts with this timeline:
*   **RMM-A Contract:** Auto-renews annually. The current term renews in **60 days**.
*   **Cancellation Policy:** Written cancellation must be submitted at least **30 days** prior to the renewal date.
*   **Current Plan:** Leadership agreed to send the cancellation notice immediately upon decision, while the migration is still ongoing.

---

## Decision

The MSP will proceed with the migration of all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B. The cancellation of RMM-A will be initiated on the same day the migration decision is signed, regardless of migration progress. The migration will follow a phased approach over 90 days, starting with the smallest client environments.

---

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences
*   **Cost Reduction:** Monthly per-endpoint spend drops significantly, as RMM-B pricing is lower.
*   **Process Compliance:** Native patch-approval workflows eliminate the manual bottlenecks associated with RMM-A.
*   **Scalability:** The new platform supports our projected growth better than RMM-A's current per-endpoint model.

### Negative Consequences (Operational)
*   **Loss of Historical Data:** RMM-A deployment history (patch logs, change tickets) will not transfer to RMM-B. This creates a gap in audit trails for clients migrated before the migration is complete.
*   **Learning Curve:** Engineers must adapt to RMM-B's UI and command structure, potentially slowing incident response during the transition.
*   **Re-onboarding Latency:** If a client needs to be re-added to RMM-A after cancellation, it requires a new license purchase and device re-registration.

### Critical Risk: Timing Conflict (Day 30 vs. Day 90)
The migration is planned for **90 days**, but the contractual cancellation window closes effectively at **Day 30** (60-day renewal date minus 30-day notice requirement).

**What happens at Day 30:**
*   RMM-A cancellation notice is sent.
*   RMM-A license is forfeited.
*   **RMM-B may only have 10–60% of clients migrated.**

**What happens if RMM-B fails after Day 30 (The Failure Scenario):**
If RMM-B experiences an outage, a data corruption event, or a critical bug after the RMM-A cancellation notice has been sent, the following occurs:
1.  **Unmigrated Clients (Day 31–90):** These clients are **completely blind**. They have lost their RMM-A connection and do not yet have an RMM-B connection. There is **no fallback mechanism**.
2.  **No Reversal:** Because the cancellation notice was sent at Day 0/1, the 30-day grace period to reverse the cancellation is gone. Even if leadership wants to pause the migration, RMM-A is now a "dead" license that cannot be reactivated without a new contract.
3.  **Incident Response Paralysis:** If a critical client issue arises during the unmigrated window, the team cannot push patches, run diagnostics, or verify uptime remotely.
4.  **Client Churn Risk:** If this window lasts longer than expected, clients will notice the loss of remote monitoring and may terminate their MSP contract independently.

### Required Mitigations for the Failure Scenario
To make this decision safe for a future hire, the following conditions must be met before sending the cancellation notice:
1.  **Do Not Send Cancellation Until RMM-B Stability Threshold:** The cancellation notice should **not** be sent on Day 1. It should be sent on **Day 30** only after RMM-B has successfully managed all previously migrated clients for a minimum of 14 days without critical incidents.
2.  **Emergency RMM-A License Reserve:** If the budget allows, purchase a single "emergency reactivation" add-on license from RMM-A's provider to retain the ability to provision a license if the cancellation letter is sent prematurely.
3.  **Communication Plan:** All 40 clients must be notified of the migration prior to Day 30, with a specific fallback assurance that monitoring will not be interrupted, regardless of the RMM-A cancellation status.

---

*   [ ] Legal/Finance to confirm RMM-A cancellation penalties or "force majeure" clauses that might allow re-activation.
*   [ ] Engineering to establish a **RMM-B Stability Baseline** (e.g., 14 days of clean monitoring) before Day 30.
*   [ ] Operations to draft a client communication plan for the 90-day migration window.

---

## Notes for Future Hires
*This decision was driven by cost and workflow needs, but it is predicated on the assumption that RMM-B will work.* If RMM-B fails, the "unmigrated clients" window is a **critical operational hole**. Do not send the RMM-A cancellation notice until RMM-B is proven stable on every migrated client. The contract terms do not allow a "pause" button.

# Architecture Decision Record (ADR): RMM Platform Migration
