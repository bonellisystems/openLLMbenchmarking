## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

Your MSP runs a weekly backup-verification process for a structural-engineering firm's
project file server, which stores stamped structural calculation packages and construction
drawings. Most state licensing boards require engineers to be able to produce these stamped
records for a statute-of-repose period (commonly a decade or more after a project finishes)
in case a professional-liability claim is filed years later -- so a silently failed backup
here isn't just an inconvenience, it can leave the firm unable to defend a claim long after
the project is closed out. Right now, verification is informal: whoever is free on Friday
afternoon glances at a couple of recent filenames and calls it done.

Write a Standard Operating Procedure, as a numbered list, for the recurring weekly
backup-verification process. It must: confirm the backup job actually completed rather than
just started, include a real restore-test of a sample file rather than only checking a
completion log, and state exactly what to do if the same backup job fails verification two
weeks in a row.

3.  **Select Sample File for Restoration:**

        *   **Hold:** Until verification passes for three consecutive weeks, the backup job must be flagged as "Unverified," and all critical project files must be backed up manually to an independent, verified medium until risk is mitigated.

Project File Server (Structural‑Engineering Firm)**  

**Purpose**  
To ensure that the weekly full/incremental backup of the project file server is completed successfully and that a representative sample file can be restored without corruption, thereby satisfying licensing‑board statute‑of‑repose requirements and protecting the firm from liability.

---

### 1. Preparation (Day Prior to Verification)

1.1 **Schedule Verification**  
- Reserve the Friday 2:00 – 3:30 p.m. window in the shared calendar.  
- Only the assigned *Backup Verification Lead* (BVL) may perform the verification.  
- Notify the server admin, the project‑lead engineering manager, and the compliance officer of the scheduled time.

1.2 **Select Sample Files**  
- BVL selects **three** distinct files from the past month (e.g., a stamped calculation PDF, a DWG drawing, and a project‑summary Excel).  
- Record the full path, file size, and SHA‑256 checksum of each sample file *before* the backup run.  
- Store these values in the *Weekly Verification Log* (see §4).

1.3 **Verify Backup Job Schedule**  
- Confirm that the automated backup job (e.g., “ProjectFileServer‑Full”) is scheduled to run Monday‑Friday at 01:00 a.m. local time.  
- Ensure that the job is set to *stop on error* and that the retention policy is set to keep at least 12 months of snapshots.

---

### 2. Post‑Backup Confirmation (Early Friday Morning)

2.1 **Confirm Job Completion**  
- Log into the backup appliance (e.g., Veeam, Acronis) and open the *Job History* for the most recent run.  
- Verify the following fields:  
  - **Start Time** – matches scheduled time.  
  - **End Time** – present and indicates successful completion.  
  - **Status** – “Completed” (not “Started”, “Running”, or “Failed”).  
  - **Data Volume** – matches the size of the data set (within ±5 %).  

2.2 **Collect Automated Alerts**  
- Retrieve any automated email or SMS alerts generated by the backup software.  
- If the job status is “Failed” or “Partial”, **do not** proceed to §3. Instead, record the failure in the *Weekly Verification Log* and notify the IT support team immediately (see §8).  

2.3 **Document Completion**  
- Record the job ID, start/end timestamps, and status in the *Weekly Verification Log*.  
- Capture a screenshot of the job history page and attach it to the log entry.

---

### 3. Real Restore‑Test

3.1 **Restore Environment Setup**  
- Create a temporary test folder on the local workstation (e.g., `C:\TestRestore\YYYY-MM-DD`).  
- Ensure the folder has the same NTFS permissions as the original target directory.

3.2 **Perform Restore**  
- Using the backup client, restore each of the three sample files to the test folder.  
- For each file, use the “Verify checksum on restore” option if available; otherwise, restore normally and then compute the SHA‑256 checksum locally.

3.3 **Integrity Verification**  
- Compute the SHA‑256 checksum of each restored file.  
- Compare the checksum against the pre‑backup value recorded in §1.2.  
- If any checksum differs, flag the file as “Corrupted” and note the discrepancy in the log.

3.4 **Document Results**  
- Record the restored file paths, the checksum comparison outcome (Pass/Fail), and any error messages.  
- Attach the checksum output and any error logs to the *Weekly Verification Log*.

### 5. Escalation for Failed Verification

5.1 **First Failure**  
- If any checksum fails or the backup job status is not “Completed”, BVL must:
  1. Stop further restore tests for that week.  
  2. Log the failure in the *Weekly Verification Log* (see §4).  
  3. Notify the *Backup Administrator* (BA) and the *Engineering Manager* via email and the ticketing system.  
  4. Create a ticket in the IT Service Management tool (e.g., ServiceNow) with the subject “Weekly Backup Verification Failure – [Server]”.

5.2 **Second Consecutive Failure**  
- If the same backup job fails verification **two weeks in a row**:
  1. **Escalate to Senior IT Lead** immediately.  
  2. Conduct a root‑cause analysis (RCA) involving the BA, the backup vendor’s support, and, if needed, the server hardware vendor.  
  3. Document the RCA in the ticket and the *Weekly Verification Log*.  
  4. **Suspend automated restores** from the affected backup until the issue is resolved.  
  5. Perform a manual restore of the entire project dataset to a staging environment to confirm recoverability.  
  6. If the manual restore fails, execute a **hot‑fix**: re‑run the backup job with the “force‑full” option and validate.  
  7. Once the issue is fixed, **re‑run** the restore‑test for all three sample files and update the log.  
  8. Close the ticket only after the Senior IT Lead signs off on the resolution and the verification log shows a successful test.

---

### 6. Documentation & Record‑Keeping

6.1 **Retention**  
- Keep the *Weekly Verification Log* for a minimum of **12 years** (statute‑of‑repose period).  
- Store the log in a version‑controlled repository (e.g., SharePoint, Confluence) with read‑only access for compliance auditors.  

6.2 **Audit Trail**  
- All log entries, screenshots, and error logs must be timestamped automatically (system clock).  
- The log should include the BVL’s signature (digital or handwritten) to certify completion.

---

### 7. Continuous Improvement

7.1 **Quarterly Review**  
- The Compliance Officer reviews the *Weekly Verification Log* each quarter to detect trends (e.g., increasing failure rate).  
- If a pattern emerges, adjust the backup strategy (e.g., change retention policy, upgrade storage, or modify job schedule).  

7.2 **Training**  
- BVLs receive annual refresher training on backup software, checksum tools, and disaster‑recovery procedures.  
- New hires are trained on this SOP within their first week.

**Purpose:** Ensure recoverability of stamped structural calculations and construction drawings to support statute-of-repose compliance and professional-liability defense.

Disregard statuses such as "Started," "Running," "Queued," or "Aborted." Record the **Completion Timestamp** and ensure it falls within the expected window (e.g., completed by 06:00 on the verification day). If the status is anything other than "Completed," or if the job has no recorded completion timestamp, treat this as a failed verification and proceed to remediation immediately.

2. **Select High-Fidelity Sample File**  
   Identify a sample file from the most recent completed backup set to restore. The sample must meet the following criteria to test the specific risk of losing legally sealed records:
   - The file must be a **current or recent stamped calculation package or construction drawing** (e.g., a PDF with a digital seal or a DWG containing a signed header).
   - Selection must be **randomized weekly** from files created in the preceding seven days to prevent gaming the test. Document the selected filename, path, and file hash (if available) in the verification log.

3. **Execute Live Restore to Isolated Path**  
   Initiate a restore of the selected sample file to an isolated **test directory** (e.g., `Z:\Backup_Verification_Results\Week_[Date]\`) that is disconnected from the production file server and the live backup target. Do not restore over existing files. Verify that the restore operation completes without errors and that the restored file matches the file size and hash of the source metadata recorded in Step 2. If the file size differs or the hash does not match, abort verification and flag as corrupted.

4. **Validate File Integrity and Seal**  
   Open the restored file in the native application (e.g., PDF viewer for stamped calculations, CAD software for drawings) and perform the following checks:
   - Confirm the document renders without corruption, missing pages, or garbled data.
   - **Critical:** Verify the **digital seal, signature, or stamp is intact and visible**. If the restored file lacks a valid seal, the backup is non-compliant for statute-of-repose defense, even if the file is readable.
   - Close the file and confirm no error messages regarding file integrity.

5. **Document Results and Archive**  
   Record the verification outcome in the MSP ticketing system or shared verification log, including:
   - Date and time of verification.
   - Backup job completion status and timestamp.
   - Sample filename, path, and hash.
   - Result of restore (Success/Failed).
   - Result of integrity/seal check (Pass/Fail).
   - Administrator name.
   Retain these records for the duration of the statute of repose (minimum 10 years) to demonstrate due diligence in the event of a future liability claim.

6. **Two-Week Consecutive Failure Remediation Protocol**  
   If the same backup job fails verification in **two consecutive weeks** (e.g., Week N and Week N+1), initiate the following mandatory actions immediately:
   - **Halt Reliance on Automated Job:** Mark the automated backup job as "Untrusted" in the monitoring system. Do not wait for the next scheduled run.
   - **Immediate Manual Off-Site Copy:** Perform an immediate manual copy of the affected directory to a secondary, geographically distinct storage target (e.g., secondary NAS, cloud bucket, or physical media) to restore coverage while the root cause is investigated.
   - **Vendor Escalation:** Open a Priority 1 ticket with the backup software vendor or infrastructure provider, providing the failure logs from both weeks. Request a joint root-cause analysis within 4 business hours.
   - **Client Notification:** Notify the firm's designated Principal Engineer or Risk Officer within 1 hour of the second failure, disclosing the risk to the statute-of-repose record retention and confirming that the manual off-site copy has been secured.
   - **Resume Only After Dual Validation:** The automated job may not be re-trusted until it passes two consecutive verified restores after the remediation is complete. Document the final resolution in the client's risk file.

    *   Attempt to open the file using the appropriate engineering application (e.g., AutoCAD, Excel, or PDF viewer) to ensure the content is readable and stamps/signatures are visible and uncorrupted.

**Standard Operating Procedure: Weekly Backup Verification**  
*Purpose:* Ensure reliable, long-term retention of critical project files to support potential professional-liability claims.  
*Frequency:* Every Friday, immediately after the backup job completes.  

1. **Access Backup System**  
   Log in to the backup management console (e.g., Veeam, Commvault, or similar) using authorized credentials.  

2. **Verify Job Completion Status**  
   Locate the backup job for the project file server. Confirm:  
   - The job has a **"Completed" status** (not just "Started" or "Running").  
   - The **success rate is 100%** (no failed files or skipped data).  
   - The **timestamp** reflects completion within the last 24 hours.  
   *If status is incomplete or failed, proceed to Step 7.*  

3. **Select Sample File for Restore Test**  
   Choose one file from the current week’s backup:  
   - Preferably a **recently modified, critical file** (e.g., a stamped structural calculation PDF or a CAD drawing).  
   - Verify the file’s existence in the project server’s active directory.  

4. **Initiate Restore Test**  
   - Restore the selected file to a **temporary test folder** (e.g., `\\Server\RestoreTest`).  
   - Use the **"Instant Recovery"** or **"File-Level Restore"** function from the backup console.  

5. **Validate Restored File**  
   After restore completes:  
   - Confirm the file matches the original in **size, date, and checksum** (using tools like `fc` or `sha256sum`).  
   - Open the file to verify **readability and content integrity** (e.g., check for corruption, missing pages, or unreadable elements).  
   - If validation fails, proceed to Step 7.  

6. **Document Success**  
   Record in the backup log:  
   - Date, time, and operator name.  
   - Job status: **"Verified - Restore Test Successful"**.  
   - Sample file name and location.  

7. **Handle Backup Failure**  
   If verification fails (job incomplete or restore test unsuccessful):  
   - **Immediate Action:** Notify the MSP’s Tier-2 support and the engineering firm’s IT manager within 1 hour.  
   - **Manual Backup:** Initiate an emergency backup via secondary method (e.g., USB transfer to offsite location).  
   - **Troubleshoot:** Resolve the issue and re-verify within 24 hours. Document all actions.  

8. **Consecutive Failure Protocol**  
   If the **same backup job fails verification two weeks in a row**:  
   - **Escalate:** Notify the MSP’s operations director and the engineering firm’s CEO within 2 hours.  
   - **Investigation:** The MSP must provide a **root-cause analysis report** within 48 hours.  
   - **Corrective Action:**  
     - Implement a temporary backup solution within 24 hours (e.g., cloud storage).  
     - Conduct a **full system audit** of the backup infrastructure.  
     - Update the backup configuration or replace hardware/software if required.  
   - **Review Meeting:** Schedule a meeting with the engineering firm’s legal counsel to assess compliance risk and define interim retention procedures.  
   - **Audit Trail:** Document all steps in the incident log and notify the state licensing board if legally mandated.

- **Retention:** All logs and restored files must be retained for 15+ years to cover the statute-of-repose period.  
- **Log Storage:** Backup logs are stored in a tamper-proof location (e.g., encrypted cloud storage).  
- **Training:** Operators complete annual training on SOPs, restore testing, and incident reporting.  
- **Testing Frequency:** Restore tests use different sample files weekly to ensure diverse coverage.

**Standard Operating Procedure – Weekly Backup‑Verification for the Structural‑Engineering Project File Server**  
*(Version 1.0 – Effective [Date])*  

1. **Scope & Responsibility**  
   1.1. This procedure applies to the *weekly verification* of the automated backup of the Project‑File Server (PFS).  
   1.2. The **Backup Administrator (BA)** is responsible for executing the verification each Friday afternoon and for documenting the outcome.  
   1.3. The **IT Service Manager (ITSM)** must be notified immediately if the verification fails on two consecutive weeks.

2. **Pre‑Verification Preparation (Friday 08:00 – 08:30)**  
   2.1. Verify that the backup schedule for the previous week (Monday 00:00 – Sunday 23:59) ran without interruption:  
   • Open the backup orchestration console (e.g., Veeam/Altaro).  
   • Confirm the job status shows **“Completed”** and not **“Started/Running”**.  
   • Check the job‑completion timestamp – it must be within the scheduled window (≤ 02:00 AM on Monday).  
   2.2. Pull the *Backup‑Job‑Log* for the most recent run and note the **Job‑ID, start‑time, end‑time, and total‑bytes‑processed**.  
   2.3. Ensure that any error‑level entries (ERROR, WARNING) are absent; if present, skip to step 3 and follow the “Failure” workflow.

3. **Completion Confirmation (Friday 08:30 – 09:00)**  
   3.1. **Checksum Validation** – Compare the MD5/SHA‑256 hash of the *source* file (e.g., `Project_001_Design.stamp.pdf`) with the hash recorded in the backup repository for the same file.  
   3.2. **File‑Count Audit** – Verify that the number of files listed in the backup‑report matches the count reported by the PFS inventory tool (± 1 file tolerance).  
   3.3. **Retention‑Policy Check** – Confirm that all files older than the statutory‑repose period (e.g., 10 years) are still present in the backup set.  
   3.4. Document the results in the *Weekly‑Verification‑Log* (template: Date, Job‑ID, Status = Pass/Fail, Hash‑Match = Yes/No, File‑Count = X, Retention‑OK = Yes/No, Comments).

4. **Real‑Restore Test (Friday 09:00 – 10:30)**  
   4.1. **Select a Sample File** – Use the *most recent stamped calculation package* (e.g., `Project_2024_001_Design.stamp.pdf`) that meets the following criteria:  
   • File size > 5 MB,  
   • Contains both PDF and source CAD files,  
   • Has a non‑zero modification date within the last 30 days.  
   4.2. **Restore Procedure** – From the backup repository, restore the selected file to a **test‑restore directory** (`/mnt/backup‑verify/tmp/restore_test/`).  
   4.3. **Integrity Verification** –  
   a. Compute the hash of the restored file and compare it to the hash recorded during step 3.1.  
   b. Open the file in its native application (e.g., Adobe Acrobat) to confirm it is not corrupted.  
   c. Verify the file’s metadata (creation/modification dates) matches the source record.  
   4.4. **Log the Outcome** – Record in the *Weekly‑Verification‑Log*:  
   • Test‑File Name,  
   • Restore‑Path,  
   • Hash‑Match = Yes/No,  
   • Visual‑Check = OK/FAIL,  
   • Any Errors Encountered.  
   4.5. If the restore test fails at any point, treat the verification as **FAIL** and proceed to step 5.

5. **Failure Handling & Escalation**  
   5.1. **Single‑Week Failure** – If the verification fails (log entry “FAIL”), the BA must:  
   a. Immediately run a **manual backup job** for the affected data set and re‑run the verification steps 3‑4 on the fresh backup.  
   b. Document the root cause (e.g., network glitch, storage quota exceeded) in the log.  
   c. Schedule a remediation task for the next business day and close the incident once the backup passes verification again.  
   5.2. **Two Consecutive Weekly Failures** – If the verification fails **on the same backup job for two successive weeks**, the BA must:  
   a. **Escalate** to the IT Service Manager within 2 hours of the second failure.  
   b. **Initiate an Incident Ticket** (IT‑INC‑BKUP‑####) and assign a **Backup‑Recovery Lead** (BRL).  
   c. **Diagnostic Actions** performed by the BRL within 24 hours:  
      • Review job scheduler configuration and recent changes.  
      • Verify storage‑array health (SMART, RAID status).  
      • Check network bandwidth and latency between PFS and backup target.  
      • Confirm that backup‑retention policies have not been altered.  
   d. **Implement a Temporary Mitigation** (e.g., switch to an alternate backup target, increase backup‑job priority, or manually copy critical files) and confirm that the mitigation resolves the failure.  
   e. **Root‑Cause Resolution** – Within 72 hours of escalation, the BRL must deliver a written action plan (including timeline, responsible parties, and verification steps) to the ITSM.  
   f. **Post‑Resolution Verification** – The BA must re‑run the weekly verification for **three consecutive weeks** after the fix to confirm sustained success before closing the incident ticket.  
   g. **Documentation** – All escalation steps, diagnostic logs, and final resolution notes are filed in the *Backup‑Verification‑Incident‑Register* (indexed by ticket number).

6. **Sign‑off & Continuous Improvement (Friday 11:00 – 11:30)**  
   6.1. The BA signs the *Weekly‑Verification‑Log* and forwards a copy to:  
   • IT Service Manager,  
   • Compliance Officer (for statutory‑repose record‑keeping).  
   6.2. During the monthly backup‑process review meeting, the BA presents any trends, near‑misses, or suggested improvements (e.g., adding additional checksum algorithms, automating hash‑report generation).  

7. **Audit & Review**  
   7.1. The SOP will be reviewed annually by the **IT Governance Committee** or after any major change to the backup infrastructure.  
   7.2. Audit evidence (verification logs, incident tickets, restoration test results) must be retained for **minimum 5 years** to satisfy professional‑liability and licensing‑board requirements.
