## 10 -- Frontier
The answer correctly distinguishes general legal information from jurisdiction-specific legal advice, and flags when licensed counsel review is genuinely required. Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated statute, regulation, or case citation presented as real.
- Definitive legal conclusion stated with no jurisdiction or consult-counsel caveat on a matter that clearly needs one.
- General information presented as a substitute for licensed advice on a high-stakes ask.
- No privilege or confidentiality flag when the scenario implies sensitive written communication.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Clear line drawn between general information and advice requiring licensed counsel.
- Only real, correctly-applied citations used -- never invented ones.
- Privilege or confidentiality caution raised when the scenario implies it.
- Jurisdictional caveat stated explicitly rather than assumed.

### **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**
**Scope:** Shop-Floor Tablets and Corporate Laptops  
**Effective Date:** [Date]  
**Applies To:** All employees and contractors issued corporate devices.

---

#### **1. Permitted Personal Use and Monitoring**
**1.1 Reasonable Personal Use**  
The Company recognizes that employees may have incidental personal needs while using corporate devices. Reasonable personal use of shop-floor tablets and corporate laptops is permitted, provided such use:
*   Does not interfere with job duties, production schedules, or safety protocols.
*   Consumes minimal network bandwidth and storage resources.
*   Is conducted on networks designated for general IT access (e.g., corporate Wi-Fi), unless specific operational requirements dictate otherwise.

**1.2 Monitoring Rights**  
To ensure security, protect intellectual property, and maintain operational safety, the Company reserves the right to monitor all activity on corporate devices and network traffic. This includes, but is not limited to, inspecting email content, internet usage, application usage, file transfers, and network logs. All corporate devices and network resources are Company property and may be accessed, inspected, or audited by authorized personnel at any time without prior notice.

---

#### **2. Protected Concerted Activity**
**2.1 Carve-Out for Wages, Hours, and Conditions**  
Nothing in this AUP, including the monitoring and enforcement provisions in Sections 1 and 4, shall be interpreted to restrict, discourage, or penalize employees' rights under federal labor laws (including the National Labor Relations Act) to engage in protected concerted activity. This explicitly includes the right to discuss and share information regarding wages, hours, benefits, and working conditions with fellow employees. The Company does not use its monitoring capabilities to surveil or suppress protected speech, and any detection of such discussions shall not result in disciplinary action.

---

#### **3. Prohibited Activities**
Employees are strictly prohibited from the following actions to protect the integrity of production systems and data:

**3.1 Personal Email Attachments on OT/ICS Networks**  
Under no circumstances shall an employee open, forward, or process personal email attachments on any device connected to the Operational Technology (OT) or Industrial Control Systems (ICS) network. Personal email attachments may introduce malware or unverified code that poses a severe risk to production safety. This prohibition applies to all devices capable of bridging or accessing the OT/ICS environment.

**3.2 Unapproved Removable Media on Production Systems**  
Employees must not connect unapproved personal USB drives, external hard drives, or personal mobile devices to production-line PCs or safety-critical workstations. Only Company-approved, sanitized, and tracked storage media may be used for data transfer. Personal USB connections pose a significant security risk and are strictly prohibited on shop-floor terminals.

---

#### **4. Violations and Consequences**
**4.1 General Violations**  
Violations of this AUP may result in disciplinary action, up to and including revocation of device privileges, depending on the severity and frequency of the infraction.

**4.2 Safety-Critical OT Violations**  
The Company classifies certain OT systems and production-line functions as "Safety-Critical." Any violation that impacts or has the potential to impact the safety, integrity, or operation of these systems will be treated with zero tolerance. This includes:
*   Bypassing security controls on OT/ICS networks.
*   Introducing malware or unauthorized code via personal attachments or media.
*   Unauthorized modifications to system configurations or PLCs.
*   Failure to report security anomalies on production equipment.

**4.3 Termination**  
Violations involving safety-critical OT systems, or repeated violations demonstrating willful disregard for policy, constitute grounds for immediate termination of employment. The Company will also pursue all available legal remedies for damages resulting from compromised safety systems.

---

#### **5. Acknowledgment and Device Issuance**
**5.1 Disclosure and Signing**  
Clear disclosure of monitoring and policy requirements is provided prior to device issuance. No employee shall be issued a shop-floor tablet or corporate laptop until they have:
1.  Received and reviewed this AUP and the associated monitoring disclosure.
2.  Signed the **Device Acknowledgment Form**, which explicitly confirms understanding of monitoring rights, prohibited uses, and consequences.

**5.2 Retention**  
Signed Acknowledgment Forms will be retained in the employee's HR file. Failure to sign the form will result in denial of device access.

---

### **Pre-Rollout Checklist**

Before distributing this AUP and rolling out devices, the following steps must be completed to ensure legal compliance, operational safety, and employee clarity:

1.  **Legal Review of NLRA Carve-Out:** Have employment/labor counsel review **Section 2** to ensure the carve-out language is robust and aligns with the current NLRB guidance on employer monitoring policies. The policy must not contain a "catch-all" phrase (e.g., "misconduct" or "disruptive behavior") that could be interpreted by the NLRB as chilling protected activity.
2.  **Technical Enforcement Verification:** IT and OT engineering must verify that technical controls align with the policy. For example, ensure that USB ports on production-line PCs are blocked or restricted via endpoint management software, and that network segmentation prevents shop-floor tablets from accessing personal email servers if they are connected to the OT network.
3.  **Manager Training:** Train floor supervisors and managers on the distinction between prohibited OT violations and protected discussions. Managers must understand that while they can enforce safety and security protocols, they cannot discipline employees for discussing wages or conditions, even if done on company devices (provided no company data is leaked).
4.  **Acknowledgment Form Update:** Create the digital or physical **Device Acknowledgment Form** referenced in Section 5. It should include a checkbox explicitly acknowledging the monitoring disclosure and the specific prohibitions regarding OT/ICS safety.
5.  **OT Risk Assessment:** Conduct a brief risk assessment to confirm which systems qualify as "Safety-Critical" under Section 4.2. This list should be documented and shared with relevant teams so employees understand the gravity of violations in those zones.
6.  **Communication Strategy:** Frame the rollout around safety and productivity. Emphasize to the shop floor that these restrictions on USBs and personal email on OT networks exist to prevent production stoppages and ensure worker safety, rather than solely as a surveillance measure. This builds buy-in and reduces resistance.

**Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) – Shop‑Floor Tablets & Corporate Laptops**  
*Version 1.0 – Effective [Date]*  

---

### 1. Permitted Personal Use  
Employees may use corporate‑issued tablets and laptops for limited personal activities (e.g., checking personal email, reading news, accessing non‑work‑related web sites) **provided that such use does not interfere with work performance, does not expose the company’s network or OT/ICS environment to unnecessary risk, and complies with all other policy provisions**.  

The Company reserves the right to monitor device and network activity for security, safety, and operational‑integrity purposes. Monitoring will be conducted in accordance with applicable privacy laws and internal procedures; it is **not intended to restrict** employees’ protected concerted activity (see Section 5).

---

### 2. Prohibited Activities  
The following uses are expressly prohibited on any corporate device **connected to the OT/ICS network** or on production‑line PCs:

| Prohibited Action | Reason |
|-------------------|--------|
| Opening personal email attachments or files received from external sources on a device that is linked to the OT/ICS network | Prevents introduction of malware or unauthorized data onto safety‑critical systems. |
| Connecting any unapproved personal USB drive, external hard‑drive, or other removable media to a production‑line PC or OT workstation | Mitigates risk of unauthorized data transfer, malware injection, or accidental re‑configuration of control parameters. |
| Use of personal devices (e.g., smartphones, tablets) for work‑related tasks unless they have been formally enrolled, patched, and approved for OT/ICS access | Ensures that only vetted, security‑hardened endpoints interact with critical control environments. |

*All other personal use that complies with the “Permitted Personal Use” clause is allowed.*

---

### 3. Disclosure & Acknowledgment  
Every employee who receives a corporate tablet or laptop must sign an **Acknowledgment of Monitoring and Acceptable Use** form **prior to device issuance**. The acknowledgment confirms that the employee:

1. Has read and understands this AUP.  
2. Consents to the Company’s monitoring of device and network activity as described in Section 1.  
3. Agrees to comply with all prohibited‑use rules outlined in Section 2.  

A copy of the signed form will be retained in the employee’s personnel file and a duplicate filed with IT Security.

---

### 4. Consequences for Violations (Safety‑Critical OT Systems)  
Violations that compromise the integrity, safety, or availability of OT/ICS systems—including but not limited to the prohibited activities in Section 2—will be treated as **serious misconduct**. Consequences may include, but are not limited to:

* Immediate suspension of device privileges.  
* Mandatory remedial training on OT security.  
* Formal written warning.  
* Revocation of device access.  
* Disciplinary action up to and including **termination of employment**, where the violation results in or could have resulted in a safety incident, production interruption, or data loss.  

The severity of the response will be matched to the risk created and the employee’s prior record.

---

### 5. Protection of Concerted Activity  
The Company **does not** intend to restrict, nor shall the monitoring language be interpreted as restricting, employees’ rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) to:

* Discuss wages, hours, benefits, or working conditions with coworkers.  
* Participate in protected concerted activities such as forming a union, collective bargaining, or mutual aid.  

To make this protection explicit, the following statement is included in the acknowledgment form and in all communications about this AUP:

> *“This policy’s monitoring and enforcement provisions are limited to technical and operational safeguards of corporate devices and the OT/ICS environment. They are not intended to, and shall not be construed to, prohibit employees from engaging in protected concerted activity, including but not limited to discussing wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment with fellow employees.”*

---

## Note on Pre‑Rollout Activities  

Before the AUP becomes operational, the following steps should be completed to ensure a smooth and compliant implementation:

1. **Legal & HR Review** – Verify that all language complies with federal labor law, privacy regulations, and any collective bargaining agreements.  
2. **IT Security Validation** – Confirm that the OT/ICS network segmentation, endpoint hardening, and USB‑control policies are fully functional and documented.  
3. **Stakeholder Sign‑Off** – Obtain formal approval from Safety, Production Management, and the Works Council (or union representatives, if applicable).  
4. **Communication Plan** – Schedule department‑wide briefings, provide a plain‑language summary of the AUP, and distribute the acknowledgment form electronically and in print.  
5. **Training & Simulation** – Conduct hands‑on workshops demonstrating prohibited USB connections, safe email attachment handling on OT devices, and the monitoring tools that will be used.  
6. **Support Infrastructure** – Establish a clear process for reporting suspected violations, a channel for employees to raise questions about their rights, and a mechanism for revoking device access quickly if needed.  
7. **Pilot Deployment** – Roll out the policy to a small, cross‑functional pilot group, gather feedback, and adjust the wording or procedures before enterprise‑wide release.  

Completing these preparatory actions will help ensure that employees understand the AUP, that the Company’s monitoring is lawful and transparent, and that protected concerted activity remains fully safeguarded.

# Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
**Applicable Devices:** Shop-Floor Tablets & Corporate Laptops
**Effective Date:** [Date]

### Section 1: Personal Use and Monitoring Rights

**1.1 Reasonable Personal Use**
Employees are permitted to use corporate devices for reasonable personal purposes during non-business hours or during breaks, provided such use does not interfere with work performance, violate security policies, or incur significant data costs. Examples of permitted personal use include checking personal email or browsing personal websites during approved break times.

**1.2 Monitoring Right**
The Company reserves the right to monitor all device activity, including but not limited to email content, internet browsing history, application usage, file transfers, and network traffic. This includes data transmitted over or stored on the Corporate Network and the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control-Systems (ICS) Network.

**1.3 Protected Concerted Activity Exclusion**
Nothing in this Section 1 shall be construed as authorizing monitoring or discipline for any activity protected under Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), or any applicable state labor law. Specifically, this policy **does not** apply to employees discussing:
*   Wages, hours, and other terms of employment;
*   Working conditions with co-workers or representatives;
*   Union organizing or collective action.

Monitoring data that reveals participation in such protected activity will not be used to discipline the employee for the protected activity itself.

---

### Section 2: Prohibited Uses

The following activities are strictly prohibited, regardless of the employee's job role or location:

**2.1 OT/ICS Network Restrictions**
*   **Personal Email on ICS Network:** Employees shall not open, send, or receive personal email attachments or access personal email accounts while connected to the OT/ICS Network. The OT/ICS Network is a restricted zone dedicated to manufacturing control systems, and personal traffic introduces malware, phishing vectors, and potential safety risks.
*   **Network Segmentation:** Devices authorized on the Corporate Network are **not** authorized on the OT/ICS Network. Physical or logical separation must be maintained at all times.

**2.2 USB and Storage Device Restrictions**
*   **Unapproved Personal USB Drives:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, flash drives, or external storage devices to production-line PCs or shop-floor tablets is prohibited.
*   **Approved Media Only:** Only Company-approved storage media, configured with the latest antivirus definitions and endpoint protection, may be connected to production-line systems.

---

### Section 3: Disclosure and Acknowledgment

**3.1 Prior Disclosure**
The Company shall provide a copy of this AUP (including the specific sections regarding monitoring and OT safety) to every employee prior to the issuance of their assigned device. The Company shall make a good-faith effort to ensure the employee has read and understood the policy through verbal explanation by the direct supervisor or IT security team.

**3.2 Written Acknowledgment**
Prior to receiving a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet, the employee must sign a Device Acknowledgment Form indicating that they:
*   Have received a copy of this AUP;
*   Understand their obligations regarding OT/ICS network security and USB device restrictions;
*   Acknowledge that they understand the Company's right to monitor device and network activity as described in Section 1;
*   Confirm that they understand the disciplinary consequences for violations, including those tied to safety-critical systems.

The signed form must be retained in the employee's personnel file.

---

### Section 4: Consequences of Violation

**4.1 General Violations**
Violations of Sections 1 and 2 that do not directly impact safety-critical systems may result in a range of disciplinary actions, up to and including termination, depending on the severity, frequency, and intent of the violation.

**4.2 Safety-Critical OT Violations**
Violations involving the OT/ICS Network or production-line PCs that pose a risk to physical safety, product quality, or critical infrastructure integrity are treated with zero tolerance. Disciplinary actions for such violations may include immediate suspension without pay or immediate termination of employment. This applies specifically to:
*   Unauthorized use of personal email on the ICS network;
*   Connection of unapproved USB drives to production-line PCs;
*   Any action that disrupts automated control systems, sensors, or manufacturing processes.

**4.3 Protected Activity Safeguard**
Discipline imposed under this Section 4 is solely based on the technical violation of the AUP (e.g., connecting a USB drive to a production PC). **Nothing in this Section 4 shall be applied to, or used to punish, employees for engaging in protected concerted activity regarding wages, hours, or working conditions.** If an investigation reveals that a violation was reported by or in the context of a protected activity (e.g., a worker reporting an unsafe condition on the production line), the employee shall be protected from retaliatory discipline.

---

### Section 5: Pre-Rollout Implementation Notes

Before the rollout of this AUP, the following steps should be taken to ensure legal defensibility and operational effectiveness:

1.  **Employment Counsel Review:** This is the most critical step. Employment counsel should review the monitoring carve-out (Section 1.3 and 4.3) to ensure it withstands an NLRB challenge. Language that is too broad or vague regarding monitoring can inadvertently create an "unfair labor practice." Counsel should verify that the NLRA carve-out is explicit enough to avoid a "chilling effect" on Section 7 rights.
2.  **ICS Security Validation:** The IT/OT security team should verify that the network segmentation described in Section 2.1 is physically and logically enforceable. If the OT/ICS network is not truly air-gapped or segmented from the Corporate Network, the policy cannot be enforced, and the company could be liable for negligence regarding safety.
3.  **Employee Training:** Do not simply distribute the document. Conduct mandatory training sessions for shop-floor staff and supervisors to explain the OT/ICS risks and the USB restrictions.
4.  **Device Configuration:** Ensure that endpoint protection and network access controls are configured *before* devices are issued to enforce the policy technically (e.g., block personal USB ports on production PCs, disable personal email on OT VLANs).
5.  **Union Communication (If Applicable):** If the workforce is unionized or if there is potential for unionization, legal counsel should advise on the timing of the rollout relative to any NLRB election or collective bargaining agreement negotiations to avoid accusations of pre-empting protected rights.

This policy establishes the rules for acceptable use of all company‑issued tablets and laptops that access the shop‑floor Operational‑Technology (OT) and Industrial‑Control‑Systems (ICS) network. Its goal is to protect employee safety, operational integrity, and corporate data while allowing reasonable personal use.

### 2. Scope  
Applies to:
- All employees, contractors, and temporary workers who receive a company tablet or laptop that can connect to the OT/ICS network.  
- Any device that is physically or virtually part of the OT/ICS infrastructure (e.g., tablets, laptops, mobile‑managed devices).

### 3. Definitions  
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **OT/ICS Network** | The network segment that hosts industrial‑control‑systems critical to production safety and performance. |
| **Personal Use** | Non‑business activities (e.g., checking personal email, browsing non‑banned websites) that do not interfere with job duties, safety, or network security. |
| **Monitoring** | The collection, logging, and analysis of device and network activity for compliance, security, and performance purposes. |
| **Protected Activity** | Any discussion about wages, hours, or working conditions, as defined by federal labor law. |

### 4. General Use Guidelines  
1. **Reasonable Personal Use** – Employees may use company devices for personal purposes **as long as** it:
   - Does not interfere with safety‑critical operations.  
   - Does not consume bandwidth that would degrade OT/ICS performance.  
   - Does not involve downloading or executing software that could compromise device security.  
2. **Safety First** – Any activity that could compromise the integrity of safety‑critical OT systems is strictly prohibited (see Section 5).  

### 5. Prohibited Uses  
The following actions are *explicitly prohibited* on all company‑issued tablets and laptops that access the OT/ICS network:

| Prohibited Action | Why it’s prohibited | Consequence (for safety‑critical OT violations) |
|-------------------|---------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Opening personal e‑mail attachments** on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. | Risk of malware that can disrupt or compromise safety‑critical controls. | Immediate suspension of device access; potential termination. |
| **Connecting unapproved personal USB drives** to production‑line PCs. | USB media can introduce malware or data exfiltration vectors that threaten OT integrity. | Removal of device privileges; disciplinary action up to termination. |
| **Installing unauthorized software** or changing system configuration without IT approval. | Can alter device behavior and expose OT systems to vulnerabilities. | Disciplinary action; possible termination. |
| **Using the device to access, share, or store information that violates company data‑classification or intellectual‑property policies.** | Breaches confidentiality and compliance obligations. | Disciplinary action; possible termination. |

### 6. Monitoring & Disclosure  
1. **Monitoring Rights** – The company reserves the right to monitor all device and network activity, including but not limited to:
   - Network traffic logs.  
   - Application usage reports.  
   - File transfer records.  
   - Remote‑management logs.  
2. **Employee Disclosure** – Prior to receiving a device, every user will be provided a *Monitoring Disclosure Statement* that explains:
   - What types of activity will be monitored.  
   - How monitoring data will be used (security, compliance, safety).  
   - The retention period for logs.  
3. **Acknowledgment** – Users must sign an *Acceptable Use & Monitoring Acknowledgment* before the device is issued. The acknowledgment confirms:
   - Receipt of the disclosure.  
   - Understanding of the monitoring scope.  
   - Agreement to comply with this AUP.

### 7. Reporting Violations & Disciplinary Actions  
1. **Reporting** – Employees who suspect a policy violation should report to their supervisor or the IT Security team immediately.  
2. **Investigation** – IT Security, in coordination with Safety and Human Resources, will investigate all reported incidents.  
3. **Disciplinary Process** – Depending on the severity and context:
   - **Minor non‑safety violations** – Warning, mandatory training, or device restriction.  
   - **Safety‑critical OT violations** – Immediate suspension of device/network access, followed by disciplinary action up to termination.  
4. **Right to Appeal** – Employees have the right to appeal any disciplinary decision through the established grievance procedure.

### 8. Protected Activities – Wages, Hours, Working Conditions  
**Explicit Carve‑Out:**  
- Employees may discuss wages, hours, overtime, and working conditions without fear of disciplinary action.  
- Any monitoring or disciplinary action **must not** be tied to, or interpreted as, a response to such protected discussions.  

### 9. Policy Review & Updates  
- This policy will be reviewed annually by Legal, IT Security, Operations, and HR.  
- Updates will be communicated to all employees via email, the intranet, and device issuance procedures.
