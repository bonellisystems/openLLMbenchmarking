## Anchors

# Cybersecurity -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS identifiers presented as real.
- Eradication or patching recommended before containment on an active incident.
- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.
- Ignoring implied breach-notification or regulatory timelines when sensitive data is involved.
- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Severity correctly triaged and prioritized against the described impact.
- Containment-before-eradication-before-recovery ordering respected.
- Real, correctly-applied CVE/CWE/ATT&CK references where relevant.
- Evidence-preservation explicitly called out.
- Notification or compliance timeline awareness when data exposure is implied.

**1. Regulatory timeline for notifying the covered entity after discovering a breach of unsecured PHI**  
- **HIPAA Breach Notification Rule** requires that any breach of unsecured protected health information (PHI) be reported to the covered entity **no later than 60 calendar days** after the discovery of the breach.  
- Our incident‑response plan is configured to **identify, contain, and assess** the breach within the first 24–48 hours, with a formal **notification draft prepared and approved by legal/compliance** by day 5.  
- The final breach‑notification letter (including required content—description of the breach, dates of occurrence, types of PHI involved, steps taken to mitigate harm, and contact information for affected individuals) is sent to the covered entity **no later than day 55** to provide a safety margin before the 60‑day deadline.  
- If a breach is later determined to be “low‑risk” (e.g., after a risk‑assessment concludes low probability of harm), we still retain the formal 60‑day window for reporting but may elect not to send the notification to the covered entity if the risk assessment supports a “no‑notification” determination, in which case we document the justification per 45 CFR 164.404.

**2. Enforcement of multi‑factor authentication (MFA) for all remote administrative access to systems that touch PHI**  
- **Policy:** All remote administrative access to any system that stores, processes, or transmits PHI **must use MFA**. This includes VPN, RDP/SSH jump servers, privileged‑access‑management (PAM) tools, and any SaaS admin consoles that can view or modify PHI‑related data.  
- **Technical controls:**  
  - **Primary factor:** Password or passphrase that meets our password‑complexity standards (≥12 characters, mix of case, numbers, symbols).  
  - **Secondary factor:** One of the following, required for every remote session:  
    - TOTP‑based authenticator app (e.g., Microsoft Authenticator, [model] Authenticator).  
    - FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware token (e.g., YubiKey, Feitian).  
    - Certificate‑based client authentication for VPN.  
  - MFA devices are **issued, provisioned, and monitored** through our identity‑and‑access‑management (IAM) platform; lost or compromised tokens trigger immediate revocation and re‑issuance.  
- **Monitoring & enforcement:**  
  - Our PAM solution enforces MFA challenges at the gateway level; any session lacking a successful second factor is automatically terminated.  
  - Quarterly compliance audits verify that MFA is enabled for 100 % of active administrative accounts; any exception must be documented, approved by the CISO, and re‑reviewed within 30 days.  
- **Exceptions:** None. Any temporary exception (e.g., for emergency maintenance) must be approved by the CISO, logged, and the session must still employ an approved MFA factor.

**3. Process for verifying whether a third‑party software component (e.g., a managed file‑transfer tool) has a known CVE before deployment**  
1. **Vulnerability Intelligence Feed Integration**  
   - We subscribe to and ingest feeds from the **National Vulnerability Database (NVD)**, **CVE Details**, ** vendor security advisories**, and **commercial threat‑intel platforms** (e.g., Rapid7, Qualys, Recorded Future).  
   - A scheduled nightly job parses these feeds for any newly disclosed CVEs affecting the component’s version or prior versions.  

2. **Version‑Specific CVE Mapping**  
   - Our **Software Composition Analysis (SCA)** tool (e.g., Snyk, Black Duck, or an internal scanner) automatically correlates the component’s version against a curated CVE database, generating a “vulnerability impact report.”  
   - The report flags any **known CVEs with a CVSS‑v3 score ≥ 7.0** that are exploitable in the context of the component (e.g., remote code execution, privilege escalation).  

3. **Pre‑Deployment Review Workflow**  
   - **Step A – Automated Check:** The CI/CD pipeline runs the SCA scan on the proposed component version. If any qualifying CVE is found, the pipeline is halted.  
   - **Step B – Manual Review:** The security engineering team reviews the CVE details (exploit availability, mitigation patches, vendor’s response).  
   - **Step C – Patch/Upgrade Decision:** If a vulnerability exists, we either:  
     - **Apply the vendor’s security patch** (if released and vetted), or  
     - **Upgrade to a newer, patched version** that is free of known high‑severity CVEs.  
   - **Step D – Regression Testing:** The patched/upgraded component undergoes functional and security regression testing in a staging environment that mirrors the production data flow.  
   - **Step E – Sign‑off:** The security lead signs off only after the component meets our **“no high‑severity CVE”** criterion and passes all compliance checkpoints (e.g., data‑handling controls, audit‑log requirements).  

4. **Ongoing Post‑Deployment Monitoring**  
   - After deployment, the component is continuously monitored via **runtime vulnerability scanning** and **log‑based anomaly detection** for any emergent threats.  
   - We subscribe to the vendor’s **security bulletin feed** and automatically trigger a re‑assessment whenever a new advisory is released for that component.  

5. **Documentation & Audit Trail**  
   - All CVE‑verification activities—including feed sources, scan results, remediation actions, and final sign‑off—are recorded in our **Secure Change Management System** and retained for at least three years to satisfy audit and compliance requirements.  

By following this structured, automated‑plus‑manual workflow, we ensure that any third‑party component introduced into the practice’s environment has been vetted for known CVEs, patched or upgraded as necessary, and only then authorized for production use.
