# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Operations -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The process redesign is tied concretely to the specific bottleneck and constraint stated in the prompt, with measurable KPIs that include a baseline and target, not just a goal restated as a metric. Implementation steps are sequenced with a clear first action, change-management or rollout risk is addressed, and resourcing constraints given in the prompt are respected rather than assumed away.
Example character: a redesign that names the actual bottleneck, gives a baseline-to-target KPI, and sequences the first three implementation steps.

## 7 -- Strong
The process improvement is sound and directly usable, correctly targeting the stated bottleneck. The KPI baseline or rollout sequencing is thinner than ideal, but the core recommendation would work as described.
Example character: a correct fix for the stated bottleneck with a named metric but no baseline value given.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic process-improvement guidance ("streamline the workflow," "reduce handoffs") that is directionally correct but not tied to the specific constraint described in the prompt.
Example character: general efficiency advice that would apply to almost any operational bottleneck.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misidentifies the bottleneck, or proposes a process that ignores a stated hard constraint (headcount, budget, system limitation), with no measurable KPI anywhere in the response.
Example character: a redesign that requires more staff despite the prompt stating headcount is fixed.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated industry-benchmark figures as if real, or recommends a change that outright violates a stated hard constraint with no acknowledgment. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated industry-benchmark numbers presented as real data.
- Ignoring an explicitly stated resource or system constraint.
- No measurable KPI or success criterion anywhere in the response.
- Steps that restate goals rather than being actually sequenced and actionable.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Root cause or bottleneck correctly identified from the details given.
- Measurable KPI with a stated baseline and target.
- Rollout sequenced with a clear, concrete first step.
- Resourcing constraints from the prompt respected in the design.

### 1. Redesigned SOP: "Batch-Zone Direct-Print" Model

**The Core Problem:** The current workflow inserts a "human-in-the-loop" transcription step (re-keying) between the system of record and the physical warehouse, and forces pickers to hunt through an unsorted queue.

**The New Flow:** Orders flow directly from the Order Management System (OMS) to a printed, zone-organized pick list. Pickers work by zone batches rather than individual order sheets.

#### The New SOP Sequence
1.  **Order Intake & Validation (OMS):** Orders enter the system. The Operations Lead generates a daily "Ready-to-Pick" CSV export from the OMS at 08:00. **Stop all manual re-keying.**
2.  **Zone Sorting (Excel/Spreadsheet):** The daily CSV is imported into the existing spreadsheet tool. Orders are filtered and grouped by **Warehouse Zone** (e.g., Zone A = Tops, Zone B = Bottoms, Zone C = Outerwear). A single "Zone Pick Slip" is generated for each zone containing the consolidated SKU, location code, and quantity required for *all* orders in that zone.
3.  **Print & Dispatch:** Zone Pick Slips are printed on thermal paper or standard office paper with clear large-font SKU-to-Location mapping. Slips are posted at the start of each zone.
4.  **Zone Batch Picking:** Warehouse staff begin their shift by picking **all** orders in one zone before moving to the next. They scan the item barcode against the printed SKU (or visually confirm SKU + Location code) to prevent transposition.
5.  **Consolidation & Packing:** Once a zone is complete, all items are swept to the packing station, consolidated into individual orders, and shipped.

## 4. Detailed SOP Flow (High‑Level)

1. **Order Creation** – Order enters the ERP; an automated export pushes the data into the pick‑list spreadsheet.  
2. **Zone Assignment** – Warehouse staff (or a simple “zone picker” algorithm) determines which zone(s) contain the majority of the order’s SKUs.  
3. **Pick‑Lane Station** – Worker receives the zone‑specific pick‑list, scans each SKU **before** pulling.  
4. **Item Retrieval** – Worker pulls the item and places it in a dedicated “check‑box”.  
5. **Packing Station** – Worker scans each SKU again, cross‑checking against the pick‑list.  
6. **Final Verification** – If all scans match, the order is bagged, labeled, and queued for shipment.  
7. **Audit** – Scan logs are automatically uploaded to a shared folder for daily quality review.

## 3.  Redesign – “Zero‑Re‑Key Zone‑Pick” SOP  

### Overview  
1. **Automated Pick‑List Generation** – Pull order data straight from the order‑management system (OMS) into a printable “zone‑pick sheet” (PDF/Excel) – **no manual re‑keying**.  
2. **Zone‑Based Batch Picking** – Orders are grouped by warehouse zone (e.g., A‑zone shirts, B‑zone pants). Pickers work a single zone at a time, pulling all items for that zone in one pass.  
3. **Barcode‑Enabled Confirmation** – Every SKU is scanned **twice** (at pick & at pack) using the handheld scanners already used for receiving/stocking.  
4. **Two‑Person “Pick‑Check” Handoff** – After a picker completes a zone batch, a second associate performs a quick visual/scan verification before items move to the packing station.  
5. **Pack‑Station Scan‑and‑Seal** – The packer scans the final SKU list (auto‑generated from the same pick sheet) and the shipping label; the system logs a “pick‑complete” timestamp, feeding directly into the shipping manifest.

### 3.2  How an order flows through the new SOP  

1. **Order Consolidation (0‑15 min after order entry)**  
   * The order‑management system (OMS) automatically groups all orders received in the last hour into **zone‑batches** (based on SKU location).  
   * For each batch, the OMS prints **one pick‑sheet** per zone‑bin set (e.g., “Batch‑12 – Zone A – 23 items”).  

2. **Pick‑Sheet Distribution (15‑30 min)**  
   * The printed sheet is placed on a **dedicated pick‑station** in the appropriate zone.  
   * The QR code on the sheet encodes **all order numbers** that belong to that batch, so a single scan validates the whole batch.  

3. **Picking (30 min‑2 h)**  
   * Associates walk the zone, locate each bin, scan the QR code **once** (captures the batch ID).  
   * For each SKU, they scan the bin label → system confirms the correct SKU (the scanner reads the bin‑SKU pairing).  
   * After pulling the required quantity, they **scan the pick‑sheet QR again** to log the pick and move the tote forward.  

4. **Pick‑Verify Checkpoint (immediately after picking a tote)**  
   * The tote is sealed and moved to the **packing dock**. Before sealing, the associate scans the QR code on the pick‑sheet **one final time**.  
   * The handheld scanner cross‑checks the scanned SKUs/qty against the printed list; any mismatch triggers a **pop‑up alert** on the handheld screen (or a colored light on the tote).  

5. **Packing & Shipping (within the same shift)**  
   * Because totes are now **batch‑validated**, the packing step is a simple “place the tote on the carrier pallet” – no extra sorting needed.  
   * The carrier pick‑up schedule is aligned to the **zone‑batch completion time**, which naturally clusters shipments and reduces dwell time.
