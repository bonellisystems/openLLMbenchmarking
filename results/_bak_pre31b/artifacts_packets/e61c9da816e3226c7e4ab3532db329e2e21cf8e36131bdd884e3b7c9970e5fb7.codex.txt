# KB Article: Troubleshooting WiFi Dropouts in Cold Storage & Freezer Environments

**Ticket ID:** [Reference Ticket ID]  
**Affected Systems:** Handheld Barcode Scanners, Lot-Tracking Software, Freezer Area WiFi APs  
**Impact Level:** High (USDA Compliance Risk)

## Symptom
*   Handheld barcode scanners in freezer/cold-storage areas experience intermittent WiFi disconnections.
*   Scans fail to sync to the central server, requiring workers to manually re-scan pallets.
*   Significant risk of gaps in USDA lot-tracking coverage, potentially halting operations during audits.

## Root Cause
*   **Physical Placement:** The Access Point (AP) was mounted on a metal support beam directly adjacent to a walk-in cooler door. Closing the metal-clad door obstructed the signal.
*   **Frequency Interference:** The AP was likely utilizing the 5GHz band, which has poor penetration capabilities through metal surfaces and dense cold environments compared to 2.4GHz.
*   **Environmental Damage:** Cold air entering the enclosure caused condensation to form inside the outdoor-rated housing, potentially degrading the radio hardware over time.

## Resolution
*   **Hardware Relocation:** The AP was physically moved away from the metal support beam and outside the swing path of the cooler door to ensure clear line-of-sight.
*   **Configuration Change:** 2.4GHz band steering was enabled specifically for this AP. This frequency provides superior penetration through metal and cold-storage environments compared to 5GHz.
*   **Verification:** Post-implementation testing confirmed no scan drops and successful syncing of lot-tracking data.

## Prevention & First-Tier Diagnostics
**Do not dispatch a technician for hardware relocation immediately.** When encountering similar connectivity issues in cold storage, perform the following **First-Tier Diagnostics** to rule out configuration or placement issues remotely or via site staff assessment.

1.  **Check AP Configuration (Remote):**
    *   Log into the AP management interface.
    *   Verify if **2.4GHz Band Steering** is enabled. If the AP defaults to 5GHz in cold environments, this is the first setting to toggle.
2.  **Assess Physical Proximity (Visual/Site Staff):**
    *   Ask site staff to photograph the AP location.
    *   **Check for Metal Obstruction:** Is the AP mounted on or near a metal beam, rack, or door frame?
    *   **Check Door Swing:** Is the AP in the direct path of a closing metal door?
3.  **Inspect Enclosure Condition:**
    *   If accessible, check the AP outdoor enclosure for signs of **condensation** or moisture ingress, which indicates environmental sealing failure.
4.  **Run a Quick Signal Survey:**
    *   Before sending a truck, have a user perform a signal strength test near the door while it is closed. If signal drops below -75dBm, this confirms the root cause is physical placement or frequency, not hardware failure.

**Decision Tree:** If the AP is within 5 feet of a metal door/beam or if 2.4GHz steering is disabled, apply the configuration fix or request relocation. If the issue persists after these checks, then dispatch the field team.

### **Symptom**
Handheld barcode scanners operating in walk-in freezers or cold-storage areas experience intermittent WiFi connectivity loss.  
**Specific Indicators:**
*   Scanners fail to sync lot-tracking data; workers are forced to re-scan pallets.
*   Risk of compliance gap: Intermittent sync failures threaten USDA lot-tracking coverage.
*   Issue may correlate with the opening/closing of walk-in cooler doors.

### **Root Cause**
Investigation identified three compounding factors in cold-storage environments:

1.  **Metal Interference:** The WiFi Access Point (AP) was mounted on a metal support beam or within the swing path of metal-clad cooler doors. Metal structures reflect and absorb RF signals, causing significant signal degradation and multipath interference.
2.  **Condensation in Enclosure:** Despite using outdoor-rated enclosures, large temperature differentials caused condensation to form on internal components or the enclosure interior. This moisture can alter RF characteristics and damage sensitive electronics over time.
3.  **Frequency Mismatch:** The environment favored 5GHz operation, but 5GHz signals are more susceptible to attenuation by metal and dense cold-air environments compared to 2.4GHz. Lack of band steering caused clients to latch to a weaker 5GHz signal or fail to fall back to the more robust 2.4GHz band.

### **Resolution**
*   **Relocation:** Moved the AP away from the metal support beam and positioned it outside the door's swing path to eliminate metal reflection and blockage.
*   **Band Steering Configuration:** Enabled **2.4GHz Band Steering** for the affected AP specifically. This forces the AP to prioritize 2.4GHz for clients in this location, as 2.4GHz provides superior penetration through the metal/cold environment at this site.
*   **Validation:** Verified continuous scan sync with zero drops post-implementation.

### **Prevention & Diagnostics**
**⚡ Faster Diagnostic: Check Configuration Before Relocation**  
Next time a ticket opens for connectivity loss in a freezer/cold-storage area, **do not immediately schedule hardware relocation.** Follow this prioritized checklist to resolve faster:

1.  **Check AP Band Steering & RF Bands (FIRST)**  
    *   Log into the AP configuration. Verify that **2.4GHz Band Steering** is enabled for this AP.  
    *   Ensure 2.4GHz is not disabled or suppressed. In metal-heavy or extreme cold environments, 2.4GHz often performs better than 5GHz due to lower attenuation.  
    *   *Action:* If 2.4GHz is available, enable steering to force clients to use the band with better penetration. This often resolves the issue without moving hardware.

2.  **Review AP Diagnostics & Logs**  
    *   Check RSSI/RSSI levels and error rates on the AP. Look for signal drops that correlate with door movement (indicating interference vs. environmental issues).  
    *   Review client lists: Are scanners associating with 5GHz when 2.4GHz would be more stable?

3.  **Environmental & Physical Checks (If Config Checks Pass)**  
    *   **Condensation:** Inspect the AP enclosure for moisture. If condensation is present, the enclosure may need a desiccant pack, heating element, or a sealed, insulated mounting solution.  
    *   **Metal Obstruction:** Verify the AP is not mounted on metal beams or within 3 feet of metal doors/structures. Relocation is required if band steering does not resolve the issue.

**Pro-Tip for Cold Storage:** Always prioritize 2.4GHz availability in metal/cold environments. Band steering is the fastest fix for interference caused by RF propagation limitations before resorting to physical relocation.

### Symptom
Handheld barcode scanners deployed in a poultry plant's freezer/cold-storage area experienced frequent WiFi disconnections. This led to failed synchronization of lot-tracking scans, necessitating workers to re-scan pallets. The situation posed a significant risk of creating gaps in USDA lot-tracking coverage, potentially impacting compliance and inventory accuracy.

### Root Cause
The primary cause of the connectivity issues was twofold:
1. **Physical Interference:** The WiFi access point (AP) was mounted on a metal support beam adjacent to the walk-in cooler door. The metal structure and the door's metal-clad outer skin severely degraded the WiFi signal, especially when the door was closed, creating a significant barrier to wireless transmission.
2. **Environmental Factors:** Condensation had formed inside the AP's outdoor-rated enclosure due to the cold environment. This condensation further impaired the AP's performance, exacerbating the signal degradation.

### Resolution
To address the connectivity issues, the following steps were taken:
1. **Relocation of the AP:** The WiFi access point was moved away from the metal support beam and out of the door's swing path. This relocation minimized physical obstructions that were attenuating the WiFi signal.
2. **Band Steering Adjustment:** Enabled 2.4GHz band steering specifically for this access point. The 2.4GHz frequency band was chosen over 5GHz due to its superior penetration capabilities through metal and in cold environments, ensuring more reliable connectivity for the handheld barcode scanners in the freezer area.

Post-relocation, no instances of WiFi drops or scan synchronization failures were reported, restoring efficient operations and compliance with USDA lot-tracking requirements.

### Prevention
To prevent recurrence of similar issues and to streamline diagnostics in the future, follow these steps in order before considering hardware relocation:

1. **Signal Strength and Quality Assessment:**
   - Use network diagnostic tools to measure WiFi signal strength and quality at multiple points within the freezer/cold-storage area, especially near the scanners' operating zones.
   - Pay particular attention to areas adjacent to large metal structures or when doors are closed, as these are common sources of signal degradation.

2. **Environmental Monitoring:**
   - Check for signs of condensation or moisture inside the AP's enclosure. Ensure that the AP is appropriately rated for the environmental conditions or consider additional protective measures (e.g., desiccant packs) to mitigate condensation risks.

3. **Band Performance Evaluation:**
   - Analyze the performance of both 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands in the specific environment. Tools that can perform band steering analysis or that offer insights into client device band preferences should be utilized.
   - If 2.4GHz is determined to offer significantly better penetration and reliability in the given conditions, ensure that band steering or client connection policies favor this band for devices operating in challenging environments.

4. **Physical Obstruction Review:**
   - Conduct a walkthrough to identify any new or existing physical obstructions (e.g., equipment, shelving) that could impact WiFi signal propagation. Adjust AP placement or consider additional access points if necessary to ensure coverage.

By systematically addressing these areas, the helpdesk team can quickly identify and mitigate WiFi connectivity issues without immediately resorting to hardware relocation, thereby reducing downtime and operational disruptions.

KB Article: Handheld Scanner WiFi Drops in Freezer/Cold-Storage Areas

Symptom: Handheld barcode scanners in freezer or walk-in-cooler areas repeatedly drop WiFi
and fail to sync lot-tracking scans, forcing workers to re-scan pallets. Risk: repeated
failures in this area can create a gap in USDA lot-tracking coverage if scans aren't
eventually captured.

Root Cause: The area's access point is mounted on or near metal structure (support beams,
walk-in cooler doors). Metal reflects/absorbs WiFi signal, and the effect gets worse when a
metal-clad cooler door is closed between the AP and the scanner. In cold-storage
installations, condensation can also form inside an outdoor-rated AP enclosure, degrading the
radio over time. Both factors combine to cause intermittent, hard-to-reproduce drops that look
like a generic "flaky WiFi" issue but are actually environmental/RF, not a scanner or
controller misconfiguration.

Resolution: Relocate the AP away from metal beams and out of the door's swing path (line of
sight to the scanning area, away from the door seal). Enable 2.4GHz band steering for APs in
metal/cold environments specifically -- 2.4GHz penetrates metal and cold-affected material
better than 5GHz at these distances, even though 5GHz is usually preferred elsewhere in the
plant for throughput.

physically move an AP, pull the AP's signal-strength (RSSI) and retry/retransmission logs for
the affected scanners over the last few failed sync events. If RSSI is weak specifically near
the cooler door and improves elsewhere in the same room, that confirms a placement/RF issue
without a site visit. If RSSI is consistently strong but syncs still fail, suspect the
controller/roaming configuration instead, which points to a different fix entirely and avoids
an unnecessary hardware move.

---

## 1. Symptom  
- Hand‑held barcode scanners used in the freezer/cold‑storage zone were intermittently losing Wi‑Fi connectivity.  
- When the connection dropped, scans could not be uploaded to the lot‑tracking system, forcing staff to re‑scan pallets.  
- The loss of connectivity created gaps in USDA lot‑tracking coverage and increased operational risk.  

**Typical user report:**  
> “Scans are failing in the freezer. The scanner shows ‘no network’ and we have to put the pallet back on the cart and scan again.”

---

## 2. Root Cause  
| Observation | Why it mattered |
|-------------|-----------------|
| **AP location** – The wireless access point (AP) serving the area was mounted on a metal support beam directly above the walk‑in cooler door. | Metal structures reflect/absorb RF energy, especially at higher frequencies. |
| **Door closure** – When the door (with its metal‑clad outer skin) closed, the AP’s signal was further attenuated. | The door’s metal surface acted like a Faraday cage, causing periodic signal loss each time the door shut. |
| **Condensation inside the AP enclosure** – The outdoor‑rated AP housing collected moisture from the cold environment. | Water inside the enclosure degraded the antenna performance and contributed to intermittent drops. |
| **Band selection** – The AP was configured for dual‑band (2.4 GHz / 5 GHz) with no band‑steering. | 5 GHz signals attenuate even more quickly in cold, metal‑rich environments; 2.4 GHz performs better for penetration. |

**Result:** Periodic Wi‑Fi drops directly correlated with door operation and the physical placement of the AP, leading to lost scans and re‑work.

---

## 3. Resolution  
1. **Relocated the AP**  
   - Moved the access point off the metal support beam and out of the door’s swing path.  
   - Mounted it on a non‑metallic wall/ceiling location that still covered the scanner work zone.  

2. **Enabled band‑steering for that AP only**  
   - Configured the AP to **force 2.4 GHz usage** for client devices in this zone.  
   - 2.4 GHz provides better penetration through metal and performs more reliably in cold, high‑humidity environments.  

3. **Verified fix**  
   - After relocation and band‑steering, scanners maintained a stable connection for > 48 h with **zero drop‑outs**.  
   - No re‑scanning of pallets was required; USDA lot‑tracking coverage remained continuous.  

---

## 4. Prevention – What to Check First Next Time  

When a similar symptom appears, follow this **diagnostic shortcut** before dispatching a truck for hardware relocation:

1. **Visual / Physical Quick‑Check**  
   - **Is the AP mounted on or near metal?**  
     - Look for metal beams, frames, or doors directly adjacent to the antenna.  
   - **Is the door (or any metal enclosure) frequently opened/closed in the area?**  
     - Note the swing path and whether the door closes over the AP.  

2. **Environmental Sensors / Status Page**  
   - Verify AP health metrics (RSSI, link quality, temperature, humidity).  
   - High humidity or condensation warnings often precede performance degradation.  

3. **Band‑Steering / Frequency Utilization**  
   - Confirm whether the AP is set to **dual‑band with steering** or fixed to 5 GHz.  
   - If the problematic zone relies on 5 GHz, try forcing 2.4 GHz for that SSID as a test.  

4. **Signal‑Strength Test**  
   - Use a handheld Wi‑Fi analyzer or a scanner with a Wi‑Fi client to measure RSSI **both with the door open and closed**.  
   - A > 10 dB drop when the door closes is a strong indicator of interference from metal.  

5. **Document Findings**  
   - Capture a screenshot of the AP’s signal‑strength chart or a photo of the mounting location.  
   - Add a note to the ticket referencing the “Metal‑Beam/Door‑Swing Interference Check”.  

**If any of the above checks reveal:**  
- Metal proximity or door swing overlap → **Re‑mount the AP** away from metal and out of the swing path.  
- Predominant 5 GHz usage → **Enable 2.4 GHz‑only** (or band‑steer) for that SSID.  

By performing these quick checks, the team can isolate the root cause in **under 5 minutes** and avoid unnecessary site visits, while still ensuring a robust, long‑term solution.

| **AP Placement** | The Wi‑Fi access point (AP) was mounted on a metal support beam directly adjacent to the walk‑in cooler door. |
| **Metal & Door** | The cooler door is a metal‑clad panel. Closing the door creates a “metal wall” between the AP and the scanners, causing severe signal attenuation. |
| **Condensation** | The AP’s outdoor‑rated enclosure was exposed to the cold environment, causing condensation inside the unit. Moisture further degraded the radio performance and led to intermittent drops. |
| **Frequency Band** | The default 5 GHz band was used. 5 GHz signals are less effective at penetrating metal and cold‑air barriers than 2.4 GHz. |

---

### 3. Resolution  
1. **Relocated the AP**  
   * Moved the AP to a position away from the metal beam and the door’s swing path.  
   * Placed it in a warmer, less humid location (e.g., inside a non‑metal cabinet or a dedicated AP rack).  

2. **Enabled 2.4 GHz Band Steering**  
   * Configured the AP to steer 2.4 GHz traffic for that particular location.  
   * 2.4 GHz penetrates metal and cold air better, providing stable connectivity for the handheld scanners.  

3. **Checked and Removed Condensation**  
   * Opened the enclosure, drained moisture, and replaced any damaged seals.  
   * Re‑sealed the unit with appropriate weather‑proofing.  

**Result:** No further scan drops have been observed since relocation and band‑steering configuration.

### 4. Prevention – Quick Diagnostic Checklist  
When encountering similar issues in cold‑storage or metal‑heavy environments, follow this step‑by‑step “first‑look” diagnostic before rolling out hardware changes:

1. **Verify AP Placement**  
   * Is the AP mounted on or near a metal beam, metal door, or other metal structure?  
   * Does the AP’s line‑of‑sight to the handheld scanners cross a metal barrier when doors are closed?  

2. **Inspect Enclosure & Environment**  
   * Open the AP enclosure (if safe) and look for condensation or moisture.  
   * Check that the enclosure is rated for the temperature range of the area.  

3. **Check Frequency Band Usage**  
   * Are 5 GHz radios being used in a cold, metal‑dense environment?  
   * Enable or enforce 2.4 GHz band steering or use 2.4 GHz radios for the affected AP.  

4. **Measure Signal Strength & Quality**  
   * Use a Wi‑Fi analyzer or the AP’s built‑in monitoring tools to measure RSSI and SNR when the door is open vs. closed.  
   * Look for significant drops (> 20 dB) when the door closes.  

5. **Review AP Logs & Client Disconnects**  
   * Check the AP logs for deauth or disconnect events correlated with door operation.  

6. **Test with a Handheld Scanner**  
   * Perform a quick scan test while opening and closing the door to confirm stable connectivity.

### 🔍 Symptom
- Handheld barcode scanners in freezer/cold-storage areas experience intermittent or complete WiFi disconnections.
- Lot-tracking scans fail to sync to the central server, forcing workers to re-scan pallets.
- Creates risk of gaps in USDA lot-tracking compliance documentation and increases labor time.

### 🧠 Root Cause
- **AP Placement:** The access point was mounted directly on a metal support beam and positioned within the swing path of a walk-in cooler door with a metal-clad outer skin. Metal structures cause severe RF attenuation and multipath signal reflection.
- **Band Selection:** Default band steering was favoring 5GHz. At this location, 5GHz signals attenuate significantly faster through metal and dense cold environments than 2.4GHz.
- **Environmental Factor:** Cold temperatures caused condensation to form inside the AP’s outdoor-rated enclosure, further degrading internal RF components and antenna performance.

### ✅ Resolution
- **Relocated AP:** Moved the access point off the metal support beam and outside the door’s swing path to eliminate primary RF obstructions.
- **Band Reconfiguration:** Enabled 2.4GHz band steering specifically for this AP, optimizing signal penetration for the metal/cold environment.
- **Validation:** Confirmed stable RSSI/SNR at worker scan positions with the cooler door both open and closed. Zero scan drops or sync failures recorded since deployment.

### 🛡️ Prevention (First-Check Diagnostic Protocol)
*Before dispatching a truck or physically relocating hardware, run these checks in order. This sequence typically identifies 80%+ of cold-storage connectivity issues in <15 minutes.*

1. **🥇 Check AP Placement vs. Metal & Door Swing Paths**  
   Confirm the AP is not mounted on, behind, or within the direct arc of metal beams, metal-clad walk-in doors, or heavy steel racking. Metal is the #1 cause of RF attenuation in cold-storage environments.

2. **🥈 Test Signal Variance: Door Open vs. Closed**  
   Use a WiFi analyzer app or the affected handhelds to capture RSSI and throughput with the door open, then closed. A drop of ≥15–20 dBm when closed confirms an obstruction/placement issue, not a hardware fault.

3. **🥉 Verify Band Steering Configuration for Cold/Metal Environments**  
   Default band steering often favors 5GHz, which performs poorly through metal and dense cold. Explicitly enable 2.4GHz band steering (or lock the AP to 2.4GHz) on any AP servicing walk-ins, freezers, or metal-heavy zones.

4. **🌡️ Inspect Outdoor Enclosures for Condensation/Ice**  
   Open the AP enclosure and check for moisture, ice, or compromised gaskets. Install or replace desiccant packs, verify IP rating integrity, and consider heated enclosures for sub-freezing zones with recurring condensation.

5. **📍 Validate at the Actual Scan Position (Not the AP)**  
   Measure connectivity at the exact worker standing position with loaded pallets present. Pallets, product packaging, and ambient cold air density can create dead spots that only appear under operational conditions.

---
**Notes for Tier 1:** If the above checks confirm placement or configuration issues, resolve via AP relocation + band steering change. Do not escalate to hardware replacement unless the AP shows physical damage, failed radio diagnostics, or inconsistent behavior after environmental corrections.

**Title:** WiFi Drops and Sync Failures for Barcode Scanners in Freezer/Cold Storage Areas
**Category:** Network / Environmental / Hardware
**Severity:** High (USDA Compliance Risk)
**Keywords:** Freezer, Cold Storage, Barcode Scanner, WiFi Drop, Lot Tracking, Condensation, Metal Interference, Band Steering, USDA

---

### **Symptom**
*   Handheld barcode scanners in freezer or cold-storage zones (e.g., poultry plant walk-in coolers) repeatedly drop WiFi connectivity.
*   Scanners fail to sync lot-tracking data to the central database.
*   Workers are forced to re-scan pallets, causing workflow delays.
*   **Critical Impact:** Risk of gaps in USDA lot-tracking coverage, potentially triggering compliance violations.
*   Issues often correlate with the opening/closing of heavy insulated cooler doors or occur in areas with visible metal infrastructure.

### **Root Cause**
Investigation typically reveals a combination of physical placement, environmental factors, and RF propagation limitations:

1.  **Physical Placement & Interference:**
    *   **Metal Mounting:** Access Points (APs) mounted directly on metal support beams suffer from signal shielding and reflection.
    *   **Door Blockage:** APs located near walk-in cooler doors with metal-clad outer skins experience significant signal degradation when the door is closed, acting as a barrier to RF propagation.
    *   **Swing Path:** APs placed in the swing path of heavy doors can be physically obstructed during operation.
2.  **Environmental Degradation:**
    *   **Condensation:** Moisture buildup inside AP enclosures due to temperature differentials can affect internal components and signal integrity, even in outdoor-rated enclosures if seals are compromised or the microclimate is trapped.
3.  **RF Frequency Limitations:**
    *   **5GHz Penetration:** In dense cold-storage environments, 5GHz signals struggle to penetrate metal structures and maintain stability compared to 2.4GHz. Scanners may latch onto a weak 5GHz signal and fail to roam or sync.

### **Resolution**
Apply the following fixes to restore connectivity and ensure compliance:

1.  **Relocate Access Point:**
    *   Move the AP away from metal support beams. Use non-conductive mounts if necessary.
    *   Ensure the AP is positioned **outside the swing path** of cooler doors and clear of metal-clad door skins.
    *   Verify line-of-sight coverage for the scanner usage zone without obstruction by doors or metal racking.
2.  **Adjust RF Configuration:**
    *   **Enable 2.4GHz Band Steering:** Configure the specific AP to prioritize the 2.4GHz band for client association. In this environment, 2.4GHz provides superior penetration and reliability through metal and cold-density air.
    *   *Note:* Ensure band steering is tuned to **steer to 2.4GHz** for this AP, rather than the standard practice of steering to 5GHz.
3.  **Inspect Enclosure Integrity:**
    *   Check the AP enclosure for condensation. Dry the unit if necessary and verify weather seals/gaskets are intact to prevent moisture ingress.
4.  **Validation:**
    *   Monitor scan logs and AP client tables for 24 hours post-change. Confirm zero sync failures and stable lot-tracking coverage.

### **Prevention & Diagnostics**
To prevent recurrence and avoid unnecessary truck rolls, follow the **Remote Diagnostic Checklist** below before dispatching a technician.

#### **🛑 Remote Diagnostic Checklist (Check FIRST)**
*Before rolling a truck, perform these remote checks to rule out config or placement issues:*

1.  **Review AP Asset Map/Photos:**
    *   Check the physical location of the AP. Is it mounted on metal? Is it adjacent to a walk-in cooler door? If yes, relocation is likely required; do not attempt remote fixes for metal obstruction.
2.  **Verify Band Steering Configuration:**
    *   Log into the AP and confirm **2.4GHz Band Steering** is enabled for that specific SSID/AP group. Ensure the setting is not defaulting to 5GHz preference.
3.  **Analyze Client Association Logs:**
    *   Check if scanners are associating on 5GHz with low RSSI/SNR. If clients are stuck on 5GHz with poor signal, force a config push for 2.4GHz preference or reboot APs to re-negotiate association.
4.  **Correlate Door Cycles with Drops:**
    *   If facility management provides door cycle data, compare timestamps with WiFi drop events. A direct correlation suggests physical obstruction by the door, requiring relocation.
5.  **Check Environmental Warnings:**
    *   Review AP health telemetry for temperature or humidity warnings that may indicate condensation risks or enclosure failure.

#### **Installation Standards for Cold Storage Zones**
*   **Mounting:** Never mount APs on structural metal beams. Use standoff brackets or locate on drywall/wood supports where possible.
*   **Door Clearance:** APs must be installed >2 meters away from the centerline of heavy insulated/metal doors.
*   **Enclosures:** Use cold-rated enclosures with desiccant packs if required. Inspect seals during quarterly maintenance.
*   **Default Config:** All APs tagged as "Cold Storage" or "Freezer" in the network management system should automatically inherit a profile that **prioritizes 2.4GHz** and disables aggressive 5GHz roaming thresholds.

#### **Operational Reminders**
*   **USDA Compliance:** Emphasize to site contacts that WiFi stability in freezer zones is a regulatory requirement, not just a connectivity issue.
*   **Scanner Battery:** In cold environments, scanner batteries drain faster. While not a WiFi issue, remind users to charge scanners in ambient temperature zones to prevent battery-induced disconnects that mimic WiFi drops.
