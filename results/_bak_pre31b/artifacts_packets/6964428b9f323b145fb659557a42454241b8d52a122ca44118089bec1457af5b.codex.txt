## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Marketing -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics presented as real.
- Generic copy with no connection to the specific brand voice or audience given.
- No success metric or measurement plan anywhere in the response.
- Ignoring a stated budget or resource constraint.
- Recommending tactics that misrepresent the product or service.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Concrete, measurable KPI tied directly to the stated goal.
- Channel choice explicitly justified against the stated audience.
- Honest acknowledgment of what needs testing or data before scaling spend.
- Messaging reflects the specific brand voice and constraints given, not a generic template.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

You lead marketing for a mid-size architecture firm that designs K-12 school facilities. A
rival firm, Meridian Design Group, just announced a 15% fee discount on new institutional
(K-12) contracts. In the past 6 months your firm lost 5 institutional bids to Meridian. Sales
debrief notes from those 5 losses show the client's stated reason for choosing Meridian in
each case:

- Loss 1: "Meridian promised a design-development timeline 20% shorter than yours."
- Loss 2: "Their fee was lower, but honestly the deciding factor was they could start sooner
  and finish design faster -- our board wanted to break ground this fiscal year."
- Loss 3: "Price was a factor, we went with the lower bid."
- Loss 4: "We needed the design phase done fast to hit a grant-funding construction deadline,
  and Meridian's schedule commitment was the only one that worked."
- Loss 5: "Their proposed schedule was significantly faster than yours, which mattered more to
  the board than the fee difference."

Write a competitive-response memo to firm leadership. Quantify, from the debrief notes, how
many of the 5 losses were actually price-driven versus schedule-driven, and use that to argue
for or against matching Meridian's 15% fee discount. Recommend a concrete response strategy.

### Executive Summary

Meridian Design Group has announced a 15% fee discount on K-12 contracts. Based on an analysis of our five institutional losses to Meridian over the past six months, **I recommend we do NOT match this discount.**

Our data indicates we are losing to Meridian primarily on schedule performance, not price. Matching a fee discount would erode our margins without addressing the root cause of our losses. Instead, we should deploy a "Schedule-to-Value" response that protects our pricing power while neutralizing Meridian's competitive advantage.

---

### 1. Loss Analysis: Price vs. Schedule

A review of the sales debrief notes from the five losses reveals a distinct pattern. While Meridian is attacking on price, our clients are choosing them based on speed.

| Loss # | Client Stated Reason | Primary Driver | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Meridian promised a timeline 20% shorter than yours. | **Schedule** | 100% Schedule |
| **2** | Fee was lower, but deciding factor was start sooner/finish faster; board wanted to break ground this fiscal year. | **Schedule** | ~70% Schedule / 30% Price |
| **3** | Price was a factor; we went with the lower bid. | **Price** | 100% Price |
| **4** | Needed design phase done fast to hit grant-funding construction deadline; Meridian's schedule was the only one that worked. | **Schedule** | 100% Schedule |
| **5** | Schedule significantly faster; mattered more to the board than fee difference. | **Schedule** | ~80% Schedule / 20% Price |

**Quantified Results:**
*   **Schedule-Driven Losses:** 4 of 5 (80%)
*   **Pure Price-Driven Losses:** 1 of 5 (20%)
*   **Note on Loss 3:** Even in the sole price-driven loss, there is no mention of schedule as a disqualifier. In losses 2, 3, and 5, the clients acknowledge price but explicitly state that **board priorities (fiscal year deadlines, grant compliance)** override fee savings.

### 2. Strategic Argument: Why We Should NOT Match the Discount

**A. We Would Be Fighting the Wrong War**
Meridian's discount assumes price is the marginal factor. Our data proves that in 80% of our losses, the client would likely have chosen us even with a fee premium if we could match their schedule. By discounting, we leave money on the table without fixing the variable that actually determines the win/loss.

**B. Margin Erosion with Low Conversion Probability**
If we match a 15% fee discount, we assume this closes the gap. However, Loss 1 shows our timeline is already 20% slower than Meridian's. If Meridian's speed comes from a process advantage that is subsidized by cash, matching their fee only deepens the hole without restoring our schedule competitiveness. We risk a scenario where we discount 15% and still lose to Meridian because they are faster.

**C. The "Schedule Premium" is Real to Clients**
The debriefs highlight that clients are losing access to grant funding and fiscal year construction windows when their design phases drag. For our clients, a delay in design can mean the loss of millions in state/federal funding. **Speed has a dollar value to the client that often exceeds a 15% fee discount.** We must articulate this value rather than devaluing our services.

**D. Signaling Risk**
A blanket 15% discount signals desperation and can trigger a race to the bottom that mid-size firms rarely win against firms willing to burn capital for market share. It also risks devaluing our reputation for quality and stability in the K-12 sector.

### 3. Recommended Response Strategy: "Operation: Accelerate"

We will hold our fee structure but aggressively pivot our competitive posture to address the schedule gap. The goal is to make Meridian's schedule commitment a moot point.

**Recommendation 1: Internal Audit of the "20% Lag"**
*   **Action:** Commission a cross-functional review (Design Ops, Project Architects, PMs) to diagnose why we are losing 20% of design time compared to Meridian.
*   **Output:** Identify bottlenecks. Is this a staffing model issue? Technology gap? Review protocol inefficiency?
*   **Timeline:** Complete audit within 30 days; implement process fixes within 60 days.

**Recommendation 2: Deploy a "Schedule Guarantee" Proposal Model**
*   **Action:** For all institutional bids over the next 90 days, replace the standard proposal format with one that leads with the schedule.
*   **Tactic:** Offer a **Performance-Linked Schedule Commitment**.
    *   *Proposal Language:* "We guarantee design development completion by [Date]. If we miss this milestone, [concrete penalty, e.g., reduced fee for that phase or free acceleration resources]."
    *   *Effect:* This transfers risk to us and mirrors the certainty Meridian is selling, but backed by our proposal, not just their verbal promise.

**Recommendation 3: Reframe Marketing to "Grant-Ready" Messaging**
*   **Action:** Update institutional marketing collateral and pitch decks to focus on client outcomes related to speed.
*   **New Taglines:** "Design phases that hit your grant deadlines," "Groundbreaking by [Month], guaranteed."
*   **Value Calc:** Include a "Cost of Delay" calculator in proposals showing the financial impact of missing a grant cycle vs. our fee. This positions our fee as an investment in securing funding, not just design services.

**Recommendation 4: Tactical "Value-Add" Pricing (No Discount)**
*   **Action:** If a specific bid requires a financial lever, use value-adds instead of fee reduction.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Phased billing tied to schedule milestones (improves client cash flow).
    *   Included "Fast-Track Review" services (third-party design review) at no cost to keep the project on the accelerated path.
    *   This protects our top-line revenue while addressing client budget concerns.

### 4. Financial Impact Estimate

*   **Risk of Matching:** If we match the 15% discount, we reduce revenue by 15% on institutional work. With institutional contracts averaging $X million in fees, a 15% haircut represents significant margin loss across the pipeline.
*   **Benefit of Strategy:** By holding fees and winning bids through schedule differentiation, we retain full margin. Even if we win only 50% of these competitive bids (up from our current loss rate), the revenue retained is vastly superior to winning 100% of bids at a 15% discount.

### 5. Conclusion & Immediate Actions

Meridian is trying to win on price, but the data shows they are winning on time. Matching their fee discount validates their strategy and punishes our margins.

**I request leadership approval to:**
1.  **Reject** the 15% fee discount proposal.
2.  **Authorize** the "Operation: Accelerate" initiative, including the Design Ops audit and the development of the Schedule Guarantee proposal template.
3.  **Direct** the BD team to reframe all upcoming institutional pitches around schedule certainty and grant-cycle protection.

By responding with speed guarantees and process improvements rather than price cuts, we defend our market position and our profitability.
