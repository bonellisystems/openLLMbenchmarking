# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

## Investigation Plan: Pregnancy Discrimination / Retaliation Complaint

**Case Classification:** Potential violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act (Pregnancy Discrimination), the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA), and the County's Equal Employment Opportunity policy.
**Priority Level:** High (Active pregnancy, active retaliation risk, direct supervisor accused).

---

### 1. Impartial Investigator Selection

**Selection Criteria:**
*   **No Chain-of-Command Conflict:** The investigator must not report to, nor be in a position of authority over, the alleged supervisor. If the supervisor is in a high-ranking leadership position (e.g., Department Head), an internal investigator may be conflicted.
*   **Competency:** The investigator must be trained in administrative investigations, EEOC/PWFA protocols, and interviewing techniques.
*   **Recommendation:** Assign a senior HR Business Partner or a designated EEO/Compliance Officer with prior investigation experience. If the supervisor's rank suggests potential influence over HR personnel, engage an **external third-party investigator** to ensure perceived and actual neutrality.

**Conflict Check:** Before assignment, verify the investigator has no prior professional or personal relationship with the complainant, the respondent, or any potential witnesses.

---

### 2. Evidence to Gather

To build a complete factual record, the following evidence should be secured immediately:

**Documentary Evidence:**
*   **The Complaint:** The original written letter (already secured).
*   **Project Records:** Timeline of the "vendor-logistics project," meeting minutes, email chains, and the official reassignment memo. Verify if the reassignment coincided with the disclosure of pregnancy.
*   **Performance History:** The employee's performance reviews, attendance records, and any prior informal feedback to establish a baseline (looking for regression or sudden change).
*   **HR Leave/Accommodation Files:** Any prior requests for leave, lactation breaks, or accommodations.
*   **Supervisor's File:** Performance reviews, prior complaints, and disciplinary records to check for patterns.

**Witness Identification:**
*   Identify colleagues who worked on the vendor-logistics project.
*   Identify anyone who may have overheard the verbal comments.
*   Identify any other employees who interacted with the supervisor regarding the pregnancy.

---

### 3. Interview Sequence

Interviews must be conducted individually, in private, and documented with verbatim notes.

**Phase 1: The Complainant (Employee)**
*   **Timing:** Conduct within 24–48 hours of receiving the complaint.
*   **Purpose:** Secure the employee's account while memories are fresh. This establishes the baseline facts without the presence of the accused, which reduces the risk of intimidation.
*   **Format:** Open-ended questions. Allow the employee to provide a full narrative. Do not lead or suggest answers.

**Phase 2: The Respondent (Supervisor)**
*   **Timing:** Conducted *before* interviewing witnesses.
*   **Purpose:** To give the supervisor a chance to explain their side while the evidence is still fresh. Interviewing witnesses first risks them being coached by the supervisor.
*   **Notice:** The supervisor must be informed that a formal investigation is underway against them regarding workplace conduct. (See Confidentiality Section for nuance).

**Phase 3: Witnesses**
*   **Timing:** After both the complainant and respondent have been interviewed.
*   **Purpose:** To corroborate or contradict statements.
*   **Protection:** Witnesses should be informed that their participation is voluntary and that retaliation against them is also prohibited.

**Phase 4: Review & Conclusion**
*   Analyze all statements against documentary evidence.
*   Determine if there is a pattern of behavior or isolated incidents.
*   Draft a written report with findings and recommendations for HR/Director review.

---

### 4. Interim Measures to Protect the Employee

While the investigation is pending, the County has an affirmative duty to prevent further harm and retaliation.

**A. Separation of Parties (Immediate)**
*   **Action:** Place a "No Contact" directive between the employee and the supervisor.
*   **Implementation:** If they share an office, relocate the employee to a different workspace. If they share a team, temporarily reassign the employee to a neutral role or provide remote work options (if available).
*   **Rationale:** Prevents the supervisor from making further verbal comments or influencing the employee's work environment.

**B. Retaliation Protection**
*   **Action:** Issue a verbal and written reminder to all staff (specifically the supervisor's direct reports) that retaliation for filing a complaint is a fireable offense.
*   **Action:** Monitor the employee's schedule, assignments, and interactions for any signs of retaliation (e.g., sudden schedule changes, negative reviews, exclusion from meetings).

**C. Light Duty / Accommodation**
*   **Action:** If the employee requests it, evaluate reasonable accommodations under the PWFA (e.g., more frequent breaks, reduced physical workload).
*   **Action:** Do not unilaterally lower her workload; accommodate her request rather than reassigning the work without consent.

**D. Timeline Monitoring**
*   **Action:** Set a strict deadline for the investigation to conclude (e.g., 30 days) and communicate this to the employee to reduce anxiety and demonstrate good faith.

---

### 5. Response to the Confidentiality Request

**The Core Promise:**
HR must respond to the employee with transparency and empathy. The following script/response structure is recommended:

> *"We take your request for confidentiality very seriously. We will protect your identity to the greatest extent possible and will limit who knows you have made this complaint. However, I need to be honest about what we can and cannot promise you."*

**What HR Can Promise:**
1.  **Identity Protection:** Your name and medical information will not be shared with coworkers, your supervisor, or other departments without a compelling legal or safety reason.
2.  **Process Privacy:** The investigation process will not be publicized. You will not be asked to participate in disciplinary meetings that could expose you.
3.  **Retaliation Ban:** If anyone retaliates against you because you filed this complaint, it is illegal and will result in disciplinary action. We will monitor this closely.

**What HR Cannot Promise (and Why):**
1.  **That the Supervisor Won't Be Told an Investigation Exists:**
    *   **Why:** Under federal employment law (Title VII) and due process, the accused party (the supervisor) has the right to know that a formal investigation is occurring so they can respond to the allegations. An investigation where the accused is kept in the dark is a "sham investigation" and could expose the County to further liability if findings are overturned in court.
    *   **Mitigation:** HR can inform the supervisor that "a formal complaint has been received regarding workplace conduct" without immediately disclosing the specific nature of the pregnancy claims until the investigation is complete or legally required. This balances the employee's safety with the supervisor's right to due process.

2.  **That No One Will Find Out:**
    *   **Why:** HR staff involved in the investigation (the investigator, the HR manager, the Director) will know. This is necessary to manage the investigation, document the process, and ensure the supervisor is held accountable.
    *   **Mitigation:** The number of people who know is minimized to only those directly involved in the investigation.

3.  **That the Supervisor Won't Be Disciplined:**
    *   **Why:** HR cannot promise a specific outcome regarding the supervisor's employment. We can only promise a fair process.
    *   **Mitigation:** The employee is informed that findings are based solely on evidence and will result in appropriate action.

**Why This Distinction Matters:**
If HR promises 100% confidentiality and cannot keep it, the County loses the trust of the complainant and potentially exposes itself to EEOC claims for failing to investigate properly. If HR *does* share the investigation with the supervisor, it is a necessary legal safeguard. The compromise is to delay the *specific details* to the supervisor until the investigation concludes, allowing the supervisor to be notified of the investigation generally without being told the employee fears pregnancy-based retaliation.

**Documentation of the Request:**
The employee's request for confidentiality must be recorded in the investigation file verbatim. This demonstrates that HR made a good-faith effort to protect the employee and that any disclosure was necessary for the investigation, not a willful breach of privacy.

# Investigation Plan for Pregnancy Discrimination Complaint

## **Investigator Selection**

**Impartial Selection Process:**
- Assign an investigator from a different department (preferably HR or legal) who has no prior relationship with the complainant or supervisor
- Ensure the investigator has no personal or professional connections that could compromise objectivity
- Provide the investigator with training on pregnancy discrimination laws and investigation procedures

## **Interview Sequence**

**Phase 1: Initial Assessment (Day 1-2)**
- Interview complainant privately to document facts and concerns
- Interview supervisor separately to hear their perspective
- Interview the colleague who received the project reassignment

**Phase 2: Evidence Gathering (Day 3-5)**
- Interview witnesses who may have observed the supervisor's comments
- Review relevant documentation (project assignments, communications, performance records)
- Interview the supervisor's direct reports if relevant

**Phase 3: Final Review (Day 6-7)**
- Consolidate findings
- Review legal requirements and potential remedies
- Prepare final report

## **Evidence to Gather**

**Documentary Evidence:**
- Written communications (emails, memos) between supervisor and employee
- Project assignment records and timelines
- Performance evaluations and reviews
- Any previous complaints or disciplinary records
- Company policies on pregnancy accommodation and discrimination

**Witness Statements:**
- Colleagues who may have heard discriminatory comments
- Anyone who observed the project reassignment timing
- Any other relevant parties

**Employee Documentation:**
- Medical documentation of pregnancy status
- Records of work performance before and after pregnancy disclosure
- Notes of specific comments and incidents

## **Interim Measures to Protect Employee**

**Immediate Protections:**
- Maintain the employee's current work assignments and status
- Ensure no adverse employment actions based on pregnancy
- Provide temporary accommodation if needed for pregnancy-related duties
- Monitor for any retaliation or hostile work environment
- Establish clear communication channels between investigator and employee

**Confidentiality Considerations**

**What Can Be Guaranteed:**
- Investigation process will be handled with utmost discretion
- Information will be shared only with individuals directly involved in the investigation
- Employee's name will not be disclosed to the supervisor or other coworkers
- All investigation materials will be kept in a secure, confidential file

**What Cannot Be Guaranteed:**
- Complete confidentiality to the supervisor - this is a legal requirement that cannot be waived
- Complete anonymity from all county employees - some reporting requirements may necessitate disclosure
- Absolute protection from all workplace retaliation - while we will investigate and address any retaliation, we cannot guarantee protection from all possible consequences

**Legal Requirements That Limit Confidentiality:**
- **EEOC Requirements**: Federal law requires reporting of discrimination complaints to appropriate agencies
- **Employment Law**: Supervisors have a right to know about workplace issues affecting team performance
- **Institutional Obligations**: County may have internal reporting requirements for employment discrimination
- **Due Process**: Fair investigation requires full disclosure to all parties involved in the process

**Why These Limitations Exist:**
The confidentiality request, while understandable, conflicts with legal requirements that mandate reporting and investigation processes. The law protects employees from retaliation, but cannot override the need for legitimate investigation procedures. The goal is to investigate thoroughly while protecting the complainant's interests to the maximum extent legally possible.

- **Week 1**: Initial interviews and evidence gathering
- **Week 2**: Analysis and report preparation
- **Week 3**: Final report and recommended actions
- **Ongoing**: Implementation of corrective measures and monitoring

2. Begin documentation and evidence collection
3. Implement protective measures for the employee
4. Communicate investigation process to all parties

This investigation plan is designed to address potential violations of the Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA) and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, specifically focusing on allegations of harassment and retaliation.

### 1. Selection of Investigator
To ensure impartiality and protect the integrity of the process, the investigator must be someone who is perceived as neutral and has no reporting relationship to the supervisor in question.

*   **Recommended Selection:** An external, third-party investigator or a high-level HR Director from a different department/division.
*   **Rationale:** Because the complainant has expressed a direct fear of career retaliation, an internal investigator within the same department may be viewed as biased. An external investigator minimizes "organizational politics" and provides the most robust defense against claims of a "sham investigation."

### 2. Interview Sequence
The order of interviews is critical to prevent witness collusion and to allow for a thorough follow-up.

1.  **The Complainant:** To establish the scope of the investigation. We need specific dates, times, exact quotes, locations of comments, and any witnesses to the verbal comments or the project reassignment.
2.  **Witnesses:** Individuals who may have overheard the comments or were present during the conversation regarding the project reassignment.
3.  **The Respondent (Supervisor):** Once the allegations are fully mapped, the supervisor must be presented with the specific allegations to allow them to respond. This provides them due process.
4.  **Follow-up Interviews:** To clarify any discrepancies or inconsistencies identified during the first three stages.

### 3. Evidence to Gather
The investigation will rely on a combination of testimonial and documentary evidence to determine if a policy violation occurred.

*   **Testimonial Evidence:** Detailed statements from the complainant, the respondent, and any identified witnesses.
*   **Documentary Evidence (The "Paper Trail"):**
    *   **Performance Records:** The complainant’s recent performance reviews to see if there were any documented performance issues *prior* to the pregnancy disclosure.
    *   **Project Documentation:** Documentation regarding the county-fair vendor-logistics project (why it was reassigned, who was chosen to take it over, and the official reason given).
    *   **Communication Logs:** Emails, Slack messages, or texts that may contain comments regarding the pregnancy or the workload.
    *   **Comparative Data:** Examining how the county handles project reassignments for other employees to determine if the supervisor’s actions were consistent with standard operating procedure or if they were an outlier (disparate treatment).

### 4. Interim Measures
The goal is to protect the employee from retaliation without inadvertently punishing the complainant (which could itself be seen as a retaliatory action).

*   **No-Retaliation Directive:** The respondent must be formally instructed, in writing, that any form of retaliation—direct or subtle—is strictly prohibited and is grounds for immediate termination.
*   **Reporting Structure Adjustment:** If the complainant's fear of daily interaction is paralyzing her work, the county may offer a temporary change in reporting structure (e.g., reporting to a different manager) while the investigation is ongoing. **Note:** This must be done in consultation with the complainant to ensure she does not feel she is being "demoted" or "punished" by the move.
*   **Monitoring:** HR should conduct periodic check-ins with the complainant to ensure no subtle shifts in workload or treatment have occurred during the investigation.

### 5. Addressing the Confidentiality Request
This is the most delicate part of the initial intake. The HR representative must be empathetic but legally and procedurally honest.

**The Response Strategy:**
"I understand your fear, and I want you to know that your privacy is a priority for us. I will protect your identity to the maximum extent possible by sharing your information only with those who have a strict 'need to know' to complete this investigation.

However, **I cannot promise 100% confidentiality.** There are two reasons for this:
1.  **Due Process:** In order for this investigation to be fair and legally sound, your supervisor has the right to know the specific nature of the allegations against him so that he can respond to them. If we don't tell him, the investigation is incomplete.
2.  **Fact-Finding:** To determine the truth, I may need to speak with colleagues who may have witnessed these events.

What I *can* promise is that we will treat this information with extreme discretion, and that the county has a strict anti-retaliation policy. If you experience any change in your work environment or feel you are being treated differently after this meeting, you must report it to me immediately."

**Why this approach?**
*   **Honesty avoids litigation:** If you promise 100% confidentiality and then have to reveal her identity to the supervisor, the employee can claim they were misled, potentially adding a "failure to protect" or "bad faith" element to their legal claim.
*   **Managing Expectations:** It sets a realistic boundary while reinforcing the company's commitment to non-retaliation.

### 5.1  What the Employee Asked For  
> “Please keep this 100 % confidential — I don’t want ANYONE, including my supervisor, to know I filed this, or I’m afraid of what will happen to my career here.”

### 5.2  What the Law Allows  
| Legal Principle | Practical Implication |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Confidentiality vs. Investigation** | The agency must conduct a **fair, thorough investigation**. This often requires disclosing the complaint to the **alleged harasser** and to witnesses. Absolute secrecy is **not legally possible** without jeopardizing the investigation. |
| **Statutory Obligations** | Under Title VII and state anti‑discrimination statutes, employers are required to **investigate and remediate**. The investigation file is a **record** that may be subject to discovery if a lawsuit arises. |
| **Need‑to‑Know Principle** | Only individuals who have a **legitimate role** in the investigation (HR investigators, legal counsel, witnesses) may be told the details. All other staff must be kept out of the loop. |
| **Retaliation Prohibition** | Any action that **reveals** the complainant’s filing to others in a way that could cause retaliation is itself a violation of law. |

### 5.3  How to Communicate the Limits  
1. **Acknowledge the Request** – “We understand how important confidentiality is to you, and we will protect your privacy to the fullest extent allowed by law.”  
2. **Explain What Must Be Shared** – “In order to conduct a fair investigation, we must share the details of your complaint with the person you are reporting about, any witnesses we need to interview, and certain members of the HR team who need the information to manage the process.”  
3. **Set Clear Expectations** – “Only those individuals who are directly involved in the investigation will be told what you have shared. No one else — including your supervisor — will receive this information unless it is required by law or a court order.”  
4. **Document the Acknowledgment** – Have the employee sign a **confidentiality acknowledgment form** that records she was informed of the limits. Keep this form in the case file.  
5. **Re‑affirm Protection Against Retaliation** – “Any attempt by anyone to disclose the contents of this complaint to others, or to retaliate against you, is a violation of county policy and will be investigated and disciplined.”  

### 5.4  Sample Script for the Initial Conversation  

> *“Thank you for bringing this to our attention. We take allegations of pregnancy‑related discrimination very seriously, and we are committed to protecting your privacy. While we cannot guarantee that **no one** will ever learn of your complaint — because a thorough investigation requires sharing the information with a limited group of people who must know in order to complete the facts — we will only disclose it to those who have a direct role in the investigation (e.g., the investigator, legal counsel, and any witnesses). All of these individuals are under a legal duty to keep the matter confidential. We will also take steps to ensure that no one uses this information to treat you unfairly. If anyone tries to reveal your complaint to others or to retaliate against you, that will be treated as a separate violation and will be investigated immediately.”*  

### 5.5  What the Employee May Still Request  
- **Written confirmation** of the confidentiality safeguards.  
- **Assurance** that any disclosure will be limited to the smallest group possible.  
- **Escalation** to an external investigator if she believes internal confidentiality cannot be protected.  

*The investigator should note that if the employee continues to request absolute secrecy after being told the limits, the agency must still proceed with the investigation; refusal to cooperate may be considered in assessing the merits of the claim, but it cannot be used as a basis for retaliation.*

5.  **No Reassignment of Employee:** **CRITICAL.** Do not move the employee to a different department or supervisor at this stage. Reassigning the employee could be construed as retaliation or an admission of guilt. Keep the employee in their current role with the interim controls above.

---

#### 5. Interview Sequence
*Order is critical to prevent witness contamination.*

1.  **Complainant (Employee):**
    *   Goal: Gather detailed facts, timeline, impact, and evidence.
    *   Focus: Specific dates/times of comments; exact wording; project status prior to pregnancy announcement; criteria for colleague selection; any other instances of bias.
    *   *Note:* Allow the employee to bring evidence. Record the interview per county policy.

2.  **Witnesses:**
    *   **The Colleague (Project Recipient):**
        *   Goal: Determine the basis for reassignment.
        *   Questions: Who requested the reassignment? Why were you chosen? Do you know of any legitimate reasons the original employee was removed? Did the supervisor mention the pregnancy to you?
    *   **Other Witnesses (Optional):**
        *   Any employees who may have overheard the supervisor's comments.
        *   *Caution:* Interviewing witnesses requires discretion to avoid alerting the supervisor prematurely.

3.  **Supervisor (Accused):**
    *   Goal: Obtain their perspective and document their response.
    *   Approach: Present allegations neutrally. "We are conducting an inquiry regarding your interactions with [Employee] and the reassignment of the County-Fair project. We are asking for your account of events."
    *   Focus: Reason for reassignment; awareness of pregnancy; rationale for comments regarding workload; management of the project transition.

4.  **Supervisor's Manager / Operations Lead (If applicable):**
    *   Goal: Verify organizational context, project priorities, and resource allocation.
    *   Focus: Was the project reassignment approved at a higher level? Are there legitimate operational reasons for the change?

---

#### 6. Evidence to Gather
**Documentary Evidence:**
*   **Email/Communication Chain:** Search for all communications regarding the project reassignment. Look for timestamps, requestors, and approvals.
*   **Project Files:** Scope of work, budget, and assignment logs for the County-Fair vendor project.
*   **Performance Records:** Employee's performance reviews prior to pregnancy announcement. Look for consistency or sudden changes.
*   **Workload Analysis:** Documentation of the employee's actual workload prior to the reassignment to assess the validity of the supervisor's concerns.
*   **Meeting Minutes:** Any relevant department meetings where the pregnancy was discussed or the project was reassigned.
*   **Policy Documents:** County policies on pregnancy accommodation, project assignment criteria, and anti-harassment training.

**Digital Forensics (If applicable):**
*   Review chat logs (Slack/Teams) if the "pop" comment was made in a written format or if there are logs of project assignment requests.

**Corroboration:**
*   Timeline analysis: Create a visual timeline of the pregnancy announcement, the reassignment, and the comments. The "two weeks ago" link is the primary indicator of causation.

---

#### 7. Next Steps & Outcome
1.  **Analysis:** The investigator will compare statements, review evidence, and assess credibility.
2.  **Finding:** Determine if the conduct constitutes a violation of Title VII/PDA, state pregnancy discrimination laws, and county policy.
3.  **Reporting:**
    *   **To Employee:** Share the outcome, the findings regarding their specific concerns, and any remedies. *Note: The employee should know the general result (e.g., "Conduct upheld," "Insufficient evidence") without necessarily being told exactly what actions were taken against the supervisor if privacy is a factor, though transparency is generally preferred.*
    *   **To Management:** Report findings and recommend disciplinary action if violations are found.
4.  **Remediation:** Depending on findings, remedies may include reassignment of the project to the employee, supervisor training, counseling, or disciplinary action.
