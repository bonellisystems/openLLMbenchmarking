## Anchors

# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

Build the investigation plan: impartial investigator selection, interview sequence, evidence to
gather, and any interim measures to protect the employee during the investigation. Specifically
address how to respond to the confidentiality request -- be honest about what can and can't be
promised, and explain why.

# Investigation Plan: Pregnancy Discrimination and Harassment Complaint

## 1. Response to Confidentiality Request (Immediate Action)
**The Core Constraint:** The employee demands "100% confidentiality" (including hiding the fact that the complaint was filed from the supervisor).

**HR Response Strategy:**
We must manage expectations immediately. HR **cannot promise absolute confidentiality** in a formal investigation. However, HR can promise **strict "need-to-know" confidentiality.**

**Explanation to Provide to the Employee:**
*   **Why "100%" is impossible:**
    1.  **Due Process:** To conduct a fair investigation, the accused supervisor must be notified of the allegations (at least the general nature) to provide an explanation. Without this, the investigation is legally vulnerable and potentially void.
    2.  **Fact-Finding:** To corroborate the claim, investigators may need to speak with other team members regarding the project reassignment or the supervisor's management style.
    3.  **Public Sector Mandate:** As a county entity, we are bound by open records laws and civil service rules that may require documentation of adverse actions and investigations.
*   **What We *Can* Promise:**
    1.  **Limited Disclosure:** Information will be shared only with the investigator, HR leadership, and the supervisor (as necessary for due process).
    2.  **Anti-Retaliation:** The supervisor will be explicitly warned that retaliation against the employee is grounds for termination.
    3.  **Identity Protection:** In witness interviews, the specific identity of the complainant will be protected where feasible (e.g., "A team member raised concerns about...").

**Action Item:** HR must send a written acknowledgment immediately stating: *"We take this seriously. We cannot guarantee absolute silence due to legal due process requirements, but we will protect your identity to the maximum extent possible and strictly enforce our anti-retaliation policy."*

---

## 2. Impartial Investigator Selection
Given the sensitivity of a pregnancy discrimination claim involving a direct supervisor, the internal HR office should not investigate itself if there is any conflict of interest.

*   **Preferred Option:** **External Investigator.**
    *   *Reasoning:* If the County HR Director is the supervisor's peer, or if the HR office is small, an external investigator ensures neutrality and reduces the risk of perceived bias. It also protects the employee from fear of internal politics.
*   **Internal Option (If External is not feasible):** **Senior HR Manager / Compliance Officer.**
    *   *Criteria:* Must be at least two levels above the accused supervisor. Must have no prior personal or professional conflict with the supervisor.
*   **Union Representation:** Ensure the investigator coordinates with the County’s Union Rep if the employee is unionized.

---

## 3. Interim Measures (Protection During Investigation)
*Goal: Prevent retaliation and stabilize the employee's position while the investigation is pending.*

1.  **Anti-Retaliation Notice:** Issue a formal written warning to the supervisor regarding the complaint. Remind them that retaliation is a violation of County policy and federal law (Title VII/PDA).
2.  **Operational Freeze on Project:**
    *   Since the employee was removed from the project immediately after pregnancy disclosure, the reassignment should be reviewed.
    *   **Measure:** Suspend the finality of the reassignment. If possible, restore the employee to the project *temporarily* until the investigation concludes, or ensure the colleague is not tasked to replace the employee in a way that suggests replacement.
3.  **Monitoring:** HR should schedule weekly check-ins with the employee (without requiring them to report on the investigation itself) to monitor the supervisor's behavior.
4.  **EAP Referral:** Offer access to the Employee Assistance Program to support the employee's mental well-being during the stress of the investigation.

## 4. Evidence to Gather
The investigation must establish a timeline and a "pattern of conduct" (for harassment) or "adverse action" (for discrimination).

**Documentary Evidence:**
1.  **Email/Chat Logs:** Search the supervisor and employee's communication channels (Slack, Teams, Email) for the specific comments ("can you handle this," "when's your last day").
2.  **Project Assignments:** Retrieve the project charter or task assignment logs for the "County-Fair Vendor-Logistics Project" showing the change in leadership *immediately* following the pregnancy disclosure date.
3.  **Performance Reviews:** Gather the employee's last 2 years of performance reviews to establish a history of competence (to rebut the "can you handle this" claim).
4.  **Medical Accommodation Requests:** Verify if the pregnancy disclosure was formalized through HR or was verbal (this affects privacy protocols).
5.  **HR Records:** Check the supervisor's prior complaints or history of discrimination claims.

**Testimonial Evidence:**
1.  **The Supervisor:** Must be interviewed to hear their justification for the project reassignment and their explanation of the comments.
2.  **Project Colleagues:** Colleagues who worked with the employee on the project to verify competence and observe the supervisor's comments.
3.  **Other Witnesses:** Other employees who may have heard the supervisor make similar comments to others.

## 5. Interview Sequence
The order of interviews is critical to prevent "coaching" and ensure evidence is preserved.

1.  **Complainant (Employee):**
    *   *Timing:* Immediate (within 24 hours).
    *   *Purpose:* Get the full narrative, specific dates, times, and locations of the comments. Clarify the timeline of the pregnancy disclosure vs. project reassignment.
    *   *Note:* Since the employee submitted a written complaint, this interview is for nuance, but it must be recorded (with consent).

2.  **Respondent (Supervisor):**
    *   *Timing:* Within 48 hours of Employee Interview.

3.  **Project Team Witnesses:**
    *   *Timing:* After Supervisor Interview.
    *   *Purpose:* Ask about their observation of the project leadership change. Ask if they heard the supervisor make comments about pregnancy or workload.
    *   *Anonymity:* Assure them that their identity will not be disclosed to the supervisor.

4.  **HR File Review (Post-Interviews):**

**To:** County HR Director / Legal Counsel

### 1. Response to Confidentiality Request
**Critical Communication to Employee:**
Before proceeding, HR must meet with the employee to manage expectations regarding confidentiality. This is the most sensitive aspect of the intake.

**What HR CAN Promise:**
*   **Strict Need-to-Know:** Information regarding the investigation will be restricted to the investigator, HR leadership, and Legal Counsel. It will not be shared with department staff, peers, or the general public.
*   **Anti-Retaliation Protection:** The County has a zero-tolerance policy regarding retaliation. You are explicitly protected from punishment, schedule changes, workload increases, or hostile treatment for filing this complaint. Any evidence of retaliation will result in immediate disciplinary action against the retaliator.
*   **Safe Reporting Channels:** You have access to the County's EAP (Employee Assistance Program) for confidential counseling support during this process.
*   **Anonymity for Witnesses:** If other employees are interviewed, their identities can often be withheld in reports where possible, depending on legal requirements.

**What HR CANNOT Promise:**
*   **100% Secrecy:** We **cannot** promise that your supervisor will never know you filed this complaint.
*   **No Contact with Colleagues:** We **cannot** promise that the colleague who received the reassignment will not be interviewed.

**Why These Limits Exist:**
*   **Due Process & Fairness:** An investigation requires that the Respondent (the supervisor) is informed of the allegations against them. They have a right to know the nature of the complaint and the basis for any potential disciplinary action. Without this, the investigation cannot be considered fair or thorough.
*   **Investigative Necessity:** To verify the facts, the investigator may need to speak with the colleague who received the project to understand the business rationale for the reassignment.
*   **Preventing Further Harm:** If the allegations are true, the supervisor needs to be made aware of their conduct to stop the behavior immediately. Furthermore, if the supervisor remains unaware of a formal complaint, they cannot be held accountable, and the County fails in its duty to provide a discrimination-free workplace.
*   **Legal Requirement:** In the event of litigation, a court or agency (like the EEOC) will review the investigation. If the supervisor was not notified, the investigation may be deemed deficient, and the County could face severe penalties for failure to correct known discrimination.

**Mitigation Strategy:**
While we cannot hide the investigation, we will issue a **written directive** to the supervisor explicitly stating that this is a formal investigation, outlining appropriate conduct, and strictly prohibiting any adverse actions or discussions regarding the employee's status.
