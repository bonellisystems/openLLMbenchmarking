## 10 -- Frontier

Implementation steps are sequenced with a clear first action, change-management or rollout risk is addressed, and resourcing constraints given in the prompt are respected rather than assumed away.

## 7 -- Strong

Example character: a correct fix for the stated bottleneck with a named metric but no baseline value given.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic process-improvement guidance ("streamline the workflow," "reduce handoffs") that is directionally correct but not tied to the specific constraint described in the prompt.
Example character: general efficiency advice that would apply to almost any operational bottleneck.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misidentifies the bottleneck, or proposes a process that ignores a stated hard constraint (headcount, budget, system limitation), with no measurable KPI anywhere in the response.
Example character: a redesign that requires more staff despite the prompt stating headcount is fixed.

### Redesigned SOP: Zone-Sorted Fulfillment (No Re-Keying)

**Current State:** Order Received $\rightarrow$ Staff manually re-keys order to paper pick sheet (Error Point 1) $\rightarrow$ Staff pulls items from unsorted queue (Inefficiency Point 1) $\rightarrow$ Packs item (Error Point 2) $\rightarrow$ Ships.

**New State:** Order Received $\rightarrow$ System generates PDF pick list $\rightarrow$ Manifest is printed and sorted by warehouse zone (Eliminates Error Point 1) $\rightarrow$ Staff picks sequentially by zone (Eliminates Inefficiency Point 1) $\rightarrow$ Visual SKU verification at pack station (Eliminates Error Point 2) $\rightarrow$ Ships.

#### The New Process Steps

1.  **System-Generated Manifest:** At the end of each batch (e.g., every 2 hours), the order system is queried, and a physical pick list is printed directly from the screen. **No manual data entry.** The printed list contains the SKU, description, and quantity required.
2.  **Zone Sort at Intake:** Immediately upon printing, the manifest is sorted by warehouse zone (e.g., Zone A = Tops, Zone B = Bottoms) using pre-labeled bins or a physical rack system. Pickers follow the bins, not the order sequence.
3.  **Sequential Zone Picking:** Staff pick items strictly within their assigned zone, following the printed list. They move to the next zone only when the current zone is cleared.
4.  **Visual Verification at Pack:** Before sealing the box, the picker must match the physical item's size tag or barcode to the printed manifest. They circle the line item on the sheet to confirm the match.

### KPI Targets

| Metric | Baseline | Target | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time-to-Ship** | 3.2 days | **2.0 days** | Zone sorting eliminates wasted travel time between aisles; batch printing eliminates the 1–2 hour lag of manual re-keying. |
| **Wrong-Item Rate** | 4.5% | **<0.5%** | Removing the re-keying step eliminates the primary cause of SKU transposition; visual verification catches packing slips. |

### First Three Implementation Steps (No Capital Expenditure)

**Step 1: Eliminate the Re-Keying Step (Process Change)**
*   **Action:** Prohibit staff from manually creating paper pick sheets. Instead, designate one existing printer to generate a "Zone-Sorted Manifest" directly from the order system's export/print function every 90 minutes.
*   **Tool:** Existing office printer and order system export function.
*   **Why:** This removes the root cause of 4.5% wrong-item orders (manual data entry). It converts a 15-minute re-keying task into a 2-minute print task per batch.

**Step 2: Install Zone-Labeling and Sort Bins (Physical Layout)**
*   **Action:** Label the existing staging area with permanent zone markers (e.g., "Zone A: Tops," "Zone B: Bottoms"). Place stackable cardboard bins (sourced from internal supply or local vendors) in that area to physically segregate orders by zone before they reach the pickers.
*   **Tool:** Markers, cardboard bins, existing staging space.
*   **Why:** This forces the queue to be sorted by zone *before* picking begins, ensuring pickers walk in a straight line rather than zigzagging across the entire warehouse to find the next order.

**Step 3: Enforce a "Circle-to-Verify" Check at Pack (Quality Control)**
*   **Action:** Place a red marker and a small clipboard at every packing station. Pickers must physically circle the SKU line item on the manifest after placing the item in the box. A random spot-check (by a supervisor or peer) verifies that the circle is present and the item matches.
*   **Tool:** Red markers, clipboards (existing stock), and supervisor time.
*   **Why:** This adds a friction point to the packing process that catches remaining human error without requiring expensive barcode scanners. It shifts the verification to the moment of truth (packing) rather than relying on the earlier re-keying step.

### Redesigned Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

**Phase 1: Order Extraction & Sorting (The "Batch" Phase)**
1.  **Eliminate Re-Keying:** Instead of manual transcription, the Operations Lead will use the existing order system to "Export to PDF" or "Print Pick List" directly for all new orders. If the system lacks a direct print function, a CSV export to Excel followed by a single-click print command will be used. 
2.  **Zone Sorting:** Before any picking begins, orders will be sorted into "Zone Batches" based on product location (e.g., Zone A: Tops, Zone B: Bottoms, Zone C: Accessories). This replaces the unsorted queue.

**Phase 2: The Picking Process (The "Zone" Phase)**
1.  **Zone Picking:** Pickers are assigned to a specific warehouse zone rather than walking the whole warehouse for every order.
2.  **Direct Verification:** Pickers will use the *directly printed* pick list (not a handwritten one) to check off items. The printed SKU must be visually matched against the physical item tag.

**Phase 3: Packing & Final Audit (The "Verification" Phase)**
1.  **The Two-Point Check:** Before an item is placed in a shipping mailer, a packer (different from the picker, if headcount allows) performs a "Visual Match" between the item and the printed pick list to ensure the SKU matches exactly.

## 2.  Revised Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

> **Goal:** Move from “manual re‑key → paper pick → pick → pack” to a **digital‑first, zone‑driven, barcode‑verified** workflow while keeping the same IT footprint.

### 2.1  High‑Level Flow (new)

1. **Order Capture** – Order is entered in the existing Order Management System (OMS).  
2. **Automatic Pick‑Ticket Generation** – A macro / saved‑report in the OMS (or in a connected Excel/Access file) creates a **print‑ready pick ticket** that includes:  
   * Order #, Customer, Shipping deadline  
   * SKU, Qty, Zone code, barcode image (or SKU barcode printed on the ticket)  
3. **Zone‑Batching (Wave) – “Sort‑by‑Zone”** – At the start of each shift, the pick‑ticket list is **printed in zone order** (e.g., all Zone A tickets together, then Zone B, etc.).  
4. **Pick‑Confirm (Scan‑to‑Pick)** – Picker scans the barcode on the ticket *and* the barcode on the physical SKU. The handheld scanner logs a **pick‑confirm record** (order, SKU, qty, timestamp).  
5. **Pack‑Confirm (Scan‑to‑Pack)** – At the packing station, the packer scans the **order barcode** (or a “packing slip” barcode) and then each SKU barcode again. The system logs a **pack‑confirm** and prints a packing slip.  
6. **Ship** – Packages are staged for carrier pickup. The system automatically timestamps the ship event (or the shipper scans the final “ship” barcode).  

> **Key Point:** No hand‑written re‑keying anywhere. All “data entry” is done by **scanning** or **printing** from the existing system.

### 2.2  Detailed SOP Steps (per order)

| Step | Action | Who | Tools | Controls |
|------|--------|-----|-------|----------|
| **A** | **Print Pick Ticket** – Run the “Pick‑Ticket” macro (OMS → Excel → Print). | Shift Lead | OMS, Excel macro, thermal/laser printer | Ticket includes zone label and SKU barcode. |
| **B** | **Zone Sort** – Place printed tickets into zone trays (A‑F). | Floor Staff | Pre‑marked trays, floor signage | Visual check that each tray contains only its zone tickets. |
| **C** | **Pick‑Confirm** – For each ticket in the tray: <br>1. Scan ticket barcode (identifies order). <br>2. Scan SKU barcode on the shelf. <br>3. Confirm quantity on scanner (press “+” for each unit). | Picker | Hand‑held barcode scanner (already used for receiving). | Scanner logs “Pick‑OK”; if SKU mismatch, alarm tone + on‑screen warning; picker must re‑scan. |
| **D** | **Move to Packing** – Place picked items in a **zone‑specific staging bin** (same colour as zone). | Picker | Staging bins, colour tags | Bin label matches ticket zone – prevents cross‑zone mix‑ups. |
| **E** | **Pack‑Confirm** – At packing desk: <br>1. Scan order barcode on the ticket. <br>2. Scan each SKU barcode again. <br>3. Print packing slip (auto‑generated). | Packer | Hand‑held scanner, label printer | System flags any SKU that was not previously “Pick‑Confirmed”. |
| **F** | **Ship** – Scan final “Ship” barcode on the packing slip; package moves to carrier dock. | Shipping Clerk | Scanner, carrier dock label printer | Ship timestamp recorded – feeds into cycle‑time KPI. |
