Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The answer correctly distinguishes general legal information from jurisdiction-specific legal advice, and flags when licensed counsel review is genuinely required. Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated statute, regulation, or case citation presented as real.
- Definitive legal conclusion stated with no jurisdiction or consult-counsel caveat on a matter that clearly needs one.
- General information presented as a substitute for licensed advice on a high-stakes ask.
- No privilege or confidentiality flag when the scenario implies sensitive written communication.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Clear line drawn between general information and advice requiring licensed counsel.
- Only real, correctly-applied citations used -- never invented ones.
- Privilege or confidentiality caution raised when the scenario implies it.
- Jurisdictional caveat stated explicitly rather than assumed.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

Your client, an oilfield-services company, is comparing NDA drafts from two potential
joint-venture partners for a pipeline-monitoring data-sharing pilot involving the client's
proprietary monitoring methodology.

NDA A, Section 3 (Exclusions): "Confidential Information shall not include information that (i)
is or becomes publicly available through no fault of Receiving Party, (ii) was rightfully known
by Receiving Party prior to disclosure, (iii) is independently developed by Receiving Party
without use of or reference to the Confidential Information, or (iv) is rightfully obtained from
a third party without breach of any confidentiality obligation."
NDA A, Section 5 (Term): "This Agreement shall survive for a period of three (3) years from the
Effective Date, after which Receiving Party's obligations hereunder shall terminate."

NDA B, Section 3 (Exclusions): the same four exclusions as NDA A, plus a fifth: "(v) is retained
in the unaided mental impressions, memory, or recollection of Receiving Party's personnel who
had access to the Confidential Information (Residuals), and Receiving Party shall have no
obligation to account for or limit its use of Residuals, provided such personnel do not
intentionally memorize Confidential Information for the purpose of avoiding their
confidentiality obligations."
NDA B, Section 5 (Term): "This Agreement shall survive for a period of three (3) years from the
Effective Date."

Compare the two drafts. Identify the substantive difference, assess which one poses greater risk
to the client's proprietary methodology, and recommend a course of action.

**Comparison of NDA A and NDA B**

Both NDAs are substantively similar in key respects:

- **Exclusions from Confidential Information (Section 3):**  
  Both NDAs exclude information that:  
  (i) is or becomes publicly available (without fault),  
  (ii) was known prior to disclosure,  
  (iii) is independently developed without use of the disclosed information, and  
  (iv) is lawfully obtained from a third party.

  **Key Difference:**  
  **NDA B adds a fifth exclusion—(v)—for "Residuals"**: information retained in the unaided memory, mental impressions, or recollection of the Receiving Party’s personnel. This clause allows the Receiving Party to use such retained knowledge without obligation, as long as personnel did not *intentionally memorize* the Confidential Information to circumvent the agreement.

- **Term of Obligations (Section 5):**  
  Both NDAs impose confidentiality obligations for **three years from the Effective Date**. However, the **practical duration of protection differs significantly** due to the Residuals clause.

---

### **Substantive Difference**

The **Residuals clause in NDA B** is the critical distinction.

- In **NDA A**, once the three-year term ends, the Receiving Party’s formal obligations terminate, but during the term, all Confidential Information—including knowledge gained through exposure—is protected.
- In **NDA B**, even **during the term**, the Receiving Party may retain and use knowledge absorbed into personnel memory—so long as it wasn't intentionally memorized. After the three-year term, this residual knowledge becomes even more valuable to the Receiving Party, effectively allowing long-term use of the client’s proprietary methodology *without restriction*.

This **undermines the core purpose of confidentiality**—protecting sensitive, non-public know-how—because human memory becomes a backdoor for lawful use.

---

### **Risk Assessment**

**NDA B poses significantly greater risk to the client's proprietary monitoring methodology.**

**Why:**

1. **Proprietary Methodology Is Likely Know-How–Based**  
   Oilfield services often rely on trade secrets and operational know-how—precisely the type of information vulnerable to the Residuals clause. If personnel absorb the methodology through training or collaboration, the clause permits its use indefinitely.

2. **Difficulty Proving Intentional Memorization**  
   The "no intentional memorization" safeguard is weak. It is nearly impossible to prove that someone intentionally memorized information. Once learned naturally during project work, it falls under "unaided recollection"—and becomes fair game.

3. **Undermines the Three-Year Term**  
   Even if the formal NDA lasts three years, the Residuals clause effectively **extends the Receiving Party’s rights beyond the term**, allowing them to build on or replicate the client’s methodology using internalized knowledge.

4. **Asymmetry of Benefit**  
   The clause primarily benefits the Receiving Party (the joint-venture partner), not the disclosing party (your client). It reduces the cost and risk of accessing proprietary information while offering little in return.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Do not accept NDA B with the Residuals clause in its current form.**

#### **Recommended Course of Action:**

1. **Negotiate to Remove the Residuals Clause**  
   Request deletion of subsection (v) from Section 3 of NDA B. Argue that:
   - The client is disclosing highly sensitive, proprietary methodology.
   - Protection must extend to knowledge gained through exposure, regardless of storage medium (digital, written, or human memory).
   - Industry best practices for protecting trade secrets do not permit such loopholes.

2. **If the Partner Insists on a Residuals Provision, Propose Narrow Safeguards:**  
   If removal is not possible, propose modifications to limit risk:
   - **Limit scope:** Exclude trade secrets, technical methodologies, algorithms, or processes from the Residuals exclusion.
   - **Define "unaided recollection" narrowly:** Require that residual knowledge must arise without reference to any written or recorded materials derived from Confidential Information.
   - **Add a survival clause for trade secrets:** State that trade secret protection continues indefinitely, regardless of the Residuals clause or the three-year term.
   - **Include a non-use covenant:** Prohibit use of Confidential Information (including residuals) for competitive purposes for a longer period (e.g., 5 years).

3. **Prefer NDA A (or a clean-room version of it)**  
   NDA A—lacking the Residuals clause—provides clearer, more robust protection. It aligns better with the client’s interest in safeguarding proprietary methodology.

4. **Supplemental Protections (if moving forward):**
   - Limit data sharing to aggregated or anonymized outputs rather than exposing the full methodology.
   - Implement role-based access and logging to track exposure.
   - Include audit rights and return/destruction obligations.

---

### **Conclusion**

- **Substantive Difference:** NDA B includes a **Residuals clause** allowing use of memorized confidential information.
- **Greater Risk:** **NDA B** poses significantly higher risk to the client’s proprietary methodology.
- **Recommendation:** **Reject NDA B as drafted. Negotiate removal of the Residuals clause or, at minimum, restrict its scope. Prefer NDA A or a revised version without the Residuals exception.**

Protecting proprietary know-how requires closing loopholes—especially those that turn human memory into a vehicle for lawful misappropriation.

### Comparison of NDA A and NDA B

There are two substantive differences between the two drafts that affect the client's risk profile.

**1. The Residuals Clause (Section 3)**
*   **NDA A:** Lacks a Residuals Clause entirely. The four exclusions (public domain, prior knowledge, independent development, third-party source) apply strictly to the *Receiving Party*.
*   **NDA B:** Includes a fifth exclusion for "Residuals." This permits the Receiving Party to retain proprietary information in the unaided memory of its personnel, provided they did not intentionally memorize it to evade the agreement. It explicitly states that Receiving Party has "no obligation to account for or limit its use of Residuals."

**2. Post-Term Obligations and Agreement Survival (Section 5)**
*   **NDA A:** Explicitly states that after three years, the Receiving Party's obligations "shall terminate." This creates a clear sunset date where the client's proprietary methodology is no longer legally protected.
*   **NDA B:** States that the agreement "shall survive for a period of three (3) years." It does not state that the agreement or the obligations *terminate* at the end of the period. This creates ambiguity regarding whether obligations persist indefinitely or simply end after 3 years. In many jurisdictions, "survival" clauses are interpreted as perpetual unless a specific termination trigger is defined.

---

### Risk Assessment

**NDA A poses lower risk.** The draft provides a clear termination date for obligations and does not allow the partner to retain the methodology in employees' memories.

**NDA B poses greater risk to the client's proprietary methodology** for two compounding reasons:

1.  **The Residuals Clause is the primary risk.** For a proprietary monitoring methodology, the most dangerous form of leakage is human memory. Employees can memorize calibration formulas, algorithm weights, or specific data processing techniques and use them in future projects without ever violating the NDA. By removing the obligation to destroy or not use "Residuals," NDA B effectively converts the client's trade secret into a free license for the partner's internal use.
2.  **Ambiguity in Term.** NDA B's failure to explicitly terminate obligations creates uncertainty. If the JV relationship lasts longer than three years, the client is unsure if its proprietary methodology remains protected. Conversely, if the obligations are interpreted as surviving the three-year mark indefinitely, the client is locked into perpetual liability or, worse, the partner is locked into perpetual restriction, making the pilot a permanent barrier to future data usage.

---

### Recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed with NDA A as the baseline and reject NDA B.**

NDA A is the stronger starting point for protecting the client's proprietary methodology. However, the following course of action should be considered:

1.  **Adopt NDA A as the template.** Do not sign NDA B without amendments, as the Residuals Clause alone is unacceptable for proprietary methodology.
2.  **Negotiate the Residuals Clause.** If NDA B is the only viable option (e.g., required by the other JV partner's internal legal standards), demand the deletion of the Residuals Clause. If the partner insists on a Residuals Clause, negotiate a "Carve-Out": require that Residuals are limited to *factual understanding* of the project outcomes, not the *specific methodology or algorithms* used.
3.  **Clarify the Term.** Ensure NDA B's Section 5 is amended to read identically to NDA A, explicitly stating that obligations "shall terminate" after the specified period, or ensure the agreement includes a clear termination clause that triggers the survival period.
4.  **Data Sanitization Protocol.** Regardless of which NDA is signed, implement a protocol requiring the Receiving Party to return or destroy all documents, data files, and notes containing the proprietary methodology upon conclusion of the pilot, ensuring no "residual" retention occurs in practice.
