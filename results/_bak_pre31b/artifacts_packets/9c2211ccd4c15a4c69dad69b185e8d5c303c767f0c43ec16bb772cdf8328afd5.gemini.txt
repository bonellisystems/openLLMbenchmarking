## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Operations -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The process redesign is tied concretely to the specific bottleneck and constraint stated in the prompt, with measurable KPIs that include a baseline and target, not just a goal restated as a metric. Implementation steps are sequenced with a clear first action, change-management or rollout risk is addressed, and resourcing constraints given in the prompt are respected rather than assumed away.
Example character: a redesign that names the actual bottleneck, gives a baseline-to-target KPI, and sequences the first three implementation steps.

## 7 -- Strong
The process improvement is sound and directly usable, correctly targeting the stated bottleneck. The KPI baseline or rollout sequencing is thinner than ideal, but the core recommendation would work as described.
Example character: a correct fix for the stated bottleneck with a named metric but no baseline value given.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic process-improvement guidance ("streamline the workflow," "reduce handoffs") that is directionally correct but not tied to the specific constraint described in the prompt.
Example character: general efficiency advice that would apply to almost any operational bottleneck.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misidentifies the bottleneck, or proposes a process that ignores a stated hard constraint (headcount, budget, system limitation), with no measurable KPI anywhere in the response.
Example character: a redesign that requires more staff despite the prompt stating headcount is fixed.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated industry-benchmark figures as if real, or recommends a change that outright violates a stated hard constraint with no acknowledgment. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated industry-benchmark numbers presented as real data.
- Ignoring an explicitly stated resource or system constraint.
- No measurable KPI or success criterion anywhere in the response.
- Steps that restate goals rather than being actually sequenced and actionable.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Root cause or bottleneck correctly identified from the details given.
- Measurable KPI with a stated baseline and target.
- Rollout sequenced with a clear, concrete first step.
- Resourcing constraints from the prompt respected in the design.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

You're redesigning the handoff between a county's Planning Department (initial application
review) and Building Department (inspection scheduling and permit issuance) for residential
building permits. Current baseline: average handoff time from Planning sign-off to Building
Department scheduling the first inspection = 12 business days.

Root cause: Planning emails a PDF application packet to a shared Building Department inbox
that staff only checks once a week, every Monday -- so a packet that clears Planning on a
Tuesday can sit untouched for up to 6 business days before anyone even opens it. Separately,
30% of packets get bounced back to Planning for a missing required document (the site survey)
that Planning does not verify is attached before sending, adding a further ~5 business day
round trip whenever it happens.

Identify the actual bottleneck driver (it's not overall department workload). Redesign the
handoff mechanism and give a baseline -> target KPI for both handoff time and the
missing-document bounce-back rate. Sequence the first two implementation steps. There is no
new headcount approved this budget cycle and no budget for new paid software this fiscal year
-- your fix has to use existing tools and staffing.

### 1. Actual Bottleneck Driver
**The bottleneck is not departmental workload; it is *Information Flow Integrity and Consumption Frequency*.**

Specifically, the handoff is built on a **passive, decoupled protocol**:
1.  **Decoupled Verification:** Planning sends without confirming completeness (the site survey), meaning the receiver must re-perform the verification work *and* absorb the resulting rework.
2.  **Passive Consumption:** The handoff relies on a shared inbox checked on a single weekly cycle (Monday), creating a fixed, non-negotiable latency ceiling regardless of staffing levels.

The system has no real-time feedback loop. A packet is "in flight" but invisible to the receiver until a weekly check or a bounce-back event occurs.

### 2. Redesign Mechanism: "Active Verified Intake Protocol"
The redesign shifts the handoff from a **passive email drop** to an **active, verified queue event** using existing tools.

*   **Channel Shift:** Move the PDF packet from the shared Building Department inbox to a **designated Shared Drive folder** (e.g., `[Building Dept] / Active Intake`). Existing Document Management System (DMS) or shared drive notification features are configured to alert a specific Building Intake Officer via email immediately upon upload.
*   **Verification Gate:** Planning staff must complete a **Pre-Submission Checklist** (a simple form or sticky-note template added to the existing email header) confirming the site survey is attached *before* they can upload. If the checklist is incomplete, the packet is not accepted.
*   **Routing:** Instead of a shared mailbox, the notification goes to a **single designated Building Intake Officer**. This eliminates the "who will open it?" ambiguity and ensures 100% of packets are visible to the assigned reviewer immediately.

### Root Cause Analysis

The current handoff process from the Planning Department to the Building Department for residential building permits results in an average handoff time of 12 business days. The primary root cause of this delay is the inefficient method of communication and lack of verification for required documents:

1. **Infrequent Checking of Shared Inbox**: Planning Department emails a PDF application packet to a shared Building Department inbox that is only checked once a week, every Monday. This means that an application packet can sit untouched for up to 6 business days before it is even opened.

2. **Missing Required Document**: 30% of the packets are bounced back to the Planning Department because a required document (the site survey) is missing. This happens because the Planning Department does not verify if the site survey is attached before sending the packet, leading to a further ~5 business day round trip.

Create a dedicated board or channel for tracking residential building permit applications. The Planning Department can upload the application packet directly to this board/channel upon approval, ensuring immediate visibility for the Building Department.

2. **Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Document Verification**: Develop and enforce an SOP within the Planning Department that requires verification of all required documents, including the site survey, before an application packet is considered complete and sent to the Building Department. This can be achieved through checklists or templates that must be completed before submission.

### 1. Bottleneck Driver Identification
**Actual Bottleneck Driver:** **Batched transmission latency compounded by upstream quality defects.**

It is not workload; it is process architecture. The weekly email check creates a mandatory, high-variance queuing delay (average wait ~3.5 days, max 6 days). Simultaneously, the 30% bounce rate introduces a predictable rework loop that re-queues valid files and adds ~5 days whenever errors occur. The system is designed to wait for a weekly pull rather than a daily push, and it lacks a "gate" to prevent defective work from entering the flow. The combination of these two design flaws creates the 12-day average.

### 2. Handoff Redesign (No New Tools/Staff)
Leveraging existing email/inbox tools and staff behaviors, the redesign shifts from **Weekly Batch Pull** to **Daily Immediate Push with Upstream Validation**.

*   **Transmission Mechanism:**
    *   **Action:** Planning sends the application packet to the shared Building inbox **immediately** upon sign-off rather than batching for Monday.
    *   **Building Response:** Building staff change their routine to check the shared inbox **daily** (e.g., first thing in the morning) rather than once a week.
    *   *Impact:* Eliminates the weekly wait. Files are seen within 1 business day of Planning sign-off.
*   **Quality Gate:**
    *   **Action:** Planning implements a mandatory **"Site Survey Verification" checkpoint** in their sign-off checklist. Planning staff must confirm the site survey is attached before the packet is released to Building.
    *   *Impact:* Eliminates the bounce-back loop. Building receives complete packets every time.
*   **Result:** The handoff becomes a straight-through process. Building schedules the inspection based on receipt of a complete packet, removing both the weekly queue and the rework loop.
