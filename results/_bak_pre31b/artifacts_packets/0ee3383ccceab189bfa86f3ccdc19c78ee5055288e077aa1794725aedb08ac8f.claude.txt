# Project Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Scope, schedule, and risk are tied concretely to the specifics given -- stated dependencies, resourcing, and deadline -- rather than generic project-plan language. Estimates show their basis (a breakdown, an assumption, or a comparable), the top risks are named with an owner and mitigation, and the critical path or a key dependency is called out explicitly.
Example character: a plan with a dated milestone list, an estimate that shows its basis, and the top risk named with an owner and mitigation.

## 7 -- Strong
The plan structure is sound and usable, correctly tied to the stated scope. Risk mitigation is thinner than ideal, or the estimate basis is not fully shown, but the schedule and scope logic hold together.
Example character: a workable schedule and scope breakdown with risks named but no owner assigned to them.

## 5 -- Adequate
The plan follows a generic template (phases labeled "planning," "execution," "closing") that is not tied to the specific scope or constraints given in the prompt.
Example character: a phase list that could describe almost any project regardless of its actual scope.

## 3 -- Weak
The plan ignores a stated hard constraint (deadline, budget, headcount), identifies no risk at all, or gives an estimate as an unexplained round number.
Example character: a timeline that assumes more people than the prompt states are available.

## 0 -- Unusable
Presents fabricated resourcing or cost figures as sourced fact, or the plan is internally inconsistent (dates that do not add up against stated dependencies). Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Estimate or timeline internally inconsistent with dates or dependencies stated elsewhere in the same answer.
- Fabricated resourcing or cost figures presented as fact.
- No risk register or mitigation despite a clearly risky scope.
- Ignoring an explicitly stated hard constraint (deadline, budget, headcount).

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Estimates show their basis -- a breakdown, assumption, or comparable.
- Top risks named with an owner and mitigation.
- Critical path or a key dependency called out explicitly.
- Plan structure tied to the specific scope given, not generic phase names.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

A hospital is preparing to launch a new patient-portal product and needs a dependency map
across six workstreams before go-live:

T1 -- Vendor contract signature. No dependency; must happen first.
T2 -- HIPAA security risk assessment of the vendor's system. Depends on T1 (the contract must
be signed before the vendor's system can be formally assessed).
T3 -- Clinical staff training content build. Depends on T1 (needs the contracted product to
build training material against).
T4 -- Integration with the hospital's EHR system. Depends on T2 (cannot connect a system
handling PHI to the EHR until the security risk assessment clears it).
T5 -- Compliance sign-off / go-live approval. Depends on T2 AND T4 (compliance needs both the
cleared risk assessment and a working integration before approving go-live).
T6 -- Staff training delivery. Depends on T3 AND T4 (delivery needs both the built content
AND the live integration to train staff on).
Go-live depends on T5 AND T6.

Map the dependency order. Identify which tasks can run in PARALLEL with each other. Identify
the single task that is a blocking prerequisite for the most downstream work, and explain
specifically why rushing or skipping that task would be the highest-risk shortcut available,
even though it is not the last task before go-live.

**3. The single “blocking‑prerequisite” task that carries the highest risk if rushed or skipped**

**Task:** **T2 – HIPAA security risk assessment of the vendor’s system**

**Why it is the most‑downstream blocking prerequisite**

1. **Position in the graph** – T2 sits *directly* upstream of **four** downstream activities:
   - **T4 (EHR integration)** – cannot connect a PHI‑handling system to the hospital’s EHR without a cleared risk assessment.
   - **T5 (Compliance sign‑off)** – compliance requires the risk assessment *and* a working integration; without T2, T5 cannot even be considered.
   - **Go‑live** – because T5 is a gate, Go‑live stalls without T2.
   - **Indirectly, T6 (Staff training delivery)** – training needs a live integration (T4), which in turn needs T2.

2. **Regulatory & legal exposure** – HIPAA mandates a **formal security risk assessment** before any system that stores, transmits, or processes protected health information (PHI) is connected to a healthcare organization’s infrastructure. Skipping or “rushing” this step:
   - **Violates federal law** → potential civil penalties (up to $50,000 per violation, with a maximum of $1.5 M per year) and criminal liability if willful neglect is proven.
   - **Creates a breach‑ready environment** – unknown vulnerabilities could be exploited, leading to a data breach, mandatory breach notification, reputational damage, and possible class‑action lawsuits.
   - **Triggers audit findings** – CMS, OCR, and accrediting bodies (e.g., The Joint Commission) will flag the omission during any compliance audit, possibly resulting in corrective‑action plans, fines, or loss of accreditation.

3. **Not the last task before go‑live** – While T5 (Compliance sign‑off) and T6 (Training) occur *after* T2, they are **dependent** on T2’s outcome. If T2 is omitted, those later tasks cannot legally or safely proceed, making T2 the **single point of failure** that blocks the entire downstream chain.

4. **Risk magnitude vs. schedule gain** – Accelerating T2 (e.g., by doing a superficial checklist instead of a full risk assessment) might shave a few days off the timeline, but the **potential financial, legal, and reputational cost** of a subsequent breach far outweighs any modest schedule benefit. Conversely, skipping T2 altogether would halt the project at the integration stage, forcing a costly rollback or redesign.

**Conclusion:**  
Among all tasks, **T2 – HIPAA security risk assessment** is the critical “blocking prerequisite” whose omission or hasty execution poses the greatest downstream risk. It is essential to allocate sufficient time and resources to perform a thorough, documented risk assessment before moving to integration, compliance sign‑off, training, and ultimately Go‑live.
