# Marketing -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)

- Generic copy with no connection to the specific brand voice or audience given.
- No success metric or measurement plan anywhere in the response.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Concrete, measurable KPI tied directly to the stated goal.
- Channel choice explicitly justified against the stated audience.
- Honest acknowledgment of what needs testing or data before scaling spend.
- Messaging reflects the specific brand voice and constraints given, not a generic template.

## The 90-Day Technical Nurture Sequence

### Touch 1: The Technical Recap (Day 2 Post-Contact)
**Goal:** Validate the technical discussion and establish immediate credibility. Do not ask for a meeting.
**Cadence:** 2 days after Demo/Lead Capture.

*   **Subject:** Technical specs recap: [Company Name] Pipeline Integrity
*   **Content Focus:**
    *   Brief recap of the specific methodology discussed (e.g., "As discussed, the MFL vs. Ultrasonic approach for your 24-inch line").
    *   Attach a **one-page Technical Data Sheet** (not a marketing brochure) highlighting sensor resolution, coverage area, and compliance standards (e.g., API 1104).
    *   **The Hook:** "I've attached the raw specification sheet we mentioned. I know you have to run these against your internal standards, so feel free to share with your team."
*   **Why it works:** It signals you understand their technical landscape and aren't just trying to sell a meeting. It provides the first "data" they can review offline.

### Touch 2: Industry Context (Day 25)
**Goal:** Position the vendor as a thought leader, not a vendor. Keep the pipeline open without asking for anything.
**Cadence:** 23 days after Touch 1.

*   **Subject:** New integrity standards for [Target Region/Industry]
*   **Content Focus:**
    *   Reference a new regulation, safety standard, or industry trend relevant to pipeline integrity.
    *   Provide a short summary of how the vendor's technology aligns with these changes.
    *   **The Hook:** "This just came out regarding corrosion detection thresholds. It might be relevant to your upcoming budget cycle. No need to reply, just wanted to flag it in case it helps your compliance review."
*   **Why it works:** It shows you are monitoring the landscape for them. It keeps the brand top-of-mind without applying pressure. It frames the conversation around *compliance*, which is a key driver for technical managers.

### Touch 3: The Objection Handler – Field Data & Validation (Day 55)
**Goal:** **Directly address the "Field Data/Case Study" objection.** This is the most critical touch for conversion.
**Cadence:** 30 days after Touch 2.

*   **Subject:** Field validation data: Similar pipeline scenarios
*   **Content Focus:**
    *   **Do not** attach a generic PDF case study. Attach a **Data Summary Sheet** (anonymized field data) or a link to a technical whitepaper with raw data tables.
    *   Highlight specific metrics: Detection rates, false positive rates, ROI, and operational downtime reduced.
    *   Reference a peer: "We recently completed a project for [Similar Company] in a similar environment. Here is the data on how the integrity metrics shifted."
    *   **The Hook:** "I know you mentioned needing field data before budget approval. I've pulled the relevant technical metrics from similar sites so you have that info ready for your review."
*   **Why it works:** It anticipates their objection before they voice it. By providing the data *in this email*, you remove the friction of "finding" the data later. It satisfies the technical manager's need for verification.

### Touch 4: Strategic Check-in (Day 85)
**Goal:** Re-engage for the final push before the 90-day mark without being pushy.
**Cadence:** 30 days after Touch 3.

*   **Subject:** 90-day update: [Company Name] tech roadmap
*   **Content Focus:**
    *   Brief update on the product roadmap or a new capability relevant to their specific pipe diameter or environment.
    *   Offer a "soft" availability check.
    *   **The Hook:** "No pressure on a decision, but we are finalizing the Q4 roadmap. If you decide to move this forward in the next month, I want to make sure we prioritize your specs. Let me know if you need more validation."
*   **Why it works:** It acknowledges the long sales cycle (7 months) and shows patience. It keeps the door open for the economic buyer to eventually engage, while maintaining the technical relationship.

## Implementation Rules for Success

1.  **CRM State Change:**
    *   **Crucial:** Leads must **not** be marked "Cold" or dropped at Day 30.

    *   If a lead replies asking for a meeting: Move to Sales immediately.

3.  **Content Formatting:**
    *   Keep emails under 150 words.
    *   Use bullet points for data.
    *   Avoid "I/We" language; use "You/The Team" language (e.g., "This helps your team avoid downtime" vs. "We help you").

*   **Volume:** Increasing from 1 touch to 4 touches over 90 days statistically increases the probability of a positive engagement event within the window.

### 🔧 Why This Cadence Works for Engineering Managers
- **Progressive Disclosure:** Engineers prefer to evaluate in stages. Dumping all data on Day 1 causes decision paralysis. This sequence releases data/methodology/context incrementally.
- **Zero Sales Language:** No CTAs like “Book a demo” or “Get a quote.” All CTAs are technical: “Download data dictionary,” “Review methodology,” “Compare against benchmarks.”
- **Peer Validation Over Vendor Claims:** Engineers trust other engineers. Case studies feature engineering leads, not sales reps, and focus on measurable technical outcomes.
- **Actionable but Low-Risk:** Raw data samples and templates let engineers validate internally before asking for budget approval. Pilot framing removes perceived risk.
- **Respects the 7-Month Cycle:** The 90-day sequence builds the technical foundation. Once engaged, leads move to sales with documented validation, compressing the 7-month timeline to 4–5 months for qualified pipelines.

### Execution Rules for High Engagement

1.  **Segmentation is Mandatory:**
    *   You cannot send a generic sequence to engineers. The "Case Study" and "Deep Dive" touches must be dynamically tagged based on the engineer's industry, pipe type, or specific concerns raised in the demo. If an engineer clicks a case study about "Cathodic Protection Interference" and you send them one about "Flowline Corrosion," the sequence fails.
    *   *Action:* Use the demo data to tag the sequence flow.

2.  **Sender Identity:**
    *   Emails should come from a **Technical Solutions Engineer** or **Application Specialist**, not "Sales Rep." The signature should reflect technical credentials. This increases open rates and reply rates from this persona.

3.  **Metrics for Optimization:**
    *   **Reply Rate:** Target >15% (Engineers reply when the question is technical and relevant).
    *   **Asset Download Rate:** Track which field data assets drive clicks.
    *   **Negative Engagement:** Monitor "Unsubscribe" or "Mark as Spam." If rates spike, the cadence is too pushy.
    *   **90-Day Engaged Rate:** Defined as any lead who replied, clicked a data asset, or viewed a case study in the 30 days prior to the 90-day mark.

4.  **Handling the "Cold" Drop:**
    *   If a lead goes silent after Touch 3 or 4, do not mark them cold immediately. Move them to a quarterly "Technical Insight" emailer that shares anonymized industry data. This keeps the 90-day engaged rate up by allowing re-engagement later in the cycle without re-triggering the nurture.

Engineers are more likely to reply to "Does this data match your constraints?" than "Let's schedule a call." This shift is what drives the increase from 22% to the 35% target.
