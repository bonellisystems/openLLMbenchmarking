## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.


## Task

A regional bank completed a market comp study and reclassified the "Loan Processor" role into a

Context the manager should expect: a coworker in the same role recently mentioned, in a casual
conversation, that they're paid $61,000. The employee is likely to ask why their pay didn't
change if the band changed, and may bring up the coworker's number.

Draft the manager's talking points for this conversation. Address how to respond if the employee
raises the coworker's pay -- employees discussing their own pay with each other is generally
protected activity, so the manager should not discourage or penalize that -- and specify what the
manager should NOT commit to unilaterally in this conversation (e.g., a raise), instead routing
any adjustment question to compensation/HR leadership.

## Manager's Talking Points: Pay Band Communication

### **Opening & Context**
"Thank you for taking the time to meet with me today. I wanted to share some important information about our recent market compensation study and how it impacts our Loan Processor role. As you know, we've been conducting a comprehensive review of our pay bands to ensure we remain competitive in the market. Based on this study, we've reclassified the Loan Processor role into a new pay band of $52,000-$64,000."

### **Addressing the Current Situation**
"Your current compensation of $58,500 falls within this new band, which means there's no change to your pay at this time. This is consistent with our goal of maintaining internal equity while ensuring market competitiveness. The new band simply reflects our updated market positioning for this role."

### **Handling the Coworker Reference**
**If the employee brings up the coworker's pay:**
*"I understand you've heard that another Loan Processor is being paid $61,000. I recognize that salary discussions are a natural part of our workplace culture and that employees have the right to discuss their compensation. This is a protected activity, and I encourage open communication about pay. However, I should note that different employees may be positioned at different points within the same band based on their individual experience, performance, and other factors, and I don't have the authority to make individual pay adjustments."*

### **What to Emphasize**
- The change was based on market research, not individual performance
- The new band is more competitive overall
- The employee's current position is appropriate within the range
- Individual salary decisions require compensation leadership review
- Internal equity is maintained through this process

### **What NOT to Commit To**
- **No individual pay increases** - Even if the employee is at the lower end of the band or feels underpaid
- **No specific timelines** - For future adjustments or reviews
- **No promises about future band changes** - Without proper authorization
- **No direct comparisons** - Between specific employees' compensation

### **Next Steps**
*"If you have any questions about the band changes or would like to discuss your compensation further, I recommend speaking with our Compensation team or HR leadership. They can provide more detailed guidance on the compensation process and address any individual concerns about your pay. I'm here to support you in any way I can, and I understand you may have questions about your role or compensation that are important to you."*

### **Closing**
*"Thank you for your continued strong work and dedication to our team. I'm confident this adjustment aligns with our goal of maintaining competitive compensation while supporting your career growth. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions."*

**Key Principle:** Always redirect compensation-related questions to HR/Compensation leadership while acknowledging the employee's right to discuss pay and their concerns.

    > *   **Experience & Skills:** Specific technical knowledge or tenure can influence placement.
    > *   **Performance History:** Compensation is also tied to performance reviews and growth.
    >
    > Your current pay of $58,500 reflects the value of your experience and performance. While I cannot disclose individual salaries or compare specific paychecks due to confidentiality, I can assure you that our process is designed to be fair and based on those objective factors."

However, **I cannot unilaterally make salary adjustments or commit to changes outside the established band.** Compensation adjustments require a formal review by our Compensation and HR leadership team, who analyze market data, internal equity, and individual performance holistically.

#### **5. Routing & Next Steps**
*   **Goal:** Move equity questions to the proper channel without the manager making a decision.

    > [Option A: Direct Connection] I can send an email introducing you to [HR Contact Name], who can schedule a time to speak with you directly.

#### **6. Closing**

**6. Routing Future Adjustments to HR/Compensation**
"Any questions about salary adjustments, merit increases, or how pay is determined within this band should be directed to our Compensation and HR leadership. ### Manager's "Cheat Sheet": Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Affirm the right** to discuss pay explicitly. | **Do not** discourage the conversation or imply the employee is "bringing politics to work." |
| **Focus on the band** and the process, not individual peer numbers. | **Do not** disclose, confirm, or deny the specific number of the coworker ($61k) unless HR has authorized the disclosure. |
| **Route adjustment questions** to Comp/HR leadership. | **Do not** promise a raise, a future review, or a specific dollar amount. |
| **Keep it open.** "I want to ensure you feel supported." | **Do not** imply that the pay band is wide enough that the employee should "wait" for a raise without guidance. |
| **Document the conversation** in the manager's notes. | **Do not** make the meeting feel punitive or adversarial. |

### Why This Approach Works
1.  **Legal Safety:** By explicitly stating that pay discussions are protected, the manager removes the risk of violating the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) or state equivalent labor laws. This prevents the employee from feeling coerced into silence.
2.  **Transparency without Liability:** The manager explains the *why* (market study) without needing to disclose other employees' private data.
3.  **Clear Boundaries:** By explicitly stating what they *cannot* commit to, the manager protects the bank from accidental over-promising while showing the employee they are being treated with respect and honesty.
