# Marketing -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics presented as real.
- Generic copy with no connection to the specific brand voice or audience given.
- No success metric or measurement plan anywhere in the response.
- Ignoring a stated budget or resource constraint.
- Recommending tactics that misrepresent the product or service.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Concrete, measurable KPI tied directly to the stated goal.
- Channel choice explicitly justified against the stated audience.
- Honest acknowledgment of what needs testing or data before scaling spend.
- Messaging reflects the specific brand voice and constraints given, not a generic template.

    *   **Problem:** The headline "We Guarantee You'll Protect 100% of Your Assets From Probate -- Guaranteed Results or Your Money Back" is ethically and potentially legally problematic for attorneys.
        *   **Ethics:** Attorneys generally cannot guarantee specific legal outcomes (like avoiding probate entirely or saving a specific dollar amount). State bar associations often explicitly prohibit such guarantees as they mislead clients and undermine the reality that legal outcomes depend on numerous factors beyond the attorney's control (client cooperation, court rulings, unforeseen assets, changing laws).
        *   **Compliance Risk:** This framing violates advertising rules in most jurisdictions (e.g., ABA Model Rule 7.1, similar state rules). It invites regulatory scrutiny, complaints, and potential sanctions. It also creates unrealistic client expectations that can lead to disputes.
        *   **Trust Killer:** While intended to build confidence, it often does the opposite for savvy prospects who recognize it as an unattainable promise, contributing to the high bounce rate.

2.  **Unsubstantiated Testimonial (Major Legal & Ethical Violation):**
    *   **Problem:** The testimonial "John saved his family $200,000!" with no disclaimer is non-compliant and risky.
        *   **Ethics & Compliance:** Most state bar rules mandate that attorney testimonials include a clear disclaimer stating that "results may vary" or similar language. Omitting this is a violation. It implies a guarantee of specific financial results, which is similarly problematic to the headline.
        *   **Specificity Risk:** Stating a specific dollar figure ("$200,000") without context or qualification is highly problematic. It suggests a promise of a specific outcome, which is unethical and often illegal. It also makes the testimonial vulnerable to challenges (e.g., Was that net savings? Gross? Including attorney fees?).

The gap between this page's 1.2% conversion rate and the firm's 3.8% average is **3.2x**. The root cause is not just a marketing problem; it is a regulatory and structural problem. ---

### 1. Headline: "Guarantee" Framing
**Current Copy:** *"We Guarantee You'll Protect 100% of Your Assets From Probate -- Guaranteed Results or Your Money Back."*

**Attorney Advertising Violations (Primary Issue):**
*   **ABA Model Rule 7.1 (False or Misleading Communications):** This rule prohibits lawyers from making statements that are false or misleading. Most state bars (and the ABA) interpret absolute guarantees of specific outcomes ("100% of Your Assets") as misleading because estate planning outcomes depend on variables outside the attorney's control (court rulings, tax law changes, client-specific assets).
*   **State Bar "No Guarantee of Results" Rules:** Over 20 state bar associations explicitly prohibit attorneys from guaranteeing results. Promising a "money-back guarantee" tied to the *legal* result creates a specific performance contract that may not be enforceable or may trigger disciplinary action.
*   **CRO Impact:** While "guarantees" seem persuasive, they raise skepticism among sophisticated PPC users who have seen this exact phrase on low-quality lead-gen sites.

**Concrete Fix:**

- **Why it’s dangerous:** A promise that all assets will be protected from probate is **not legally accurate**—estate planning outcomes depend on asset type, titling, jurisdiction, and client compliance. A "money-back" guarantee is virtually unenforceable in legal services and implies a level of certainty that misrepresents legal risk.

#### 2. **Testimonial: "John saved his family $200,000!" — No Disclaimer, Likely Misleading**
- **Problem:** Testimonials citing specific dollar amounts **require clear disclaimers** (e.g., “Results do not guarantee similar outcomes” or “Savings depend on individual circumstances”). Many state bars (e.g., Florida Bar Rule 4-7.15, New York Rule 7.1(d)(5)) mandate that client testimonials include a conspicuous disclaimer to avoid creating unjustified expectations.
- **Additional risk:** The statement is vague, lacks context (was it tax savings? Probate costs avoided?), and implies a typical outcome, which could mislead consumers.
