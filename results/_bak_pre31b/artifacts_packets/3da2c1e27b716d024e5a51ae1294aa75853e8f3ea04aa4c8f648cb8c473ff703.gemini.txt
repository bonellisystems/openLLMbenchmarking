## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this

# SEO -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Technical recommendations are correct for the stated platform (canonical tags, redirects, schema, robots directives used properly), keyword strategy is matched to actual search intent, and every recommendation is prioritized by expected impact versus effort. No black-hat tactic is suggested, and the answer is calibrated about ranking-factor uncertainty rather than asserting exact algorithm weights as fact.
Example character: a prioritized fix list starting with the highest-impact technical issue, each item tied to a specific, correctly-described mechanism.

## 7 -- Strong
The technical guidance is sound and usable for the stated platform, and keyword direction matches intent. It is missing an explicit effort-versus-impact prioritization, or one recommendation is generic rather than platform-specific, but nothing here is technically wrong.
Example character: correct on-page and technical fixes listed without ranking them by which matters most.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is SEO-fundamentals-correct (use target keywords, build backlinks, improve page speed) but reads as a generic checklist not tied to the specifics of the site or platform given.
Example character: a standard keyword-and-backlink checklist with no reference to the site's actual issues.

## 3 -- Weak
Contains outdated or incorrect technical claims (keyword stuffing helps rankings, more backlinks always help regardless of quality), or recommends tactics unrelated to the actual ask.
Example character: advice built around an outdated ranking factor treated as still authoritative.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends black-hat or manipulative tactics (cloaking, link farms, hidden text, doorway pages) as safe practice with no penalty-risk warning, or fabricates specific ranking-factor claims as verified fact. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Black-hat tactics (cloaking, PBNs, hidden text, keyword stuffing) recommended without a penalty-risk warning.
- Fabricated or overconfident claims about exact Google ranking-factor weights.
- Technical errors in canonical, redirect, or robots guidance that would deindex pages.
- Ignoring a stated platform or CMS constraint.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Recommendations prioritized by effort versus expected impact.
- Correct technical implementation detail for the stated platform (canonical, redirects, schema, robots).
- Keyword strategy matched to actual search intent, not just volume.
- Honest flag of algorithm-update or ranking-factor uncertainty.

2. **Faceted/parameter URLs unintentionally indexable** – the redesign introduced query‑string parameters (e.g., `?view=full`, `?lang=en`, `?amp=1`) that are **not** blocked by robots.txt nor excluded via URL‑parameter settings, so [model] crawls each variant. The canonical points to the clean URL, but because the canonical is wrong or missing on the variant pages, [model] ends up treating the variants as separate pages and then de‑indexes them (hence the “crawled – not indexed” surge).

3. **Duplicate content from template fragments** – the new layout may be rendering the same main content block inside multiple paths (e.g., a “service detail” page accessible via both `/services/permit` and `/departments/permits`). If the canonical on both points to a different URL (or is missing), [model] selects one arbitrarily and flags the others as duplicates.

4. **Sitemap mismatch** – the sitemap generated by the CMS now lists **all** URL variants (including parameters, pagination, and possibly draft URLs). [model] attempts to index them, sees they’re duplicates, and drops them.

All of the above manifest as **a massive increase in duplicate‑canonical warnings and “crawled – not indexed” pages**, exactly what you’re seeing.

## Diagnostic Plan: Organic Traffic Drop Post-Relaunch

### 1. Baseline & Post-Drop Metrics
- **Baseline:** 28,000 monthly organic sessions
- **Post-Drop (30-day window):** 16,240 monthly organic sessions
- **Decline:** 42% (11,760 sessions lost)

### 2. Hypotheses Ruled Out
- **Content-quality decline:** Ruled out. The content team confirms zero content was rewritten or removed. More critically, content-quality issues manifest as relevance/ranking signals, not as massive spikes in `Crawled – currently not indexed` and `Duplicate, [model] chose different canonical`. Those flags are technical indexation behaviors, not editorial quality signals.
- **Algorithm update:** Ruled out. [model] did not announce a core or policy update during this window. Algorithmic drops impact multiple properties simultaneously and do not align precisely with a single site’s relaunch date.
- **Manual penalty:** Ruled out. Search Console shows no manual action notice. Manual penalties trigger explicit account alerts and typically cause sharper, uniform traffic suppression across all properties.

### 3. Most Likely Technical Root Cause
**CMS template misconfiguration producing broken/inconsistent canonical tags and unintended duplicate or low-value page variants.**

The `Duplicate, [model] chose different canonical than user` jump (0 → 380) is the primary indicator. It means the new templates are outputting author-specified canonicals that point to non-final URLs (e.g., HTTP instead of HTTPS, `www` vs. non-`www`, trailing-slash variants, old URLs, or paginated/faceted duplicates), causing [model] to override the tag and select a different canonical. 

The parallel surge in `Crawled – currently not indexed` (40 → 1,100) is a downstream symptom: when canonicals conflict or new template logic auto-generates thin/duplicate pages (search results, filter/sort views, pagination, or new template variants), Googlebot crawls them but declines to index due to perceived redundancy, low uniqueness, or accidental template-level `noindex` directives introduced during the relaunch.

### 4. Diagnostic Plan (Prioritized Order)
Execute these checks sequentially. Each step directly maps to a specific Search Console signal and isolates the root cause before moving to secondary factors.

**Step 1: Audit Canonical Tag Implementation in New Templates** *(Addresses: Duplicate/canonical flag)*
- Crawl the live site (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, or Ahrefs) and export all pages.
- Verify every canonical tag is **self-referencing** and points to the final canonical URL (HTTPS, correct `www`/non-`www`, consistent trailing-slash convention).
- Cross-reference the export with Search Console’s `Duplicate, [model] chose different canonical` report to identify exact URL pairs where [model] overrode the tag.
- Check template logic for pagination, facets, and sort parameters—are they correctly canonicalizing to the base URL or properly marked `noindex, canonical`?

**Step 2: Inspect for Accidental `noindex`/`nofollow` in New CMS Templates** *(Addresses: Crawled – not indexed flag)*
- Pull HTML source for a sample of the 1,100 `Crawled – not indexed` pages.
- Look for `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`, missing canonicals, or `X-Robots-Tag` headers blocking indexing.
- Verify theme/template files weren’t hardcoded with `noindex` for new page types (search, filters, author pages, or template variants that should be indexable).

**Step 3: Audit URL Structure, Redirects, and Protocol/Host Consolidation** *(Addresses: Both flags)*
- Map pre-launch → post-launch URL changes. Confirm all legacy URLs issue clean 301s to their correct new equivalents.
- Check for duplicate URL variants: HTTP↔HTTPS, `www`↔non-`www`, trailing slash vs. none, and query-parameter duplicates.
- Validate the post-relaunch XML sitemap contains **only** canonical, indexable pages and is properly referenced in robots.txt.

**Step 4: Review Crawl Efficiency & Crawl Budget Allocation** *(Addresses: Crawled – not indexed volume)*
- In Search Console → Crawling → Crawl stats, compare crawl rate pre- vs. post-relaunch.
- If crawl rate is stable or up but indexed pages are down, Googlebot is spending budget on low-value template pages (facets, search, pagination) that should be excluded or properly consolidated.
- Confirm robots.txt isn’t accidentally blocking new template directories or core assets.

**Step 5: Verify Internal Linking & Navigation Structure** *(Addresses: Crawl priority & indexation)*
- Audit main navigation, breadcrumbs, and footer links to ensure they point to canonical, indexable URLs.
- Broken, circular, or parameter-laden internal links reduce crawl priority and push pages into the `Crawled – not indexed` bucket.

**Step 6: Validate Template-Generated Content Thinness** *(Addresses: Crawled – not indexed)*
- Spot-check high-volume auto-generated pages (new category, tag, filter, or search result pages).
- If they render with minimal unique content, [model] will crawl but not index them. Either enrich them with unique value or apply `noindex, canonical` + remove from sitemap.

### 5. Confirmation & Remediation Path
- **If Step 1 reveals mismatched/missing canonicals:** Fix template canonical logic, align all self-referencing tags to final canonical URLs, resubmit XML sitemap, and use URL Inspection to batch-request reindexing.

- **If Step 3 reveals redirect/URL variant issues:** Implement correct 301s, consolidate host/protocol variants, and monitor Search Console for 2–4 weeks as [model] re-crawls and re-evaluates canonicals.
- **Expected recovery window:** 2–6 weeks post-fix, assuming crawl budget is restored and canonical conflicts are resolved.
