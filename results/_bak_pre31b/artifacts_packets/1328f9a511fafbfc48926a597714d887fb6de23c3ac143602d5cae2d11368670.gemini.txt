## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

### What **Claims Must Be Avoided** (Pending FDA Clearance)

| Forbidden Claim Type | Example of **Disallowed** Language | Acceptable Substitute |
|----------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Clinical Accuracy / Diagnostic Sensitivity & Specificity** | “Our test detects 99 % of carriers for cystic fibrosis.” | “Our forthcoming test will include information on carrier status for a range of conditions; performance data will be released after FDA clearance.” |
| **Diagnostic Performance / Predictive Value** | “The test predicts a 70 % chance you’ll develop Alzheimer’s.” | “Genetic markers can provide insight into relative risk, but they do not guarantee disease onset.” |
| **Medical Efficacy / Treatment Recommendations** | “Taking this supplement based on your result will reduce your risk of heart attack.” | “Lifestyle choices can influence health outcomes; discuss any interventions with your healthcare provider.” |
| **Absolute Risk Statements** | “You have a 1 in 5 chance of developing hereditary breast cancer.” | “Having a particular genetic variant may be associated with an increased relative risk; absolute risk varies among individuals.” |
| **Interpretation of Results as Diagnosis** | “A positive result means you have the disease.” | “A positive genetic finding indicates the presence of a variant; clinical correlation is required for diagnosis.” |
| **Comparisons to FDA‑Cleared Tests Without Clearance** | “Our test is more accurate than X‑company’s FDA‑cleared assay.” | “When available, we will publish comparative performance data in a cleared submission.” |
| **Any Direct Marketing of the Test as a Medical Device** | “Buy now to get a clinically validated health report.” | “We are preparing to launch a new at‑home test; pre‑launch information will focus on education and awareness only.” |

**Bottom‑line rule for every piece:**  
- **Never** state that the test *will*, *has*, or *can* provide a medical diagnosis, treatment guidance, or definitive health outcome.  
- **Always** qualify any discussion of risk with language such as “may be associated with,” “relative risk,” or “based on current scientific understanding.”  
- **Reference** that any performance or validation data will be shared **after** FDA clearance, not before.

### 3. Explicit Claims & Language to Avoid
Given the pending clearance, **all content must be reviewed by Legal/Regulatory Affairs.** The following claims are strictly prohibited:

*   **Performance Metrics:** No mentions of accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, error rates, or validation percentages.
*   **Diagnostic/Medical Claims:** No use of words like "diagnose," "detect," "identify," "predict," "screen," "treat," "cure," or "monitor" in relation to the new test or specific conditions.
*   **Indication Claims:** Do not state what the test is *for* (e.g., "Our test helps you know your risk for X condition"). Avoid naming specific diseases, genes, or biomarkers the test targets.
*   **Regulatory Status Misrepresentation:** Never state "FDA Cleared" or "FDA Approved." If mentioned, use only "Pending FDA clearance" or "Undergoing regulatory review."
*   **Efficacy & Outcomes:** No claims about how the test improves health outcomes, changes lifestyle, or prevents disease.
*   **Comparison Claims:** No comparisons to competitors or clinical lab standards.
*   **Result Interpretation:** No advice on how to act on results. No "next steps" that imply medical action based on test output.
*   **Availability:** Do not imply the test is ready for use or available for purchase before the official launch date.
