# Helpdesk -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Triage priority matches the actual business impact described, and the response tone is consistent with the urgency that priority implies. Troubleshooting steps are ordered from least to most disruptive, a clear escalation path is given for when the fix is out of the agent's scope, and the answer is calibrated about what genuinely needs remote hands or an on-site visit.
Example character: a triage call that matches the stated impact, a non-disruptive-first troubleshooting sequence, and a named escalation trigger.

## 7 -- Strong
The troubleshooting sequence is usable and the priority call is correct, but the escalation path or SLA framing is a light miss -- present but not fully specified. Nothing here jumps to a disruptive step too early.
Example character: a correct non-disruptive troubleshooting sequence with no stated escalation trigger if it fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is a generic troubleshooting checklist (restart, check cables, reinstall) not tied to the specific symptom or business impact described in the ticket.
Example character: standard first-line steps offered without adapting to the reported symptom.

## 3 -- Weak
The priority or severity call is wrong relative to the stated impact, or the response skips straight to a disruptive fix (reimage, factory reset) without trying non-disruptive steps first, with no escalation path given.
Example character: recommends reimaging a device as the first step for a minor, non-blocking symptom.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends a data-destructive action (wipe, reformat, delete profile) with no backup step or warning, or fabricates vendor or product behavior presented as known fact. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Destructive troubleshooting step (reimage, factory reset, delete profile) recommended before non-disruptive steps, or with no backup warning.
- Priority/SLA call that does not match the stated business impact.
- No escalation path given when the issue is clearly out of scope.
- Fabricated product or vendor behavior presented as known fact.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Triage priority matches the stated business impact.
- Troubleshooting steps ordered least-to-most disruptive.
- Explicit escalation trigger and path given.
- SLA/urgency framing consistent with the ticket's actual severity.

### Identity Verification and Account Recovery Path

Given the constraints (no SMS, no push notifications, no cell signal, saturated VSAT link, and no shared credentials), I would implement a **multi-factor identity verification process using Knowledge-Based Authentication (KBA) combined with operational context verification**. This ensures the technician’s identity is rigorously confirmed without compromising security or MFA enforcement. Below is the step-by-step path:

---

#### **Step 1: Establish a Secure Communication Channel**
- **Method**: Use the VSAT link for a **brief, scheduled encrypted video call** (e.g., via Teams or Zoom). Schedule it during a lull in SCADA telemetry to minimize bandwidth impact. If video is infeasible, use a secure text-based chat (e.g., Signal or WhatsApp) over the VSAT.  
- **Purpose**: Enable real-time interaction for verification questions while reducing data usage.  
- **Bandwidth Management**: Keep the exchange short (<5 minutes) to avoid disrupting drilling telemetry.

---

#### **Step 2: Multi-Layered Identity Verification**
Verify the technician’s identity through **two distinct categories of KBA questions**:  
1. **Personal KBA**: Pre-enrolled questions with non-public answers (e.g., "What was your childhood street name?" or "What is your favorite book title?").  
   - *Requirement*: Technician must answer **3 out of 5** correctly.  
   - *Rationale*: These questions are private and not tied to external systems.  

2. **Operational KBA**: Rig-specific questions only the technician would know, derived from real-time drilling data or historical logs.  
   - *Examples*:  
     - "What is the current drill bit size in inches?"  
     - "What was the mud weight (ppg) used yesterday at 14:00 UTC?"  
     - "Which sensor on Rig-Alpha reported a pressure anomaly last week?"  
   - *Requirement*: Technician must answer **2 out of 3** correctly.  
   - *Rationale*: Leverages their unique role and access to operational data, which an impersonator couldn’t easily fabricate.  

**Verification Criteria**:  
- Success requires passing **both** personal and operational KBA (total of 5 correct answers).  
- If failed, escalate to a supervisor for additional verification (e.g., cross-checking with drilling logs).

---

#### **Step 3: Account Recovery Without Disabling MFA**
- **Reset MFA via Backend Tools**:  
  - The support team uses admin tools to:  
    1. **Temporarily disable the failing MFA method** (push/SMS) but keep MFA **active** for the account.  
    2. **Enforce TOTP-based MFA** (e.g., [model] Authenticator) using a pre-shared secret key.  
    3. **Generate a one-time backup code** (printed or shared via the secure call) for immediate use.  
- **User Action**:  
  - The technician installs an authenticator app on their laptop (or uses the backup code) to generate TOTP codes offline.  
  - Password reset is handled via a secure backend link sent during the call (e.g., a time-limited reset URL).  

**Why This Works**:  
- MFA remains enforced; only the delivery method changes (push → TOTP).  
- Backup codes provide offline access without compromising security.  

---

#### **Step 4: Documentation**
Post-incident, document the following to ensure compliance and future prevention:  
1. **Incident Report**:  
   - **Details**: Timestamp, technician ID, reason for lockout (VSAT saturation), and verification steps taken.  
   - **Verification Outcome**: Record the KBA questions asked and answers provided (anonymized for audit).  
   - **Resolution**: Admin actions taken (e.g., "MFA method reset to TOTP, backup code issued").  

2. **Policy Update**:  
   - Recommend adding **rig-specific KBA questions** to standard MFA reset procedures for remote sites.  
   - Propose **offline MFA options** (e.g., hardware tokens or printed backup codes) for future deployments.  

3. **Audit Trail**:  
   - Log all verification steps (e.g., call duration, questions/answers, admin actions) in the SIEM system.  
   - Store backup codes in encrypted vaults with expiration dates.  

4. **Follow-up**:  
   - Schedule a review with the technician to ensure they understand the new MFA setup.  
   - Assess VSAT bandwidth for future MFA delivery improvements.  

---

### **Why This Approach Is Secure and Compliant**  
- **No SMS/Push Reliance**: Uses KBA and TOTP, which are independent of connectivity.  
- **Strong Verification**: Personal + operational KBA ensures it’s the legitimate technician, not an impersonator.  
- **MFA Preserved**: Only the delivery method is changed; MFA remains enforced.  
- **Audit Trail**: Documentation meets regulatory requirements for identity verification and access control.  

This path balances operational urgency with security rigor, ensuring the technician gains access without creating long-term vulnerabilities.

Here is a secure, policy-compliant recovery path that satisfies your constraints while maintaining identity assurance and auditability.

### 🔐 Immediate Recovery Path
1. **Route Through Corporate Identity Portal**  
   The technician accesses the corporate identity management (IM) portal via the VSAT link. Even if telemetry saturates real-time push channels, standard HTTP/HTTPS requests for the portal typically route successfully or can be queued/prioritized by network ops.

2. **Initiate Out-of-Band Verification Request**  
   The technician selects the “No Signal / No Device Available” recovery option, which triggers a step-up verification workflow that bypasses push/SMS and relies on:
   - Backup corporate email (if reachable via VSAT)
   - Manager cross-verification
   - Dynamic identity challenges

3. **Manager Out-of-Band Confirmation**  
   The site supervisor contacts IT Security over a verified landline or satellite phone. The manager independently verifies the technician’s physical presence, identity (badge number, work permit), and urgent business need, then provides verbal authorization to IT Security.

4. **Dynamic Identity Challenge**  
   The technician completes a secure, work-specific verification flow:
   - Uploads/verifies company photo ID or digital badge via encrypted form
   - Answers dynamic, non-public knowledge questions tied to recent field work (e.g., last calibration date of a specific rig instrument, safety incident code, recent training module ID, or well-parameter values that change daily)
   - Provides a one-time code sent to the corporate backup email or generated locally via a TOTP time-window alignment (if the app is still running offline)

5. **Just-In-Time (JIT) Access Provisioning**  
   IT Security, using the verified manager confirmation and dynamic challenge results, grants a temporary, account-specific access token via the Privileged Access Management (PAM) system:
   - Scope: Read/write access only to the drilling report submission portal
   - Duration: 2–4 hours (aligned with regulatory deadline)
   - MFA remains enforced for all other logins; this is a one-time scoped exception, not a MFA bypass or credential handout

All records are retained per corporate audit policy (typically 12–24 months) and tagged with `MFA-Exception-Justified`, `JIT-Access-Granted`, and `Remote-Field-Recovery`.

To maintain security without disabling MFA or using shared credentials, you must move from **"Possession-Based Verification"** (the phone) to **"Trusted Third-Party Verification"** (vouching) combined with a **"Time-Limited Bypass."**

### 1. The Identity-Verification Path: "Managerial Vouching"
Since the technician cannot be reached via their own registered device, you must utilize the organizational hierarchy to establish a "Chain of Trust."

*   **Step A: Establish Voice Contact via Satellite Phone/VoIP:** The Help Desk must contact the **Site Supervisor or Toolpusher** on the rig. In remote drilling operations, even if VSAT is saturated, site-to-shore voice communication is typically prioritized or handled via a separate satellite voice link (like an Iridium handset).
*   **Step B: Face-to-Face Verification:** The Help Desk instructs the Supervisor to perform a physical identity check. The Supervisor must visually confirm that the person requesting access is indeed the technician in question (checking a physical ID or matching the person to the site manifest).
*   **Step C: The "Vouching" Protocol:** The Supervisor acts as a "Trusted Witness." The Supervisor then speaks to the Help Desk via the satellite link to formally "vouch" for the technician's identity. The Help Desk confirms the Supervisor's identity through secondary means (e.g., verifying their employee ID or a pre-shared supervisor code) if the protocol allows.

### 2. The Account-Recovery Path: "Time-Limited Bypass Code"
Once identity is verified via the Supervisor, the Help Desk must **not** disable MFA. Instead, they should issue a **Temporary Emergency Bypass Code (also known as a One-Time Password or OTP bypass).**

*   **Issuance:** The Help Desk generates a single-use, 8-digit bypass code in the IAM (Identity and Access Management) system.
*   **Delivery:** To prevent interception, the code is **not** sent via email or SMS. The Help Desk provides the code to the **Supervisor** over the secure voice link, who then relays it verbally to the technician.
*   **Constraints:** 
    *   **Single-Use:** The code must expire immediately after the first successful login.
    *   **Short TTL (Time-to-Live):** The code must be set to expire within a very narrow window (e.g., 2 to 4 hours) to allow only enough time for the technician to submit the urgent report.

### 3. Post-Incident Documentation
After the technician has gained access and submitted the report, a formal incident report must be attached to the IT support ticket. This is critical for compliance (especially with regulatory requirements like BSEE or similar bodies) and for audit trails.

**The documentation must include:**
1.  **Timestamp of Incident:** When the lockout occurred and when access was granted.
2.  **Verification Method:** A detailed log stating: *"Identity verified via Face-to-Face check by [Supervisor Name/ID] and subsequent voice vouching via [Satellite Phone/VoIP] due to total loss of cellular/MFA push capability."*
3.  **Bypass Details:** The specific type of bypass used (e.g., "Temporary MFA Bypass Code") and the expiration time set. **Note:** Do *not* document the actual code itself.
4.  **Justification:** A clear explanation of the "Extenuating Circumstances" (e.g., "VSAT saturation preventing MFA push; no cellular signal for SMS/Callback; urgent regulatory deadline for Drilling Report [Report ID]").
5.  **Authorization:** The name and digital signature (if possible) of the Supervisor who authorized the identity vouching.

### Why this works:
*   **It preserves MFA integrity:** You haven't disabled MFA for the account; you have only provided a temporary, high-entropy "key" that is as secure as a voice-relayed password.
*   **It prevents social engineering:** An attacker cannot easily spoof a Site Supervisor's voice and physical presence simultaneously.
*   **It is resilient to connectivity issues:** It moves the "delivery" of the credential from a digital packet (Push/SMS) to a human voice (Voice/Radio), which is much more robust in high-interference/saturated environments.

## 2. Step‑by‑Step Identity‑Verification & Account‑Recovery Path  

> **Assumptions**  
> * The technician can reach the corporate VPN portal via the VSAT link (bandwidth is limited but TCP/HTTPS is available).  
> * The technician has **physical possession** of a *backup YubiKey* (or another FIDO2 security key) that was issued to them during onboarding and stored in a sealed, tamper‑evident pouch in their field kit.  
> * The incident‑response team has a **dedicated “Recovery Hotline”** phone number that is provisioned on the satellite phone (or on a separate VSAT‑enabled VOIP line) and is staffed 24 × 7 by a senior IAM analyst.  
> * The user’s corporate email account still has **access via webmail** (the same VSAT link) – this is used only for reading system‑generated messages, not for sending.  

### 2.1 Establish a Secure, Out‑of‑Band Voice Channel  

1. **Technician dials the Recovery Hotline** (satellite VOIP) from the field.  
2. The call is answered by an IAM analyst who immediately places the caller on a *verified* conference bridge that records the call.  
3. **Challenge‑Response Question Set** (pre‑approved, low‑entropy, but unique per user) is read to the analyst:  
   * “What is the serial number of the hardware token you received on 2023‑07‑12?”  
   * “What is the last four digits of the employee ID shown on your onboarding badge?”  
   * “What was the exact date (YYYY‑MM‑DD) of your last completed drilling report submission?”  

   *The analyst verifies the answers against the data stored in the HR‑IAM sync. If any answer is wrong, the call is terminated and a “Failed Verification” ticket is opened.*

### 2.2 Proof‑of‑Possession via Hardware Security Key  

1. While on the voice call, the technician is instructed to **insert the backup YubiKey** into a USB‑powered hub that is connected to the VSAT terminal.  
2. The analyst **initiates a FIDO2 “user verification” request** via the corporate SSO portal (e.g., Okta, Azure AD, PingIdentity). The request includes a **one‑time “recovery challenge”** that the service sends to the key.  
3. The YubiKey **signs the challenge** and returns a **signature + attestation** to the portal. Because the key is registered to *that* user’s account, the portal can confirm that the signature matches the registered public key.  

   *If the signature verifies, the portal marks the user as “verified by hardware token” and proceeds to the next step.*

### 2.3 Consume a Pre‑Generated One‑Time Recovery Token  

1. Prior to any incident, each privileged user is issued **one (and only one) recovery token** that is stored in the PAM vault (e.g., CyberArk, HashiCorp Vault) as a **cryptographically signed, single‑use artifact**.  
2. The analyst **retrieves the token** for the specific user (identified by employee number) and **reads it over the voice call** to the technician. The token is a 32‑character base‑64 string that is *short enough* to be read aloud and *long enough* to be cryptographically unique.  
3. The technician **copies the token** into the VPN login field (or pastes it into the SSO “Recovery Code” entry) **on the same terminal**.  

   *Because the token is single‑use and bound to the user’s account, it cannot be reused or shared.*

### 2.4 Re‑Enable MFA Enrollment  

1. Upon successful presentation of the recovery token, the SSO portal **automatically unlocks the user’s account** and **forces a MFA re‑enrollment flow**.  
2. The technician is prompted to **register a new authenticator** (e.g., a fresh push‑based authenticator, a new hardware token, or a mobile authenticator app). Because the field site has only the VSAT link, the recommended fallback is **registration of a new hardware token** (the same YubiKey can be re‑programmed) or **enrollment of a TOTP‑based authenticator** that can be seeded via a QR code delivered over the VSAT webmail channel.  
3. After the new factor is successfully registered, the portal **invalidates the recovery token**, **logs the event**, and **sends an email receipt** (delivered via the same VSAT connection) confirming that MFA has been restored.  

   *The technician must complete the re‑enrollment before the ticket can be closed; otherwise the account remains locked.*

### 2.5 Close the Incident  

1. The IAM analyst **updates the privileged‑access ticket** with:  
   * Time stamps for each verification step.  
   * The exact recovery token value (masked after use).  
   * Confirmation that a **new MFA factor** has been enrolled and is functional (a quick test login is performed from a separate admin workstation).  
2. The ticket is **marked “Resolved – MFA restored, account secured”** and is archived for audit.  
3. A **post‑incident note** is added to the user’s security profile, indicating that the incident was handled via hardware‑token verification and that no shared credentials were issued.

## 5. Key Take‑aways for Future Prevention  

1. **Pre‑stage a hardware‑token backup** for every field technician and store it in a tamper‑evident pouch within the field kit.  
2. **Distribute a small printed “recovery code”** (e.g., a 10‑digit base‑64 string) **only in a sealed envelope** that can be used when no network is available; the code is tied to the user’s account and can be entered manually via the VPN login.
