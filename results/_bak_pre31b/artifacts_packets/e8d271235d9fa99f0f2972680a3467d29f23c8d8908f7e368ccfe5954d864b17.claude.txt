# Marketing -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Positioning and messaging are sharply tied to the specific audience and goal stated, with channel and budget allocation backed by explicit rationale rather than a checklist. A measurable success metric is defined up front, and the answer is calibrated about what needs testing or data before committing further spend. There is no generic "increase brand awareness" filler -- every recommendation ties back to the brief.
Example character: a channel mix with a stated budget split, a named target metric, and an explicit note on what to A/B test before scaling.

## 7 -- Strong
The strategic direction and messaging are solid and usable, tied clearly to the stated brand and audience. One area is thinner than the rest -- the success metric is vague, or the channel rationale in one section is generic -- but nothing here is a template that could apply to any company.
Example character: a well-targeted campaign concept with a clear audience fit but no explicit measurement plan.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is marketing-fundamentals-correct (use social media, build a content calendar, define your audience) but loosely tied to the actual brief, with no concrete numbers, budget, or timeline attached.
Example character: generic channel suggestions that could be pasted into almost any brief with minor edits.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer misreads the stated audience or goal, or delivers copy that is template-y and interchangeable, with no success metric anywhere in the response.
Example character: campaign copy that ignores a stated target segment and pitches to a generic mass audience instead.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics as if they were real data. Empty, off-task, or entirely disconnected from the stated brand and goal.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated market-size, benchmark, or performance statistics presented as real.
- Generic copy with no connection to the specific brand voice or audience given.
- No success metric or measurement plan anywhere in the response.
- Ignoring a stated budget or resource constraint.
- Recommending tactics that misrepresent the product or service.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Concrete, measurable KPI tied directly to the stated goal.
- Channel choice explicitly justified against the stated audience.
- Honest acknowledgment of what needs testing or data before scaling spend.
- Messaging reflects the specific brand voice and constraints given, not a generic template.

### Detailed Content Breakdown

#### Touch 1: The Technical Recap (Day 1)
*   **The Angle:** "Here is the documentation for your files."
*   **Content:** A concise bulleted list of the specific technical requirements they mentioned during the demo. Attach a PDF of the technical specification sheet.
*   **CTA (Call to Action):** "If there were any specs we didn't cover that you need for your internal review, let me know."

#### Touch 2: The Methodology Deep Dive (Day 14)
*   **The Angle:** "Answering the 'How' behind the accuracy."
*   **Content:** Focus on the physics or the process. *“During our demo, we discussed the [Specific Measurement] accuracy. I thought you might find this technical brief on our [Sensor Type/Algorithm] interesting—it explains how we mitigate [Common Error Type] in high-interference environments.”*
*   **CTA:** "Does this methodology align with your current inspection protocols?"

#### Touch 3: The "Evidence" Payload (Day 30) — *CRITICAL TOUCH*
*   **The Angle:** "You asked for field data; here is the raw reality."
*   **Content:** This is a direct response to the objection. Do **not** send a marketing brochure. Send a "Technical Field Report" or a "Data Summary Case Study." Focus on the problem, the inspection methodology used, and—most importantly—the **data output** (e.g., "Detected 95% of sub-surface anomalies within X mm tolerance").
*   **CTA:** "I've attached a summary of the data captured during a similar [Pipeline Type] inspection. Does this data resolution meet the threshold required for your budget approval process?"

#### Touch 4: The Edge Case (Day 55)
*   **The Angle:** "Addressing the 'What Ifs'."
*   **Content:** Engineers worry about failure points. Address a difficult scenario (e.g., high silt, extreme temperature, or complex geometry). *“We often get asked how our [Service] handles [Specific difficult condition]. Here is a brief technical note on how we maintain accuracy in those specific environments.”*
*   **CTA:** "Is [Specific Condition] a factor in your upcoming inspection cycle?"

#### Touch 5: The Industry Context (Day 80)
*   **The Angle:** "Staying ahead of the curve/compliance."
*   **Content:** A high-level insight. *“With the new [Regulatory Body] updates regarding pipeline integrity reporting, many engineering teams are shifting toward [Specific Technology]. We’ve compiled a brief overview of how this impacts data reporting requirements.”*
*   **CTA:** (Soft Re-engagement) "No need to reply to this, but I wanted to ensure you had the latest info for your Q3/Q4 planning."

### Why this solves the problem:

1.  **Solves the Objection:** Instead of asking *if* they want data, we assume they need it and deliver it in **Touch 3**. By framing it as a "Technical Field Report" rather than a "Case Study," it feels like a tool for the engineer rather than a sales pitch for the executive.
2.  **Respects the Persona:** Engineers loathe "Sales-speak." This sequence uses "Engineering-speak." We are providing them with the tools to sell *us* to their boss.
3.  **Extends the Engagement Window:** By moving from "Demo $\rightarrow$ 1 email $\rightarrow$ Drop" to a 90-day "Technical Intelligence" stream, we stay top-of-mind during the long 7-month cycle without being an annoyance.
4.  **Measurability:** We can now track which *specific* type of content (Methodology vs. Field Data vs. Compliance) generates a reply, allowing us to refine the content for the next batch of leads.

### Strategy Overview: The "Field-Validation" Nurture
To shift the 90-day engagement rate from 22% to 35%, we must fundamentally change the relationship dynamic. The current 1-touch cadence signals "sales follow-up," which triggers resistance from a Technical Engineering Manager (TEM). The new sequence signals **"peer-level technical support."**

**Core Principles:**
*   **Cadence:** Decaying frequency. We start close to satisfy the immediate request, then widen the gap to respect the long cycle without burning out the lead.
*   **Content:** Zero marketing fluff. Every touch must deliver a tangible asset (data, analysis, or technical insight).
*   **CTA:** Low friction. No "book a call." CTAs are "Reply with specific questions," "Download raw data," or "Link to technical library."
*   **Duration:** 6 touches over 100 days. This spans the measurement window while aligning with the 7-month cycle, allowing the lead to digest information at their own pace.

### Execution & Optimization Tactics

1.  **Lead Scoring Integration:**
    *   *Action:* Track opens/clicks on data assets. If a lead clicks "Raw CSV" or "Benchmarking Matrix," trigger an alert to Sales to wait 48 hours and then reach out *only* with a specific technical question, referencing the asset viewed.
    *   *Why:* This prevents Sales from interrupting the nurture with a sales call, which would kill the 35% engagement goal.

2.  **Segmentation by Objection Type:**
    *   *Action:* If the lead explicitly asked for "Case Studies" vs. "Field Data," tag the sequence accordingly. Touch 1 should dynamically swap assets based on the tag.
    *   *Why:* Technical accuracy starts with relevance. Sending a case study to someone asking for raw data looks like evasion.

3.  **Subject Line A/B Testing:**
    *   *Action:* Test Subject Lines that include specific technical metrics vs. generic titles.
    *   *Example:* "Data: Corrosion detection in 12" pipe" vs. "Case Study: Pipeline Inspection Results."
    *   *Why:* Engineers scan for keywords. Data-heavy subject lines typically yield higher open rates for this persona.

4.  **Sales Enablement Alignment:**
    *   *Action:* Brief Sales on the "Field Data Objection." Instruct them: *If a lead from this sequence becomes "Hot," do not ask for a decision. Ask for technical validation.*
    *   *Why:* The TEM is 7 months from a decision. A sales call asking "When can we sign?" will cause immediate disengagement. The transition from Nurture to Sales must be technical.

5.  **Success Metrics:**
    *   **Primary:** 90-Day Engaged Lead Rate (Target: 35%).
    *   **Secondary:** Reply Rate (Target: >8% via low-friction CTAs).
    *   **Tertiary:** Data Asset Download Rate (Target: >40% of sequence opens).
    *   **Leading Indicator:** "Reply to pull data" triggers in Touch 6.

### Why This Hits the Goal
*   **Current State Failure:** The current sequence offers no value, drops too soon, and feels like a "chase." It likely gets marked as spam or ignored.
*   **New State Success:** This sequence provides **continuous value** that supports the TEM's job. By the day 90 mark, the lead has received multiple validations of technical accuracy, peer benchmarks, and direct access to field data. Even if they don't buy, they have engaged, downloaded resources, and likely replied to request specific data. This behavior counts as "engaged lead," pushing the metric toward the 35% target while keeping the deal alive for the long cycle.
