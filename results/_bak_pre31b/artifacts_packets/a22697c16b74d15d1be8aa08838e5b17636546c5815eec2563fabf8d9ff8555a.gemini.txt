## 2.  WARN‑Notice Timing – The “45‑Day Gap” Issue  

### A.  Legal Baseline  
| Jurisdiction | Statutory Notice Period | Typical Exception |
|--------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| **Federal (WARN Act)** | 60 calendar days *or* pay in lieu thereof | “Faltering company” or “unforeseeable circumstances” can reduce notice to **30 days** (or none if the employer pays wages in lieu). |
| **State (e.g., NY, CA, TX)** | Usually 60 days, but some states allow 30‑day notice if the employer pays severance equal to the notice period. | Same “exception” language; some states require **additional pay** for the shortfall. |

> **Bottom line:** 45 days is *short* of the standard 60‑day federal WARN requirement, and most states will treat it as a non‑compliant notice unless an exception applies.

### B.  Why the Gap Exists  
- The contract termination was **unexpected** but not “unforeseeable” in the legal sense; however, many employers argue that a sudden loss of a major contract qualifies as a “single‑event” that dramatically reduces workload, thereby invoking the “single‑site” exception.  
- Nonetheless, the safest approach is to **treat the 45‑day window as a “partial notice”** and **mitigate the exposure** through additional compensation or an agreement with employees.

### C.  Immediate Action Steps  

| Step | What to Do | Rationale |
|------|------------|-----------|
| **1. Issue a Formal WARN Notice** | Draft a notice that states: “Pursuant to the federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act, we are providing **45 days’ notice** of the planned closure. Because the notice period is less than the statutory 60‑day requirement, we will **pay an additional amount equal to the wages and benefits you would have earned during the missing 15 days** (or provide equivalent severance).” | Demonstrates good‑faith compliance and reduces the risk of a WARN claim. |
| **2. Offer “Notice Pay” or “Severance in Lieu”** | Calculate the **15‑day wage equivalent** for each employee (including base salary, overtime, and the value of benefits) and add it to the severance package. | Satisfies the legal “pay‑in‑lieu” exception; avoids litigation. |
| **3. Obtain Signed Acknowledgment** | Have each affected employee sign a **WARN‑Notice Acknowledgement** that confirms receipt of the notice and acceptance of the additional pay. | Provides a documented waiver of any claim that the notice was insufficient. |
| **4. Consult Legal Counsel Immediately** | Engage employment counsel to review the notice language, the “notice‑pay” calculation, and the overall severance offer. | Guarantees compliance with both federal and any applicable state statutes (e.g., California’s “notice‑pay” rule). |
| **5. Communicate the Gap Transparently** | In the group meeting (see Section 1), briefly explain: “Because the law requires 60 days’ notice, we are providing an additional 15‑day payment that will be included in your final paycheck.” | Keeps employees informed and reduces the perception of secrecy. |
| **6. Document All Steps** | Keep a paper/e‑trail of: notice letters, calculations, signed acknowledgments, internal memos, and legal review. | Critical if a WARN claim is later brought. |

## 2. WARN‑Notice Timing – The 45‑Day Gap  

### 2.1 Legal Baseline (Federal WARN Act)

| Requirement | Typical Threshold | Notice Period |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Mass Layoff** (≥ 50 employees at a single site) **or** **Plant Closing** (≥ 50% of workforce at a site) | 50 employees **or** 50% of workforce at a location **and** at least 33% of total workforce | **60 calendar days** written notice to affected employees, the state dis‑employment agency, and the local government. |

*Your situation*: 32 employees at one site (≈ 15% of total headcount) – **does not automatically trigger the federal WARN threshold**. However, many states (e.g., California, New York, Illinois, Washington) have **lower thresholds** (often 25‑30 employees) and/or **“mini‑WARN”** statutes that require **60‑day** notice for a plant closing of **any** size.

### 2.2 Immediate “Gap‑Mitigation” Checklist  

| Action | Who | Deadline | Notes |
|--------|-----|----------|-------|
| **Determine applicable state law** – confirm whether the state where the field office sits has a mini‑WARN or “plant closing” rule that applies to 32 employees. | HR Legal / Outside counsel | **Day 1** | Keep a copy of the statutory language for reference. |
| **If state law requires 60‑day notice** – issue a **pre‑WARN** (informal) notice today (Day 0) to employees and the state agency, stating that a formal WARN notice will follow in 15 days to meet the 60‑day requirement. | HR & Legal | **Day 0** (immediate) | This satisfies the “notice‑in‑writing” element and demonstrates good‑faith effort. |
| **If state law does NOT require 60‑day notice** – still provide **best‑practice 60‑day notice** (or at least 45‑day) to avoid perception risk and potential litigation. | HR | **Day 0** (immediate) | Document the decision‑making process. |
| **Document all communications** – keep signed copies of notices, meeting minutes, and employee acknowledgments. | HR Records | Ongoing | Essential for any later audit or claim. |
| **Prepare a “WARN Gap” FAQ** – explain why the notice period is shorter than the typical 60 days, emphasize the company’s voluntary “extra‑notice” policy, and outline support offered. | Communications | **Day 2** (after legal sign‑off) | Distribute with the written lay‑off notice. |
| **Monitor employee reactions** – set up a confidential hotline or dedicated HR email for questions. | HR | From Day 1 onward | Track trends for possible escalation. |

> **Bottom line:** If any jurisdiction mandates a 60‑day notice, **you must either (a) extend the effective closure date to 60 days** or **(b) issue a provisional notice now and a formal WARN notice in 15 days** to satisfy the statutory period. The safest route is to treat the 45‑day timeline as a **minimum** and add a **15‑day “bridge”** of continued employment (e.g., paid “transition” days) if possible, thereby achieving a full 60‑day window without losing operational flexibility.

### **(2) CRITICAL FLAG: WARN Notice Timing & Jurisdictional Risk**
*Note: This section is flagged first because it dictates the legality and timing of all other actions. The 45-day timeline presents immediate risk.*

#### **A. Threshold Analysis**
*   **Federal WARN:** Likely **NOT TRIGGERED**. Federal WARN generally requires 50+ employees at a single site or 20% of the workforce. With 32 affected employees and a total headcount of 210 (20% threshold = 42 employees), the company appears safe under federal law.
*   **State "Mini-WARN" Risk: HIGH.** Many states have lower thresholds that **WILL** trigger coverage.
    *   *Examples:* Some states trigger at 15% of the workforce, others at 25+ employees, and some have lower notice periods (e.g., 30 days) while others match the 60-day requirement.
    *   *Midstream Context:* Field offices often operate in states with active worker protections. A 32-employee closure may exceed state thresholds even if below federal ones.
*   **Union/CBA Check:** Midstream roles are frequently unionized. If these employees are covered by a Collective Bargaining Agreement, the CBA may require longer notice, bargaining rights, or specific seniority-based layoff procedures that override standard WARN calculations.

#### **B. The 45-Day vs. 60-Day Gap**
*   **Risk:** If a state law requires 60 days notice, a 45-day notice is a violation. This can result in back pay, benefits, and penalties for each day of non-compliance per affected employee.
*   **Immediate Action Required:**
    1.  **Jurisdiction Audit:** Legal counsel must confirm the specific state notice requirement *today*.
    2.  **Pay-In-Lieu-of-Notice (PILON):** If a 60-day notice is required, the company **must** cure the 15-day gap immediately.
        *   *Remedy:* Pay affected employees for the missing 15 days of notice (or provide 15 days of continued benefits). This payment must be explicitly labeled as "PILON" to satisfy the statute.
        *   *Best Practice:* If the company wishes to avoid litigation risk entirely, consider accelerating the communication date to provide the full statutory notice period, or offer a PILON that exceeds the gap (e.g., pay for 20 days to create a buffer).
