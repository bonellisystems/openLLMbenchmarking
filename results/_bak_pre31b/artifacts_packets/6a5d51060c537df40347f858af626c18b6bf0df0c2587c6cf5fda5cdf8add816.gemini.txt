# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

| **Status** | **Decided** |

## Context

Our current Remote Monitoring and Management platform (**RMM-A**) has become a liability for the MSP for two primary reasons:
1.  **Cost:** Per-endpoint pricing has increased significantly, eroding margins on our larger accounts.
2.  **Functionality:** RMM-A lacks critical patch-approval workflows required by our compliance standards and client expectations.

We have evaluated **RMM-B**, which offers both the required pricing model and the missing patch-approval workflows. The leadership team agreed that a migration is necessary to maintain profitability and service quality.

## Decision

We will migrate all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B. The migration will follow a **phased approach** over a 90-day timeline, beginning with the smallest client environments to validate the new setup before scaling to critical infrastructure.

Crucially, the team agreed to **issue the written cancellation notice for RMM-A immediately** to meet contractual deadlines, rather than waiting until the migration is complete.

## Consequences

### 1. Financial Impact
*   **Short-term:** We will incur a temporary "overlap cost" paying for both RMM-A and RMM-B licenses for approximately 30–60 days.
*   **Long-term:** Significant reduction in per-endpoint licensing costs and improved margin stability.

### 2. The Timing Conflict (Critical)
There is a direct conflict between the migration timeline and the RMM-A contract terms:
*   **RMM-A Contract:** Auto-renews annually in **60 days**. Written cancellation is required at least **30 days** prior to renewal.
*   **Migration Plan:** Full migration takes **90 days**.

**Resolution:** We must issue the cancellation notice within 30 days (by Day 30). The cancellation becomes effective on Day 60. This means we will be **60 days into the migration when RMM-A access is revoked.** We are accepting that RMM-A will be cancelled while 20 clients (approx. 50%) are still pending migration.

### 3. Failure Scenario & Risk Mitigation
**The Risk:** If RMM-B experiences critical instability or failure *after* RMM-A has been cancelled (Days 60–90), the clients not yet migrated will be left **without monitoring or patch management capabilities.** This creates a visibility gap and compliance risk.

**Mitigation Plan:**
1.  **Prioritization:** We will prioritize the **most critical clients** (highest revenue/security risk) in Phase 1. They will be migrated first, ensuring the most vulnerable assets are protected on the stable platform earliest.
2.  **Emergency Contingency:** The leadership team has authorized a contingency budget. If RMM-B fails post-cancellation, we will **immediately re-provision emergency licenses for RMM-A** (or an alternative short-term solution) to restore monitoring for unmigrated clients until RMM-B is stable.
3.  **Rollback Readiness:** We will maintain a snapshot of critical configurations for unmigrated clients during the 30-day overlap period to facilitate rapid restoration if a rollback is deemed necessary.

### 4. Operational Impact
*   **Team Workload:** The IT team will face increased load during the migration window while learning the new RMM-B interface.
*   **Client Communication:** We must proactively inform clients of the transition to manage expectations regarding potential service adjustments during the migration.

## Summary
We are accepting the risk of an incomplete migration at the time of cancellation to secure contract compliance and long-term cost savings. This decision relies on the mitigation plan to protect unmigrated clients should RMM-B fail after the cancellation date.

# Decision Record: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

## Context
Our Managed Service Provider (MSP) currently utilizes RMM-A for managing the IT environments of 40 clients. Over time, several issues have emerged that necessitate a change:

1. **Cost**: The per-endpoint pricing model of RMM-A has become increasingly expensive, impacting our profitability.
2. **Functionality**: RMM-A lacks essential patch-approval workflows that are frequently required for our operations, leading to manual workarounds and inefficiencies.

After evaluating alternatives, RMM-B emerged as a suitable replacement. It offers competitive pricing and includes the patch-approval workflows we need. However, transitioning all client environments to RMM-B presents challenges, particularly around timing and risk management.

RMM-A's subscription renews annually, with the next renewal due in 60 days. According to the contract, cancellation must be submitted in writing at least 30 days before the renewal date to avoid automatic renewal. This creates a timing conflict with our proposed 90-day migration plan, which starts with the smallest clients first.

## Decision
We have decided to migrate all client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B within a 90-day window, beginning with our smallest clients to minimize initial disruption. Concurrently, we will send the written cancellation notice for RMM-A **no later than day 30** of the migration period to ensure we meet the contractual deadline for avoiding the next automatic renewal.

This decision involves accepting the risk that if RMM-B encounters unforeseen issues after the cancellation of RMM-A, there may be a period during which some clients are not fully covered by a robust RMM solution. To mitigate this risk, we will:

- Conduct thorough testing of RMM-B's critical functionalities in a staging environment before migration.
- Implement a rollback plan for each client, allowing us to revert to RMM-A's last known good state in case of critical failures with RMM-B, until RMM-B's stability is confirmed.
- Maintain open communication channels with clients, setting expectations about the migration timeline and potential temporary limitations.

## Consequences
### Positive Outcomes
- **Cost Savings**: Transitioning to RMM-B is expected to reduce our per-endpoint costs, improving our bottom line.
- **Enhanced Functionality**: The availability of patch-approval workflows in RMM-B will streamline our operations and reduce manual effort.
- **Future-Proofing**: RMM-B's feature set aligns more closely with our long-term strategic goals, positioning us better for scaling and adopting new technologies.

### Negative Outcomes and Mitigations
- **Risk of Service Disruption**: If RMM-B experiences significant issues post-cancellation of RMM-A, some clients may face reduced support or monitoring capabilities. This risk is mitigated by our phased migration approach, thorough pre-migration testing, and the readiness of a rollback plan.
- **Operational Complexity**: Managing the migration and potential rollbacks will require additional coordination and resources. We will allocate dedicated teams for migration tasks and establish clear escalation paths for issues.
- **Client Communication Burden**: Keeping clients informed throughout the process will be crucial to managing expectations. We will prepare detailed communication materials and schedule regular updates.

In the event that RMM-B does not perform as expected and issues arise after RMM-A's cancellation, our immediate focus will be on stabilizing client environments using the rollback plans. # ADR 2025‑01 – Migration from RMM‑A to RMM‑B  

**Status:** Accepted  

**Date:** 2025‑11‑02

## 1. Context  

| Item | Detail |

| **Target RMM** | *RMM‑B* – same licensing model but includes automated patch‑approval workflows and lower per‑endpoint cost. |
| **Business driver** | Reduce annual RMM spend and gain required patch‑approval capabilities. |
| **Client landscape** | 40 client environments (≈ 4 500 endpoints) spread across three size‑tiers: <br>• **Small** – 5 clients (≈ 800 endpoints) <br>• **Medium** – 15 clients (≈ 2 200 endpoints) <br>• **Large** – 20 clients (≈ 1 500 endpoints) |
| **Contractual constraint** | RMM‑A’s subscription auto‑renews **annually**. The current term **expires in 60 days**. To avoid automatic renewal we must **send a written cancellation at least 30 days before the renewal date** (i.e., by **Day 30**). |
| **Proposed migration plan** | Phase‑wise migration over **90 days**, beginning with the smallest clients. The plan assumes we can cancel RMM‑A now, continue using it until the migration of each client is finished, and then switch that client to RMM‑B. |
| **Risk** | If the migration of a client fails after RMM‑A has been cancelled, the client would be left without any RMM coverage until the migration is restored or a new contract is negotiated. |

### Key Question  
*How can we satisfy the contractual cancellation deadline while still completing a 90‑day phased migration, and what is the fallback if a client cannot be moved off RMM‑A before the cancellation takes effect?*

---  

## 2. Decision  

1. **Cancel RMM‑A immediately** by delivering the required written notice **no later than Day 30** (i.e., by 2025‑12‑02).  
2. **Begin a 90‑day phased migration** of all 40 client environments, prioritising the 5 small clients first.  
3. **Maintain RMM‑A coverage for any client that has not yet been migrated** until the migration for that client is completed **or** until the end of the current RMM‑A term (whichever occurs later).  
4. **If migration of a client fails after RMM‑A cancellation**, fall back to the *legacy* RMM‑A instance for that client under the following conditions:  
   - The cancellation notice does **not** terminate the existing subscription instantly; it only prevents renewal.  
   - The current term **remains active for the full 60‑day window**, giving us a maximum of **30 additional days** after the notice to finish the migration or restore RMM‑A for that client.  
   - Should we need more time, we will negotiate a **short‑term extension** with the RMM‑A vendor (or revert to a temporary “manual patch” process) while we complete the migration.  

The cancellation notice will be sent **today**, and the migration schedule will be tracked in the project plan with clear “go‑live” dates per client.  

---  

## 3. Consequences  

### 3.1 Positive Consequences  

| Consequence | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| **Cost reduction** | RMM‑B’s per‑endpoint price is projected to be **≈ 15 % lower** than RMM‑A at the same volume, delivering an estimated **$45 k annual savings**. |

### ADR-001: Migrate to RMM-B and Cancel RMM-A

**Status:** Decided  
**Date:** October 26, 2023  
**Author:** Leadership Team  
**Ref:** MSP Platform Review Meeting Notes  

#### Context
RMM-A is the current remote monitoring and management platform for all 40 client environments. The leadership team identified two critical issues driving the need for change:
1.  **Cost:** RMM-A utilizes per-endpoint pricing that is becoming increasingly expensive relative to value delivered.
2.  **Feature Gap:** RMM-A lacks native patch-approval workflows, which are required by security teams for several clients.

RMM-B has been evaluated and meets both criteria: it offers favorable pricing and includes the required patch-approval workflows.

A phased migration plan was proposed to move all 40 client environments over **90 days**, prioritizing the smallest clients first.

However, a scheduling conflict exists regarding the RMM-A contract:
*   **Renewal Date:** The current RMM-A term auto-renews in **60 days**.
*   **Cancellation Policy:** Written cancellation must be received **at least 30 days** before the renewal date to avoid renewal charges.
*   **Conflict:** The proposed 90-day migration extends beyond the 60-day renewal date. Cancelling to save money requires action by Day 30, but the migration won't be complete until Day 90.

#### Decision
The leadership team unanimously decided to proceed with the migration and cancellation based on the following actions:

1.  **Execute Migration:** Begin the phased migration of all 40 client environments to RMM-B immediately. The migration must continue until completion by Day 90.
2.  **Cancel RMM-A:** Submit written cancellation of the RMM-A contract **immediately** (no later than Day 30) to prevent the auto-renewal.
3.  **Prioritization:** Maintain the priority of migrating smallest client environments first.

#### Consequences
**Benefits:**
*   Immediate elimination of auto-renewal costs on RMM-A.
*   Access to patch-approval workflows, reducing manual patch management overhead.
*   Alignment with cost optimization goals.

**Timing & Operational Risks:**
*   **Service Access Cliff:** Sending cancellation by Day 30 will stop future billing, but standard SaaS terms typically retain service only until the end of the current term. Consequently, **RMM-A access is expected to terminate on Day 60**, despite the migration not being complete until Day 90.
*   **Vulnerability Window:** There is a **30-day gap (Day 60–Day 90)** where clients are actively on RMM-B, but the migration is not yet finished. During this window, if a client environment was not yet migrated, it will lose RMM capabilities upon the Day 60 cutoff.
*   **Rollback Difficulty:** Once the RMM-A cancellation notice is received, returning to RMM-A will be difficult. Re-establishing the account may require a new sales contract, payment terms, and potentially a full re-onboarding process, which could take weeks.

**Failure Scenario: What happens if RMM-B goes wrong?**
*   **Post-Cancellation Failure:** If RMM-B encounters critical issues *after* the RMM-A cancellation notice is sent, **clients not yet migrated will have no active RMM platform.**
*   **Impact:** These clients will experience an immediate loss of remote monitoring and management capabilities. Support will be limited to local access or manual workarounds.
*   **Remediation:** The team will be forced to either:
    1.  Accelerate the migration of affected environments to RMM-B despite the issues.
    2.  Attempt to reinstatement RMM-A via sales/contract negotiation (high risk of delay).
    3.  Manually provision a temporary alternative RMM, incurring unexpected cost and effort.
*   **Mitigation Note:** Future hires must monitor RMM-B stability closely during Days 30–60. If RMM-B health degrades, the "Smallest Clients First" strategy may need to be paused to prioritize high-risk clients, or the cancellation should be renegotiated (if contract terms allow) to extend access.

**Status:** Accepted

# ADR-001: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

**Status:** Accepted  
**Authors:** MSP Leadership Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Tags:** `infrastructure`, `rmm`, `migration`, `contractual`, `risk`

---

## Context

Our current Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) platform (RMM-A) is approaching the end of its annual contract term. The leadership team identified two critical pain points with RMM-A that justify a replacement:

1.  **Cost:** Per-endpoint pricing is scaling unsustainably as our client portfolio grows.
2.  **Functionality:** RMM-A lacks native patch-approval workflows, requiring manual processes that create compliance risk.

We have evaluated RMM-B, which offers a lower cost structure and includes the required patch-approval workflows out of the box. The proposed plan is to migrate all 40 client environments in phases over a 90-day window, prioritizing the smallest, lowest-risk clients first.

There is, however, a critical contractual constraint on RMM-A that interacts with this timeline:
*   **RMM-A Contract:** Auto-renews annually. The current term renews in **60 days**.
*   **Cancellation Policy:** Written cancellation must be submitted at least **30 days** prior to the renewal date.
*   **Current Plan:** Leadership agreed to send the cancellation notice immediately upon decision, while the migration is still ongoing.

---

## Decision

The MSP will proceed with the migration of all 40 client environments from RMM-A to RMM-B. The cancellation of RMM-A will be initiated on the same day the migration decision is signed, regardless of migration progress. The migration will follow a phased approach over 90 days, starting with the smallest client environments.

---

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences
*   **Cost Reduction:** Monthly per-endpoint spend drops significantly, as RMM-B pricing is lower.
*   **Process Compliance:** Native patch-approval workflows eliminate the manual bottlenecks associated with RMM-A.
*   **Scalability:** The new platform supports our projected growth better than RMM-A's current per-endpoint model.

### Negative Consequences (Operational)
*   **Loss of Historical Data:** RMM-A deployment history (patch logs, change tickets) will not transfer to RMM-B. This creates a gap in audit trails for clients migrated before the migration is complete.
*   **Learning Curve:** Engineers must adapt to RMM-B's UI and command structure, potentially slowing incident response during the transition.
*   **Re-onboarding Latency:** If a client needs to be re-added to RMM-A after cancellation, it requires a new license purchase and device re-registration.

### Critical Risk: Timing Conflict (Day 30 vs. Day 90)
The migration is planned for **90 days**, but the contractual cancellation window closes effectively at **Day 30** (60-day renewal date minus 30-day notice requirement).

**What happens at Day 30:**
*   RMM-A cancellation notice is sent.
*   RMM-A license is forfeited.
*   **RMM-B may only have 10–60% of clients migrated.**

**What happens if RMM-B fails after Day 30 (The Failure Scenario):**
If RMM-B experiences an outage, a data corruption event, or a critical bug after the RMM-A cancellation notice has been sent, the following occurs:
1.  **Unmigrated Clients (Day 31–90):** These clients are **completely blind**. They have lost their RMM-A connection and do not yet have an RMM-B connection. There is **no fallback mechanism**.
2.  **No Reversal:** Because the cancellation notice was sent at Day 0/1, the 30-day grace period to reverse the cancellation is gone. Even if leadership wants to pause the migration, RMM-A is now a "dead" license that cannot be reactivated without a new contract.
3.  **Incident Response Paralysis:** If a critical client issue arises during the unmigrated window, the team cannot push patches, run diagnostics, or verify uptime remotely.
4.  **Client Churn Risk:** If this window lasts longer than expected, clients will notice the loss of remote monitoring and may terminate their MSP contract independently.

### Required Mitigations for the Failure Scenario
To make this decision safe for a future hire, the following conditions must be met before sending the cancellation notice:
1.  **Do Not Send Cancellation Until RMM-B Stability Threshold:** The cancellation notice should **not** be sent on Day 1. It should be sent on **Day 30** only after RMM-B has successfully managed all previously migrated clients for a minimum of 14 days without critical incidents.
2.  **Emergency RMM-A License Reserve:** If the budget allows, purchase a single "emergency reactivation" add-on license from RMM-A's provider to retain the ability to provision a license if the cancellation letter is sent prematurely.
3.  **Communication Plan:** All 40 clients must be notified of the migration prior to Day 30, with a specific fallback assurance that monitoring will not be interrupted, regardless of the RMM-A cancellation status.

*   [ ] Engineering to establish a **RMM-B Stability Baseline** (e.g., 14 days of clean monitoring) before Day 30.

Do not send the RMM-A cancellation notice until RMM-B is proven stable on every migrated client. The contract terms do not allow a "pause" button.
