## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

Here is the audit for KB-114. This document presents significant compliance risk. Below is the breakdown by category:

### (a) Stale Standards, Versions, or Requirements

1.  **PCI DSS v3.2.1 Reference:** The article explicitly cites "PCI DSS v3.2.1." This version was **retired in March 2022** and replaced by **PCI DSS v4.0.0**. While the requirement for quarterly external vulnerability scans (Req 11.3.6) exists in both versions, the v3.2.1 standard is no longer compliant evidence. Auditors will reject documentation referencing a superseded standard.
2.  **Date of Publication:** The article states "Last updated: March 2019." This is **over 5 years old**. In the PCI compliance landscape, standards shift every 2–3 years (v3.1, v3.2, v3.2.1, v4.0, v4.0.1). A document this age cannot reflect current ASV agreements, reporting formats, or audit expectations.
3.  **Product Name ("QualysGuard"):** While Qualys still exists, "QualysGuard" is legacy branding. The product has been restructured into the **Qualys Cloud Platform** (with specific vulnerability scanning modules). Referencing "QualysGuard" may confuse auditors regarding which specific ASV contract is active, or the portal login path referenced in the article may have changed to the new platform architecture.

### (b) Elements I Cannot Confirm From Reading (Requires Verification)

If the client has added new locations or expanded e-commerce infrastructure, the static instruction to scan "all" ranges needs to be tied to a current, approved IP inventory.
3.  **Email Addresses:** `compliance-distro@ourclient.com` and `jsmith@ourclient.com` are almost certainly stale. `compliance-distro` implies a distribution list that likely has rotated. If the email is bounced or the recipient is no longer a PCI-authorized personnel on the client side, the attestation chain is broken.
4.  **Reporting Format:** The instruction says "Export the PDF report." ### (c) Instructions That Are Actually Wrong to Follow Today

1.  **"High findings are fine... just note it in the file and move on."**
    *   **This is a direct violation of PCI DSS v4.0.0 (Req 6.5 and Req 11.3.6) and v3.2.1.**
    *   PCI DSS explicitly requires that vulnerabilities rated **High or Critical** be **remediated or mitigated** within a specific timeframe (typically 30 days under v3.2.1; defined by risk in v4.0).
    *   Tolerating High findings indefinitely by simply "noting it in the file" constitutes a failure of the Vulnerability Management Program. An auditor seeing this instruction would immediately classify the client as non-compliant.
    *   **Action Required:** The article must explicitly state the remediation SLA (e.g., "Critical/High findings must be resolved within 30 days or risk-mitigated with compensating controls documented").

2.  **Reliance on "QualysGuard" for the ASV designation.**
    *   If the MSP's contract is under the old QualysGuard branding but the current ASV agreement is under the new Qualys Cloud Platform umbrella, running scans under the old brand name might cause the report to be rejected by the payment brands (Visa/Mastercard) as invalid scan evidence.

3.  **No mention of Vulnerability Management Program (VMP).**
    *   PCI DSS v4.0.0 introduced the requirement for a formal Vulnerability Management Program (Req 6.5). This article treats scanning as a standalone quarterly event. A compliant process must demonstrate how the scan results feed into the broader VMP, not just "note it and move on."
