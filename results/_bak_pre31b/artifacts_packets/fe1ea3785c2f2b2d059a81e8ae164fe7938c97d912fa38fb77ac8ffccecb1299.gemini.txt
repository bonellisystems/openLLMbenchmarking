### Explicit Claim‑Avoidance Guidelines (due to pending FDA clearance)

1. **No Statements of Clinical Accuracy** – Do **not** say the test (or any test) “accurately detects,” “has X% sensitivity/specificity,” or “provides definitive diagnosis.”  
2. **No Diagnostic Performance Metrics** – Avoid mentioning “true positive rate,” “false negative rate,” “clinical validation results,” or any quantitative performance data.  
3. **No Medical Efficacy Claims** – Do **not** assert that using the test will “prevent disease,” “reduce risk,” “improve health outcomes,” or “replace professional medical advice.”  
4. **No Implied Clearance** – Do **not** suggest the test is “FDA‑cleared,” “FDA‑approved,” or “ready for clinical use” until formal clearance is obtained. Use neutral language such as “currently under review” or “pending regulatory clearance.”  
5. **No Comparative Advantage** – Refrain from stating the upcoming test is “more accurate than,” “faster than,” or “superior to” existing offerings.  
6. **Focus on Education & Empowerment** – All content should center on **knowledge building**, **patient empowerment**, and **understanding the testing ecosystem**. Use phrases like “learn about,” “explore,” “consider,” and “discuss with your healthcare provider.”

### **⚠️ Compliance Guardrails: Explicitly Prohibited Claims**
Given the pending clinical validation and FDA clearance status, all content must adhere to strict compliance boundaries. The following claims are **strictly prohibited** until clearance is granted:

1.  **Diagnostic Claims:** No statements implying the test can diagnose a disease, condition, or disorder.
2.  **Performance Metrics:** No mention of sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), or overall accuracy.
3.  **Medical Efficacy:** No claims that the test will influence medical treatment, prevent disease, or provide actionable clinical outcomes.
4.  **Comparative Claims:** No comparisons to clinical laboratories, physician-ordered tests, or competitor products regarding performance.
5.  **Status Misrepresentation:** Content must never imply the test is "FDA Cleared," "Approved," or "Clinically Validated." It may describe the test as "Undergoing review" or "Pending clearance" if describing status, but marketing focus must remain on education/trust, not regulatory milestones.
6.  **Risk Assessment Claims:** Avoid implying the test can accurately assess disease risk or predisposition until cleared. Educational content may discuss genetic risk concepts generally, but cannot link risk assessment to the upcoming product.

| **Diagnostic Performance Metrics** | Statements about “specificity”, “positive predictive value”, “negative predictive value”, “likelihood ratios”, or any statistical performance numbers unless they are from a fully validated, cleared assay. |
| **Medical Efficacy or Treatment Claims** | “The test will help you cure…”, “Taking the test will reduce your risk of…”, “You will know exactly how to treat your condition”. |
| **Therapeutic or Preventive Benefit Claims** | “You can prevent cancer by testing”, “Testing will tell you which diet is best for you”. |
| **Regulatory Endorsement Claims** | “FDA‑approved”, “Cleared by the FDA”, “Certified by the CLIA”, unless referencing the pending status in a factual, non‑promotional way. |
| **Outcome Guarantees** | “You will always get a definitive answer”, “No false‑negative results”. |
| **Direct Medical Advice** | “Based on your result, you should start medication X”, “You should undergo surgery now”. |
| **Comparative Claims** | “Our test is better than any other at‑home kit”, “Outperforms standard clinical testing”. |
| **Specific Gene‑Disease Links** | “If you carry the APOE‑ε4 allele you will develop Alzheimer’s”, unless presented purely as an educational example without implying certainty or medical relevance. |

**What is permissible instead?**  
- Use **neutral, educational language** such as “The test may provide information about…”, “Users can learn about their genetic makeup”, “Results are for informational purposes only and should be discussed with a qualified health professional.”  
- Emphasize **the process, purpose, and limitations** of genetic testing without promising health outcomes.  
- Keep all statements **factual, non‑promotional, and clearly labeled as educational** rather than diagnostic or therapeutic.

### Pre-Launch Content Strategy: Consumer Genetic Diagnostics

**Objective:** Build organic awareness, establish thought leadership, and foster trust in the genetic testing category over a 10-week window while strictly adhering to compliance constraints regarding pending clinical validation and FDA clearance.

#### 1. Campaign Metrics
*   **Total Content Pieces:** 20 Educational Blog Posts (10 weeks × 2 posts/week).
*   **Focus:** Category education, scientific literacy, data trust, patient journey, and lifestyle context. **No direct mentions of the specific test product, its results, or its regulatory status.**

#### 2. Explicit Claims to Avoid
Given that clinical validation and FDA clearance are pending, all content must strictly avoid the following claim types. **Legal/Medical Review is recommended before publication of any draft.**

*   **Clinical Accuracy Claims:** No statements regarding the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, or percentage accuracy of the test (e.g., "99% accurate," "detects 95% of variants").
*   **Diagnostic Performance Claims:** No language implying the ability to diagnose, identify, or confirm specific conditions or traits. Avoid terms like "screening," "detection," "diagnosis," "flagging," or "predicting" in relation to the test's output.
*   **Medical Efficacy/Outcome Claims:** No assertions that the test improves health outcomes, prevents disease, changes lifestyle for health benefits, or assists in treatment decisions. Avoid "helps you manage," "enables better care," or "optimal results."
*   **Regulatory Status Claims:** Do not mention "FDA clearance," "Pending FDA review," or "Clinical trials in progress." This avoids implying a specific status that could be construed as a claim or create false expectations about the timeline.
*   **Variant-Specific Claims:** No discussion of specific genes, mutations, or markers relevant to the test's intended use.
*   **"Gold Standard" or "Superior" Claims:** No comparison to other tests or lab methods suggesting superiority.

#### 3. Topic Categories
To build trust and awareness without violating constraints, the calendar is structured around five educational pillars:

1.  **Foundations of Genetics:** Basic science, DNA mechanics, heritability, and the difference between genotype/phenotype.
2.  **The At-Home Testing Landscape:** How sample collection works, lab logistics, data processing flows, and the role of certified laboratories.
3.  **Data Privacy & Security:** Best practices for digital health, data ownership, encryption, and consumer rights in genetic data.
4.  **Patient Journey & Clinical Context:** How to prepare for genetic testing, interpreting results generally, and the importance of healthcare provider consultation.
5.  **Genetic Wellness & Lifestyle:** Holistic health, the role of genetics in a broader health picture, and managing expectations around genetic data.
