## 10 -- Frontier
The answer correctly distinguishes general legal information from jurisdiction-specific legal advice, and flags when licensed counsel review is genuinely required. Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Clear line drawn between general information and advice requiring licensed counsel.

# Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
**Applicable Devices:** Shop-Floor Tablets & Corporate Laptops
**Effective Date:** [Date]

### Section 1: Personal Use and Monitoring Rights

**1.1 Reasonable Personal Use**
Employees are permitted to use corporate devices for reasonable personal purposes during non-business hours or during breaks, provided such use does not interfere with work performance, violate security policies, or incur significant data costs. Examples of permitted personal use include checking personal email or browsing personal websites during approved break times.

**1.2 Monitoring Right**
The Company reserves the right to monitor all device activity, including but not limited to email content, internet browsing history, application usage, file transfers, and network traffic. This includes data transmitted over or stored on the Corporate Network and the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control-Systems (ICS) Network.

**1.3 Protected Concerted Activity Exclusion**
Nothing in this Section 1 shall be construed as authorizing monitoring or discipline for any activity protected under Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), or any applicable state labor law. Specifically, this policy **does not** apply to employees discussing:
*   Wages, hours, and other terms of employment;
*   Working conditions with co-workers or representatives;
*   Union organizing or collective action.

Monitoring data that reveals participation in such protected activity will not be used to discipline the employee for the protected activity itself.

---

### Section 2: Prohibited Uses

The following activities are strictly prohibited, regardless of the employee's job role or location:

**2.1 OT/ICS Network Restrictions**
*   **Personal Email on ICS Network:** Employees shall not open, send, or receive personal email attachments or access personal email accounts while connected to the OT/ICS Network. The OT/ICS Network is a restricted zone dedicated to manufacturing control systems, and personal traffic introduces malware, phishing vectors, and potential safety risks.
*   **Network Segmentation:** Devices authorized on the Corporate Network are **not** authorized on the OT/ICS Network. Physical or logical separation must be maintained at all times.

**2.2 USB and Storage Device Restrictions**
*   **Unapproved Personal USB Drives:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, flash drives, or external storage devices to production-line PCs or shop-floor tablets is prohibited.
*   **Approved Media Only:** Only Company-approved storage media, configured with the latest antivirus definitions and endpoint protection, may be connected to production-line systems.

---

### Section 3: Disclosure and Acknowledgment

**3.1 Prior Disclosure**
The Company shall provide a copy of this AUP (including the specific sections regarding monitoring and OT safety) to every employee prior to the issuance of their assigned device. The Company shall make a good-faith effort to ensure the employee has read and understood the policy through verbal explanation by the direct supervisor or IT security team.

**3.2 Written Acknowledgment**
Prior to receiving a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet, the employee must sign a Device Acknowledgment Form indicating that they:
*   Have received a copy of this AUP;
*   Understand their obligations regarding OT/ICS network security and USB device restrictions;
*   Acknowledge that they understand the Company's right to monitor device and network activity as described in Section 1;
*   Confirm that they understand the disciplinary consequences for violations, including those tied to safety-critical systems.

The signed form must be retained in the employee's personnel file.

---

### Section 4: Consequences of Violation

**4.1 General Violations**
Violations of Sections 1 and 2 that do not directly impact safety-critical systems may result in a range of disciplinary actions, up to and including termination, depending on the severity, frequency, and intent of the violation.

**4.2 Safety-Critical OT Violations**
Violations involving the OT/ICS Network or production-line PCs that pose a risk to physical safety, product quality, or critical infrastructure integrity are treated with zero tolerance. Disciplinary actions for such violations may include immediate suspension without pay or immediate termination of employment. This applies specifically to:
*   Unauthorized use of personal email on the ICS network;
*   Connection of unapproved USB drives to production-line PCs;
*   Any action that disrupts automated control systems, sensors, or manufacturing processes.

**4.3 Protected Activity Safeguard**
Discipline imposed under this Section 4 is solely based on the technical violation of the AUP (e.g., connecting a USB drive to a production PC). **Nothing in this Section 4 shall be applied to, or used to punish, employees for engaging in protected concerted activity regarding wages, hours, or working conditions.** If an investigation reveals that a violation was reported by or in the context of a protected activity (e.g., a worker reporting an unsafe condition on the production line), the employee shall be protected from retaliatory discipline.

---

### Section 5: Pre-Rollout Implementation Notes

Before the rollout of this AUP, the following steps should be taken to ensure legal defensibility and operational effectiveness:

1.  **Employment Counsel Review:** This is the most critical step. Employment counsel should review the monitoring carve-out (Section 1.3 and 4.3) to ensure it withstands an NLRB challenge. Language that is too broad or vague regarding monitoring can inadvertently create an "unfair labor practice." Counsel should verify that the NLRA carve-out is explicit enough to avoid a "chilling effect" on Section 7 rights.
2.  **ICS Security Validation:** The IT/OT security team should verify that the network segmentation described in Section 2.1 is physically and logically enforceable. If the OT/ICS network is not truly air-gapped or segmented from the Corporate Network, the policy cannot be enforced, and the company could be liable for negligence regarding safety.
3.  **Employee Training:** Do not simply distribute the document. Conduct mandatory training sessions for shop-floor staff and supervisors to explain the OT/ICS risks and the USB restrictions.
4.  **Device Configuration:** Ensure that endpoint protection and network access controls are configured *before* devices are issued to enforce the policy technically (e.g., block personal USB ports on production PCs, disable personal email on OT VLANs).
5.  **Union Communication (If Applicable):** If the workforce is unionized or if there is potential for unionization, legal counsel should advise on the timing of the rollout relative to any NLRB election or collective bargaining agreement negotiations to avoid accusations of pre-empting protected rights.

**Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) for Corporate Devices**  
*Effective Date: [Insert Date]*  
*Applicable to: All employees issued corporate laptops or shop-floor tablets at [Company Name]*

---

### 1. **Permissible Use and Monitoring Rights**

Employees may use company-issued laptops and shop-floor tablets (collectively, “corporate devices”) for both business purposes and reasonable personal use, provided such use does not interfere with job responsibilities, consume excessive network resources, or compromise security. Personal use must comply with all company policies and applicable laws.

The company reserves the right to monitor, access, audit, and log all activity on corporate devices and associated network systems—including, but not limited to, internet usage, application activity, file storage, email communications, and connection logs. This includes monitoring on both corporate networks and remote connections. Monitoring is conducted to ensure system integrity, network security, regulatory compliance, and operational continuity, particularly within safety-critical environments.

---

### 2. **Prohibited Uses**

The following activities are strictly prohibited on corporate devices, especially those connected to or in proximity to operational technology (OT) and industrial control systems (ICS) networks:

- **Opening personal email attachments** (e.g., from Gmail, Yahoo, personal Outlook accounts, etc.) on any device connected to or capable of connecting to the OT/ICS network. This prohibition is in place to prevent malware infiltration that could compromise production systems, safety controls, or data integrity.
  
- **Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, flash drives, external hard drives, or other removable media** to any production-line PC or device interfacing with OT/ICS systems. Only company-issued and pre-approved media may be used in such environments.

Violations of these prohibitions may result in immediate disciplinary action due to the high risk they pose to operational safety and system reliability.

---

### 3. **Disclosure and Employee Acknowledgment**

All employees must be clearly informed that monitoring of corporate devices and network activity occurs as described in this policy. Prior to issuance of any corporate device, employees will:

- Receive a copy of this Acceptable Use Policy;
- Receive training or written explanation of key provisions, particularly those relating to OT/ICS security and monitoring;
- Sign a formal acknowledgment form confirming they have read, understood, and agreed to comply with this policy.

Signed acknowledgments will be retained in the employee’s personnel file. Devices will not be issued until the acknowledgment is completed.

---

### 4. **Consequences for Policy Violations**

Violations of this Acceptable Use Policy, particularly those involving the OT/ICS environment, may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment, especially when the violation:

- Poses a risk to personnel safety;
- Compromises the integrity, availability, or confidentiality of control systems;
- Introduces malware or unauthorized software into production networks;
- Demonstrates willful or repeated disregard for security protocols.

Disciplinary measures will be determined based on the nature, severity, and intent behind the violation, in consultation with Human Resources, IT Security, and relevant operational leadership.

---

### 5. **Protection of Employee Rights Under Labor Law**

Nothing in this policy is intended to restrict or discourage employees from engaging in protected concerted activities as defined under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) and other applicable federal labor laws. Specifically, employees have the right to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, safety concerns, and terms of employment with coworkers, labor representatives, or regulatory agencies—**even when using company devices or networks**, provided such communications do not involve harassment, disclosure of confidential information, or other independently prohibited conduct.

Monitoring under this policy will not be used to suppress or retaliate against employees for engaging in such protected discussions. Any interpretation of this policy that could conflict with these rights is explicitly disclaimed.

4. **Acknowledgment Process**: Finalize the employee acknowledgment form and integrate it into the device issuance workflow.
5. **Communication Plan**: Announce the policy rollout with clear messaging emphasizing safety, security, and employee rights to ensure broad understanding and acceptance.

Once these steps are complete, the policy may be formally adopted and enforced.

Before rolling this policy out to the shop floor and office, the following steps are highly recommended:

* the activity does not interfere with job performance, safety, or the security of the OT/ICS environment; and

1.2 **Monitoring** – To protect the integrity of our production systems, the Company reserves the right to monitor, record, and review:  

* all network traffic that traverses corporate or OT/ICS segments;  
* system logs, file access, application usage, and peripheral device connections on the issued devices; and  
* any data stored on, transmitted by, or retrieved from the devices.

Monitoring will be conducted in accordance with applicable law and the Company’s privacy guidelines. No expectation of privacy exists for data created, stored, or transmitted on corporate devices.

### 2. Prohibited Uses (Safety‑Critical OT/ICS Environment)

| **Prohibited Action** | **Why It Is Prohibited** | **Scope** |

| Using the corporate device as a **network gateway** (e.g., tethering, hotspot, VPN “bridge”) to route non‑company traffic through the OT/ICS network. | Provides a conduit for external threats to reach the control system. | All tablets and laptops, whether on‑site or remote. |

### 1. Scope and Reasonable Personal Use
This policy governs the use of company-issued corporate laptops and shop-floor tablets. These devices are company property provided to enable employees to perform their job duties efficiently and securely.

**Reasonable Personal Use:** Employees are permitted to use corporate devices for reasonable personal activities, provided that such use:
*   Occurs during non-work hours or designated break times;
*   Does not interfere with job performance or productivity;
*   Does not violate any other company policy, law, or security protocol; and
*   Does not involve activities that could compromise the safety or integrity of Operational Technology (OT) or Industrial Control Systems (ICS).

### 2. Monitoring and Privacy Disclosure
**Monitoring Rights:** The Company reserves the right to monitor, record, audit, and inspect all activity on corporate devices and networks at any time. This includes, but is not limited to:
*   Internet browsing history, email traffic, and instant messaging logs;
*   File creation, modification, storage, and transfer;
*   Application usage and keystroke logging where security software is deployed;
*   Location data and usage timestamps on shop-floor tablets.

**No Expectation of Privacy:** Employees should have no expectation of privacy regarding any data, communications, or activities conducted on corporate devices or networks. All data stored on or transmitted through these devices is considered company property and may be accessed by authorized IT and security personnel without notice.

### 3. Prohibited Uses and OT/ICS Safety Restrictions
To protect critical manufacturing infrastructure and ensure worker safety, the following uses are strictly prohibited. Violations of these restrictions are treated as serious safety and security breaches.

**OT/ICS Network Restrictions:**
*   **Personal Email Attachments:** Employees must **never** open, save, or interact with attachments from personal email accounts on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes shop-floor tablets and production-line PCs that interface with manufacturing control systems.
*   **Unapproved USB Devices:** Employees are prohibited from connecting personal USB drives, mobile phones, or other external storage media to production-line PCs or any terminal connected to the OT/ICS network unless explicitly approved in writing by the OT Security team.

**General Prohibitions:**
*   Installation of unauthorized software or hardware modifications.
*   Bypassing security controls, firewalls, or authentication mechanisms.
*   Use of devices for illegal activities or harassment.

### 4. Disciplinary Actions and Consequences
Violations of this AUP may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment, depending on the severity of the violation and the risk posed to the company.

**Safety-Critical Violations:**
Violations involving the OT/ICS network restrictions listed in Section 3 are considered **Gross Misconduct**. Because these violations pose a direct risk to physical safety, equipment integrity, and production continuity, they are subject to the most severe disciplinary measures. **Any violation of OT/ICS restrictions may result in immediate termination of employment, regardless of tenure or prior disciplinary record.**

### 5. Protected Concerted Activity Carve-Out
**Preservation of Labor Rights:** Nothing in this AUP prohibits, restricts, or interferes with any rights employees have under federal, state, or local law.

Specifically, this policy **does not** prohibit employees from engaging in protected concerted activities. Employees retain the right to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, terms of employment, and other matters of mutual concern with one another. Employees may use corporate devices to engage in these protected discussions, provided such use is reasonable and does not violate the specific technical safety restrictions applicable to OT/ICS devices (e.g., using a production-line PC solely for safety-critical operations remains required even during discussions). This carve-out ensures that the Company's monitoring and usage rules are not interpreted to suppress protected labor activity.

### 6. Acknowledgment and Signature Requirement
**Mandatory Acknowledgment:** To ensure clear disclosure and understanding, every employee must review and sign the AUP Acknowledgment Form **before** the issuance of any corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet.

By signing the acknowledgment, the employee confirms that:
