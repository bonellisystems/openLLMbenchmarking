# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

Your MSP runs a weekly backup-verification process for a structural-engineering firm's
project file server, which stores stamped structural calculation packages and construction
drawings. Most state licensing boards require engineers to be able to produce these stamped
records for a statute-of-repose period (commonly a decade or more after a project finishes)
in case a professional-liability claim is filed years later -- so a silently failed backup
here isn't just an inconvenience, it can leave the firm unable to defend a claim long after
the project is closed out. Right now, verification is informal: whoever is free on Friday
afternoon glances at a couple of recent filenames and calls it done.

Write a Standard Operating Procedure, as a numbered list, for the recurring weekly
backup-verification process. It must: confirm the backup job actually completed rather than
just started, include a real restore-test of a sample file rather than only checking a
completion log, and state exactly what to do if the same backup job fails verification two
weeks in a row.

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Weekly Backup-Verification Process**

   - Log into the backup management system first thing on Friday morning.  
   - Verify that the most recent backup job (executed nightly) has **completed successfully**, not merely started.  
   - Check the system status indicators and logs for a "Completed" or "Success" notification, along with the timestamp of completion.

3. **Log Verification**  
   - Open the backup job log for the current week.  
   - Confirm that the log explicitly states the backup job **finished without errors**.  
   - Note any warnings or non-critical issues, but ensure there are no critical failures listed.

4. **Sample File Selection for Restore Test**

5. **Perform Real Restore Test**

6. **Documentation of Restore Test Results**  
   - Record the outcome of the restore test in the weekly verification log, including:  
     a. Date and time of the restore test.  
     b. Names and locations of the restored files.  
     c. Any discrepancies or issues encountered during the test (e.g., file corruption, incomplete restore).  
     d. Confirmation of successful restoration or detailed notes on failures.

7. **Notification of Results**  
   - Email the verification results to the IT manager, the structural engineering firm's compliance officer, and any other relevant stakeholders.  
   - If the restore test was successful, the email should state "Weekly backup verification completed successfully."

9. **Review and Continuous Improvement**  
   - At the end of each month, review the weekly verification logs for any patterns or recurring issues.  
   - Update the SOP as necessary based on findings, improvements in backup technology, or changes in regulatory requirements.  
   - Conduct a brief training or refresher session with the team to ensure everyone is familiar with the updated procedures.

**Standard Operating Procedure: Weekly Backup Verification for Structural Engineering Project Files**

1.  **Confirm Backup Job Completion:** Access the backup management console and locate the backup job associated with the Project File Server for the current week. Verify the job status is explicitly marked as "Completed" or "Success." Confirm the job start and end times fall within the expected execution window and that the reported data size matches the baseline for the server. **Do not** consider the job verified if the status reads "Running," "Queued," "Partial Success," or "Failed." A job that has only started or is in progress does not satisfy verification requirements.

2.  **Select Representative Sample File:** Navigate to the backup repository and select a high-value sample file from a recent project folder to test integrity. Prioritize files that contain stamped structural calculation packages (e.g., native engineering files or PDFs) or critical construction drawings. Record the sample file's path, filename, and size in the verification log for audit purposes.

3.  **Execute Real Restore Test:** Initiate a restore operation for the selected sample file to a designated temporary testing directory (isolated from the production server and active project data). Ensure the restore destination is separate to prevent accidental data alteration. Wait for the restore process to finish fully and verify the restore operation completed without errors.

4.  **Validate Restored File Integrity:** Open the restored sample file on a test workstation. Verify the file opens without corruption warnings and that the data is usable. Inspect the content to confirm that the engineer's stamp, seal, date, and project-specific calculation results are legible and present. Compare the file size of the restored file against the recorded original size to ensure no data truncation occurred. If the file format supports digital signatures, verify the signature remains valid.

5.  **Document Verification Result:** Delete the restored file from the temporary testing directory. Update the MSP's backup log with the verification outcome ("Pass" or "Fail"), the date performed, the sample file details used, and a brief description of any anomalies observed.

6.  **Escalation Protocol for Consecutive Failures:** If a backup job fails verification (due to either job status not completing or a failed restore test) for two consecutive weeks, execute the following actions immediately:
    *   Notify the MSP's Senior Systems Engineer and the Backup Vendor within one business hour to initiate a technical root-cause analysis.
    *   Perform an emergency offline restore test on a critical project file from a different project to assess the scope of the failure.
    *   Review backup media integrity, replication logs, and storage health reports.
    *   Notify the engineering firm's IT Director and Professional Liability Coordinator to assess data availability against state statutory retention requirements and determine if immediate data preservation actions are required.

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP): Weekly Backup Verification for Structural Engineering Project File Server**

**Purpose:**  
To ensure the integrity and recoverability of backed-up structural calculation packages and construction drawings by verifying that backups complete successfully and that data can be restored, meeting statutory record-retention requirements and minimizing professional liability risk.

**Scope:**  
Applies to all Managed Service Provider (MSP) personnel responsible for monitoring and verifying backups for the structural-engineering firm’s project file server.

**Frequency:** Weekly, every Friday by 3:00 PM local time.

---

**1. Confirm Backup Job Completion (Beyond Start/Initiation)**  
   a. Log in to the backup management console (e.g., Veeam, Commvault, or equivalent).  
   b. Navigate to the job history for the project file server backup.  
   c. Identify the most recent scheduled weekly backup job (completed within the last 7 days).  
   d. Verify the job status is **"Success"** (or equivalent green/pass state).  
   e. Confirm the **end time** is within the expected window and that the job processed all expected data sources.  
   f. Record the job name, date, end time, and status in the Backup Verification Log.

**2. Perform a Random Restore Test on a Sample File**  
   a. Randomly select one recently backed-up file from the project server (created or modified within the last 7 days) using the following criteria:  
      - File must be a stamped structural calculation package (.PDF or .DWG with engineer’s seal).  
      - File must originate from an active or recent project (last 12 months).  
      - Use a random number generator or file list shuffle to avoid selection bias.  
   b. Initiate a **restore operation** from the backup repository (not from the source server).  
   c. Restore the selected file to an isolated, secure test folder (e.g., `\\backup-test\restore-check`).  
   d. Open the restored file and confirm:  
      - File opens without errors.  
      - Stamps, seals, and signatures are intact and legible.  
      - All pages and layers (if applicable) are present.  
      - File size and modification date match the original.  
   e. Document the filename, restore path, test result, and tester initials in the Backup Verification Log.

**3. Validate Backup Logs and Alert System Status**  
   a. Review the backup software alert history for the past 7 days to confirm no unresolved warnings or errors.  
   b. Confirm that backup completion alerts are configured and were sent to the MSP operations email and ticketing system.  
   c. Check monitoring system (e.g., RMM tool) to verify no backup-related tickets are open or overdue.

**4. Record Verification Results**  
   a. Enter all findings (steps 1–3) into the centralized **Backup Verification Log** (spreadsheet or database with date, job status, restore test result, and technician name).  
   b. Attach a screenshot of the successful job and restore confirmation if required by internal audit policy.  
   c. Save and close the log; ensure it is backed up offsite.

**5. Escalation Protocol for Failed Verification**  
   a. If the backup job did **not complete successfully** or the **restore test fails**, document the failure and:  
      - Immediately notify the MSP escalation engineer and the client’s IT contact.  
      - Open a high-priority ticket with full details (error messages, logs, file tested).  
      - Initiate root-cause analysis and corrective action within 24 hours.  
   b. **If the same backup job fails verification two weeks in a row**:  
      i. Escalate to the MSP Operations Manager and the client’s designated engineering manager.  
      ii. Suspend regular verification; initiate a full backup infrastructure audit.  
      iii. Generate a written incident report detailing:  
          - Nature and duration of the failure.  
          - Affected data scope and risk to compliance.  
          - Actions taken to date.  
      iv. Propose and implement a corrective action plan (e.g., backup target replacement, software patch, configuration fix) within 48 hours of escalation.  
      v. Perform a full validation restore of three historical project packages (from different quarters) to assess data integrity across the backup retention window.  
      vi. Schedule a client review meeting to present findings and remediation plan.

**6. Weekly Sign-Off**  
   a. After completing all steps, the technician must sign and date the weekly verification record.  
   b. The MSP supervisor must review and approve the log entry by end of business Friday.

**Documentation Retention:**  
Maintain all verification logs and incident reports for a minimum of 12 years, in compliance with statute-of-repose requirements.

**Review Schedule:**  
This SOP shall be reviewed annually or after any backup infrastructure change.

SOP: Weekly Backup Verification -- Project File Server (structural-engineering client)

Owner: MSP backup/BCDR team. Frequency: weekly, every Friday by end of day.

Prerequisites: access to the backup platform's job-completion log, and read/write access to
a scratch folder for restore-testing.

1. Open the backup platform's job log for the past 7 days and confirm the scheduled job
   reports "Completed" status for every run, not merely "Started." A job that started but
   never reported completion counts as a failure for this week, not a pass.
2. From the most recent successful-looking backup set, pick one stamped structural
   calculation package file at random (not always the same file).
3. Restore that file from the backup set into a scratch folder -- an actual restore, not a
   browse/preview of the backup catalog.
4. Open the restored file and confirm it matches the expected file (correct size at minimum;
   spot-check content if it's a common format). If it opens cleanly and matches, verification
   passes for this week.
5. Log the verification result (pass/fail, file tested, date) in the client's backup log.
6. Failure branch: if verification fails (job didn't complete, or the restore-tested file is
   missing/corrupt), re-run the verification against a second, independent file immediately
   to rule out a one-off fluke. If it fails a SECOND consecutive week in a row (whether the
   same failure mode or not), escalate to the backup platform vendor and to the client
   immediately -- do not wait for a third weekly check, because these records may need to be
   produced for a professional-liability claim years after project completion under the
   applicable statute-of-repose period, and a second straight failed week means the backup
   cannot currently be trusted at all.

*Version 1.0 – Effective [Date]*

### 1.  Prepare the Environment  

1.1. Ensure the backup‑job console shows **“Last Run Completed”** timestamp ≤ 24 hours ago.  
1.2. Verify that the **backup storage** (disk array, tape library, cloud bucket) is online and not in a degraded state (no alerts in monitoring dashboard).  
1.3. Log into the **Restore‑test workstation** and mount the backup repository read‑only.

### 2.  Confirm Backup Job Completion  

2.1. From the backup console, run a **status query** for the weekly job (e.g., `veeamjob –status “ProjectFileServer_Weekly”`).  
2.2. Capture a **screenshot** showing:  

   - Job name  
   - Start time & end time  
   - “Result: **Success**” (or equivalent)  
   - No error or warning codes  

2.3. Export the **job log file** (e.g., `ProjectFileServer_Weekly_2024-07-12.log`) and verify that the final line contains “**Backup completed successfully**”.  

2.4. **If the log shows “Completed with warnings” or “Failed”** → go to Step 7 (Failure Handling).  

### 3.  Verify Data Integrity (Statistical Check)  

3.1. Generate a **file inventory** of the source server (e.g., `find /srv/projects -type f > /tmp/source_inventory.txt`).  
3.2. Generate a **file inventory** of the backup set (e.g., `veeamexplorer –list “ProjectFileServer_Weekly” > /tmp/backup_inventory.txt`).  
3.3. Run a **diff** to confirm that the number of files matches (+/‑ ≤ 0). Document the count.  

3.4. **Checksum Sample (see Step 4)** – this step only confirms the backup job *ran*, not that the data is readable.

### 4.  Perform a Real Restore‑Test of a Sample File  

4.1. **Select Sample Files** (random but representative):  

   - Choose **5 files** from different project folders, file types, and sizes:  
     - 1 × small PDF (≈ 200 KB)  
     - 1 × large PDF (≈ 10 MB)  
     - 1 × DWG drawing (≈ 5 MB)  
     - 1 × Excel calculation workbook (≈ 1 MB)  
     - 1 × Word report (≈ 500 KB)  

   - Use a random‑number generator on the inventory list to avoid bias.  

4.2. For each selected file:  

   a. Record the **full source path** and **file size**.  
   b. **Restore** the file to a **dedicated “Restore‑Test” folder** on the test workstation (do *not* overwrite production data).  
   c. Compute a **checksum** of the original file (`sha256sum /srv/projects/.../file.pdf > original.sha256`).  
   d. Compute a **checksum** of the restored file (`sha256sum /restore-test/.../file.pdf > restored.sha256`).  
   e. Verify that the two checksum values are **identical**.  

4.3. Capture a **screen‑recording** (or a series of screenshots) showing the restore command, progress, and checksum comparison for at least **one** of the five files.  

4.4. **If any checksum mismatch occurs** → go to Step 7 (Failure Handling).  

### 5.  Document the Verification  

5.1. Open the **Weekly Backup‑Verification Log** entry for the current date.  
5.2. Fill in the following fields:

|-------|---------|
| Date / Time | Friday [date] 14:00‑15:30 |
| BVT Name | (your name) |
| Backup Job ID | ProjectFileServer_Weekly |
| Completion Status | Success / Failure (with code) |
| File‑count match | Yes / No (counts) |

5.3. **Save** the log entry and **upload** all attachments to the “Backup‑Verification Archive” (folder path: `\\auditshare\BackupVerification\2024\`).  

5.4. **Notify** the BVL via email (subject: “Weekly Backup‑Verification – SUCCESS – [date]”) with the log link attached.  

### 6.  Close the Verification Cycle  

6.1. Mark the ticket (if one was opened) as **Resolved – Verified**.  
6.2. Update the **weekly dashboard** widget that displays “Last Verified: [date]”.

### 7.  Failure Handling (Verification Not Passed)  

7.1. **Immediate Action** (within 30 min of detection)  

   a. Open a **high‑priority ticket** in the ticketing system (category: Backup‑Verification Failure).  
   b. Populate the ticket with:  

   - Exact error messages / log excerpts.  
   - Screenshots of the failed job status.  
   - List of files that failed checksum verification.  

7.2. **Escalation**  

   - **Notify** the BVL **and** the **IT Operations Manager** via phone and email.  
   - If the backup job is marked **Failed**, also notify the **Backup Vendor Support** (if under contract).  

7.3. **Remedial Steps** (to be performed **before the next scheduled backup**)  

   1. **Re‑run** the failed backup job manually (using the console’s “Run Now” option).  
   2. Repeat Steps 2‑4 for the re‑run.  
   3. If the re‑run succeeds, document the outcome and close the ticket as “Resolved – Re‑run successful”.  

   *If the re‑run also fails* → proceed to **Step 7.4**.  

7.4. **Two‑Week Consecutive Failure Procedure**  

   1. **Formal Incident Declaration** – create an **Incident Record** (ITIL “Major Incident”) with severity **P1 – Data Protection Risk**.  
   2. **Root‑Cause Analysis (RCA)** – within 24 hours, the BVL assembles a **cross‑functional team** (Backup Admin, Storage Engineer, Security Lead, Project Management).  
   3. **Immediate Mitigation** –  

      - Switch the production server to an **alternative backup method** (e.g., manual nightly copy to a secondary NAS) until the primary job is validated.  
      - Verify that the alternative backup has at least **one successful restore test** (same procedure as Step 4).  

   4. **Report to Management** – BVL prepares a **written report** (≤ 2 pages) covering:  

      - Dates of failure (Week N and Week N‑1).  
      - Findings from RCA (hardware error, configuration drift, network issue, etc.).  
      - Corrective actions taken and timeline for permanent fix.  

   5. **Regulatory Notification** (if required) – inform the firm’s **Compliance Officer** and, if the backup covers statutory‑of‑repose records, note the potential impact on licensing board requirements.  

   6. **Post‑Fix Validation** – after corrective actions, run **three consecutive weekly verifications** (including restore tests) with **no failures** before returning to normal schedule. Document each verification.  

   7. **Update SOP** – if the failure revealed a gap in the procedure, the BVL drafts an amendment and obtains sign‑off from IT Management.  

7.5. **Closure**  

   - Once the incident is resolved and three clean weeks are logged, close the incident ticket, archive the RCA documentation, and send a **final notification** to all stakeholders.

**Standard Operating Procedure: Weekly Backup Verification**  
**Asset:** Structural Engineering Project File Server  
**Frequency:** Weekly  
**Risk Profile:** Critical (Statute of Repose / Professional Liability)

1.  **Verify Backup Job Completion Status**
    *   Access the backup management console and locate the weekly job for the Project File Server.
    *   Confirm the job status is explicitly **"Completed Successfully"** (Exit Code 0).
    *   *Critical Check:* Reject any status reading "Completed with Warnings," "Partially Restored," or "Running." A backup that started but did not finish, or finished with skipped files, is considered a failure.
    *   Verify the job duration and data volume transferred against historical baselines; flag any anomalies (e.g., significantly reduced data transfer may indicate file exclusion errors).

2.  **Select Verification Sample**
    *   Using an automated randomization script or a rotating checklist, select one file for restore testing.
    *   The sample must be drawn from a directory containing active project data (e.g., Stamped Calculations or Construction Drawings).
    *   Ensure the selected file includes professional engineering deliverables (e.g., `.pdf`, `.dwg`, `.rvt`, or calculation packages) and has not been modified during the current backup window.
    *   *Note:* Do not rely on metadata-only checks or file listing; a physical file must be selected for restoration.

3.  **Perform Isolated Restore Test**
    *   Initiate a restore of the selected sample file to an **isolated test directory** (e.g., `\\MSP_Test_Restore\Weekly_Verif\YYYY-MM-DD`).
    *   *Constraint:* Never restore directly to the production path or overwrite existing data.
    *   Wait for the restore process to complete and confirm the restore task reports success.

4.  **Validate Restored File Integrity**
    *   **Checksum Comparison:** Generate a hash (SHA-256) of the restored file and compare it to the hash of the source file on the production server. A mismatch indicates corruption in transit or on the backup media.
    *   **Functional Verification:** Open the restored file in its native application. For stamped packages and drawings, verify the file is fully readable, renders correctly, and is not zero-length or truncated.
    *   If the file fails checksum validation or cannot be opened/read, mark the verification as **Failed**.

5.  **Document Verification Results**
    *   Record the verification outcome in the MSP ticketing system or shared audit log.
    *   Entry must include: Date/Time, Operator Name, Backup Job Status, Sample Filename/Path, Restore Result, Integrity Check Result, and Final Pass/Fail determination.
    *   Archive the test log and isolate the test directory for 48 hours before automated cleanup to allow for post-verification review.

6.  **Execute Consecutive Failure Escalation Protocol**
    *   *Trigger:* If the same backup job fails verification (Steps 1–4) for **two consecutive weeks**.

**Standard Operating Procedure – Weekly Backup Verification**  
*(Version 1.0 – Effective 18 Jul 2026)*  

**Purpose**  
To ensure that the Project File Server backup job completes successfully and that backed‑up data is recoverable for statutory‑repose compliance and professional‑liability defense.

**Scope**  
All scheduled full or incremental backups of the Project File Server (PFS) performed by the MSP. Applies to all licensed engineering projects stored on the server.

**Responsibility**  
- **Backup Administrator (BA)** – Executes the SOP, documents results, escalates issues.  
- **Engineering Manager (EM)** – Reviews verification reports, approves corrective actions.  
- **IT Support Team (IT)** – Implements fixes, updates backup configuration, and performs root‑cause analysis.

---

## 1. Pre‑Verification Setup

1.1. **Identify the Target Backup Job**  
- Locate the scheduled job in the backup console (e.g., Veeam, Acronis, etc.).  
- Verify job name, frequency, and target volume (PFS).

1.2. **Create a Dedicated Restore Test Folder**  
- On a separate, write‑protected test volume (e.g., `C:\RestoreTest\`), ensure it has at least 1 GB free space.  
- Map the test volume with a unique drive letter (e.g., `Z:`) for easy identification.

1.3. **Select a Representative Sample File**  
- Choose a project file that is:  
  * at least 10 MB in size,  
  * actively used during the last month,  
  * not a temporary or cache file.  
- Document the full path and checksum (SHA‑256) of the sample file.

---

## 2. Verify Backup Completion

2.1. **Confirm Job Status**  
- Open the backup console.  
- Verify that the *latest run* status shows **“Completed”** or **“Succeeded”** (not “Started” or “Partial”).  
- Note the start and finish timestamps.

2.2. **Check Backup Logs**  
- Export the job log to a text file (`BackupLog_YYYYMMDD.txt`).  
- Search for error codes, warning messages, or “Failed” entries.  
- If any errors appear, skip to Step 6 (Escalation).

2.3. **Validate Backup Integrity**  
- Use the console’s built‑in **“Verify”** or **“Integrity Check”** feature (if available).  
- Confirm that the verification completes without errors.

---

## 3. Perform a Real Restore Test

3.1. **Initiate a Point‑in‑Time Restore**  
- Restore the previously selected sample file to the test folder (`Z:\`).  
- Use the most recent backup snapshot.

3.2. **Verify Restored File Integrity**  
- Compute the SHA‑256 checksum of the restored file (`Z:\SampleFile.ext`).  
- Compare it to the original checksum recorded in Step 1.3.  
- If the checksums match, the restore is successful.

3.3. **Open the Restored File**  
- Launch the file in the appropriate engineering application (e.g., Revit, AutoCAD).  
- Verify that the file opens without errors and that key elements (stamps, drawings) are present.

---

## 4. Document Results

4.1. **Fill the Verification Log**  
Create a spreadsheet or use the backup console’s log‑entry feature. Record:  
| Date | Backup Job | Status | Log Path | Sample File | Checksum Match | Restore Success | Comments |
|------|------------|--------|----------|-------------|----------------|-----------------|----------|

4.2. **Attach Log Files**  
- Save the exported backup log and any error screenshots in the log entry.  
- Store logs in the central audit folder (`\\AuditLogs\BackupVerification\YYYYMMDD\`).

4.3. **Sign‑Off**  
- BA signs the log entry.  
- EM reviews the entry within 24 hrs and signs off if satisfied.

---

## 5. Weekly Reporting

5.1. **Compile a Summary Report**  
- Include number of successful verifications, any failures, and root‑cause notes.  
- Attach the detailed log from Step 4.

5.2. **Distribute the Report**  
- Send to EM, IT Support, and the Compliance Officer (if applicable).  
- Store a copy in the project compliance folder.

---

## 6. Escalation – Failure Handling

6.1. **Immediate Response**  
- If the backup job did not complete or the restore test fails, the BA must:  
  1. Stop the backup console to prevent further corruption.  
  2. Notify IT Support via the incident ticketing system (`INC‑YYYYMMDD‑BACKUP`).  

6.2. **Root‑Cause Analysis (RCA)**  
- IT Support performs RCA, documenting:  
  * error codes, logs, and timestamps,  
  * recent changes to backup configuration or storage hardware,  
  * network status, etc.  

6.3. **Remedial Action**  
- Fix the identified issue (e.g., re‑configure storage paths, replace failing disk, update backup software).  
- Re‑run the backup job immediately, then repeat Steps 2–4.

6.4. **Verification of Fix**  
- Confirm success by completing Steps 2–4 again.  
- Record the corrective action in the log.

---

## 7. Two‑Week Consecutive Failure Protocol

7.1. **Trigger Condition**  
- The same backup job fails verification **two consecutive weeks** (i.e., both Week N and Week N+1).

7.2. **Escalation Steps**  

| Step | Action | Owner |
|------|--------|-------|
| 7.2.1 | Immediate stop of the backup job. | BA |
| 7.2.2 | Create a high‑priority incident ticket (`INC‑HIGH‑BACKUPFAIL‑YYYYMMDD`). | BA |
| 7.2.3 | Notify the Engineering Manager and Compliance Officer via email. | BA |
| 7.2.4 | IT Support conducts a comprehensive system audit: hardware health, storage capacity, backup software version, network throughput. | IT |
| 7.2.5 | If a hardware failure is suspected, request vendor support and arrange for replacement parts. | IT |
| 7.2.6 | If software or configuration issues are suspected, roll back recent changes or apply patches. | IT |
| 7.2.7 | Perform an ad‑hoc full restore of a critical project file to a separate test environment to confirm recoverability. | IT |
| 7.2.8 | Document findings, corrective actions, and timelines in the incident ticket. | IT |
| 7.2.9 | Update the backup schedule to a higher‑frequency test (e.g., daily restores for the next week). | IT |
| 7.2.10 | Provide a written resolution and updated SOP (if needed) to EM and Compliance Officer. | IT |

7.3. **Approval and Closure**  
- EM reviews the incident resolution and approves closure.  
- BA updates the Verification Log with the incident summary.  
- The incident ticket is closed only after a successful verification in the following week.

---

## 8. Continuous Improvement

8.1. **Monthly Review**  
- EM and IT review all backup verification logs to identify trends.  
- Adjust backup schedule or configuration as needed.

8.2. **Quarterly Audit**  
- Perform a full audit of backup storage capacity, retention policy, and compliance with state licensing requirements.  

8.3. **Training**  
- Conduct refresher training for BA and IT staff every 6 months on backup verification best practices.

---

**End of SOP**
