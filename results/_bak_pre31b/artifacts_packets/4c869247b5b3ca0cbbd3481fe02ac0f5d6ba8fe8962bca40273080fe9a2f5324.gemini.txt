# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

## ⚠️ Brief Note on Pay Classification (Exempt vs. Non-Exempt)
Do **not** assume exempt status based on the title “Coordinator” or salary range. Under the FLSA, exemption hinges on the **actual duties performed**, not job titles. Verify whether the role’s primary duties meet the administrative or executive exemption tests (e.g., discretionary decision-making, manual labor exemption triggers, direct customer service, cash handling, or routine task execution). Confirm the salary level and salary-basis test align with DOL guidelines before finalizing payroll setup. Misclassification can trigger back-wage claims, penalties, and loss of overtime exemptions. If duties blur exempt/non-exempt lines, consult payroll/HR compliance or legal counsel before finalizing pay paperwork.

### ⚠️ Pay Classification Advisory

The job title "Operations Coordinator" can be ambiguous under the FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act). Before finalizing pay-related paperwork, the hiring manager must verify the employee's classification against **actual job duties**, not the title:

*   **Exempt (Salary):** Requires a primary duty of office work (e.g., managing, planning, or executing business operations) that meets the "detailed duties test" and a salary basis of at least $684/week.
*   **Non-Exempt (Hourly + Overtime):** Applicable if the employee spends significant time performing non-exempt duties (e.g., retail floor support, driving, manual labor, or tasks not meeting the administrative exemption criteria).

**Action:** If the Coordinator role involves any non-exempt duties (even occasionally), they must be classified as **non-exempt** to comply with overtime laws. Misclassifying a non-exempt employee as exempt creates significant liability. Confirm this with an employment lawyer or payroll provider before signing the offer letter.

### Note on Exempt vs. Non‑Exempt Classification  

The title “Operations Coordinator” **does not automatically dictate** exempt status. Under the FLSA, an employee is **exempt** only if:

1. **Salary basis** (minimum $684/week as of 2024) **and**  
2. **Primary duties** meet one of the exemption categories (administrative, executive, professional, etc.).  

For the **administrative exemption**, the employee must:
- Perform office or non‑manual work directly related to the management or general business operations of the employer; **and**
- Exercise **discretion and independent judgment** on matters of significance.

**Action:**  
- Review the written job description and compare the day‑to‑day tasks (e.g., scheduling, reporting, process improvement, cross‑functional coordination).  
- If the role **does not** regularly involve discretion on significant matters, classify as **non‑exempt** and track overtime.  
- Document the analysis and retain it in the employee’s file for audit purposes.
