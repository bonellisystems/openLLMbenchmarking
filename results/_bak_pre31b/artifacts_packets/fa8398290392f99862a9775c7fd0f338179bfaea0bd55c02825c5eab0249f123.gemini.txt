# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

# Investigation Plan: Pregnancy Discrimination and Harassment Complaint

## 1. Response to Confidentiality Request (Immediate Action)
**The Core Constraint:** The employee demands "100% confidentiality" (including hiding the fact that the complaint was filed from the supervisor).

**HR Response Strategy:**
We must manage expectations immediately. HR **cannot promise absolute confidentiality** in a formal investigation. However, HR can promise **strict "need-to-know" confidentiality.**

**Explanation to Provide to the Employee:**
*   **Why "100%" is impossible:**
    1.  **Due Process:** To conduct a fair investigation, the accused supervisor must be notified of the allegations (at least the general nature) to provide an explanation. Without this, the investigation is legally vulnerable and potentially void.
    2.  **Fact-Finding:** To corroborate the claim, investigators may need to speak with other team members regarding the project reassignment or the supervisor's management style.
    3.  **Public Sector Mandate:** As a county entity, we are bound by open records laws and civil service rules that may require documentation of adverse actions and investigations.
*   **What We *Can* Promise:**
    1.  **Limited Disclosure:** Information will be shared only with the investigator, HR leadership, and the supervisor (as necessary for due process).
    2.  **Anti-Retaliation:** The supervisor will be explicitly warned that retaliation against the employee is grounds for termination.
    3.  **Identity Protection:** In witness interviews, the specific identity of the complainant will be protected where feasible (e.g., "A team member raised concerns about...").

**Action Item:** HR must send a written acknowledgment immediately stating: *"We take this seriously. We cannot guarantee absolute silence due to legal due process requirements, but we will protect your identity to the maximum extent possible and strictly enforce our anti-retaliation policy."*

---

## 2. Impartial Investigator Selection
Given the sensitivity of a pregnancy discrimination claim involving a direct supervisor, the internal HR office should not investigate itself if there is any conflict of interest.

*   **Preferred Option:** **External Investigator.**
    *   *Reasoning:* If the County HR Director is the supervisor's peer, or if the HR office is small, an external investigator ensures neutrality and reduces the risk of perceived bias. It also protects the employee from fear of internal politics.
*   **Internal Option (If External is not feasible):** **Senior HR Manager / Compliance Officer.**
    *   *Criteria:* Must be at least two levels above the accused supervisor. Must have no prior personal or professional conflict with the supervisor.
*   **Union Representation:** Ensure the investigator coordinates with the County’s Union Rep if the employee is unionized.

---

## 3. Interim Measures (Protection During Investigation)
*Goal: Prevent retaliation and stabilize the employee's position while the investigation is pending.*

1.  **Anti-Retaliation Notice:** Issue a formal written warning to the supervisor regarding the complaint. Remind them that retaliation is a violation of County policy and federal law (Title VII/PDA).
2.  **Operational Freeze on Project:**
    *   Since the employee was removed from the project immediately after pregnancy disclosure, the reassignment should be reviewed.
    *   **Measure:** Suspend the finality of the reassignment. If possible, restore the employee to the project *temporarily* until the investigation concludes, or ensure the colleague is not tasked to replace the employee in a way that suggests replacement.
3.  **Monitoring:** HR should schedule weekly check-ins with the employee (without requiring them to report on the investigation itself) to monitor the supervisor's behavior.
4.  **EAP Referral:** Offer access to the Employee Assistance Program to support the employee's mental well-being during the stress of the investigation.

---

## 4. Evidence to Gather
The investigation must establish a timeline and a "pattern of conduct" (for harassment) or "adverse action" (for discrimination).

**Documentary Evidence:**
1.  **Email/Chat Logs:** Search the supervisor and employee's communication channels (Slack, Teams, Email) for the specific comments ("can you handle this," "when's your last day").
2.  **Project Assignments:** Retrieve the project charter or task assignment logs for the "County-Fair Vendor-Logistics Project" showing the change in leadership *immediately* following the pregnancy disclosure date.
3.  **Performance Reviews:** Gather the employee's last 2 years of performance reviews to establish a history of competence (to rebut the "can you handle this" claim).
4.  **Medical Accommodation Requests:** Verify if the pregnancy disclosure was formalized through HR or was verbal (this affects privacy protocols).
5.  **HR Records:** Check the supervisor's prior complaints or history of discrimination claims.

**Testimonial Evidence:**
1.  **The Supervisor:** Must be interviewed to hear their justification for the project reassignment and their explanation of the comments.
2.  **Project Colleagues:** Colleagues who worked with the employee on the project to verify competence and observe the supervisor's comments.
3.  **Other Witnesses:** Other employees who may have heard the supervisor make similar comments to others.

---

## 5. Interview Sequence
The order of interviews is critical to prevent "coaching" and ensure evidence is preserved.

1.  **Complainant (Employee):**
    *   *Timing:* Immediate (within 24 hours).
    *   *Purpose:* Get the full narrative, specific dates, times, and locations of the comments. Clarify the timeline of the pregnancy disclosure vs. project reassignment.
    *   *Note:* Since the employee submitted a written complaint, this interview is for nuance, but it must be recorded (with consent).

2.  **Respondent (Supervisor):**
    *   *Timing:* Within 48 hours of Employee Interview.
    *   *Purpose:* Inform them of the general nature of the complaint (without revealing the specific identity of the witness if possible, though the supervisor will know it is about their team). Ask for their explanation of the comments and the project reassignment.
    *   *Constraint:* Do not reveal the specific quotes from the employee *before* this interview, to prevent the supervisor from tailoring their story.

3.  **Project Team Witnesses:**
    *   *Timing:* After Supervisor Interview.
    *   *Purpose:* Ask about their observation of the project leadership change. Ask if they heard the supervisor make comments about pregnancy or workload.
    *   *Anonymity:* Assure them that their identity will not be disclosed to the supervisor.

4.  **HR File Review (Post-Interviews):**
    *   Review the supervisor's personnel file for any prior warnings regarding conduct.

---

## 6. Conclusion & Reporting
*   **Decision:** The investigator will issue a report to the County HR Director and Legal Counsel.
*   **Findings:** The report will classify the evidence as "Supported," "Not Supported," or "Inconclusive."
*   **Remediation:** If supported, potential remedies include:
    *   Mandatory training for the supervisor on Pregnancy Discrimination.
    *   Restoration of the project (if feasible).
    *   Disciplinary action up to termination (if the comments constituted a hostile work environment).
    *   Restoration of lost opportunities.

## 7. Specific Address on Confidentiality (Summary for the Employee)
*To be included in the initial correspondence to the complainant:*

> "We understand your request for 100% confidentiality. We want to be honest with you: **We cannot guarantee that your supervisor will never know you filed this complaint.** To conduct a fair investigation, we are legally required to give the supervisor notice of the allegations so they can respond.
>
> **However, we promise this:**
> 1. Your identity will be kept private from the supervisor and other staff members to the fullest extent possible.
> 2. Any investigation details will be shared only with the investigator and necessary legal counsel.
> 3. We are implementing interim measures to ensure you are safe from retaliation during this process.
>
> If we cannot protect your identity to the extent you feel safe, please know that we will not force you to participate in the investigation if you choose to withdraw the complaint. However, given the legal implications of pregnancy discrimination, we strongly encourage you to allow us to proceed with the protections we can offer."

**Investigation Plan: Pregnancy-Related Discrimination Complaint**

**Complainant:** County employee (anonymous in this document)  
**Subject:** Allegations of pregnancy-related comments and adverse employment action by direct supervisor  
**Nature of Complaint:** Potential violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (as amended by the Pregnancy Discrimination Act) and the county’s internal anti-discrimination and anti-retaliation policies  

---

### **I. Selection of Impartial Investigator**

To ensure objectivity and credibility of the investigation:

- **External Investigator:** Engage a third-party employment law attorney or professional HR investigator with expertise in discrimination and retaliation claims.  
- **Rationale:** Use of an external investigator eliminates any perception of internal bias, reinforces the seriousness of the complaint, and strengthens legal defensibility.  
- **Qualifications:** The investigator must have experience in EEOC/Title VII matters, familiarity with state/local employment laws, and no prior involvement with the parties or department in question.  
- **Appointment:** The HR Director or County Administrator will appoint the investigator, with confirmation from Legal Counsel.

---

### **II. Interim Protective Measures**

To prevent retaliation and preserve the employee’s work environment during the investigation:

1. **No Retaliation Directive:**  
   - HR will issue a written notice to the supervisor and relevant managers emphasizing that any form of retaliation—direct or indirect—is strictly prohibited and will result in disciplinary action.  
   - This notice will be documented and placed in the supervisor’s file.

2. **Work Assignment Review:**  
   - Temporarily suspend any unilateral changes to the complainant’s duties or reporting structure.  
   - If reassignment of duties is necessary for operational reasons, it must be documented, justified, and pre-approved by HR or the investigator.

3. **Reporting Mechanism:**  
   - Establish a confidential channel (e.g., direct contact with the investigator or HR designee) for the complainant to report any perceived retaliation or adverse treatment during the investigation.

---

### **III. Confidentiality: Addressing the Complainant’s Request**

**Complainant’s Request:** “Please keep this 100% confidential — I don’t want ANYONE, including my supervisor, to know I filed this.”

**HR Response – What Can and Cannot Be Promised:**

> “We understand your concern and take your request seriously. However, while we will maintain confidentiality to the greatest extent possible, **we cannot guarantee absolute confidentiality.**  
>
> **Why?**  
> - A fair investigation requires us to notify the supervisor of the allegations to give them an opportunity to respond, as required by principles of due process and internal policy.  
> - The supervisor must be interviewed and given a chance to explain their actions and comments.  
> - Evidence collection (e.g., witness interviews, project records) may lead others to infer the source of the complaint.
>
> **What We *Can* Promise:**  
> - Only individuals with a legitimate need to know (investigator, HR, legal counsel) will be informed.  
> - We will not disclose your identity as the complainant unless necessary and will use neutral phrasing when possible (e.g., “an employee raised concerns”).  
> - We will emphasize strict confidentiality to all parties involved and document any breach.  
> - We are legally obligated to prevent and address retaliation. Any retaliatory behavior will be met with disciplinary action, up to and including termination.  
>
> Your safety and job security are our priority. We encourage you to come forward because silence enables misconduct; reporting allows us to act.”

### **IV. Investigation Methodology**

#### **1. Evidence Collection**

- **Document Review:**  
  - Complainant’s personnel file (performance evaluations, project assignments, leave records).  
  - Records related to the county-fair vendor-logistics project (emails, project plans, delegation logs, meeting notes).  
  - Supervisor’s prior disciplinary history, if any.  
  - County policies on discrimination, retaliation, and accommodation.

- **Electronic Communications:**  
  - Review emails, texts, or internal messaging platforms (with IT support) for relevant conversations between the complainant, supervisor, and team members.

- **Workload and Project Data:**  
  - Assess whether the complainant’s workload or performance declined prior to the reassignment.  
  - Compare timelines: when the pregnancy was disclosed, when comments were made, when the project was reassigned.

#### **2. Interview Sequence**

Conduct interviews in a neutral, private setting with written notes or (with consent) audio recording.

1. **Complainant (First):**  
   - Gather detailed account: dates, locations, quotes of comments, context of project reassignment.  
   - Document emotional impact and any other incidents not in the written complaint.  
   - Reaffirm interim protections and reporting process for retaliation.

2. **Supervisor (Second):**  
   - Notify of the complaint in neutral terms: “An employee has raised concerns about comments and a project reassignment. We are conducting a neutral fact-finding process.”  
   - Ask supervisor to explain the comments and the rationale for reassigning the project.  
   - Inquire about performance concerns, decision-making process, and awareness of pregnancy.

3. **Colleagues and Witnesses (Third):**  
   - Interview team members who may have heard the comments or observed changes in the work environment.  
   - Speak with the colleague to whom the project was reassigned: ask how the transfer occurred, whether it was discussed as performance-related, and if any rationale was given.  
   - Interview peers who can speak to the complainant’s performance pre- and post-pregnancy disclosure.

4. **Other Relevant Parties (as needed):**  
   - Department head or HR business partner for context on departmental norms, past accommodations, or similar complaints.

### **V. Analysis and Findings**

- Compare testimony and documentary evidence for consistency.  
- Assess whether comments constitute unlawful sex/pregnancy discrimination under EEOC guidelines.  
- Evaluate whether the project reassignment was adverse action and whether it was motivated by pregnancy status.  
- Determine if retaliation occurred or is likely.

---

### **VI. Reporting and Follow-Up**

- **Investigator’s Report:**  
  - Summarize findings, credibility assessments, and conclusion (e.g., “preponderance of evidence supports/disperses the allegation”).  
  - Include recommendations: disciplinary action, training, policy changes, or no action.

- **Action by HR and Leadership:**  
  - If substantiated: impose appropriate discipline, restore project responsibilities if warranted, offer mediation.  
  - If unsubstantiated: communicate outcome with sensitivity, reinforce anti-discrimination policies.  
  - In all cases: provide anti-retaliation training to the supervisor and team.

- **Feedback to Complainant:**  
  - Provide a summary of findings and actions taken (without disclosing confidential details about others).  
  - Reaffirm support and ongoing monitoring.

---

### **VII. Preventive Measures Post-Investigation**

- Mandatory training for all managers on pregnancy discrimination and retaliation.  
- Review and clarify project assignment and performance documentation practices.  
- Consider anonymous reporting options and periodic climate surveys.

---

**Conclusion:**  
While absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed, the investigation will be conducted with the utmost discretion, fairness, and legal integrity. The county’s duty to investigate discrimination lawfully requires transparency with the accused, but every effort will be made to protect the complainant and ensure a safe, equitable workplace.

### Investigation Plan: Pregnancy Discrimination & Retaliation Complaint

**Case Reference:** [County Case Number]
**Allegations:** Pregnancy discrimination, hostile work environment (based on pregnancy), and adverse employment action (retaliation/reassignment) following pregnancy disclosure.
**Jurisdiction:** County Government (Public Sector; subject to Civil Service rules, PDA, FMLA, and applicable state anti-discrimination laws.)

### I. Response to Confidentiality Request
**This section addresses the employee's request for "100% confidentiality" and is the foundation of the intake conversation.**

**The Honest Disclosure:**
You cannot promise 100% confidentiality. Promising this would be misleading, ethically improper, and could expose the county to liability if the investigation is found to lack due process. The investigator must explain the following to the employee during intake:

1.  **Investigative Necessity:** To conduct a fair investigation, the respondent (the supervisor) must be informed of the allegations to provide a response. This is a fundamental due process right.
2.  **Identity Disclosure:** In employment investigations, the identity of the complainant is typically shared with the respondent so they can address the specific claims. *Exception:* If revealing the identity would create an immediate safety threat or severe safety risk, the investigator may redact the identity in written findings, though the respondent must still receive enough detail to respond. Given the fear of "career harm," safety is likely not at risk, but retaliation is.
3.  **Limited Confidentiality Commitment:**
    *   **What HR *can* promise:**
        *   Information will be shared only on a **strict need-to-know basis** (investigator, legal counsel, senior leadership if required by policy).
        *   **Zero Tolerance for Retaliation:** The county will strictly prohibit any retaliation against you. Any retaliatory behavior reported will be treated as a separate, serious misconduct violation.
        *   **Confidentiality of the Process:** Your personnel file will be flagged to prevent adverse actions (e.g., performance reviews, schedule changes) by the supervisor during the investigation.
        *   **Outcome Reporting:** You will be informed of the investigation's conclusion and any corrective actions taken, consistent with privacy laws.

**Script for HR Rep:**
> "I hear your fear, and I take this seriously. I want to be transparent so you can make an informed decision about moving forward. I cannot promise 100% confidentiality because, if we investigate, your supervisor will eventually need to know what you alleged so he can respond. If we don't let him respond, the investigation can be dismissed, and you lose your protection against discrimination. However, I can promise that the circle of knowledge is small, no retaliation will be tolerated, and we will take steps to protect your standing while we look into this."

**Decision Point:** If the employee refuses to proceed due to the confidentiality limitation, the office should document the complaint and the refusal, and offer alternative resources (e.g., EEO counselor, Employee Assistance Program).

---

### II. Impartial Investigator Selection

**Criteria for Selection:**
*   **No Prior Relationship:** The investigator must have no prior supervisory relationship, close personal relationship, or prior conflict with either party.
*   **Subject Matter Expertise:** Must have training/experience in pregnancy discrimination, adverse action analysis, and public sector civil service rules.
*   **Perceived Impartiality:** Given the county environment, the investigator should be viewed as independent to mitigate claims of bias.

**Recommended Selection:**
*   **Option A (Preferred): External Investigator.** Contract an independent HR firm or legal consultant specializing in employment law. This eliminates any "office politics" bias and ensures the supervisor cannot claim favoritism.
*   **Option B: Senior Internal HR (with conditions).** If an external investigator is not feasible, assign a HR Director or HR Business Partner who is *not* in the same department, has no reporting line to the supervisor's chain of command, and ideally has no prior interaction with the complainant.
*   **Vetting:** The investigator should sign a conflict-of-interest waiver before accepting the case.

---

### III. Interim Measures

**Goal:** Protect the employee, preserve evidence, and prevent retaliation during the investigation.

1.  **Non-Retaliation Directive:** Issue a written memorandum to the supervisor (and copied to the investigator) reminding them of the strict prohibition on retaliation, adverse actions, or interference with the employee's rights during the investigation. This puts the supervisor on notice.
2.  **Reporting Line/Contact Modification:**
    *   *Assessment:* Determine if the employee must interact with the supervisor for daily work.
    *   *Action:* If feasible, temporarily redirect reporting of specific tasks to an interim lead or the investigator. If the project reassignment is the issue, clarify whether the employee is expected to assist the colleague on that project.
3.  **Project Status Review:**
    *   *Caution:* Do *not* immediately return the project to the employee, as this could be seen as HR "fixing" the outcome before investigating. However, the investigator should review whether the reassignment can be paused pending the outcome or if the colleague needs to preserve all work product for review.
4.  **Schedule/Facility Adjustments:** If the employee is experiencing physical discomfort related to pregnancy, remind them of their right to request reasonable accommodations (light duty, break adjustments) separate from this complaint. This can be offered as a supportive measure.
5.  **Personnel File Freeze:** Place a administrative hold on the complainant's personnel file to prevent the supervisor from inserting negative performance notes during the pendency of the investigation.

### IV. Evidence to Gather

**Documentary Evidence:**
*   **The Complaint:** Written submission and intake notes.
*   **Pregnancy Disclosure:** Email, text, or note confirming *when* the employee disclosed pregnancy.
*   **Project Files:**
    *   Original assignment of vendor-logistics project to employee.
    *   Email/memo of reassignment to colleague.
    *   **Timeline Analysis:** Date of disclosure vs. Date of reassignment.
    *   *Business Justification:* Any documentation the supervisor provided for the reassignment (e.g., "workload concerns," "conflict of interest"). *Note: If the justification is solely related to pregnancy or expected leave, this is likely pretext.*
*   **Performance Records:** Employee's last 2-3 performance evaluations; any prior commendations. Supervisor's comments on the current workload.
*   **Communications:** All emails, Slack/Teams messages, and calendar invites between employee and supervisor regarding the project and workload.
*   **Policy Documents:** County Anti-Discrimination Policy, Pregnancy Accommodation Policy, Employee Handbook, Civil Service Rules regarding adverse actions.
*   **Comparator Data:** (If applicable and permitted by policy) How has the supervisor treated other employees who requested leave or had pregnancy-related circumstances?

**Physical/Digital Traces:**
*   Metadata on reassignment emails.
*   Office layout (if a witness can attest to hearing comments in an open area).

---

### V. Interview Sequence

**Strategy:** Gather evidence and witness statements *before* interviewing the respondent to prevent collusion, destruction of evidence, or gaslighting.

**Sequence:**

1.  **Interview 1: Complainant.**
    *   *Purpose:* Detailed narrative, identification of witnesses, collection of personal documentation (texts/emails), emotional support check.
    *   *Key Questions:* "Who heard the comments?" "Did you receive any other feedback on your workload recently?" "What was the business reason given for the project reassignment?" "Did you discuss this with the colleague who took the project?"
    *   *Action:* Obtain signed release for investigator to review personnel file and communicate with the supervisor.

2.  **Interview 2: Witnesses (The Colleague & Others).**
    *   *The Colleague:* Interview regarding the project handoff. "When were you assigned the project?" "What was the stated reason?" "Did the supervisor mention the employee's pregnancy in this conversation?" *Note: The colleague may be fearful of the supervisor; emphasize confidentiality and non-retaliation.*
    *   *Other Witnesses:* Identify anyone else who heard the comments ("pop," "handle workload"). Interview separately.

3.  **Interview 3: The Supervisor (Respondent).**
    *   *Purpose:* Present allegations, allow response, assess credibility.
    *   *Preparation:* Share a summary of allegations (redacting witness names if necessary for their safety, though the supervisor will likely deduce the source).
    *   *Key Questions:* "Please explain the reassignment of the vendor-logistics project." "What was your business justification?" "Are you aware that the employee disclosed pregnancy shortly before this reassignment?" "Have you made comments about pregnancy or workload to this employee?"
    *   *Tone:* Neutral but firm. Focus on business decision-making for the reassignment. If the supervisor claims "workload concerns," challenge with performance data.

4.  **Interview 4: Rebuttal/Synthesis (Optional).**
    *   If the supervisor provides a new explanation or evidence, the investigator may need a brief follow-up or document review to validate.

---

### VI. Risk Management & County-Specific Considerations

*   **Civil Service Rules:** If the employee is a classified employee, check if the reassignment constitutes a "demotion" or "adverse action" requiring pre-disciplinary due process. If so, procedural errors by the supervisor could be a violation independent of discrimination.
*   **Union Representation:** If the employee or supervisor is in a bargaining unit, check if union representatives need to be notified or if they have the right to be present during interviews (Weingarten rights).
*   **Mediation:** In county government, EEO complaints often have a mandatory counseling/mediation phase before a formal investigation. Determine if this complaint bypasses that phase based on the severity of the allegations (adverse action vs. hostile environment).
*   **Final Report:** The investigator should produce a report with findings of "Substantiated," "Not Substantiated," or "Inconclusive." If substantiated, recommend corrective action ranging from counseling/training for the supervisor to suspension or reassignment, depending on severity and county policy.
