# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

Your MSP's helpdesk documentation lead handed you the following prose description of how
new front-desk and clinical staff get EHR system access at an outpatient clinic client, and
asked you to convert it into a numbered checklist a brand-new helpdesk tech could follow
unaided, in the correct order, without needing to ask anyone what to do next.

"When a new employee starts, HR sends us their name, role, and start date. Before we create
anything, we need the clinic's practice manager to email formal written approval naming the
specific EHR access role -- front-desk scheduling-only, or clinical charting access --
because we've had an incident where a front-desk hire was given clinician-level chart access
by mistake, since someone assumed the access level from the job title alone instead of
getting it in writing. Once we have that written approval, we create the Active Directory
account, add the account to the correct EHR role group (never assign chart-level access to a
scheduling-only role, even temporarily, even to save time), and enable MFA on the account
before it is used for the first time. Finally, we log the account creation and a reference to
the approval email in the client's compliance folder, since this clinic is a HIPAA covered
entity and gets audited on new-user access every year."

Convert this into a numbered, testable checklist in the correct execution order. Preserve the
two points in the prose where skipping a step or doing things out of order would be an actual
compliance problem, not just an inconvenience.

   *Action:* Confirm receipt of a new hire request from HR containing the employee’s full name, job role, and start date.  
   *Test:* Request document is present, legible, and all three fields are recorded.  
   ⚠️ *Do not proceed to Step 2 without this documentation.*

2. **Obtain Formal Written Approval**  
   *Action:* Contact the Clinic Practice Manager to secure an email containing formal written approval explicitly naming the exact EHR access role: “front-desk scheduling-only” OR “clinical charting access.”  
   *Test:* Approval email is received and explicitly states the required role in writing.  
   ⚠️ **COMPLIANCE RISK IF SKIPPED/OUT OF ORDER:** If this step is skipped, delayed, or performed after account setup, staff privileges may be assigned based on job-title assumptions. This directly caused a prior HIPAA audit failure where a front-desk hire incorrectly received clinician-level charting access.

3. **Create Active Directory Account**  
   *Action:* Provision a new AD account using the verified employee details from HR.  
   *Test:* Account exists in AD, is disabled/inactive, and follows standard naming conventions.  
   ⚠️ *Do not activate the account or proceed to Step 4 until AD provisioning is confirmed.*

4. **Assign EHR Role Group**  
   *Action:* Add the AD account to the EHR role group that exactly matches the written approval from Step 2.  
   *Test:* AD account is correctly mapped to the approved role group in the EHR admin console.  
   ⚠️ **COMPLIANCE RISK IF SKIPPED/OUT OF ORDER:** Never assign chart-level access to a scheduling-only role under any circumstances. Even temporary or “testing” chart access for front-desk staff violates least-privilege requirements and constitutes a HIPAA compliance breach.

5. **Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**  
   *Action:* Configure and activate MFA on the newly created AD account.  
   *Test:* MFA is successfully configured, verified active, and passing system checks.  
   ⚠️ *Do not enable the account for initial user access until MFA is fully operational.*

6. **Log to Compliance Folder**  
   *Action:* Record the account creation event (date, employee name, AD username) and save a reference to the Practice Manager’s approval email in the client’s designated HIPAA compliance folder.  
   *Test:* Compliance folder contains complete, auditable documentation. Verify this entry exists before handing credentials/access to the new staff.  
   ⚠️ *This log is required for annual HIPAA-covered entity audits. Missing or incomplete entries will result in non-compliance findings.*

**Help‑Desk On‑Boarding Checklist – New Front‑Desk / Clinical Staff Access**  
*(All steps must be completed in the order shown. Skipping or re‑ordering any step creates a HIPAA compliance exposure.)*

1. **Receive HR Notification**  
   - Confirm you have the new hire’s **full name, job title, and start date** in the ticket.

2. **Obtain Written Role‑Specific Approval**  
   - Request (or locate) the **practice manager’s email** that:  
     - Explicitly names the **EHR access role** (e.g., “Front‑Desk Scheduling‑Only” **or** “Clinical Charting”).  
     - Is sent **before** any account is created.  
   - **Compliance safeguard:** Do **not** assume access based on job title alone; the email is the only authorized evidence of permission.

3. **Create the Active Directory (AD) Account**  
   - Use the standard naming convention (e.g., `firstname.lastname`) and fill in required attributes (phone, email, manager, etc.).  
   - Record the AD account **sAMAccountName** and **distinguished name** in the ticket.

4. **Assign the Correct EHR Role Group**  
   - Add the new AD account **only** to the group that matches the written approval (e.g., `FrontDesk_Scheduling` or `Clinical_Charting`).  
   - **Compliance safeguard:** **Never** add the account to any chart‑level or clinician‑access group, **even temporarily** or “to save time.”  

5. **Enable Multi‑Factor Authentication (MFA)**  
   - Configure MFA (e.g., Duo, Authenticator) on the account **before** the account is used for the first login.  
   - Verify that MFA is active by attempting a test login.

6. **Log the Account Creation in the Compliance Folder**  
   - Save a copy of the practice‑manager’s approval email **and** a short log entry that includes:  
     - Account name, AD path, role group added, MFA enabled, and the date of creation.  
   - Store this documentation in the client’s **HIPAA compliance folder** for annual audit review.

7. **Final Verification Before Access Is Granted**  
   - Confirm that:  
     - Written approval is on file.  
     - The AD account resides in the correct EHR role group.  
     - MFA is enabled and functional.  
     - All documentation has been saved to the compliance folder.  
   - Only after these checks are satisfied, **grant the new hire system access**.

- **Step 2 – Written Role‑Specific Approval:**

- **Step 4 – Correct Role‑Group Assignment:**  
  Assigning chart‑level access— even on a temporary basis— to a front‑desk role breaches the minimum‑necessary rule and can lead to significant compliance penalties.  

Follow the checklist exactly as written; each step is testable and required for a compliant onboarding process.
