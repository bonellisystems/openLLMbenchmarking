## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

**Timeline:** All paperwork should be collected and processed 2 weeks before start date to ensure compliance.

## 2. Systems and Access Provisioning (Ready on Day One)

**IT Setup:**
- Laptop (Windows/Mac) with company software pre-installed
- Email account creation and configuration
- Access to company network and VPN
- Scheduling software access (e.g., Shiftboard, Deputy, or equivalent)
- File sharing and cloud storage permissions
- Security training materials

**Administrative Setup:**
- Employee directory access
- Company intranet/portal login
- Calendar and meeting room booking permissions
- Point of Sale system access (if applicable)

## 3. First Week Schedule and Buddy Assignment

**Week 1 Schedule:**
- Monday: Orientation and company introduction
- Tuesday: Systems training and IT setup review
- Wednesday: Departmental introductions and role overview
- Thursday: Process walkthrough and shadowing
- Friday: Review week's learning and planning next steps

**Buddy Assignment:**
- Assign to a senior team member (preferably from Operations or Management)
- Buddy will facilitate introductions, answer questions, and provide informal guidance
- Buddy should be available for at least 2 hours/week for check-ins

## 4. 30-60-90 Day Ramp-Up Goals

**30 Days:**
- Complete all required training modules
- Become familiar with core operational processes
- Build relationships with key stakeholders
- Begin supporting daily operations with minimal supervision

**60 Days:**
- Take ownership of assigned operational tasks
- Demonstrate understanding of business processes
- Provide input on process improvements
- Begin independent scheduling coordination

**90 Days:**
- Fully operational and contributing to team goals
- Identify and implement process improvements
- Begin mentoring new hires (if applicable)
- Present initial performance review and goals for next quarter

## Pay Classification Consideration

**Important:** The pay classification (exempt vs. non-exempt) should be carefully reviewed against actual job duties before finalizing pay-related paperwork. While the title "Operations Coordinator" might suggest an exempt classification, the actual duties should be evaluated against the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) criteria:

**Key factors to assess:**
- Amount of time spent on administrative duties vs. decision-making
- Level of independence in job responsibilities
- Frequency of supervisory duties
- Complexity of tasks and decision-making authority

**Recommendation:** Review the actual job duties and responsibilities with HR/legal before finalizing pay classification, as job titles alone do not determine FLSA status. Some operations coordinators may be classified as non-exempt if they spend significant time on non-exempt duties like answering phones, handling customer service, or performing routine tasks that don't meet exempt criteria.

### ⚠️ Pay Classification Note (Exempt vs. Non-Exempt)
Do **not** assume classification based on the “Coordinator” title. Under the FLSA, exemption depends on **both**:
1. **Salary basis & threshold** (currently $844/week in 2024; increasing to $1,128/week in July 2025)
2. **Primary duties test** (administrative, executive, or professional functions that require discretion/independent judgment)

Many “coordinator” roles in retail-services lean operational/tactical (tracking shifts, updating systems, handling call-outs) and may actually meet **non-exempt** criteria. Misclassification can trigger back-pay, penalties, and audit risk.

**Action before signing pay paperwork:**
- Draft a 1-page duty breakdown (hours spent on tasks, decision-making authority, routine vs. discretionary work)
- Run the DOL FLSA Exemption Self-Assessment
- If borderline or leaning non-exempt, default to hourly with overtime tracking until a legal/HR advisor confirms otherwise

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), exemption from overtime pay depends on **both** a salary level test **and** a duties test. An Operations Coordinator title often implies administrative duties, which may qualify for the "white-collar" exemption, but only if:
1.  **Salary Level:** The employee is paid on a salary basis at or above the minimum threshold ($684/week federal minimum, though many states require higher).
2.  **Duties Test:** The primary duty is office or non-manual work, exercising discretion and independent judgment on significant matters.

**Action:** Before finalizing the pay-related paperwork (offer letter, timesheet setup), the Hiring Manager should review the actual written job description. If the role involves significant manual labor, direct client service, or hourly tasks, it must be classified as **non-exempt**, and the business must implement an accurate timesheet and overtime calculation system. Misclassification carries significant legal risk, especially for a first-time employer with no prior payroll infrastructure.
