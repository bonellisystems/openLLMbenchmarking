## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

This is a sensitive conversation because, while there is no "bad news" regarding the employee's actual salary, the employee may feel they are being "left behind" compared to their peer or that the market study somehow devalued their role.

Below are structured talking points and a guide for the manager to navigate this conversation professionally, legally, and effectively.

---

### **Manager Preparation Guide**

**The Goal:** To communicate that the salary adjustment was a structural update to ensure market competitiveness, confirm the employee is being paid fairly according to the new data, and maintain the employee's trust.

**Key Principles:**
*   **Focus on the "Why":** The study was done to ensure the bank remains a competitive employer.
*   **Focus on the "Range":** A pay band is a range, not a single number. Being "in the band" is the goal.
*   **Focus on the "Factors":** Individual pay within a band is determined by specific variables (tenure, performance, etc.).
*   **Maintain Boundaries:** You cannot discuss others' pay, but you also cannot tell them they shouldn't discuss it.

---

### **Talking Points Script**

#### **1. The Opening & The News**
*"I wanted to meet with you today to share some updates regarding our recent market compensation study. As a bank, we want to make sure our pay structures remain competitive with other institutions in our region so we can attract and retain great talent."*

*"As part of this study, we reclassified the Loan Processor role into a new pay band of $52,000 to $64,000. I reviewed your current compensation against this new range, and I’m pleased to confirm that your salary of $58,500 falls squarely within this new market-aligned band. Because you are already positioned well within the updated range, there is no change to your individual base pay at this time."*

#### **2. Addressing "Why didn't my pay change?"**
*(If the employee asks: "If the market went up, why didn't my pay go up too?")*

*"That’s a fair question. The purpose of the study wasn't necessarily to trigger automatic raises for everyone; rather, it was to ensure our pay ranges are accurate. Since your salary was already aligned with where the market is for this role, your pay is already at a competitive level. The update was essentially 'calibrating' our scales to make sure no one falls below the market rate."*

#### **3. Addressing the Coworker’s Pay**
*(If the employee says: "But [Coworker] told me they make $61,000. Why am I being paid less if we do the same job?")*

**Note to Manager:** *Do not say "I can't discuss that" (which can sound dismissive) or "You shouldn't talk about pay" (which is a legal violation). Instead, pivot to the **criteria** used for placement within a band.*

*"I can't discuss the specific compensation details of other employees, as those are confidential. However, I can explain how we determine where an individual sits within a pay band."*

*"A pay band isn't a single fixed number; it’s a range. Where a person falls within that $52k–$64k range is determined by several factors, such as:
*   **Tenure** within the specific role.
*   **Performance history** and consistent achievement of goals.
*   **Depth of experience** or specialized expertise.
*   **Complexity of work** handled."*

*"The goal of the band is to provide room for growth. As you continue to grow in your expertise and performance, there is room for your compensation to move upward within that range."*

#### **4. The "Closing" & Routing Requests**
*(If the employee asks: "What do I need to do to get to $61,000?")*

*"I hear you, and I want to make sure your career progression is clear. While I don't have the authority to unilaterally adjust salaries, I can certainly facilitate a conversation with HR and the Compensation leadership regarding your specific trajectory. I can route your request to them to see what the specific criteria for movement within the band look like for your current performance level."*

*"Let's focus on [upcoming project/goal] to ensure you're hitting the performance metrics that support that upward movement. I'll follow up with HR regarding the process for pay reviews and let you know what the next steps are."*
