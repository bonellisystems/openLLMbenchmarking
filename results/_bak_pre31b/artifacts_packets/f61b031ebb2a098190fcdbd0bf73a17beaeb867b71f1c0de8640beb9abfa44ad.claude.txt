# Panel Judge -- Battery 1 Business Task Scoring

## Role

You are one of three independent judges (Claude, Codex, Gemini) scoring a set
of blinded answers to a single MSP-realistic business task. You do not know
which model produced which answer -- every answer below is labeled only by a
letter. Do not try to guess model identity from writing style. Score only
what is on the page against the anchors below.

## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

### ADR-001: Migrate from RMM-A to RMM-B with Phased Rollout and Immediate Cancellation

**Status:** Decided  
**Date:** October 26, 2023  
**Author:** MSP Leadership Team  
**Replaces:** N/A  
**Related:** RMM Procurement Evaluation, Q4 Migration Planning

---

#### 1. Context
The MSP leadership team evaluated the current Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) strategy against cost and functionality requirements. Key findings included:
*   **Cost:** RMM-A utilizes a per-endpoint pricing model that is increasing in total cost of ownership (TCO), making it financially unsustainable.
*   **Functionality:** RMM-A lacks native patch-approval workflows, forcing the team to use workarounds that increase operational overhead and risk. RMM-B supports these workflows natively.
*   **Contractual Constraints:**
    *   RMM-A auto-renews annually in **60 days**.
    *   RMM-A requires written cancellation notice at least **30 days prior** to the renewal date to avoid reactivation.
*   **Proposed Migration:** A phased migration to RMM-B is planned over **90 days**, prioritizing the smallest client environments first.
*   **Conflict:** The 90-day migration duration exceeds the window to cancel RMM-A (30 days) and the remaining RMM-A term (60 days). Canceling RMM-A to meet the deadline leaves RMM-A unavailable during the final 30 days of the migration effort.

#### 2. Options
| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A1** | **Stay on RMM-A.** | No migration risk; guaranteed availability. | TCO increases; lack of patch-approval workflows continues; operational risk remains. |
| **A2** | **Migrate to RMM-B, extend timeline to 120 days.** Maintain RMM-A until migration complete. | Safe rollback window for all clients; no risk to clients during migration tail. | RMM-A renewal may trigger early reactivation fees or penalties if cancellation not sent; delay in cost savings; delay in feature adoption. |
| **A3** | **Migrate to RMM-B, cancel RMM-A by Day 30.** Begin migration immediately. | Immediate progress on cost savings; captures features early; complies with cancellation policy. | **Timing Conflict:** RMM-A is unavailable from Day 60 to Day 90. No RMM-A fallback exists for clients migrated after Day 60. |

#### 3. Decision
**We selected Option A3.**

*   **Action:** Send written cancellation notice to RMM-A immediately. The notice must be received by the vendor **no later than Day 30** relative to the current date to ensure cancellation effectiveness before the renewal date in 60 days.
*   **Migration Strategy:** Begin phased migration to RMM-B immediately (Day 0). The migration will proceed over **90 days**, starting with the smallest client environments.
*   **Risk Acceptance:** Leadership acknowledges that RMM-A will be fully deactivated **60 days from now**. Consequently, RMM-A cannot serve as a fallback for any client environment migrated after Day 60.

#### 4. Consequences & Rollback Implications
**Positive Outcomes:**
*   **Cost Reduction:** Avoids annual renewal of the expensive RMM-A contract.
*   **Feature Adoption:** Enables patch-approval workflows immediately for migrated clients, improving security posture and reducing manual effort.
*   **Policy Compliance:** Cancellation notice is sent within the required 30-day window, avoiding potential penalties or automatic reactivation fees.

**Negative Outcomes & Failure Modes:**
*   **RMM-A Availability Gap:**
    *   RMM-A is available for rollback **only until Day 60**.
    *   From **Day 60 to Day 90**, RMM-A is completely cancelled. No clients can be rolled back to RMM-A during this window.
*   **Client Risk (Post-Day 60):**
    *   **Scenario:** RMM-B experiences a critical failure or data loss affecting a client environment that has already been migrated after Day 60.
    *   **Impact:** There is **no RMM-A fallback**. The MSP cannot restore the client to RMM-A. Recovery must be performed entirely within RMM-B (e.g., image restore, manual intervention), which may take significantly longer than a platform-level rollback.
    *   **SLA/Business Risk:** This scenario poses a higher risk of SLA breach and client dissatisfaction due to extended downtime and lack of platform redundancy.
*   **Migration Ordering Risk:**
    *   The "Smallest Clients First" strategy may inadvertently delay the migration of larger or more critical clients until the tail end of the 90-day window. If larger clients are migrated after Day 60, they carry the highest risk of having no RMM-A safety net.

**Required Mitigations (Action Items):**
1.  **RMM-B Stability Gate:** RMM-B must undergo rigorous stability testing. No client should be migrated after Day 60 unless RMM-B has demonstrated zero critical incidents for the preceding 14 days.
2.  **Review Migration Priority:** Evaluate if "Smallest First" is safe given the Day 60 cutoff. Consider migrating high-priority/critical clients before Day 60 to ensure they have a known stable state, even if they are larger.
3.  **Emergency Response Plan:** Develop a specific runbook for RMM-B failures that does not rely on RMM-A, including RMM-B admin escalation paths and manual remediation procedures.

# Architecture Decision Record (ADR)

**Title:** Migration from RMM‑A to RMM‑B for all client environments  
**Date:** 2026‑07‑18  
**Status:** **Accepted** – implementation scheduled, review after 90‑day migration window  
**Owner:** MSP Leadership Team (Ops, Finance, Engineering)  

---

## Context

| Issue | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Cost pressure** | RMM‑A charges per‑endpoint licences. With ~40 client environments (≈ 2 500 endpoints) the annual spend is projected to increase > 30 % YoY. |
| **Feature gap** | RMM‑A does not provide the patch‑approval workflow that our security‑policy team requires for compliance reporting. |
| **Alternative** | RMM‑B offers a flat‑rate per‑environment licence and includes native patch‑approval workflows, plus a more modern API for automation. |
| **Current contract** | RMM‑A auto‑renews annually. The existing term expires **60 days** from today. Cancellation must be submitted **≥ 30 days** before renewal (i.e., by **Day 30**). |
| **Proposed migration** | Phase‑based rollout over **90 days**, starting with the smallest clients (≤ 5 endpoints) and ending with the largest (≥ 200 endpoints). |
| **Stakeholder consensus** | All present (Ops, Finance, Engineering, Service Delivery) agreed to proceed, to send the RMM‑A cancellation notice, and to begin the phased migration. |

### Timing conflict

- **Cancellation deadline:** Day 30 (must be sent by then).  
- **Migration window:** 90 days, meaning many clients will still be on RMM‑A after Day 30.  

If RMM‑A is cancelled on schedule, any client not yet migrated will lose the monitoring/management service unless RMM‑B is fully operational for them.  

---

## Decision

1. **Send a formal cancellation notice to RMM‑A on Day 0** (today) to satisfy the 30‑day notice requirement.  
2. **Extend the RMM‑A licence for the first 30 days** via a *short‑term “bridge” add‑on* (available from RMM‑A’s support team at a prorated rate). This ensures continuity while the migration is in progress.  
3. **Execute the migration in three fixed phases** (each 30 days):  

   | Phase | Client size | Approx. # of environments | Target completion |
   |-------|-------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
   | 1 | ≤ 5 endpoints | 12 | Day 30 |
   | 2 | 6‑50 endpoints | 16 | Day 60 |
   | 3 | > 50 endpoints | 12 | Day 90 |

4. **Create a “fallback” contract with RMM‑A** that can be re‑activated (no‑penalty re‑onboarding) for up to **30 days after the official cancellation date** if RMM‑B experiences a critical failure that impacts any client still on RMM‑A. This fallback is limited to the “bridge” period and will be closed once all clients are stable on RMM‑B.  
5. **Risk‑mitigation checklist** (executed before each phase):  

   - Verify RMM‑B tenant health (monitoring, patch‑approval, API connectivity).  
   - Run a full backup of RMM‑A configuration for the environment being migrated.  
   - Conduct a “smoke‑test” on a pilot endpoint (already done in Phase 0).  
   - Prepare a client‑communication plan (notice, migration window, support contacts).  

6. **Escalation path if RMM‑B fails** after RMM‑A cancellation:  

   - **Step 1:** Activate the 30‑day bridge licence for affected clients (no extra cost beyond the prorated fee).  
   - **Step 2:** If the bridge period is insufficient, invoke the re‑onboarding clause with RMM‑A (no‑penalty re‑subscription for up to 30 days).  
   - **Step 3:** Communicate transparently with impacted clients, offering temporary manual monitoring (e.g., via remote scripts) while a permanent solution is secured.  

---

## Consequences

### Positive outcomes
- **Cost reduction:** Expected 25 % lower annual spend after full migration (flat‑rate licensing).  
- **Feature parity:** Patch‑approval workflow is now native, reducing ticket volume and improving compliance reporting.  
- **Scalable architecture:** RMM‑B’s API enables future automation (e.g., auto‑remediation, custom dashboards).  

### Risks & Mitigations
| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|------------|
| **RMM‑B service outage during migration** | Clients lose monitoring, potential SLA breach. | Medium | Bridge licence + re‑onboarding clause; phased rollout limits exposure. |
| **Cancellation notice missed** | Automatic renewal → unnecessary cost and contractual lock‑in. | Low (process automated). | Automated reminder + documented email with read‑receipt. |
| **Data loss during migration** | Loss of device inventory, patch history. | Low | Full export of RMM‑A data; import verification before decommission. |
| **Client push‑back on timeline** | Delays in Phase 2/3, extending bridge costs. | Medium | Early communication; offer optional accelerated migration for willing clients (additional fee). |
| **Unexpected licensing cost on RMM‑B** | Budget overrun. | Low | Negotiate capped per‑environment price; include a “price‑review” clause at 6 months. |

### What Happens If It Goes Wrong

1. **RMM‑B fails after Day 30 (cancellation effective)**  
   - Clients still on RMM‑A will be covered by the bridge licence (paid prorated).  
   - If the bridge is exhausted and RMM‑B still unstable, we will reactivate the full RMM‑A licence under the re‑onboarding clause (no‑penalty, 30‑day window).  
   - All affected clients will receive a status update within 2 hours of the incident, a remediation plan, and a temporary manual monitoring arrangement if needed.  

2. **Migration stalls (e.g., engineering resources overloaded)**  
   - Phase schedule will be adjusted, but the bridge licence will be extended (subject to cost approval).  
   - The cancellation notice remains valid; we will keep the bridge licence active for the full 90‑day period (cost‑controlled by capping the bridge at 10 % of the original RMM‑A spend).  

3. **Financial overrun**  
   - If bridge + re‑onboarding costs exceed 5 % of the projected annual savings, a senior‑leadership review will be triggered to either renegotiate RMM‑B terms or revert to a hybrid model (some clients stay on RMM‑A).

# ADR-001: Migration from RMM-A to RMM-B

| Status | **Accepted** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Date** | October 26, 2023 |
| **Owners** | IT Leadership, MSP Operations |
| **Related** | Contract Renewal (RMM-A), Migration Plan |

## Context

Our current Remote Monitoring and Management platform (**RMM-A**) has become a financial and operational burden. The per-endpoint pricing has increased significantly, and the platform lacks critical patch-approval workflows required for compliance and security governance.

Our target platform (**RMM-B**) addresses both issues: it offers a lower per-endpoint cost structure and includes the necessary patch-approval workflows.

**Contractual Constraints:**
*   **RMM-A Term:** The current contract auto-renews annually. The next renewal date is **60 days** from today.
*   **Cancellation Deadline:** To prevent auto-renewal, written cancellation must be sent at least **30 days** before the renewal date.
*   **Migration Scope:** All 40 client environments.
*   **Migration Timeline:** Phased approach estimated to take **90 days**, starting with the smallest clients.

## Decision

The leadership team has agreed to proceed with the migration to RMM-B. The specific actions are:

1.  **Immediate Cancellation:** Send the written cancellation notice for RMM-A immediately to prevent auto-renewal.
2.  **Phased Migration:** Begin moving client environments to RMM-B immediately. The plan prioritizes the **smallest clients first** to minimize initial risk.
3.  **Timeline:** Complete migration within 90 days.

## Consequences

### Positive Outcomes
*   **Cost Reduction:** Per-endpoint pricing is reduced for all clients.
*   **Feature Parity:** RMM-B provides required patch-approval workflows, improving security posture.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Automated patching workflows reduce manual toil for technicians.

### Risks and Mitigations

#### 1. The Timeline Conflict (90-Day Migration vs. 60-Day Contract)
There is a structural conflict between the migration timeline (90 days) and the RMM-A contract end date (60 days).
*   **Impact:** RMM-A service coverage will expire on **Day 60**. The migration does not complete until **Day 90**.
*   **Gap:** There is a **30-day window (Days 61–90)** where RMM-A is no longer active, but some clients will not yet have been fully migrated to RMM-B.

#### 2. Failure Scenario: RMM-B Breaks After RMM-A is Cancelled
This is the critical risk scenario requiring explicit definition for future hires.
*   **Scenario:** RMM-B is deployed, but a critical failure occurs (e.g., connectivity loss, bug preventing agent installation) **after** RMM-A coverage has expired (i.e., after Day 60).
*   **Impact on Clients:**
    *   **Migrated Clients:** Already on RMM-B; if RMM-B is down, they are **unmonitored**.
    *   **Unmigrated Clients:** These clients were intended to be on RMM-A but cannot be on RMM-B. Since RMM-A is **cancelled/expired**, these clients will have **zero RMM coverage**. They will not be monitored, patched, or managed.
    *   **Business Impact:** Potential service outage for these clients, breach of SLA, and reputational damage.

#### 3. Failure Scenario: RMM-B Breaks Before RMM-A is Cancelled
*   **Scenario:** RMM-B fails during the initial migration phase (Days 0–60).
*   **Impact:** RMM-A is still active.
*   **Mitigation:** We can immediately rollback unmigrated clients to RMM-A. No service interruption occurs. This is the "safe" window.

#### 4. Mitigation Strategy
To minimize the risk of the **Day 60–90 Gap**, the following measures are mandated:

*   **Aggressive Scheduling:** While we start with "smallest clients," we must ensure the **majority of endpoints** (approx. 80%+) are migrated and stable **before Day 50**. This ensures that even if RMM-B fails after Day 60, the number of unmigrated clients is negligible.
*   **Emergency Budget:** A contingency budget has been approved to **re-instate RMM-A on an emergency basis** (pay-per-endpoint) if RMM-B fails catastrophically after Day 60. This is a fallback to ensure coverage for any remaining unmigrated clients until a fix is implemented.
*   **Stop-Decision Criteria:** If RMM-B performance drops below acceptable SLA thresholds **before Day 50**, the migration halts. We will not proceed to the cancellation of RMM-A until stability is confirmed.

## Summary for Future Hires
If you inherit this decision, note that we have accepted the risk of a 30-day coverage gap. The safety net is the aggressive early migration schedule and the emergency budget. **Never** assume RMM-A will be available if you are working in the Days 61–90 window. If RMM-B fails during this window, the unmigrated clients are exposed until emergency funds are deployed to reactivate RMM-A.

# ADR 001 – Migration from RMM‑A to RMM‑B  

**Status:** *Accepted*  
**Date:** 2025‑11‑03  
**Authors:** Leadership Team (MSP) – Engineering, Operations, Finance, and Sales  

---  

## 1. Context  

Our Managed Service Provider (MSP) currently operates **40 distinct client environments** on the legacy Remote Monitoring & Management platform **RMM‑A**.  

* **Pain points with RMM‑A**  
  * Per‑endpoint licensing is approaching the upper‑tier price bracket.  
  * Missing critical **patch‑approval workflow** – a capability we have been requesting for 6 months.  

* **Evaluation of alternatives**  
  * **RMM‑B** (the proposed replacement) offers comparable (and in many cases superior) features, including:  
    * Unified per‑device licensing (no tier‑based price jumps).  
    * Built‑in patch‑approval workflows with granular control and reporting.  
    * Integrated ticketing and automation that align with our SOPs.  
  * Preliminary proof‑of‑concept (PoC) on a pilot client showed a 30 % reduction in management overhead.  

* **Operational constraints**  
  * The **annual term** for the existing RMM‑A contract **renews in 60 days**.  
  * Per the contract terms, we must deliver a **written cancellation notice ≥ 30 days before the renewal date** to avoid automatic renewal.  
  * The migration plan envisions a **phased rollout of 90 days**, beginning with the smallest client segment and progressing toward the larger accounts.  

Because of these overlapping deadlines we must decide **whether to issue the cancellation notice now**, and **how to handle clients that are not yet fully migrated when the migration encounters setbacks**.  

---  

## 2. Decision Drivers  

| # | Driver | Weight (1‑5) | Rationale |
|---|--------|--------------|-----------|
| D1 | Cost reduction (licensing) | 5 | RMM‑B’s per‑device model is projected to save ~15 % annually. |
| D2 | Feature completeness (patch approval) | 5 | Critical for compliance and operational efficiency. |
| D3 | Migration risk (downtime, data loss) | 4 | Must be limited to < 2 % of total client footprint per phase. |
| D4 | Contractual timing constraints | 5 | Cancellation must be filed ≥ 30 days before renewal; migration must finish within 90 days. |
| D5 | Client impact (service continuity) | 4 | No client may be left without monitoring for > 48 h. |
| D6 | Financial exposure (early termination fees) | 3 | Early cancellation may incur a prorated fee; must be accounted for. |
| D7 | Future flexibility (ability to revert) | 3 | Need a fallback if RMM‑B proves unsatisfactory. |

The **dominant drivers** are **D1**, **D2**, and **D4** – we must resolve the contract timing conflict while ensuring the migration can be executed safely.  

---  

## 3. Considered Options  

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons | Impact on Timing Conflict |
|--------|-------------|------|------|---------------------------|
| **A – Cancel now, migrate immediately** | Send cancellation notice today, start migration on Day 0 with full 90‑day schedule. | Immediate cost savings; early exit from RMM‑A. | Migration may overrun the 90‑day window; risk of service gaps if cancellation takes effect before migration completes. | **High** – requires overlapping coverage or a “hold‑over” period; must negotiate contract extension or accept early‑termination fees. |
| **B – Delay cancellation until after migration** | Continue RMM‑A through the full 90‑day migration, cancel only after the last client is on RMM‑B (i.e., > 120 days from now). | Zero service interruption; no early‑termination fees. | Misses the 30‑day cancellation window → automatic renewal for another year; we lose the cost‑saving opportunity and lock into RMM‑A pricing. | **Low** – timing constraint satisfied, but strategic cost objective missed. |
| **C – Cancel now, but negotiate a “hold‑over” period** | Send cancellation notice now, then negotiate with RMM‑A vendor a short extension (e.g., 30 days) to allow migration completion. | Keeps cost‑saving intent; provides a safety net if migration slips. | Dependent on vendor cooperation; may involve additional fees for the hold‑over. | **Medium** – mitigates timing conflict but introduces external dependency. |
| **D – Split migration into two waves** (Phase 1: ≤ 30 days, Phase 2: ≤ 60 days) and cancel after Phase 1 | Cancel now, migrate only a subset of clients in the first 30 days; pause migration if issues arise, then resume later. | Reduces risk of overrunning the cancellation window; early cost savings for migrated clients. | Leaves a larger portion of the environment on RMM‑A longer; may delay full benefits. | **Medium** – still needs vendor agreement on hold‑over if migration stalls. |

**Chosen option:** **C – Cancel now, but negotiate a “hold‑over” period**.  
This balances the need to trigger the cancellation (to avoid automatic renewal) while preserving a contractual safety net that lets us finish the migration within the 90‑day window without incurring penalties.  

---  

## 4. Decision  

> **Decision:**  
> 1. **Issue a written cancellation notice for the RMM‑A contract** **today** (Day 0), addressing the 30‑day pre‑renewal requirement.  
> 2. **Negotiate a 30‑day hold‑over extension** with the RMM‑A vendor to keep the service active until **Day 90** (the planned end of the migration).  
> 3. **Proceed with a phased migration** of the 40 client environments, starting with the smallest clients (≤ 5 % of total endpoints) and moving outward.  
> 4. **Maintain RMM‑A coverage for any client not yet migrated** until the migration is complete or until the hold‑over period expires, whichever comes first.  
> 5. **Document a rollback / fallback plan** that allows any client still on RMM‑A after the hold‑over to revert to the previous RMM‑A environment (or to a temporary “manual” monitoring process) if RMM‑B proves unstable.  

---  

## 5. Consequences  

### 5.1 Positive / Expected Outcomes  

| Consequence | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Cost Savings** | Expected 15 % reduction in annual licensing spend once the migration completes. |
| **Feature Gains** | Immediate access to patch‑approval workflows and associated reporting, reducing patch‑related incidents by an estimated 30 %. |
| **Clear Governance** | Formal cancellation notice and hold‑over agreement create a documented process for future migrations. |
| **Risk Mitigation** | The hold‑over clause guarantees that RMM‑A remains active for the full migration horizon, preventing a service gap. |
| **Scalable Process** | Phased approach provides a repeatable template for future platform migrations. |

### 5.2 Negative / Risks  

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| **Hold‑over negotiation fails** | Medium | Migration would have to stop at Day 60, potentially leaving > 30 clients on an expired RMM‑A contract (possible penalty). | Draft a **formal amendment** with clear service‑level terms; include a clause for “mutual termination with 15‑day notice” to protect both parties. |
| **Migration downtime exceeds 48 h for a client** | Low‑Medium | Could trigger SLA breach and client dissatisfaction. | Use **shadow monitoring** (run both RMM‑A and RMM‑B in parallel for 48 h) and **rollback scripts** that restore RMM‑A configuration within 30 min. |
| **Unexpected early termination fee** | Low | Could offset projected cost savings. | Calculate fee up‑front; budget a **contingency reserve** of 5 % of the anticipated migration cost. |

### 5.3 What Happens If Something Goes Wrong After Cancellation?  

1. **Clients Still on RMM‑A**  
   * As long as the **hold‑over period** is in effect, RMM‑A remains fully operational for those clients.  
   * If the migration for a particular client stalls **beyond the hold‑over expiry**, the client will **remain on RMM‑A until the end of the current billing term** (the original 12‑month period). Because the cancellation was filed **before renewal**, there is **no automatic termination**; the service will simply continue on a month‑to‑month basis under the same terms until the term expires or until we renegotiate.  

2. **Potential Financial Exposure**  
   * If the hold‑over is not granted and the vendor terminates the agreement early, we may incur a **prorated early‑termination fee** (estimated ≤ $2,500). This fee is covered by the migration contingency budget.  

3. **Rollback Strategy**  
   * For any client that experiences **≥ 2 critical incidents** on RMM‑B within the first 30 days, we will:  
     1. Switch the client back to **RMM‑A** using the snapshot taken at migration start (≤ 5 min downtime).  
     2. Open a **post‑mortem** with the RMM‑B vendor to identify the root cause.  
     3. Re‑evaluate the migration timeline and adjust the hold‑over negotiation accordingly.  

4. **Contractual Fallback**  
   * The hold‑over agreement will stipulate that **either party may terminate with 15‑day notice** if the migration cannot be completed within the agreed 90‑day window. This provides an **exit ramp** that protects both the MSP and the client from being locked into an incomplete migration.

1. **Migrate all 40 client environments** from RMM-A to RMM-B in phased waves, ordered by client size (smallest first).

4. **Leave RMM-A agents installed but disconnected** on endpoints that have not yet been migrated, until the final phase completes.

## Consequences

### 1. The 90-Day vs. 30-Day Timing Conflict
The cancellation deadline falls **30 days from now**. Migration completion is scheduled for **90 days from now**. This creates a mandatory **60-day coverage gap** for any endpoint that remains on RMM-A if RMM-B fails or migrates slower than planned after the RMM-A license is terminated.

This gap cannot be eliminated without either extending the RMM-A contract (contradicting the cancellation decision) or compressing the migration timeline (contradicting the phased 90-day plan). The team accepts the gap but contains it through strict execution controls, biweekly go/no-go reviews, and the rollback protocol below.

### 2. Rollback Protocol: Unmigrated Clients Post-Cancellation
If RMM-B experiences a critical failure **after RMM-A has been cancelled** (defined as: >4 hours platform downtime, failed patch campaigns impacting production, or endpoint data loss), the following applies:

- **Migration Pause:** All further phases are halted. The Tech Lead declares a rollback event.
- **Unmigrated Clients:** These endpoints will temporarily lose RMM visibility. Coverage restoration follows this priority order:
  1. **Vendor Bridge License:** Leadership contacts RMM-A vendor to negotiate emergency reactivation or spot licensing for the unmigrated count. This is pre-vetted before cancellation is sent.
  2. **Dormant Agent Reversion:** If bridge licensing is unavailable, disconnected RMM-A agents are re-pointed to a legacy/staging environment or a partner-backed emergency monitoring tier.
  3. **Manual Fallback:** Remote access and patching are suspended for unmigrated environments only; core MSP services (ticketing, comms, billing) remain operational.
- **Migrated Clients:** Already-cutover phases are evaluated individually. If RMM-B failure is systemic, migrated phases will also be rolled back under the same bridge-licensing arrangement.
- **Client Communication:** Unmigrated clients receive a status update stating monitoring is in a maintenance/recovery window. SLA adjustments are applied per contract. No client-facing operational downtime is assumed for non-RMM workflows.

### 3. Migration Execution Rules
- Each phase requires: client change request → backup/validation → agent staging → test patch-approval workflow run → go/no-go sign-off → production cutover.
- The RMM-A cancellation notice is sent **before Phase 1 completes**. This is intentional to lock cost savings, but requires that early phases are low-risk, thoroughly tested, and highly documented.
- If a phase fails post-cutover, the team will troubleshoot on RMM-B first rather than reverting, unless critical SLA thresholds are breached.

### 4. Go/No-Go Criteria & Monitoring
Leadership reviews progress biweekly. Migration continues while:
- RMM-B platform uptime > 99.5%
- Patch-approval workflow passes UAT in staging environments
- Zero client escalations related to monitoring or patching gaps
- Internal team capacity remains ≥ 75%

Migration **halts immediately** if:
- RMM-B P1 incident > 4 hours
- >2 client environments in a single phase report broken remote access, monitoring, or patch routing
- Team capacity drops below 50% due to competing incidents

### 5. Long-Term Operational State
- **Cost:** Savings materialize upon RMM-A cancellation. Any bridge-licensing costs incurred during rollback are offset by the long-term per-endpoint reduction.
- **Compliance:** Patch-approval workflows become standardized across all 40 environments, reducing audit and SLA risk.
- **Institutional Knowledge:** This ADR documents a repeatable phased migration pattern that future hires can apply to other platform transitions (CDP, PSA, backup).
- **Known Risk:** The 60-day post-cancellation coverage gap remains an active risk. It will be revisited quarterly, or immediately if platform stability metrics degrade below Go criteria thresholds.
