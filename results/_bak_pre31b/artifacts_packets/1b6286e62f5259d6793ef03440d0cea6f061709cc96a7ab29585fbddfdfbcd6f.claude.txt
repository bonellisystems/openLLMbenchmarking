## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Helpdesk -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Triage priority matches the actual business impact described, and the response tone is consistent with the urgency that priority implies. Troubleshooting steps are ordered from least to most disruptive, a clear escalation path is given for when the fix is out of the agent's scope, and the answer is calibrated about what genuinely needs remote hands or an on-site visit.
Example character: a triage call that matches the stated impact, a non-disruptive-first troubleshooting sequence, and a named escalation trigger.

## 7 -- Strong
The troubleshooting sequence is usable and the priority call is correct, but the escalation path or SLA framing is a light miss -- present but not fully specified. Nothing here jumps to a disruptive step too early.
Example character: a correct non-disruptive troubleshooting sequence with no stated escalation trigger if it fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is a generic troubleshooting checklist (restart, check cables, reinstall) not tied to the specific symptom or business impact described in the ticket.
Example character: standard first-line steps offered without adapting to the reported symptom.

## 3 -- Weak
The priority or severity call is wrong relative to the stated impact, or the response skips straight to a disruptive fix (reimage, factory reset) without trying non-disruptive steps first, with no escalation path given.
Example character: recommends reimaging a device as the first step for a minor, non-blocking symptom.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends a data-destructive action (wipe, reformat, delete profile) with no backup step or warning, or fabricates vendor or product behavior presented as known fact. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Destructive troubleshooting step (reimage, factory reset, delete profile) recommended before non-disruptive steps, or with no backup warning.
- Priority/SLA call that does not match the stated business impact.
- No escalation path given when the issue is clearly out of scope.
- Fabricated product or vendor behavior presented as known fact.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Triage priority matches the stated business impact.
- Troubleshooting steps ordered least-to-most disruptive.
- Explicit escalation trigger and path given.
- SLA/urgency framing consistent with the ticket's actual severity.


## Task

30 minutes:

Ticket A -- Submitted by a name partner (flagged VIP in the ticketing system): "Need my

Your standard SLA tiers: P1 = business-critical or an imminent legal/court deadline, 1-hour
SLA. P2 = significant single-user impact, 4-hour SLA. P3 = minor or cosmetic, next-business-
day. Triage all four tickets: give each one a priority tier, order them for handling, and
justify each call using the actual business/legal impact described -- not who submitted it.

### Detailed Rationale

#### 1. Ticket **B** – “Can’t log into the court’s e‑filing portal… filing deadline in 90 minutes”
- **Legal impact**: A missed filing deadline can result in loss of pleadings, motions, or other court‑required documents, potentially leading to sanctions, case dismissal, or adverse rulings.
- **Business impact**: The attorney cannot proceed with the filing, halting progress on an active litigation matter that likely involves billable hours, client expectations, and possibly court‑ordered timelines.
- **Urgency**: 90‑minute window leaves virtually no slack – the issue must be resolved within the **1‑hour P1** SLA, making it the *most time‑critical* of all tickets.

#### 2. Ticket **D** – “DMS down for the entire firm – all 30 attorneys affected”
- **Enterprise impact**: Every attorney loses access to all active case files, which includes the documents needed for the filing in Ticket B, as well as any ongoing work.
- **Operational impact**: The outage stalls the firm’s core workflow, creates a backlog risk, and could cascade into missed deadlines across multiple matters.
- **Urgency**: Although the problem is broader than Ticket B, the firm‑wide nature still qualifies it as **P1**. However, because the filing deadline is imminent, we address the DMS issue **immediately after** the filing portal restoration.

#### 3. Ticket **C** – “Second monitor flickers on and off sometimes”
- **Impact scope**: Affects a single user (the paralegal). The flicker can cause lost work, reduced productivity, and frustration, but it does not impede any other attorney or firm‑wide process.
- **Severity**: While disruptive, the issue is **not** a system‑wide outage nor does it involve a legal deadline. It meets the definition of a *significant single‑user impact* → **P2** (4‑hour SLA). It will be tackled after the two P1 incidents.

#### 4. Ticket **A** – “Need my email signature updated with the new firm logo”
- **Impact**: Purely cosmetic; does not affect casework, client communication, or compliance.
