## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Steps that silently assume undocumented tribal knowledge.
- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

Your MSP's helpdesk documentation lead handed you the following prose description of how
new front-desk and clinical staff get EHR system access at an outpatient clinic client, and
asked you to convert it into a numbered checklist a brand-new helpdesk tech could follow
unaided, in the correct order, without needing to ask anyone what to do next.

"When a new employee starts, HR sends us their name, role, and start date. Before we create
anything, we need the clinic's practice manager to email formal written approval naming the
specific EHR access role -- front-desk scheduling-only, or clinical charting access --
because we've had an incident where a front-desk hire was given clinician-level chart access
by mistake, since someone assumed the access level from the job title alone instead of
getting it in writing. Once we have that written approval, we create the Active Directory
account, add the account to the correct EHR role group (never assign chart-level access to a
scheduling-only role, even temporarily, even to save time), and enable MFA on the account
before it is used for the first time. Finally, we log the account creation and a reference to
the approval email in the client's compliance folder, since this clinic is a HIPAA covered
entity and gets audited on new-user access every year."

Convert this into a numbered, testable checklist in the correct execution order. Preserve the
two points in the prose where skipping a step or doing things out of order would be an actual
compliance problem, not just an inconvenience.

**New‑Employee EHR‑Access Provisioning Checklist**  
*(Designed for a brand‑new help‑desk technician to follow un‑aided. Each item can be verified as “Done/Not Done.”)*  

1. **Receive HR notification**  
   - Confirm you have the new hire’s **full name, job title/role, and start date** in the ticket/HR email.  
   - *If any of these fields are missing, stop and obtain the missing information before proceeding.*

2. **Obtain written approval from the Practice Manager**  
   - Request (and receive) a **formal email** from the practice manager that:  
     - Explicitly names the **EHR access role** the employee will receive (e.g., “Front‑Desk Scheduling‑Only” **or** “Clinical Charting Access”).  
     - Includes the **new hire’s full name** and **start date**.  
   - **Compliance check:** The approval **must be in writing** and must specify the exact EHR role. *Skipping this step or using a job title alone is a HIPAA audit failure.*  
   - Save this email in a dedicated “New‑User Approvals” folder for the clinic.

3. **Create the Active Directory (AD) account**  
   - Using the naming convention defined for the clinic, create the **AD user account** (e.g., `first.last@clinic`).  
   - Set the **account expiration** to “Never” (or per clinic policy) and **do not enable the account** yet.

4. **Add the account to the correct EHR role group**  
   - Review the practice manager’s approval email and add the new AD account **only to the group that matches the approved role** (e.g., “FrontDesk_Schedulers” or “Clinical_Charting”).  
   - **Critical compliance step:** *Never add the account to any higher‑privilege group (e.g., Clinical Charting) if the approval specifies a lower‑privilege role, even temporarily.* Adding chart‑level access to a scheduling‑only hire violates policy and can trigger an audit finding.  
   - Verify group membership in the group‑membership report.

5. **Enable Multi‑Factor Authentication (MFA)**  
   - Configure MFA for the new account using the clinic’s approved method (e.g., Microsoft Authenticator, YubiKey).  
   - Confirm MFA is **enabled before the account is used for the first login**.  
   - Test a manual sign‑in to ensure MFA prompts correctly.

6. **Enable the AD account**  
   - Set the account status to **Enabled**.  
   - Ensure the account is **not enabled until after step 5 is completed**.

7. **Log the provisioning details**  
   - Create an entry in the clinic’s **Compliance Log** (or designated spreadsheet) that includes:  
     - New hire’s name and role.  
     - Date of AD account creation.  
     - Reference to the **practice manager’s approval email** (link or file path).  
   - Store this log entry in the client’s **Compliance Folder** where auditors will look for it.

8. **Verify audit readiness**  
   - Run a quick check: open the Compliance Folder → confirm the approval email is present and linked to the log entry.  
   - Confirm the AD account exists, is a member of the **correct** EHR role group, and MFA is enabled.  
   - Document any discrepancies and remediate before the account is used.

9. **Notify the new hire**  
   - Send the new employee an email with:  
     - Their temporary password (if applicable).  
     - Instructions for MFA enrollment.  
     - A reminder to log in and complete any required training.  
   - Confirm receipt and that they can log in successfully using MFA.

  - *Assigning chart‑level (clinical) access to a scheduling‑only hire—even for a few minutes—creates a compliance violation.* The audit checklist explicitly looks for “chart‑level access granted to non‑clinical roles,” and any such instance must be flagged and remediated.  

By following the checklist in the exact order above, the help‑desk technician can guarantee that every required compliance gate is passed before the new employee ever touches the EHR system.
