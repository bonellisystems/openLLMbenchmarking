# Cybersecurity -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS identifiers presented as real.
- Eradication or patching recommended before containment on an active incident.
- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.
- Ignoring implied breach-notification or regulatory timelines when sensitive data is involved.
- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Severity correctly triaged and prioritized against the described impact.
- Containment-before-eradication-before-recovery ordering respected.
- Real, correctly-applied CVE/CWE/ATT&CK references where relevant.
- Evidence-preservation explicitly called out.
- Notification or compliance timeline awareness when data exposure is implied.

## Tabletop Exercise Response: Law Firm Cyber Incident
**Incident ID:** TF-2024-LF-001
**Client:** [Mid-Size Law Firm]
**Facilitator Notes:** This response prioritizes immediate containment, legal obligations, and insurance compliance. The timeline is Day 1, with the ransom demand at the 06:30 hour mark.

---

### Priority 1: Technical Containment
**Objective:** Stop the active compromise and prevent lateral movement, acknowledging that MFA is non-functional against this specific attack vector.

**1. Immediate Network Isolation of the ADC**
The initial compromise is the NetScaler ADC. Because CVE-2023-4966 (Citrix Bleed) allows Remote Code Execution (RCE), the ADC is fully compromised, not merely hijacked.
*   **Action:** Disconnect the NetScaler ADC from the network immediately. This cuts off the attacker's C2 channel and prevents them from pivoting to the file server (FS-DOCS02) through the ADC's management interface.
*   **Rationale:** MFA is ineffective here. The attacker holds a valid session token. Resetting passwords or forcing re-authentication does not invalidate a *currently active* session that has already bypassed the MFA challenge. The only way to stop the attacker from using the ADC to access internal resources is to remove the ADC from the network entirely.

**2. Block Inbound/Outbound Traffic at the Perimeter**
*   **Action:** Configure firewall rules to block all traffic to and from the NetScaler ADC's IP address. Block outbound connections from FS-DOCS02 to prevent the ransomware from spreading to other servers.
*   **Rationale:** The ransomware on FS-DOCS02 suggests lateral movement is already underway or imminent. Isolation prevents the encryption payload from reaching other systems, including email servers and workstations.

**3. Terminate Active Sessions**
*   **Action:** Force-terminate all active sessions on the ADC. Do not rely on "session timeout" policies.
*   **Rationale:** Since the session tokens are replayed, the sessions remain valid until the ADC is taken offline or the tokens are explicitly invalidated. Forcing termination ensures the attacker cannot complete any ongoing operations (e.g., file encryption, data exfiltration).

**4. Identify Compromised Accounts**
*   **Action:** Identify all user accounts that were used to authenticate through the ADC prior to the attack.
*   **Rationale:** While MFA won't stop the *active* session, it is necessary to know which accounts are compromised for forensic analysis and to prevent future access once the ADC is re-provisioned.

---

### Priority 2: Evidence Preservation & Litigation Hold Compliance
**Objective:** Preserve forensic evidence on FS-DOCS02 without violating active litigation holds or destroying privileged work product.

**1. Notify Legal Counsel and Litigation Hold Custodians**
*   **Action:** Immediately inform the firm's outside counsel and the litigation hold custodian (typically the General Counsel or designated IT legal liaison) that FS-DOCS02 is compromised.
*   **Rationale:** The server holds case files under litigation hold. Any IT action on this server must be approved by legal counsel. Uncoordinated forensic imaging or disk wiping could be construed as spoliation of evidence, which is a contempt of court offense and could result in sanctions.

**2. Forensic Imaging of FS-DOCS02**
*   **Action:** Engage a digital forensics firm to perform a bit-for-bit forensic image of the *original* disk media and a memory dump of the server (if it can be booted from a write-blocked environment).
*   **Critical Constraint:** Do not boot the server from the compromised OS if possible. Use a write-blocked forensic environment. The encryption may have already overwritten some data, but the disk image preserves the *state* of the server before encryption completed.
*   **Rationale:** The encryption payload has destroyed the *files*, but the *forensic artifacts* (logs, metadata, partial encryption keys, process memory) on the disk are critical evidence for law enforcement (FBI) and for the insurance claim.

**3. Preserve Access Logs**
*   **Action:** Securely copy all access logs from the NetScaler ADC, the file server, and any DNS/proxy logs.
*   **Rationale:** These logs prove the breach and establish the timeline. They are essential for the insurance claim (to prove the incident occurred) and for law enforcement (to prove the attacker's identity). They must be preserved in a tamper-evident manner to avoid spoliation claims.

**4. Do Not Attempt to Decipher or Restore Files**
*   **Action:** Do not attempt to decrypt the files on FS-DOCS02 without explicit legal counsel approval.
*   **Rationale:** Any attempt to restore or manipulate the files could destroy the forensic image and violate the litigation hold. The files are held in litigation; the forensic image is the evidence of their existence and compromise.

---

### Priority 3: Insurance & Consent-to-Settle Compliance
**Objective:** Ensure all remediation spending is pre-approved by the carrier to avoid voiding the cyber insurance policy.

**1. Engage the Cyber Insurance Carrier BEFORE Remediation Spending**
*   **Action:** Contact the firm's cyber insurance broker and claims adjuster immediately. Do not engage an IR vendor until the carrier has approved the engagement and budget.
*   **Rationale:** The policy is **claims-made** with a **consent-to-settle clause**. This means:
    *   The firm must report the incident to the carrier *before* settling or remediating.
    *   The carrier must consent to any vendor engagement or cost before the firm spends.
    *   **If the firm hires an IR vendor without carrier approval, the carrier can deny the claim entirely**, leaving the firm to pay for all remediation out-of-pocket.

**2. Obtain Pre-Approval for the IR Vendor**
*   **Action:** Submit the IR vendor's proposal and scope of work to the carrier for pre-approval.
*   **Rationale:** The carrier typically has a preferred vendor list. Using an approved vendor ensures that the costs incurred are eligible for reimbursement. If the vendor is not on the carrier's list, the carrier may deny coverage for their services.

**3. Document All Costs and Decisions**
*   **Action:** Keep a detailed log of all communications with the carrier, all vendor invoices, and all decisions regarding remediation.
*   **Rationale:** In the event of a claim dispute, the carrier will scrutinize whether the firm acted in accordance with the policy terms. A clear paper trail demonstrates good faith and compliance with the consent-to-settle clause.

---

### Priority 4: Ransom Negotiation
**Objective:** Address the ransom demand without unconditionally recommending payment, prioritizing legal compliance and insurance coverage.

**1. Do Not Pay the Ransom Without Carrier and Legal Approval**
*   **Action:** Do not pay the 12 BTC demand unconditionally. Paying the ransom without carrier approval could void the insurance policy.
*   **Rationale:**
    *   **Insurance:** The carrier's consent-to-settle clause likely prohibits paying the ransom without their approval. Paying without approval could void the claim.
    *   **Legal:** Paying a ransom may have legal implications depending on jurisdiction (e.g., potential criminal liability, ethical rules for attorneys).
    *   **Effectiveness:** Paying does not guarantee decryption or data recovery.

**2. Engage Law Enforcement**
*   **Action:** Report the incident to the FBI (IC3) and the local law enforcement agency.
*   **Rationale:** Law enforcement can provide intelligence on the threat actor, which may help in negotiating a lower ransom or recovering data without payment. The FBI also works with ransomware groups and may facilitate negotiations on behalf of the victim.

**3. Negotiate Through the Insurance Carrier**
*   **Action:** Engage the carrier's negotiation team to handle the ransom demand.
*   **Rationale:** The carrier has experience negotiating with ransomware groups and can leverage relationships with law enforcement. They can also provide guidance on the legal and financial implications of paying.

**4. Evaluate the Risk of Non-Payment**
*   **Action:** Work with legal counsel and the carrier to assess the risk of non-payment, including the potential for data exfiltration and the impact on litigation matters.
*   **Rationale:** While paying is generally not recommended, the firm must also consider the impact of the data breach on its clients and the litigation matters. If the data is exfiltrated, the firm may face additional legal and reputational risks that could be mitigated by paying. However, this decision must be made in consultation with the carrier and legal counsel.

---

### Summary of Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Contain:** Disconnect ADC, block perimeter traffic, terminate sessions.
2.  **Notify:** Inform legal counsel and litigation hold custodian.
3.  **Preserve:** Secure forensic image of FS-DOCS02 and all logs.
4.  **Comply:** Engage cyber insurance carrier and obtain pre-approval for IR vendor.
5.  **Negotiate:** Report to law enforcement, engage carrier for ransom negotiation, do not pay without approval.

# Tabletop Exercise Response: Mid-Size Law Firm Ransomware Incident
**Scenario Trigger:** CVE-2023-4966 (Citrix Bleed) → FS-DOCS02 Encryption → 12 BTC Ransom Demand
**Response Cadence:** Day 1, 08:14 – 15:00+
**Guiding Principle:** Prioritize litigation hold compliance, privilege preservation, and insurance coverage protection before technical remediation spend.

---

## PRIORITY 1: Technical Containment (Citrix Bleed / Session-Token Theft)
*MFA bypass is inherent to this vulnerability. Resetting or re-enrolling MFA does not invalidate a stolen session token. Containment must target token lifecycle, network access, and post-exploitation persistence.*

1. **Network Isolation of NetScaler ADC/Gateway**
   - Apply firewall rules to drop all inbound/outbound traffic to the NetScaler, except from a hardened jump host under legal/IR team direction.
   - Do NOT power off the appliance; volatile session data and connection tables may be needed for forensics.

2. **Session Token Invalidiation (Critical)**
   - Execute Citrix’s emergency token reset: revoke all active session cookies, force logout of all authenticated users, and purge the session database.
   - Reset the NetScaler’s internal session key material and rotate any API/service accounts that authenticated through it.
   - Recognize that MFA enrollment/reset is irrelevant here: token replay continues until tokens are cryptographically invalidated at the ADC level.

3. **Identify Initial Access & Persistence**
   - Hunt for indicators of compromise (IoCs) from SOC telemetry: unexpected outbound connections, new local accounts, modified scheduled tasks, dropped binaries, or WebAdmin backdoors.
   - Validate whether the initial foothold was unpatched ADC, compromised credentials, or supply chain. If credentials were leveraged, begin emergency rotation of any accounts that authenticated through the NetScaler.

4. **Isolate FS-DOCS02**
   - Block all network traffic to FS-DOCS02 at the switch/firewall layer to halt ongoing encryption and lateral movement.
   - Preserve power and memory state for forensic acquisition (see Priority 2).

5. **Apply Vendor Mitigation**
   - Deploy Citrix emergency patch or disable the vulnerable components (per Citrix Emergency Workaround Guide for CVE-2023-4966).
   - Enable enhanced logging on all ADC/Gateway endpoints and monitor for C2 callbacks, credential dumping, or domain admin activity.

---

## PRIORITY 2: Evidence Preservation on FS-DOCS02 (Litigation Hold & Privilege)
*Mass encryption is actively threatening spoliation exposure. Preservation must fulfill litigation holds without violating attorney-client privilege or work product doctrine.*

1. **Engage Outside Counsel Immediately**
   - Direct all forensic activity under legal supervision. The IR/forensic team must be deemed an agent of counsel so that privilege and work product doctrine attach to findings, reports, and access logs.
   - Counsel will issue a formal preservation directive that aligns with the active litigation holds and documents the chain of custody.

2. **Forensic Acquisition (No Alteration of Originals)**
   - Capture volatile memory (RAM) first, then create a bit-for-bit forensic disk image of FS-DOCS02 using write-blockers.
   - Do NOT attempt to decrypt, run antivirus, delete files, or bypass ransomware encryption on the original system. Alteration risks spoliation sanctions, destroys metadata, and may trigger additional destructive routines.
   - All analysis occurs on forensic copies. The original remains sealed under legal hold.

3. **Privilege & Work Product Safeguards**
   - Maintain a privilege log documenting any documents accessed during forensic triage.
   - Limit scope to case files, matter metadata, and system artifacts directly tied to the 3 held matters. Avoid broad scanning of unrelated firm data.
   - Communications with the forensic team, threat actor, or insurers regarding this incident should be routed through counsel to preserve privilege.

4. **Litigation Hold Compliance Documentation**
   - Document what was encrypted/locked vs. what remains intact. This creates a contemporaneous record showing good-faith preservation efforts.
   - If decryption fails and matter deadlines approach, counsel will assess whether to notify opposing counsel or seek court guidance. Actively preserving the encrypted dataset satisfies the hold; it does not constitute spoliation.

5. **Log & Telemetry Preservation**
   - Export EDR alerts, NetScaler session logs, filesystem audit logs, and Active Directory authentication logs. Store in a forensically sound, access-controlled repository under counsel oversight.

---

## PRIORITY 3: Insurance & Vendor Engagement Protocol
*The policy is claims-made with a consent-to-settle clause. Unauthorized spend or vendor selection can void coverage or trigger carrier subrogation rights.*

1. **Who Must Be Engaged BEFORE Remediation Spend Begins**
   - **Cyber Insurance Carrier:** File a formal notice of incident immediately. Request activation of the claims handler, reserve allocation, and panel vendor list.
   - **Cyber Insurance Counsel:** Engage a firm experienced in policy interpretation, consent-to-settle mechanics, and carrier negotiation. They will manage privilege, align response scope with policy definitions, and protect coverage.
   - **Carrier-Approved IR Vendor:** Do not select your own firm until the carrier approves vendor selection. Panel vendors are typically pre-negotiated, coverage-aligned, and carry the required professional liability coverage.
   - **eDiscovery / Litigation Support Counsel:** To align forensic preservation with hold requirements and ensure carrier-approved vendors understand privilege boundaries.

2. **Why Acting First Jeopardizes Coverage**
   - **Consent-to-Settle / Material Expenditure Clause:** Most cyber policies require carrier pre-approval for vendor selection, incident-response retainers, and any spend beyond minimal emergency containment. Unilateral engagement is typically treated as a voluntary payment or unauthorized material decision, which can trigger coverage denial or reduction.
   - **Claims-Made Trigger:** Coverage responds to claims made during the policy period. Late notification, unauthorized spend, or failure to use panel vendors can violate notice-and-cooperation conditions, giving the carrier grounds to deny or limit coverage.
   - **Subrogation & Residual Value:** Carriers often retain subrogation rights against IR vendors and decryption services. Selecting non-panel vendors can forfeit those protections and shift liability back to the firm.

3. **Immediate Action**
   - Notify carrier within policy-defined notice windows (typically 24–72 hours). Even emergency containment spend may require retroactive approval if done without consent.
   - Document all carrier communications, approvals, and scope decisions. Do not incur any third-party costs until written consent is received, unless counsel advises otherwise under emergency containment carve-outs.

## PRIORITY 4: Ransom Demand Assessment (No Unconditional Payment Recommendation)
*12 BTC / ~$780k / 96-hour deadline. Payment is a last-resort option, not a default.*

1. **Do Not Pay Unconditionally**
   - Payment does not guarantee decryption. Historical data shows 15–30% of paying actors deliver functional decryption keys; others deliver corrupt files or disappear.
   - Payment funds criminal operations, incentivizes repeat targeting, and may violate future federal/state ransomware payment restrictions.

2. **Mandatory Pre-Payment Checks**
   - **Actor Attribution & Sanctions Screening:** Use threat intelligence (carrier panel vendor, OSINT, or specialized firm) to identify the ransomware group. Run against OFAC SDN, EU sanctions, and UK HMT lists. Paying sanctioned entities is a federal felony.
   - **Reputation & Decryption Track Record:** Verify whether the group has publicly demonstrated successful decryption for similar file types/volumes.
   - **Backup Viability:** Confirm whether clean, recent, offline backups exist for FS-DOCS02. If yes, payment is almost never justified.
   - **Litigation & Regulatory Impact:** Paying could trigger mandatory reporting (FTC, state AGs, bar association ethics obligations if client matter data is exposed) and affect ongoing matters.

3. **Decision Framework (Counsel-Guided)**
   | Factor | Payment Favored | Payment Opposed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Backup availability | None or untested | Clean, recent, offline |
   | Litigation deadlines | Imminent, high-stakes matters at risk | Flexible or extendable |
   | Actor sanctions status | Unverified/clear | OFAC/sanctioned |
   | Decryption track record | Proven for similar scale | Unknown or negative |
   | Carrier/Counsel approval | Written pre-approval obtained | Not yet secured |

4. **If Payment Becomes the Advised Last Resort**
   - Require written approval from General Counsel, Managing Partner, and carrier.
   - Use escrow or negotiated payment terms: partial payment upfront, verification of decryption key delivery before final transfer, and contractual-style assurances (as practical with criminal actors).
   - Document the decision rationale thoroughly for regulatory, bar ethics, and insurance audit purposes.
   - Simultaneously begin restoration from backups or forensic reconstruction of encrypted files.

5. **Alternative Pathways**
   - Engage carrier-approved negotiation specialists if attribution suggests a group that accepts structured dialogue.
   - Pursue law enforcement coordination (FBI IC3, local cyber task force) for threat intel and potential recovery resources.
   - Activate matter contingency plans: request court extensions, engage alternative counsel for held matters, and communicate with clients under attorney direction.

### 2. Preserving Evidence on FS‑DOCS02 While Honoring the Litigation Hold & Work‑Product Privilege

**Why Acting First Could Jeopardize Coverage**

- **Policy Language**: A *claims‑made* policy with a *consent‑to‑settle* provision typically states: *“The Insured shall not incur any costs or retain any vendor without prior written consent of the Insurer.”*  
- **Trigger of Coverage**: The insurer’s obligation to indemnify begins **only after** the notice of loss and **after** the insured has obtained the insurer’s consent for remediation activities.  
- **Risk of Denial**: If the firm engages a forensic firm, hires a ransom‑negotiator, or pays the ransom **without** that consent, the carrier can argue a **breach of the consent condition**, thereby **nullifying** the claim. The firm would then be forced to absorb the entire cost (potentially millions) plus any legal penalties.  
- **Audit Trail**: The carrier will request **invoices, contracts, and a detailed expenditure log**. Without prior approval, those documents become *unsupported* and may be rejected.

**Procedure to Safeguard Coverage**

1. **Immediate Notice of Loss** – Submit a formal incident report to the insurer (often within 24 hours).  
2. **Request Written Consent** – Provide a **detailed cost estimate** (e.g., forensic imaging $X, external IR firm $Y, ransom‑negotiator $Z).  
3. **Obtain Carrier Acknowledgment** – Keep the carrier’s written approval (email or formal letter) as part of the chain‑of‑custody documentation.  
4. **Track All Spend** – Every vendor engagement, hourly rate, and travel expense must be logged and matched against the approved budget.  

Only after this **approval cascade** should the firm authorize any external spend.

2.  **Session Invalidation:** Force a global logout of all sessions. This is the only way to invalidate the stolen tokens. This must be done via the Citrix administration console or by resetting the private keys used for session encryption (which invalidates all existing tokens).
3.  **Credential Rotation:** Rotate all administrator and privileged credentials on the NetScaler appliance, Active Directory, and any associated service accounts. Assume all compromised accounts are breached.
4.  **Patch/Upgrade:** Apply the emergency security patch for CVE-2023-4966 immediately. If a reboot is required to patch, ensure all sessions are already invalidated (Step 2) to prevent the patch itself from being intercepted.

### What Happens If You Spend First?
1. **Policy Violation** – The insurer may deem the expenditure “unauthorized” and refuse to reimburse, even if the expense is later deemed reasonable.
2. **Potential Bad‑Faith Claim** – If the insurer later discovers that the firm paid a ransom or a vendor without consent, it could allege “failure to mitigate” or “breach of policy conditions,” jeopardizing the entire claim.
3. **Precedent for Future Claims** – A pattern of “spend‑first” behavior can lead the carrier to impose stricter consent requirements on all future incidents, increasing administrative burden.

> **Bottom line:**  **All remediation‑related spend must be approved by the insurer *before* contracts are signed or payments are made.**  The approval process typically involves:
- Submitting a detailed scope of work (e.g., forensic imaging, ransom‑negotiation services).
- Providing cost estimates.
- Obtaining a written “Letter of Authorization” that states the carrier will reimburse up to a defined limit.

Only after that authorization is received can the firm proceed with the vendor(s) and incur the expense.
