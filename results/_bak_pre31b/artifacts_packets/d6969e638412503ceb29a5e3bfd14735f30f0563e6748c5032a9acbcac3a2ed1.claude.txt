## Anchors

# SEO -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Technical recommendations are correct for the stated platform (canonical tags, redirects, schema, robots directives used properly), keyword strategy is matched to actual search intent, and every recommendation is prioritized by expected impact versus effort. No black-hat tactic is suggested, and the answer is calibrated about ranking-factor uncertainty rather than asserting exact algorithm weights as fact.
Example character: a prioritized fix list starting with the highest-impact technical issue, each item tied to a specific, correctly-described mechanism.

## 7 -- Strong
The technical guidance is sound and usable for the stated platform, and keyword direction matches intent. It is missing an explicit effort-versus-impact prioritization, or one recommendation is generic rather than platform-specific, but nothing here is technically wrong.
Example character: correct on-page and technical fixes listed without ranking them by which matters most.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is SEO-fundamentals-correct (use target keywords, build backlinks, improve page speed) but reads as a generic checklist not tied to the specifics of the site or platform given.
Example character: a standard keyword-and-backlink checklist with no reference to the site's actual issues.

## 3 -- Weak
Contains outdated or incorrect technical claims (keyword stuffing helps rankings, more backlinks always help regardless of quality), or recommends tactics unrelated to the actual ask.
Example character: advice built around an outdated ranking factor treated as still authoritative.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends black-hat or manipulative tactics (cloaking, link farms, hidden text, doorway pages) as safe practice with no penalty-risk warning, or fabricates specific ranking-factor claims as verified fact. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Black-hat tactics (cloaking, PBNs, hidden text, keyword stuffing) recommended without a penalty-risk warning.
- Fabricated or overconfident claims about exact Google ranking-factor weights.
- Technical errors in canonical, redirect, or robots guidance that would deindex pages.
- Ignoring a stated platform or CMS constraint.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Recommendations prioritized by effort versus expected impact.
- Correct technical implementation detail for the stated platform (canonical, redirects, schema, robots).
- Keyword strategy matched to actual search intent, not just volume.
- Honest flag of algorithm-update or ranking-factor uncertainty.

### 3. Most Likely Technical Root Cause

- **“Crawled – currently not indexed”** jumped from ~40 pages to **1,100 pages**.

These patterns are classic symptoms of **canonical‑tag or URL‑structure misconfiguration** introduced during a redesign:

3. **Noindex / Robots.txt Issues** – The redesign could have inadvertently added `noindex` directives or blocked pages via robots.txt that were previously crawlable.

**Most plausible single root cause:** **Canonical‑tag inconsistency / duplicate‑content signaling caused by the new templating engine** (e.g., hard‑coded canonical URLs, templating variables not resolving, or canonical URLs pointing to the old domain/structure).

*   **Goal:** Ensure the most important service pages are no more than 3 clicks away from the homepage.

## 3.  The most plausible technical root cause  

The simultaneous spikes in:

1. **“Crawled – currently not indexed”** (from ~40 → 1,100 URLs)  
2. **“Duplicate, chosen canonical differs from user”** (from 0 → 380 URLs)

are classic symptoms of **incorrect or missing canonical directives after a URL‑structure change**.  

Typical scenarios that create this pattern:

| Scenario | How it produces the observed symptoms |
|----------|----------------------------------------|
| **New URL template that drops “/pages/” and rewrites everything to “/content/”** while *keeping* the old URLs alive via 302 redirects or by not adding proper 301 redirects. | [model] crawls the new URLs, sees duplicate content (the same page rendered under both old and new URLs), and often **chooses the canonical that matches the user‑visible URL** (the new one) → many old URLs become “duplicate, canonical differs”. |
| **Pagination or “view‑all” pages created by the new template that generate infinite URL parameters** (e.g., `?page=2`, `?tab=overview`) without `rel=canonical` pointing to the primary page. | Crawl budget is spent on low‑value variations; the primary page may not be explicitly canonicalized, so [model] may index the variations as separate pages while the canonical URL is dropped → “Crawled – not indexed”. |
| **Robots.txt or meta‑robots changes** that unintentionally block important sections of the new template (e.g., a `noindex` tag added to a layout component). | [model] crawls the URLs, sees they’re blocked, marks them as “Crawled – not indexed”. |
| **Loss of internal linking & sitemap updates** after the redesign. | Important pages become “orphaned” – [model] still discovers them via external links but can’t associate them with a strong canonical, leading to indexing loss. |

Given the numbers (1,100 “Crawled – not indexed” + 380 “canonical differs”), **the canonical‑mismatch issue is the most likely catalyst**. It explains why many URLs that used to rank are now invisible to [model], causing the 42 % organic traffic collapse.
