## 1. Quick‑look verdict – is the “first read” correct?

**Short answer:** *No.*  
The two clauses together do **not** give the client a clean, uncapped safety net for a data‑breach claim caused by ordinary negligence. The contract creates a *gap*:

| Clause | What it says | Effect on a data‑breach caused by ordinary negligence |
|--------|--------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **8.1 Indemnification** | Vendor must indemnify only for **gross negligence or willful misconduct**. | The vendor would **not** be required to indemnify the client because the breach is only ordinary negligence. |
| **8.2 Limitation of Liability** | “The limitation shall **not apply** to claims arising from a breach of Section 9 (Data Protection).” | Because the limitation **does not apply**, the **12‑month‑fees cap is removed** for this claim. The vendor’s **direct liability** (i.e., the damages it must pay) is therefore **uncapped**.  |

Result:  
- **Client:** gets uncapped exposure to the vendor’s *direct* liability for the breach (good for the client).  
- **Vendor:** gets **no indemnification** (because the breach is not “gross negligence”) **and no liability cap** (because the cap is carved out).  

Thus the “favorable‑to‑client” reading holds only for the *client’s* perspective; it also creates a **massive, potentially unlimited** exposure for the vendor that they will almost certainly push back on. If the client’s goal is simply to **ensure the vendor can’t hide behind a cap**, the language works – but if the client also wants the vendor to *actually* bear the risk (i.e., be obligated to indemnify), the current drafting fails.

## 2. Detailed legal analysis

### 2.1. Interaction of 8.1 and 8.2

1. **Indemnification trigger (8.1)** – limited to *gross negligence* or *willful misconduct*.  
   - *Ordinary negligence* = **not** covered.  
   - The clause is *affirmative* (vendor must *indemnify* the client) but only for the defined fault level.

2. **Limitation carve‑out (8.2)** – states the aggregate liability cap **does not apply** to:
   - breaches of confidentiality (Sec 6)  
   - breaches of data‑protection (Sec 9)  
   - indemnification obligations under 8.1  

   The phrase *“claims arising from a party's breach of Section 9”* is **broad** – it does **not** qualify the breach by fault level. Consequently, **any** claim that can be linked to a Section 9 breach (including ordinary negligence) is removed from the cap.

3. **Resulting risk matrix**

| Fault level | Covered by 8.1? | 8.2 cap applies? | Net result |
|-------------|----------------|------------------|------------|
| Gross negligence / willful misconduct | **Yes** (indemnify) | **No** (carve‑out) | Vendor must indemnify; no cap on indemnified amount |
| Ordinary negligence | **No** (no indemnity) | **No** (carve‑out) | Vendor liable for damages *directly*; no cap |
| Strict liability / breach of statutory duty (e.g., GDPR fines) | **No** (unless gross/willful) | **No** (carve‑out) | Unlimited exposure (often undesirable to vendor) |

### 2.2. Does a “data breach” automatically equal a “breach of Section 9”?

- **Section 9 (Data Protection)** is typically a *contractual obligation* to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures, to comply with applicable privacy laws, and to promptly report incidents.  
- A breach of that clause can arise **regardless of the degree of fault** – the contract does not say “only for gross negligence.”  
- Therefore, a data breach caused by ordinary negligence **does satisfy** the “claim arising from a breach of Section 9” trigger in 8.2.

### 2.3. Practical consequences

- **For the client:** the contract does give an *uncapped* avenue to recover damages from a data‑breach, even if the vendor was merely careless. That is a strong protection.
- **For the vendor:** the contract imposes *potentially unlimited* liability without the safety net of indemnification. A prudent vendor will demand either:
  1. **Broadening the indemnity** to include *any* breach of Section 9 (or at least ordinary negligence), **or**
  2. **A specific monetary cap** on *direct* liability for data‑protection breaches (e.g., 3‑5× the annual fees) while still preserving an uncapped indemnity for *gross* fault.

If the parties cannot reconcile these positions, the negotiation may stall or the vendor may walk away.

1. **Clarify that any breach of Section 9—regardless of fault—removes the cap**, *and*  
2. **Require the vendor to indemnify the client for *all* third‑party claims arising from such a breach**, not just gross negligence.  

> **Proposed amendment to Section 8.2 (Limitation of Liability):**  
> 
> “Notwithstanding the foregoing, the aggregate liability limitation set forth in this Section shall **not apply** to:  
> 
> (a) any claim arising from a breach of Section 6 (Confidentiality) or Section 9 (Data Protection), **regardless of whether such breach is caused by negligence, gross negligence, willful misconduct, or any other fault level**; and  
> 
> (b) any indemnification obligations of Vendor under Section 8.1.  
> 
> In addition, Vendor shall **indemnify, defend, and hold harmless** Client for **all** third‑party claims, damages, losses, fines, penalties, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys’ fees) **directly resulting from Vendor’s breach of Section 9**, whether such breach is caused by ordinary negligence, gross negligence, or willful misconduct.  The indemnification obligations in paragraph (b) are **in addition to** any other remedies available at law or in equity.”

**Short answer**

*Under the draft language, an ordinary‑negligence data‑breach claim would **not** be covered by the indemnification carve‑out and therefore **would be subject to the 12‑month‑fees cap** in Section 8.2.*  
The indemnity carve‑out only triggers when the vendor’s conduct meets the **“gross negligence or willful misconduct”** threshold in Section 8.1. Ordinary negligence falls short, so the claim falls back onto the general liability‑cap provision.  

Because the contract is high‑value and the carve‑out is the only place where the client hopes to escape the cap for data‑related claims, the current wording creates a **gap** that should be closed.

---

## 1.  How the two sections interact

| Provision | Text (relevant excerpt) | What it covers |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| **8.1 – Indemnification** | “Vendor shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Client from any third‑party claims **arising out of Vendor's gross negligence or willful misconduct** in performing the Services.” | Only **gross negligence / willful misconduct** triggers the indemnity obligation. |
| **8.2 – Limitation of Liability** | “Except for Vendor's indemnification obligations under Section 8.1, in no event shall either party's aggregate liability … exceed the fees paid in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim… **Notwithstanding the foregoing, this limitation shall not apply to claims arising from a party's breach of Section 6 (Confidentiality), Section 9 (Data Protection), or a party's indemnification obligations under Section 8.1**.” | The cap **does not apply** to (i) breach of confidentiality, (ii) breach of data‑protection obligations, or (iii) **any claim that gives rise to an indemnification obligation under §8.1**. |

### Logical flow

1. **Is the claim covered by §8.1 indemnification?**  
   *Only if the vendor’s conduct is “gross negligence or willful misconduct.”*  
   Ordinary negligence **does not satisfy** this trigger.

2. **If not covered by §8.1, does any other carve‑out apply?**  
   The carve‑out in §8.2 refers to “breach of Section 9 (Data Protection).”  
   That carve‑out **does not hinge on fault**; it simply says that *any* claim arising from a breach of Section 9 is exempt from the cap **regardless of the underlying negligence level**.

   *However*, the carve‑out is **conditional** on the claim *also* being one for which the vendor has an indemnification obligation under §8.1. In practice, the draft reads as:

   > “The limitation shall not apply to … a party's indemnification obligations under Section 8.1 **and** to claims arising from a breach of Section 9.”

   Because “indemnification obligations under Section 8.1” only arise when the vendor’s conduct meets the **gross negligence/willful‑misconduct** test, the carve‑out for a data‑protection breach is **only triggered when the vendor’s breach also satisfies that higher fault standard**.

   Consequently, a data‑breach caused by **ordinary negligence** does **not** satisfy the §8.1 trigger, and therefore the claim falls back onto the generic 12‑month‑fees cap.

---

## 2.  What the current language actually permits

- **Ordinary‑negligence data‑breach** →  
  1. No indemnification obligation under §8.1 (fault threshold not met).  
  2. Not a “claim arising from a breach of Section 9 **that also gives rise to an indemnification obligation**.”  
  3. Therefore the claim is subject to the **aggregate liability cap** of 12 months’ fees.

- **Gross‑negligence or willful‑misconduct data‑breach** →  
  1. Triggers indemnification under §8.1.  
  2. The claim is expressly exempt from the cap under §8.2’s carve‑out.  
  3. Result: ** uncapped** liability.

Thus, the “broad carve‑outs” that *look* favorable to the client **only work when the vendor’s conduct meets the higher fault standard**. Ordinary negligence is left exposed to the cap.

---

## 3.  Redline to close the gap

Below is a **plain‑text redline** that can be inserted into Section 8.2 (right after the existing “Notwithstanding the foregoing…” sentence) or alternatively as a new sub‑paragraph. The language adds an explicit, fault‑agnostic carve‑out for data‑protection breaches.

```
Section 8.2 (Limitation of Liability)

Except for Vendor's indemnification obligations under Section 8.1, in no event shall either party's aggregate liability under this Agreement exceed the fees paid in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim. Notwithstanding the foregoing, this limitation shall not apply to claims arising from a party's breach of Section 6 (Confidentiality), Section 9 (Data Protection), or a party's indemnification obligations under Section 8.1.

**Redline Insertion (new sentence):**  
For the avoidance of doubt, any claim arising from the Vendor's breach of Section 9 (Data Protection) shall not be subject to the aggregate liability cap set forth in this Section 8.2, **regardless of the degree of fault** (including ordinary negligence) attributable to the Vendor.
```
