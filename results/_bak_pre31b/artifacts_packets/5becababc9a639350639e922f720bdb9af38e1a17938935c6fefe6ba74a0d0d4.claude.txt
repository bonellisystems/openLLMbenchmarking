**Effective Date:** [Date]  
**Applies To:** All employees issued shop-floor tablets, corporate laptops, and related network access.

---

#### 1. Scope and Reasonable Personal Use
This policy governs the use of all corporate-issued devices, including shop-floor tablets and corporate laptops. The Company recognizes that employees may have legitimate reasons for occasional personal activity while using these assets.

*   **Permitted Personal Use:** Reasonable personal use is permitted, provided such use does not interfere with job performance, compromise security, violate safety protocols, degrade network performance, or violate applicable laws. Examples may include occasional personal email checks or accessing personal communication tools during authorized breaks.
*   **Device Responsibility:** Employees are responsible for securing devices against unauthorized access and reporting loss or theft immediately.

#### 2. Monitoring Disclosure
You acknowledge that corporate devices and network resources are Company property provided for business purposes.

*   **Right to Monitor:** The Company reserves the right to monitor, inspect, access, and record all activity on Company devices and network traffic. This includes, but is not limited to, tracking usage patterns, inspecting data stored on devices, and logging network communications.
*   **No Expectation of Privacy:** By using Company assets, you waive any expectation of privacy regarding activity conducted on or through these devices and the corporate network. Monitoring is conducted to ensure security, productivity, compliance, and protection of Company and operational technology (OT) systems.

#### 3. Prohibited Activities (OT/ICS Safety Constraints)
To protect the integrity, safety, and reliability of our production environment, the following activities are strictly prohibited. Violations involve significant risk to personnel and equipment and are subject to heightened consequences.

*   **No Personal Media on Production Systems:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, portable storage devices, or removable media to any production-line PC or equipment connected to the OT/ICS network is prohibited. Only Company-approved, sanitized storage devices may be used on OT/ICS systems.
*   **No Personal Email on OT/ICS:** Opening, downloading, printing, or processing personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network is prohibited. Personal email must only be accessed on devices connected strictly to the corporate IT network.
*   **Other Prohibitions:** Bypassing security controls, installing unauthorized software, or attempting to access systems outside your authorized role is prohibited.

#### 4. Protected Employee Rights
Nothing in this policy, including the monitoring provisions or disciplinary consequences, is intended to prohibit, restrict, or discourage employees from engaging in protected concerted activity.

*   **Explicit Carve-Out:** Employees retain the right under applicable federal labor law (including the National Labor Relations Act) to discuss wages, hours, benefits, working conditions, and other terms and conditions of employment with coworkers, supervisors, or third parties.
*   **Monitoring Limitation:** Company monitoring will not be used to retaliate against, penalize, or discourage employees for such protected discussions. Monitoring data will not be relied upon to discipline an employee solely for engaging in protected concerted activity.
*   **Clarification:** While discussion is protected, this policy does not grant immunity for other violations. For example, while discussing wages is protected, using a device to bypass a USB ban on a production PC is not.

#### 5. Consequences and Safety Violations
Violations of this AUP may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment and legal action.

*   **General Violations:** Unauthorized personal use that degrades performance, minor policy infractions, or non-critical security violations may result in warnings or suspension, depending on severity and frequency.
*   **Safety-Critical OT Violations:** The Company maintains a zero-tolerance stance toward actions that compromise safety-critical OT systems or the ICS network.
    *   **Immediate Termination:** Any violation involving the introduction of unauthorized media to production-line PCs, tampering with OT/ICS configurations, opening personal email attachments on OT/ICS networks, or any action that endangers personnel safety or product integrity will result in **immediate termination of employment**.
    *   **Legal Remediation:** The Company reserves the right to pursue legal action for damages resulting from such violations.

#### 6. Acknowledgment
*   **Mandatory Acceptance:** No device will be issued until the employee has reviewed this AUP and provided written acknowledgment.
*   **Sign-Off:** Upon device issuance, you must sign the **Device Acceptance & Monitoring Acknowledgment** form, confirming that you have read this policy, understand the monitoring provisions, acknowledge the prohibited activities, and agree to comply with all terms.

# ACCEPTABLE USE POLICY (AUP)
**Corporate Laptops & Shop-Floor Tablets**
**Effective Date: [Date] | Version: 1.0**

## Section 1: Scope and Purpose
This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) applies to all corporate-owned laptops, shop-floor tablets, and any peripheral devices issued by [Company Name] ("the Company"). This policy governs how these devices may be used on both the general corporate network and the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control-System (ICS) network.

## Section 2: Reasonable Personal Use and Monitoring Disclosure
**2.1 Permitted Personal Use.** Employees are permitted to use Company devices for reasonable personal purposes during non-work hours, provided such use does not interfere with work performance, violate security protocols, or incur excessive data costs. This includes limited use of social media, email, and web browsing for personal matters.

**2.2 Monitoring of Device and Network Activity.** The Company reserves the right to monitor all activity on Company devices and networks, including but not limited to email, internet browsing, file transfers, and device connectivity logs. This monitoring is conducted for legitimate business purposes, including network security, asset protection, and operational efficiency.

**2.3 Protected Concerted Activity.** Notwithstanding Section 2.2, the Company acknowledges that under Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), employees retain the right to engage in **protected concerted activity**. Monitoring activity shall **not** be used to identify, target, or penalize employees for discussing wages, hours of employment, benefits, or working conditions with each other, or for organizing or engaging in lawful collective bargaining. The Company will not use monitoring tools to collect data specifically intended to surveil protected conversations, and all monitoring logs shall be reviewed with the understanding that protected NLRA activity is exempt from disciplinary action.

**2.4 Acknowledgment and Issuance.** No employee shall be issued a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet until they have received a clear explanation of this AUP, specifically including the monitoring provisions described in Sections 2.2 and 2.3. The employee must sign an **AUP Acknowledgment Form** confirming that they have read, understood, and agree to comply with this policy. Unsigned devices will not be released.

## Section 3: Prohibited Uses
**3.1 OT/ICS Network Restrictions.** The Operational-Technology (OT) and Industrial-Control-System (ICS) networks control critical manufacturing equipment, assembly line robotics, and production diagnostics. These networks are strictly segmented from the general corporate network. The following are strictly prohibited on devices connected to the OT/ICS network:
*   **Personal Email Attachments:** Employees shall not open, download, or execute personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes email received on a personal account accessed via the OT network, or any attachment not originating from an approved Company distribution list.
*   **External Media:** The execution or import of files from external sources onto OT/ICS connected devices is prohibited unless explicitly authorized by the IT Security and OT Engineering teams.

**3.2 Production-Line USB Restrictions.**
*   **Unapproved USB Drives:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, memory sticks, or external storage devices to production-line PCs or shop-floor tablets is strictly prohibited.
*   **Approved Media:** Only approved, Company-issued external storage media (e.g., encrypted drives issued by IT) may be connected to production-line systems.
*   **Rationale:** Unauthorized media connections pose a significant risk of introducing malware, ransomware, or data exfiltration that could halt production lines or compromise safety-critical systems.

## Section 4: Disciplinary Consequences
**4.1 General Violations.** Violations of this AUP that do not impact safety-critical systems may result in a verbal warning, written reprimand, performance improvement plan (PIP), or suspension, at the Company's sole discretion.

**4.2 Safety-Critical OT Violations.** Violations of Section 3.1 (OT/ICS Network Restrictions) or Section 3.2 (Production-Line USB Restrictions) that are determined to have the potential to impact safety-critical systems, production integrity, or data security are treated as serious offenses.
*   **Termination:** The Company reserves the right to terminate an employee's employment **immediately and without notice** for violations involving the OT/ICS network or production-line PCs.
*   **No-Recourse:** Employees who violate these provisions shall not be eligible for severance, unemployment benefits related to the violation, or other post-employment benefits associated with the termination.
*   **Legal Action:** In cases where a violation results in physical injury, equipment damage, or significant data breach, the Company reserves the right to pursue legal action against the individual and their employer.

## Section 5: Policy Review and Amendments
The Company reserves the right to modify this AUP at any time. Revised policies will be communicated to all employees via [Company Intranet / Email] and require re-acknowledgment upon issuance of a new device.

## Pre-Rollout Recommendations

Before deploying this AUP, the following steps should be taken to ensure legal compliance, operational readiness, and employee buy-in:

1.  **Legal Counsel Review:** Have employment counsel in relevant jurisdictions review the "Protected Concerted Activity" carve-out (Section 2.3) to ensure it is not inadvertently overbroad or creates a liability for the company. Ensure the termination language (Section 4.2) complies with at-will employment statutes in the state(s) where facilities operate.
2.  **IT/OT Security Audit:** Verify that the technical controls match the policy language. For example, ensure that USB ports on production-line PCs are actually disabled or blocked at the hardware level if "prohibited" is the stated policy. A policy that says "don't plug it in" but allows it via a bypassable port is unenforceable.
3.  **Employee Training Session:** Host a mandatory town hall or departmental briefing explaining the "why" behind the OT restrictions (specifically cybersecurity and physical safety risks). A policy signed without understanding is easily contested in arbitration or litigation.
4.  **NLRB Consultation:** Confirm that the monitoring carve-out for Section 7 activity is sufficient to withstand scrutiny. If the company uses monitoring tools (e.g., keystroke logging, network packet inspection) that inherently capture protected activity, ensure the data retention policy is set to purge such data immediately to minimize risk.
5.  **Sign-Off Logistics:** Establish a process for tracking signed acknowledgments. If a device is issued without a signed form, that device should not be activated until compliance is documented.

**ACCEPTABLE USE POLICY (AUP)**
*Scope: Shop-Floor Tablets & Corporate Laptops Issued to Employees*
*Applicable to: Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology/Industrial Control Systems (OT/ICS) environments*

---

### 1. Permitted Personal Use & Company Monitoring Rights
Employees may engage in reasonable, incidental personal use of corporate-issued tablets and laptops. Personal use should be brief, occasional, and conducted outside of safety-critical operational periods. It must not interfere with job duties, degrade system performance, consume disproportionate network resources, or violate any other company policy.

All corporate-issued devices and the networks they connect to remain the exclusive property of the company. Accordingly, the company reserves the right to monitor, log, inspect, and audit device activity, network traffic, stored data, and communications at any time, with or without advance notice to the individual user. Monitoring is conducted for purposes including security threat detection, OT/ICS network integrity verification, compliance enforcement, and policy adherence.

---

### 2. Prohibited Uses & OT/ICS Security Controls
Given the safety-critical nature of our production environment, the following uses are strictly prohibited:
- Opening, forwarding, clicking, or executing personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network.
- Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, external storage media, or personal mobile devices to production-line PCs, HMI panels, or any OT/ICS-connected workstation.
- Bypassing USB-port restrictions, network segmentation controls, or endpoint protection agents deployed on shop-floor devices.
- Installing unauthorized software, accessing unverified websites, or bridging OT/ICS segments to untrusted networks while logged into production devices.

These restrictions are non-negotiable due to their direct impact on personnel safety, equipment integrity, and production continuity.

---

### 3. Monitoring Disclosure & Device Issuance Acknowledgment
Employees are explicitly notified that all activity on corporate-issued devices and the networks they access may be monitored, recorded, and reviewed by authorized personnel (IT, OT/ICS security, compliance, and leadership). This monitoring may include, but is not limited to, network traffic analysis, endpoint logging, application and file access records, and communication metadata.

Prior to device issuance, each employee must:
- Receive a written briefing covering this monitoring disclosure and the full scope of this AUP.
- Sign and return the **Device Issuance & AUP Acknowledgment Form**, confirming they have read, understood, and agree to comply with these terms.

Possession, activation, or first-time login of a corporate device constitutes acceptance of this monitoring and these policy terms. Signed acknowledgments are retained in the employee’s personnel and IT asset records and will be collected before any device is handed out.

---

### 4. Violations & Disciplinary Consequences
Violations of this AUP will be investigated and addressed in accordance with the company’s progressive discipline framework, up to and including termination of employment. Consequences will be calibrated based on the nature, severity, intent, and impact of the violation, as well as the employee’s prior compliance history.

**Safety-Critical OT/ICS Violations:** Any confirmed violation tied to the OT/ICS network, production-line PCs, or safety-critical systems—particularly unauthorized email attachments, unapproved USB connections, or bypassing security controls—may result in immediate disciplinary action, up to and including summary termination. Such violations are treated as gross misconduct due to their potential to cause equipment damage, production downtime, or physical harm to personnel. In cases involving willful misconduct or conduct that may violate applicable safety or cybersecurity regulations, the company reserves the right to refer the matter to external authorities.

Employees will be informed of the specific policy provisions at issue and provided an opportunity to respond before disciplinary action is finalized, consistent with applicable employment law.

---

### 5. Protected Concerted Activity & Employee Rights
Nothing in this Acceptable Use Policy is intended to restrict, prohibit, or penalize employees from engaging in activity protected under federal labor law, including but not limited to the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Employees retain the full right to freely discuss and exchange information regarding wages, hours, benefits, working conditions, workplace safety, scheduling, and other terms and conditions of employment with fellow employees, regardless of union affiliation or collective bargaining status.

The monitoring, logging, and disciplinary provisions in this AUP apply exclusively to unauthorized device usage, security violations, and actions that compromise system integrity or safety. They are not intended to, and shall not be construed to, monitor, interfere with, or discipline employees for protected communications about wages, hours, or working conditions. The company will not take adverse action against any employee for exercising rights protected by federal, state, or local labor laws.

---

### Pre-Rollout Implementation Notes
Before deploying this AUP across the shop floor, the manufacturer should complete the following steps:

1. **Legal & Compliance Review:** Have employment counsel and OT/ICS security leadership review the final draft to ensure alignment with state privacy notice requirements, NLRB guidance on monitoring policies, OSHA communication standards, and IEC 62443/ISA-62443 OT security frameworks.
2. **Technical Enforcement Verification:** Confirm with IT/OT engineering that USB-port controls, network segmentation, email attachment filtering on OT endpoints, and endpoint logging are actively enforced *before* policy rollout. Policy language alone cannot secure a safety-critical network.
3. **Supervisor & Workforce Communication:** Schedule briefings for both office and floor supervisors. Distribute the AUP in plain language, with translations as needed, and establish a dedicated channel for employee questions prior to acknowledgment collection.
4. **Acknowledgment Collection Process:** Implement a secure, auditable workflow for signing and storing acknowledgments (digital or physical). Devices should remain locked in asset control until signed forms are returned and verified.
5. **Pilot & Feedback Loop:** Run a controlled pilot on a single production line or department. Gather input from shop-floor users, safety personnel, and OT engineers, then adjust monitoring tooling, policy wording, or issuance procedures for clarity and practicality before enterprise-wide deployment.

### 1. Purpose  
This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) establishes the rules for use of company‑issued tablets and laptops to protect the integrity, safety, and confidentiality of our operational‑technology (OT) and industrial‑control‑systems (ICS) networks while allowing reasonable personal use.

### 2. Scope  
All employees, contractors, consultants, and temporary staff who receive a company‑issued tablet or laptop (“Corporate Device”) are covered by this AUP. The policy applies to all devices in the workplace and when the device is used in any capacity that accesses company OT or corporate networks.

### 3. Definitions  
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Corporate Device** | Any tablet or laptop issued by the Company. |
| **OT/ICS Network** | The production‑line control network that directly manages equipment and safety systems. |
| **Personal Use** | Activities that are non‑work related (e.g., email, social media, browsing) that do not interfere with work duties or compromise security. |
| **Prohibited Use** | Any activity that is expressly forbidden by this AUP. |
| **Monitoring** | The Company’s right to observe, record, and analyze activity on Corporate Devices and associated networks. |

### 4. Acceptable Use  
1. **Reasonable Personal Use** – Employees may use Corporate Devices for legitimate personal activities (e.g., checking personal email, social media, light web browsing) during non‑production hours or when such use does not interfere with job duties or network security.  
2. **Work‑Related Use** – All primary use of Corporate Devices must support the employee’s assigned duties.  
3. **Security Practices** – Employees must keep devices up to date with approved software, use strong passwords, and immediately report any lost or stolen device to IT.

### 5. Prohibited Use  
The following activities are strictly forbidden on Corporate Devices, especially when connected to OT/ICS networks:  
1. **Opening Personal Email Attachments** – No personal email attachments may be opened on any device that is connected to the OT/ICS network.  
2. **Unapproved USB Devices** – Connecting personal USB drives, flash disks, or any external storage media to production‑line PCs or OT/ICS‑connected devices is prohibited.  
3. **Other Prohibited Actions** – Any activity that compromises OT/ICS integrity, safety, or data confidentiality (e.g., installing unauthorized software, tampering with network configurations).

### 6. Monitoring  
The Company reserves the right to monitor all activity on Corporate Devices and the networks to which they connect, including:  
* Network traffic logs  
* File transfer logs  
* Application usage records  
* Email communications (subject to legal and privacy safeguards)  

Monitoring is conducted in accordance with applicable laws and Company privacy policies.

### 7. Disclosure & Acknowledgment  
1. **Disclosure** – Prior to issuance, all employees will receive written notice that Corporate Devices and OT/ICS networks are subject to monitoring.  
2. **Acknowledgment** – Employees must sign an acknowledgment form confirming they understand and accept the monitoring, the acceptable and prohibited use guidelines, and the consequences for violations before the device is issued.

### 8. Consequences of Violations  
Violations that compromise the safety or integrity of OT/ICS systems will be treated with the utmost seriousness. Potential disciplinary actions include:  

| Severity | Example Violation | Disciplinary Action |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Low** | Minor non‑security related policy breach | Verbal warning, retraining |
| **Medium** | Unauthorized use of personal USB on a non‑OT device | Written warning, temporary revocation of device access |
| **High** | Opening a malicious attachment on an OT/ICS device or connecting unapproved USB to a production‑line PC | Immediate suspension, investigation, possible termination |

Any violation that endangers life, property, or the company’s critical operations may result in immediate termination of employment.

### 9. Employee Rights & Protected Activities  
The Company explicitly recognizes that employees have the right to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, and other employment matters with one another.  
**Discussions concerning wages, hours, or working conditions are *not* prohibited** by this AUP and are expressly protected under federal labor law. The monitoring and enforcement provisions herein are limited to the use of Corporate Devices and OT/ICS networks and do **not** extend to employee‑to‑employee conversations about employment conditions.

### 10. Policy Review and Update  
This AUP will be reviewed annually or as needed to reflect changes in technology, regulatory requirements, or company operations. Employees will be notified of any changes and will be required to re‑acknowledge the updated policy.

### 11. Rollout & Implementation  
Before the policy takes effect:  

1. **Legal & Compliance Review** – Confirm that the AUP complies with all applicable federal, state, and local labor and privacy laws.  
2. **IT & Security Consultation** – Ensure monitoring tools and logging mechanisms are properly configured and documented.  
3. **Management Sign‑off** – Obtain approval from senior leadership and HR.  
4. **Training & Communication** – Conduct mandatory training sessions for all employees and distribute the AUP and acknowledgment form.  
5. **Device Issuance** – Issue devices only after the employee signs the acknowledgment.  
6. **Monitoring SOPs** – Publish standard operating procedures for IT security staff on how and when to review logs.  

Once these steps are complete, the policy will be formally adopted and all new Corporate Devices will be issued under the AUP framework.

**Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) – Corporate Devices & Shop-Floor Equipment**

**I. Authorized Use, Personal Use, and Monitoring**

Employees are granted access to corporate laptops and shop-floor tablets to perform their job duties. The Company recognizes that limited, reasonable personal use of these devices may occur (e.g., personal web browsing during breaks, non-business communication), provided such use does not interfere with job performance, compromise security, or violate Company policy.

However, all devices issued by the Company are Company property. The Company reserves the right to monitor, inspect, and record all activity on corporate devices and the corporate network, including internet usage, file transfers, and communications. By accepting a device, employees acknowledge that there is no expectation of privacy regarding Company data, device usage, or network activity.

**Labor Law Protection:** Notwithstanding the monitoring provisions above, this Policy is not intended to, and shall not be interpreted to, restrict or prohibit employees from engaging in protected concerted activity under federal labor law (including but not limited to Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act). Employees retain the right to discuss wages, hours, and working conditions with one another. The Company does not monitor communications between employees regarding protected concerted activity.

**II. Prohibited Activities: OT and Production Security**

Due to the safety-critical nature of our manufacturing operations, specific prohibitions apply to devices connected to the Operational Technology (OT) / Industrial Control Systems (ICS) network.

1.  **Personal Email Attachments:** Employees are strictly prohibited from opening, downloading, or executing personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes, but is not limited to, shop-floor tablets used for equipment diagnostics or laptops assigned to production supervisors.
2.  **USB Devices:** Only Company-approved USB drives (e.g., encrypted drives issued by IT) may be connected to production-line PCs or OT interfaces. Connecting unapproved personal USB drives to these systems is a violation of this Policy due to the risk of introducing malware or unauthorized data transfers.

**III. Disclosure and Acknowledgment**

The Company provides clear disclosure to all employees that the use of Company devices and networks is subject to monitoring. Before a corporate laptop or shop-floor tablet is issued, the employee must:

1.  Read this Acceptable Use Policy in its entirety.
2.  Complete a brief training module covering OT security and the monitoring provisions.
3.  Sign a digital or physical Acknowledgment Form confirming they understand the Policy, the monitoring rights, and the prohibited activities.

Failure to sign the Acknowledgment Form prior to device issuance will result in the device being withheld.

**IV. Disciplinary Action and Consequences**

Violations of this Policy will be addressed in accordance with the Company’s Disciplinary Action Framework. The severity of the response will depend on the nature of the violation and the potential risk to operations.

*   **General Violations:** Minor violations (e.g., excessive personal browsing) may result in a verbal or written warning, suspension of device privileges, or retraining.
*   **Safety-Critical OT Violations:** Violations involving the OT/ICS environment (e.g., connecting unauthorized USB drives to production PCs or introducing malware via email on OT networks) pose a direct threat to worker safety and operational continuity.
    *   **Consequence:** Violations of this nature will result in immediate disciplinary action, **up to and including termination of employment.**
    *   **Investigation:** The Company reserves the right to conduct a forensic investigation of the device to determine the scope of the violation and potential impact on the production environment.

Personal use may occur during breaks or non-working hours. The Company reserves the explicit right to monitor, access, record, and review all activity on corporate devices and across all company networks. This includes, but is not limited to, network traffic, email communications, file transfers, application usage, location data (where applicable), and keystrokes. Employees should have no expectation of privacy regarding any data stored on, transmitted through, or accessed via company devices and networks.

Due to the safety-critical nature of agricultural equipment manufacturing and the integrity of our automation infrastructure, the following actions are **strictly prohibited** on devices connected to Operational Technology (OT), Industrial Control Systems (ICS), or production-line networks:

*   **Personal Email Attachments:** Opening, downloading, or executing attachments from personal email accounts on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. This includes shop-floor tablets, HMIs, engineering workstations, and any laptop docked to production systems.
*   **Unapproved External Media:** Connecting personal USB drives, external hard drives, smartphones, or other unapproved storage media to production-line PCs, CNC controllers, robotics controllers, or any OT-connected device. Only Company-approved, sanitized, and encrypted media may be used for data transfer to production systems, and only via designated secure protocols.

### 3. Monitoring Disclosure and Pre-Issuance Acknowledgment

This policy serves as clear disclosure that all device and network activity is subject to monitoring by IT Security and authorized management personnel. Monitoring is conducted to ensure security, protect intellectual property, maintain operational reliability, and ensure compliance with safety standards.

No corporate laptop, shop-floor tablet, or access to OT/ICS networks will be issued or enabled until the employee has signed the **AUP Acknowledgment Form**. This signature confirms that the employee has read, understood, and agrees to abide by this policy. IT will not provision devices for employees with unsigned acknowledgments on file.

### 4. Disciplinary Consequences and OT Safety Criticality
Violations of this AUP may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination, depending on the severity of the infraction.

**Safety-Critical OT Violations:**
Violations involving safety-critical OT/ICS systems pose direct risks to personnel safety, heavy machinery operation, and production integrity. Therefore, prohibited actions on OT networks are treated as **Gross Misconduct**.
*   Connecting unapproved personal USB drives to production-line PCs or OT devices.
*   Opening personal email attachments on OT-connected devices.
*   Attempting to bypass OT security controls.

**Consequence:**
Violations of OT/ICS prohibitions will typically result in **immediate termination**, regardless of intent, due to the potential for catastrophic safety incidents, equipment damage, or prolonged production shutdown. The Company may also pursue legal action if violations result in significant liability or harm.

### 5. Protected Concerted Activity Carve-Out
**Preservation of Labor Rights:**
Nothing in this AUP, including its monitoring provisions or disciplinary consequences, shall be construed to prohibit, interfere with, or limit employees' rights to engage in protected concerted activity under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) or applicable state labor laws.

Employees are expressly permitted to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, terms of employment, and other labor-related topics with coworkers using company devices and networks. The Company will not discipline employees for such discussions, nor will monitoring be used to penalize employees for engaging in these protected communications.

**Clarification of Safety vs. Rights:**
While discussions regarding wages and working conditions are protected, these rights do not authorize the use of prohibited methods that endanger safety-critical systems. For example, employees may discuss working conditions, but they must not do so by connecting personal USB drives to production-line PCs or opening personal email attachments on OT devices, as the safety risk to the operation remains prohibited regardless of the content of the discussion.

6.  **Signature Workflow:** Implement a digital signature workflow in the HRIS that tracks issuance. Ensure IT device provisioning is technically gated to this signature status to prevent accidental issuance.
