# Cybersecurity -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Severity is triaged correctly against the actual impact described, response actions follow containment before eradication before recovery, and any CVE, CWE, or ATT&CK reference used is real and correctly applied (never invented). Evidence-preservation is called out before disruptive remediation, and breach-notification or compliance timelines are flagged when the scenario implies regulated or sensitive data. The answer is calibrated about what needs live forensics versus what can be assessed from the facts given.
Example character: a triaged action list that isolates the affected system first, preserves logs, then patches, with the real CVE cited only where it is genuinely relevant.

## 7 -- Strong
The response plan is sound and correctly ordered (containment before eradication), and is directly usable by a responder. References to standards or CVEs are generic rather than specific, or evidence-preservation/notification timing gets only a light mention. Severity is judged reasonably even if not formally scored.
Example character: a correct incident-response sequence that never names a specific CVE or ATT&CK technique even where the prompt supports one.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice reads as a generic security checklist (patch systems, enable MFA, monitor logs) that is directionally correct but not prioritized by the actual severity or tailored to the specific incident described.
Example character: a list of general hardening steps with no sense of which one matters most given the described exposure.

## 3 -- Weak
The response gets the ordering wrong (patches or removes an attacker's access before containing them), misjudges severity, or pads the answer with boilerplate ("enable a firewall") that does not engage the specific scenario.
Example character: jumps straight to "eradicate the malware" on an active intrusion without isolating the host first.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS score as if verified, or recommends actively dangerous steps (disable logging, do not isolate an active breach) as sound practice. Empty or off-task response.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated CVE, CWE, or CVSS identifiers presented as real.
- Eradication or patching recommended before containment on an active incident.
- Recommending disabling logging or otherwise destroying evidence.
- Ignoring implied breach-notification or regulatory timelines when sensitive data is involved.
- Treating every finding as equally critical with no severity differentiation.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Severity correctly triaged and prioritized against the described impact.
- Containment-before-eradication-before-recovery ordering respected.
- Real, correctly-applied CVE/CWE/ATT&CK references where relevant.
- Evidence-preservation explicitly called out.
- Notification or compliance timeline awareness when data exposure is implied.

### 1. Regulatory Timeline for Notifying the Covered Entity

**Answer:**
Upon discovery of a breach involving unsecured Protected Health Information (PHI), the MSP is contractually and operationally obligated to notify the Covered Entity **immediately**, defined as within **one to two hours**.

*   **Rationale:** Under the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule, the Covered Entity has a strict **60-day calendar clock** to notify HHS and, if necessary, the media and individuals, starting from the date the breach was *discovered*. As the MSP, we act as the eyes and ears for infrastructure and system integrity. Delaying notification to the practice would jeopardize its ability to meet the federal 60-day deadline.
*   **Process:** Our incident response protocol triggers immediate escalation to the practice's designated Breach Response Lead via our agreed-upon out-of-band communication channels. If the practice is unresponsive for more than four hours, our Service Level Agreement (SLA) grants us the authority to initiate a "break-glass" notification to the practice's Board of Directors or Compliance Officer to ensure regulatory obligations are not missed. We maintain detailed logs of the time of discovery and the timestamp of the notification to the CE to demonstrate due diligence.

### 2. Multi-Factor Authentication for Remote Administrative Access

**Answer:**
**Yes.** The MSP enforces Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for **all** remote administrative access to systems that contain, process, or transmit PHI.

*   **Implementation:** We utilize cryptographic factors for administrative roles, including FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware tokens or TOTP (Time-Based One-Time Password) generators. Password-only authentication is prohibited for remote administrative sessions.
*   **Scope:** This policy applies to all network infrastructure, virtualization layers, database consoles, and application management portals accessible remotely. We ensure that privileged access management (PAM) solutions enforce MFA and just-in-time access for all admin accounts, regardless of whether the user is technically managing PHI directly.
*   **Alignment:** This requirement aligns with NIST SP 800-63B guidelines for authentication and addresses the HIPAA Security Rule's recommendation for access control safeguards. We also enforce MFA for any vendor portals that provide the practice with access to the MSP's managed environments.

### 3. Verification Process for Third-Party Software CVEs

**Answer:**
The MSP employs a multi-layered "Secure by Design" verification process to assess third-party software components (such as managed file transfer tools) for known vulnerabilities before deployment in the practice's environment. This process includes:

1.  **CISA KEV Catalog & NVD Verification:** Prior to deployment, we check the specific software version against the **CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog** and the National Vulnerability Database (NVD). We reject any component listed in the KEV catalog or containing unpatched Critical/High severity CVEs unless a validated remediation or compensating control is approved by the practice's compliance team.
    *   *Context:* For instance, regarding tools like MOVEit, we would verify if the vendor has provided a supported patch for CVE-2023-34362. If the software is End-of-Life (EOL) or lacks a patch for a known exploitable vulnerability, deployment is blocked.
2.  **Vendor Assurance & Patch Policy Review:** We require vendors to demonstrate an active patch management lifecycle. We review the vendor's security posture, including their SLA for patching critical vulnerabilities (typically requiring patches within 30 days of disclosure) and their support status.
3.  **Automated Scanning & SCA:** We run the software through our Software Composition Analysis (SCA) tools and vulnerability scanners to identify known CVEs in the binary and dependencies. Additionally, we perform threat intelligence scanning to ensure the vendor's domain and infrastructure do not exhibit indicators of compromise.
4.  **Contractual Safeguards:** Our Master Services Agreement (MSA) includes clauses requiring the vendor to notify the MSP within 24 hours of any CVE disclosure affecting the software. The MSP retains the right to remove or replace components if the vendor fails to patch critical vulnerabilities within the agreed remediation window.
5.  **Deployment Gate:** No software is deployed to the production environment without a "Clear for Deployment" sign-off, which confirms that the CVE status is acceptable and that mitigation strategies (if applicable) are documented in our risk register.

This process ensures that the MSP does not introduce known, exploitable risks into the practice's environment, directly addressing the lessons learned from recent healthcare-sector breaches.

2. **Component‑Specific Scan & Impact Assessment**  
   - The flagged component is **queried against our inventory database** to confirm it is a direct dependency of the service we intend to deploy in the practice environment.  
   - An automated **CVE impact script** pulls the CVE description, severity (CVSS v3.1 score), affected versions, and any known exploit paths.

- Require phishing-resistant authentication (FIDO2/WebAuthn security keys or platform certificates) for all human administrators; TOTP is permitted only for legacy systems during a documented migration window.

- Enforce device compliance (encrypted, patched, EDR-protected) before granting access.

- Automatically revoke or step-up authentication for anomalous locations, risky IPs, or elevated privilege operations.

Our pre-deployment vetting process for third-party software follows a risk-based, repeatable workflow designed to prevent supply-chain and vulnerability-driven incidents:

3. **Isolated Staging Validation:** Approved builds are deployed in an air-gapped or microsegmented staging environment mirroring production. We track vendor patch cycles, subscribe to security bulletins, and automate alerts for newly disclosed CVEs. Network segmentation, EDR/XDR behavioral monitoring, and data loss prevention controls are layered to contain potential exploitation.
