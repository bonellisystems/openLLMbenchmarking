## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# Finance -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
Numbers reconcile and are internally consistent, and the correct concept is applied to the work shape at hand -- accurate AP/AR aging or reconciliation logic for transactional asks, or sound forecast/valuation methodology for strategic asks. Assumptions behind any calculation are stated explicitly, work is shown rather than a bare final number, and the answer is calibrated about when a licensed CPA or controller must sign off.
Example character: a reconciliation or forecast with the formula and inputs shown, an explicit assumption flagged, and a concrete next action (a specific entry, adjustment, or budget line).

## 7 -- Strong
The methodology is correct and the numbers are usable, but one assumption is left unstated, or a reconciliation step is skipped without affecting the bottom line. The work shape (transactional versus strategic) is correctly matched to the ask.
Example character: a correct forecast or reconciliation that does not show its underlying assumptions explicitly.

## 5 -- Adequate
The direction is correct but the numbers are rounded or generic without shown work, or don't fully reconcile against the figures given in the prompt.
Example character: a plausible-looking total that cannot be traced back to the specific inputs provided.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer applies the wrong treatment (conflates cash and accrual basis, misapplies an AP/AR concept), contains a math error, or ignores the specific figures given in the prompt in favor of generic placeholders.
Example character: a calculation that silently mixes cash and accrual figures without flagging the inconsistency.

## 0 -- Unusable
States fabricated financial figures as if sourced or verified, is internally inconsistent to the point of being unusable (totals do not sum, signs are wrong), or gives advice that would misstate a regulated filing with no caveat. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Math that does not reconcile -- totals that do not sum, or an obviously wrong sign.
- Fabricated financial statistics or rates presented as real sourced data.
- Conflating cash and accrual basis without flagging it.
- Advice that would misstate a regulated filing (tax, GAAP) with no caveat that a professional should verify.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Work or formula shown, not just a final number.
- Assumptions behind any calculation stated explicitly.
- Correct AP/AR or forecast-methodology reasoning matched to the stated work shape.
- Explicit flag of when CPA or controller sign-off is needed.

### **Critical Note: Trust-to-Fee Transfer Requirements**
**Client A has a $22,000 balance in the IOLTA trust ledger.**
While this amount exceeds the $18,500 fee arrears, **the trust balance cannot be swept or applied to fees automatically.** IOLTA funds are held in fiduciary trust for specific purposes defined in the engagement letter.

**Proper Trust-to-Fee Transfer Requirements:**
Before any trust funds can be applied against fee invoices, the following conditions must be met:
1.  **Client Authorization:** The client must provide explicit, written authorization (e.g., a signed Trust Release or Transfer Form) instructing the firm to move funds from the IOLTA account to the Operating account.
2.  **Engagement Letter Compliance:** The transfer must align with the terms of the engagement letter. Since the current trust advance is designated for "future expert-witness costs," the client must acknowledge that they are diverting funds from the specific purpose of the advance to pay fees.
3.  **Designation of Purpose:** The transfer documentation must clearly state that the funds are being moved from Trust to Operating status to satisfy fee obligations, ensuring a clear audit trail that the trust is not being commingled or misused.
4.  **Firm Approval:** The transfer must be approved by the billing manager or designated trust officer to ensure the transfer is logged correctly in both IOLTA and Operating ledgers.

*Without these steps, applying the trust balance would constitute a violation of IOLTA rules and ethical obligations regarding client funds.*

### 3. Client A: Trust-to-Fee Transfer Protocol

**⚠️ PROHIBITION:** The $22,000 in the IOLTA trust ledger **must not** be applied against the fee invoice. This funds are Client A's property, held in trust, and are not firm revenue until properly released. Unauthorized conversion of trust funds is a violation of professional conduct rules and could result in disciplinary action.

**Requirements for a Proper Trust-to-Fee Transfer:**
Before any portion of the $22,000 can be moved to the operating account to cover fees, the following conditions must be satisfied:

1.  **Fees Must Be Earned:** The firm must confirm that the $18,500 fee invoice represents work fully performed and earned. Invoicing alone does not equate to earning; the work underlying the invoice must be complete.
2.  **Specific Purpose Verification:** The trust funds are an advance specifically designated for **expert-witness costs**. A transfer is only permissible if:
    *   The expert-witness costs have been incurred and paid from other sources; OR
    *   The matter status has changed such that the expert witnesses will no longer be needed; OR
    *   Sufficient trust funds remain to cover the estimated expert-witness costs after the transfer.
3.  **Authorization Mechanism (Choose One):**
    *   **Client Written Consent:** The client must provide a signed written authorization specifying the amount to be transferred and acknowledging the impact on the trust balance.
    *   **Engagement Letter Provision:** Review the engagement letter. If it contains a specific clause authorizing the firm to transfer trust funds to fees upon default/delinquency, this may permit the transfer. If no such clause exists, client consent is required.
    *   **Court/Arbitration Order:** In many jurisdictions, if the client refuses to pay fees and the engagement letter lacks a transfer clause, the firm must petition the court for an order directing the release of trust funds to satisfy the fee. Self-help transfer without authorization is prohibited.
4.  **Accounting Compliance:** Any transfer must be documented with a clear ledger entry showing the source (trust), the amount, the authorization basis, and the date, and must be reported to the client in the next statement of account.

**Immediate Step for Client A:** Contact the client to discuss the overdue fee and determine if the engagement letter contains a trust-to-fee authorization clause. If it does, propose the transfer. If it does not, request either cash payment or written consent to transfer funds now that the expert-witness phase may be concluding.
