notification analysis.

### Detailed Analysis

The state law summary indicates notification is triggered when **unencrypted personal information** (defined as Name + SSN or Account Number) is acquired by an unauthorized person.
*   **Risk:** While the account numbers were encrypted, the extract contained **Names and Partial SSNs that were unencrypted.**
*   **Determination:** Although many breach-notification statutes technically require *full* SSNs or account numbers to trigger, the presence of unencrypted Names combined with partial SSNs creates a significant risk that a regulator will deem this combination sufficient to identify the individual or facilitate fraud. Because the safe harbor requires **ALL** personal information to be encrypted, the existence of unencrypted Name/Partial SSN data means the union cannot definitively claim the safe harbor. **Prudent triage dictates assuming the trigger has occurred.**

#### 2. Who Must Be Notified
*   **Affected Residents:** The incident affects **1,200 members**. Since the state law summary implies the applicable law governs the credit union's residents (or the union operates within that state), all 1,200 members must receive notification.

#### 3. Notification Deadline
*   **Discovery Date:** July 1.
*   **Legal Requirement:** Notification must be sent "without unreasonable delay" and no later than **30 days after discovery**.
*   **Deadline Calculation:** July 1 + 30 days = **July 31**.
*   **Target:** While July 31 is the statutory maximum, "without unreasonable delay" suggests the union should aim to notify members much sooner (e.g., within a few days of investigation completion) to mitigate harm.

#### 4. Impact of Mixed Encryption on the Safe Harbor
The safe harbor provision states it applies only if **"ALL of the personal information involved was encrypted."**
*   **The Conflict:** The extract contained two types of data: Account Numbers (Encrypted) and Names/Partial SSNs (Unencrypted).
*   **The Result:** The safe harbor is **compromised**. Even though the account numbers were protected, the unencrypted Names and Partial SSNs prevent the union from claiming that *all* personal information was encrypted.
*   **Critical Nuance:** If the state statute explicitly excludes "Partial SSNs" from the definition of PII, the safe harbor *could* technically apply (as the only PII—the Account Numbers—was encrypted). However, relying on this interpretation is high-risk. Given the prompt's instruction to triage the incident, the **mixed nature of the data** acts as a failure condition for the safe harbor because the unencrypted Name/Partial SSN data creates a reasonable belief that unencrypted PII was exposed.

#### 5. Federal GLBA Context (Background)
As noted in the background, the credit union is subject to GLBA safeguarding rules separate from state law. While this analysis focuses on the state notification trigger, the union must also consider **GLBA breach reporting obligations** (e.g., to the NCUA, FTC, or CFPB) if the incident meets federal thresholds, regardless of the state safe harbor outcome.
