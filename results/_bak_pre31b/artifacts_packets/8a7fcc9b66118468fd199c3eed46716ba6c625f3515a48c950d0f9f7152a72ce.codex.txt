## 10 -- Frontier

Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable

### Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) for Corporate Devices  

#### **I. Purpose and Scope**  
This policy governs the use of all company-issued devices, including corporate laptops and shop-floor tablets, to ensure security, compliance, and operational integrity. It applies to all employees accessing company networks, systems, and data.  

#### **II. Permissible Use**  
Reasonable personal use of company devices (e.g., brief personal email or browsing during breaks) is permitted, provided it does not interfere with job performance, security, or productivity. The company reserves the right to monitor all device and network activity to protect assets and enforce compliance.  

#### **III. Prohibited Uses**  
Strictly prohibited activities include:  
1. **Opening Personal Email Attachments on OT/ICS Networks**: Employees must **not** open attachments from personal email accounts on devices connected to operational technology (OT) or industrial control systems (ICS) networks. Such actions risk malware introduction, compromising critical manufacturing processes.  
2. **Connecting Unapproved Personal USB Drives**: Employees must **not** connect personal USB drives or other unapproved removable media to production-line PCs or OT/ICS-connected devices. Only IT-sanctioned, encrypted devices are permitted to prevent data breaches or system disruption.  

#### **IV. Monitoring and Privacy Disclosure**  
The company monitors device usage, network traffic, and system logs to detect security threats, enforce policy compliance, and optimize performance. **Employees are explicitly notified that monitoring occurs** prior to device issuance. Monitoring is not intended to invade privacy but to safeguard company infrastructure. No expectation of privacy exists on company devices or networks.  

#### **V. Employee Acknowledgment**  
All employees must sign an acknowledgment form confirming receipt of this AUP and understanding of monitoring requirements before being issued company devices. The signed acknowledgment will be retained in the employee’s personnel file. Failure to sign may delay or deny device access.  

#### **VI. Consequences for Violations**  
Violations of this policy will result in disciplinary action based on severity:  
- **Non-Safety-Critical Violations**: Verbal warnings, written reprimands, or suspension.  
- **Safety-Critical OT/ICS Violations** (e.g., opening personal email attachments on OT networks): Immediate investigation, with consequences ranging from suspension to **termination of employment**. Such actions endanger personnel, equipment, or production; violations will be reported to regulatory authorities if required by law.  

#### **VII. Protected Communications**  
**Nothing in this policy prohibits employees from discussing wages, hours, or working conditions with coworkers.** Such communications are protected under federal labor law (National Labor Relations Act). The company will not discipline employees for engaging in these discussions, which are considered concerted activity.  

#### **VIII. Policy Review**  
The company will review and update this policy annually or as needed. Revisions will be communicated to all employees. Continued use of company devices constitutes acceptance of updated terms.  

---  
### **Pre-Rollout Requirements**  
Before implementation, the company must:  
1. **Legal Review**: Validate the policy with legal counsel to ensure compliance with labor laws (e.g., NLRA carve-out) and privacy regulations.  
2. **Stakeholder Alignment**: Coordinate with IT, HR, and department heads to refine OT/ICS-specific prohibitions and consequences.  
3. **Employee Training**: Conduct mandatory sessions explaining policy highlights (e.g., prohibited OT/ICS actions, protected speech) and provide written materials.  
4. **Documentation**: Distribute the APU via email, intranet, and physical copies. Collect signed acknowledgments upon device issuance.  
5. **Feedback Mechanism**: Establish a channel for employees to ask questions or report policy concerns without fear of retaliation.  

This balance ensures security, legal compliance, and employee rights while mitigating risks to operational technology systems.

# Acceptable Use Policy (AUP): Corporate and Production Devices

## 1. Purpose and Scope
This policy defines the acceptable use of computing devices issued by [Company Name] (the "Company"). This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors who utilize Company-owned equipment, including but not limited to:
*   **Corporate Laptops:** Used primarily for administrative, sales, and management functions.
*   **Shop-Floor Tablets:** Used for production tracking, inventory, and assembly guidance.
*   **Production-Line PCs:** Industrial computing units integrated into the manufacturing process.

## 2. Acceptable and Prohibited Use

### 2.1 Reasonable Personal Use
The Company recognizes that employees may occasionally need to use corporate devices for limited personal tasks (e.g., checking a personal weather update or a quick non-work-related message) during breaks. Reasonable personal use is permitted provided that:
*   It does not interfere with the employee’s work performance or productivity.
*   It does not consume excessive bandwidth or storage.
*   It does not involve illegal, harassing, or offensive material.
*   It does not violate any other Company policies.

### 2.2 Prohibited Uses (High-Risk Activities)
To ensure the safety of our staff and the integrity of our manufacturing processes, the following actions are strictly prohibited:
*   **OT/ICS Network Contamination:** Employees are strictly prohibited from opening personal email attachments (e.g., .zip, .exe, .pdf) on any device connected to the Operational Technology (OT) or Industrial Control Systems (ICS) networks. These networks are reserved exclusively for production-related traffic.
*   **Unauthorized Hardware Connection:** Employees are strictly prohibited from connecting any unapproved personal USB drives, external hard drives, or non-company-issued peripherals to any Production-Line PC. Only Company-sanctioned, scanned, and approved storage media may be used.
*   **Unauthorized Network Bridging:** Employees may not attempt to bridge the corporate Wi-Fi/LAN with the OT/ICS network via any device to bypass security controls.

## 3. Monitoring and Privacy

### 3.1 Notice of Monitoring
To ensure network security, protect intellectual property, and maintain operational uptime, the Company reserves the right to monitor, audit, and log all activity on Company-issued devices and the networks to which they connect. This includes, but is not limited to, internet browsing history, file access, email metadata, and device login timestamps. **By accepting the device, the user acknowledges they have no expectation of privacy regarding any data stored on or transmitted through Company equipment.**

### 3.2 Legal Protections (Labor Law Carve-out)
Nothing in this monitoring policy is intended to, or shall be construed to, interfere with, restrain, or prevent employees from exercising their rights under federal or state labor laws, including the right to engage in "protected concerted activity." The Company’s monitoring is conducted for the sole purposes of cybersecurity, operational integrity, and protecting company assets. This policy shall not be used to monitor or prohibit employees from discussing their wages, hours, working conditions, or other terms of employment with one another.

## 4. Compliance and Enforcement

### 4.1 Disciplinary Actions
Violations of this policy will be investigated and may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

### 4.2 Safety-Critical Violations
The Company distinguishes between administrative policy violations and violations affecting the production environment. Due to the potential for significant physical harm to personnel, environmental damage, or catastrophic equipment failure, **any violation of the rules regarding the OT/ICS network or Production-Line PCs is considered a safety-critical breach.** Violations of these specific sections will be subject to immediate and severe disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

**Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) – Corporate and Shop-Floor Devices**

**Scope:** This policy applies to all corporate laptops, shop-floor tablets, and production-line PCs issued by [Company Name]. It governs the use of devices connected to corporate IT networks, Operational Technology (OT) networks, and Industrial Control Systems (ICS).

---

### 1. Permitted Uses & Monitoring Rights
**Reasonable Personal Use:**
Employees are permitted reasonable personal use of corporate laptops and non-production shop-floor tablets during breaks or downtime, provided such use does not interfere with work duties, violate safety protocols, compromise security, or consume excessive bandwidth. Personal use is a privilege, not a right, and is subject to the restrictions outlined in this policy.

**Monitoring Disclosure:**
The Company reserves the right to monitor, log, record, audit, and inspect all activity on corporate devices and networks, including but not limited to network traffic, installed software, file access, and communication metadata. This monitoring applies to all devices, including shop-floor tablets and production-line PCs, to ensure system integrity, protect intellectual property, and maintain safety-critical operations. **Employees have no expectation of privacy regarding the use of Company devices or networks.**

### 2. Prohibited Uses: OT/ICS Safety Restrictions
To protect the safety of personnel and the integrity of manufacturing operations, the following uses are **strictly prohibited** on devices connected to OT/ICS networks, production-line PCs, and shop-floor tablets interfacing with machinery:

*   **Personal Email Attachments on OT/ICS Devices:** Opening, saving, or executing attachments from personal email accounts on any device connected to the OT/ICS network or production-line PC. This restriction applies regardless of whether the attachment appears benign, as OT networks lack the consumer-grade security controls of corporate IT networks.
*   **Unapproved Removable Media:** Connecting personal USB drives, external hard drives, mobile phone chargers with data transfer capabilities, or any unapproved removable media to production-line PCs or OT-connected shop-floor tablets. Only Company-issued, sanitized, and cryptographically signed media approved by the OT Security Team may be connected.
*   **Bypassing Controls:** Attempting to disable security software, firewall rules, or endpoint protection on any device, particularly those integrated with ICS.

*Note: Reasonable personal use permitted in Section 1 is restricted on devices designated as OT/ICS assets. Shop-floor tablets and production-line PCs are designated as OT/ICS assets and are limited to authorized operational applications only.*

### 3. Acknowledgment & Issuance
**Clear Disclosure:**
By accessing or using any Company device, you acknowledge that you have read, understood, and agree to the terms of this AUP. You understand that monitoring is active and continuous and that the Company may access data stored on devices at any time.

**Signed Acknowledgment Requirement:**
No device shall be issued to an employee until a signed acknowledgment of this policy is on file. Acknowledgment may be captured via a digital signature workflow prior to device provisioning or via a physical signature form upon handover. Failure to acknowledge this policy will result in the withholding of device issuance until compliance is achieved.

### 4. Consequences for Safety-Critical Violations
Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment, depending on the severity and context of the violation.

**OT/ICS Safety Violations:**
Due to the potential for physical harm, equipment damage, and catastrophic production failure, violations involving safety-critical OT systems are treated with the highest severity. **Any intentional or grossly negligent violation of the prohibitions listed in Section 2 (e.g., connecting unapproved USBs to production PCs or introducing malware via personal attachments to OT networks) may result in immediate suspension pending investigation and termination of employment.** This applies regardless of whether actual damage occurred; the risk introduced by such actions is sufficient grounds for severe discipline.

### 5. Protection of Concerted Activity & Labor Rights
**Explicit Carve-Out for Protected Activity:**
Nothing in this policy is intended to, and shall be interpreted to, restrict employees from exercising their rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) or other applicable labor laws.

*   **Protected Discussions:** Employees retain the right to discuss wages, hours, working conditions, and terms of employment with each other, both in person and via electronic means, on Company devices, provided such discussions do not interfere with safety-critical operations or violate specific OT security restrictions in Section 2.
*   **Monitoring Limitation:** While the Company monitors device usage for security and safety, monitoring is not conducted for the purpose of interfering with, restraining, or coercing employees in the exercise of their protected concerted rights. Disciplinary actions will not be taken based solely on employees engaging in protected discussions regarding labor relations.

### **Pre-Rollout Note: Critical Actions Before Implementation**

Before rolling out this AUP, the following steps are strongly recommended to ensure enforceability, safety, and legal compliance:

1.  **Legal Review:** Have this draft reviewed by employment counsel specializing in the jurisdiction of operation. While Section 5 contains an NLRA carve-out, local laws regarding monitoring disclosures and personal use allowances vary. Counsel should verify that the "termination for OT violations" language is defensible in your specific labor environment.
2.  **Technical Enforcement Alignment:** Ensure IT and OT security teams can technically enforce the prohibitions in Section 2. For example:
    *   Can the network firewall block personal email traffic on the OT VLAN?
    *   Are USB ports on production-line PCs physically disabled or controlled by endpoint management software that blocks unauthorized media?
    *   If technical controls are not in place, the policy is merely advisory and may be difficult to enforce.
3.  **Device Classification Audit:** Clearly tag and inventory devices. IT systems should distinguish between "Corporate IT," "Shop-Floor OT Tablet," and "Production PC" so that security policies and usage rights are applied correctly. Employees should be able to visually identify which devices have restricted personal use.
4.  **Training & Communication:**
    *   Conduct training sessions for shop-floor staff that emphasize *why* these rules exist (safety and equipment protection) rather than just *what* the rules are.
    *   Provide translations if a significant portion of the workforce is non-native English speaking.
    *   Ensure the acknowledgment process is user-friendly but auditable.
5.  **Grace Period:** Consider a brief grace period (e.g., 14 days) after rollout where violations result in counseling rather than discipline, allowing time for habits to adjust, except for willful OT safety breaches which may carry immediate risk.

No device shall be activated, and no shop-floor tablet shall be issued to a production line, until this signed acknowledgment is on file in the Department of Human Resources.

### Section 4: Consequences for Safety-Critical OT Violations

**4.1 Classification of Violations**
Violations involving the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control Systems (ICS) network are classified as **Critical Safety Violations**. Given the nature of ag-equipment manufacturing, these systems directly control production processes, safety interlocks, and potentially hazardous machinery operations.

**4.2 Disciplinary Action**
Any violation of the prohibitions in Section 2.1 or Section 2.2 constitutes grounds for **immediate termination of employment**, regardless of the employee's tenure or history of prior disciplinary warnings. In addition to termination, the Company reserves the right to pursue legal action regarding data theft, intellectual property infringement, or regulatory fines resulting from compromised OT systems.

### Section 5: Protected Concerted Activity Exclusion

**5.1 Scope of Monitoring Limitation**
Nothing in this Acceptable Use Policy, nor in the Company's exercise of its monitoring rights, shall be construed or interpreted as authorizing, encouraging, or permitting the Company to take adverse employment action against an employee for engaging in **protected concerted activity**.

**5.2 Explicit Carve-Out**
The Company explicitly recognizes that monitoring may incidentally capture communications regarding:
*   Wages, hours of employment, and benefits.
*   Working conditions and workplace safety.
*   Collective bargaining and grievance procedures.
*   Discussions with co-workers or representatives regarding these matters.

The Company shall not use data gathered through monitoring for the purpose of identifying, disciplining, or retaliating against employees engaging in such protected discussions as defined by the **National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), Section 7**, or applicable state labor laws. Monitoring logs shall be reviewed strictly for security and operational compliance purposes, not for labor relations surveillance.
