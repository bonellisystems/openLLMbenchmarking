# Knowledge Management -- scoring anchors (0-10)

## 10 -- Frontier
The runbook or document is testable: a newcomer could follow it step by step and reach the stated outcome without needing undocumented tribal knowledge. Prerequisites are listed up front, failure or rollback branches are included for steps that can go wrong, and a currency or ownership signal (last verified, owned by) is present when the prompt supports it.
Example character: numbered steps with prerequisites stated first, a rollback note on the one risky step, and a last-verified date.

## 7 -- Strong
The document is mostly step-by-step and usable as a runbook, but is missing one prerequisite check or a failure-branch note for a step that could plausibly fail. Nothing here requires guesswork to follow.
Example character: a clear numbered procedure that omits what to do if the third step fails.

## 5 -- Adequate
The content is a correct-gist prose description of the process rather than an executable, testable runbook -- a new reader would need to reverse-engineer the actual steps.
Example character: a paragraph explaining how the process generally works, without numbered, followable steps.

## 3 -- Weak
The document skips steps a newcomer would need, silently assumes undocumented tribal knowledge, and gives no ownership or currency signal at all.
Example character: a procedure that jumps from step one to step four with an unstated step in between.

## 0 -- Unusable
References fabricated tools, systems, or menu paths that do not exist, or gives a dangerously wrong sequence for a risky operation. Empty or off-task.

- Fabricated tool, system, or menu-path names presented as real.
- No failure or rollback branch for a step that can fail destructively.
- Prose narrative presented as a runbook instead of testable numbered steps.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Numbered steps a newcomer could execute unaided.
- Prerequisites listed explicitly up front.
- Failure or rollback branch given for risky steps.
- Currency or ownership signal (last-verified, owner) included when supported by the prompt.

### 1. iManage Matter Folder Creation
**Rule:** Do not create a new matter folder in iManage until **Records** has confirmed that the client conflict check has cleared.

*   **Why this matters:** If a conflict exists and you create the folder anyway, you may grant file access to attorneys representing the opposing side. This is not just a data management error—it is a legal ethics violation (conflict of interest). Once access is granted, it is extremely difficult to reverse without creating an audit trail of unauthorized exposure. Wait for the "all clear" from Records before touching the folder structure.

### 2. Restricted Client Verification ("Ethical Walls")
**Rule:** When a request involves a client or matter that appears anywhere near a client on the firm's **Restricted List**, pause and verify with Records. Do not assume a matter is safe based on the name alone.

*   **Why this matters:** Client names can be similar, or a new matter may not yet have a visible name in the system. Guessing based on intuition or matter naming conventions creates a risk of breaching client confidentiality or inadvertently working on a conflicting matter. The Restricted List maintained by Records is the only source of truth for this information.

### 3. Access Permissions via Intake Form (Not Email)
**Rule:** When setting up iManage access for a new matter, use the **Matter Team List from the Intake Form** exclusively. Do not grant access to anyone based on an email request sent directly to your inbox.

*   **Why this matters:** Email requests are not auditable proof of authorized participation. A matter team list from the intake form is a pre-verified record of who is authorized to work on that file. Granting access based on ad-hoc email requests bypasses the audit trail and risks giving non-authorized personnel access to privileged work product.

### 4. Partner Password Resets
**Rule:** All password reset requests for Partners must be routed through the Partner's **Executive Assistant (EA)**. Never process a reset request addressed directly to the Partner.

*   **Why this matters:** Partners are high-value targets for social engineering and phishing. A request that appears to come from a partner may be spoofed by a third party trying to gain access to a high-level account. The EA serves as a verified identity gatekeeper. If a request does not come through the EA, it is not the partner, and the reset should not proceed.

**Rule:** Never create a new matter folder in iManage until Records has confirmed the conflicts check has cleared.

**Rule:** When a client appears to be on the restricted list, always check with Records rather than guessing from the matter name.

**Rule:** Partner password resets must be requested through the partner's Executive Assistant, not directly to the partner.
**Why:** This creates an audit trail and ensures that requests are legitimate. Direct requests from partners can be easily forged or misunderstood, and the Executive Assistant acts as a verification point to confirm that the request is genuine and authorized.

*   **Step:** Upon receiving a request for a new matter, pause all folder creation. Contact Records Management to verify the status of the conflicts check. Only proceed to create the folder once Records confirms the check is **cleared**.
*   **Reason:** Creating a folder prematurely can inadvertently grant file access to individuals involved in a potential conflict. If the check later returns positive, you have already violated attorney-client ethics by exposing sensitive matter access. This is an ethics breach, not just a technical cleanup issue.

### 2. Access Grants & The Intake Form
**Rule:** iManage access is granted strictly according to the matter team list on the intake form, not based on ad-hoc requests.

*   **Step:** Locate the official matter team list on the intake form. Grant iManage access **only** to the individuals listed there. If a user emails you requesting access but is not on the intake form list, deny the request and direct them to the partner or intake coordinator to update the form.
*   **Reason:** The intake form is the authorized source of truth. Relying on emails exposes the firm to unauthorized access via spoofed addresses, mistaken identity, or well-meaning but incorrect requests. Access must match the firm's official record of the team.

### 3. Ethical Walls & Restricted Clients
**Rule:** Consult Records for any matter involving or related to restricted clients; never infer restrictions from matter names.

*   **Step:** Review the client name and matter details. If the client appears on the restricted list, or if the matter name suggests any tangential relationship to a restricted client, immediately contact Records to verify ethical wall requirements. Do not guess or assume based on the matter name alone.
*   **Reason:** Restricted clients require specific, high-level access controls. Matter names can be abbreviated, misleading, or shared across different matters. Guessing can lead to accidental breaches of ethical walls. Records holds the authoritative status on client restrictions and wall requirements.

### 4. Partner Password Resets
**Rule:** All partner password resets must be verified through the Executive Assistant; never reset directly for the partner.

*   **Step:** When a partner requests a password reset, instruct them to contact their Executive Assistant for verification. Do not perform the reset until the EA has confirmed the request is legitimate. If the partner insists or appears urgent, still require EA verification.
*   **Reason:** This protocol prevents impersonation and unauthorized access. Direct requests can be spoofed by external actors or made by individuals using compromised credentials. The Executive Assistant serves as the trusted verification layer to ensure the partner is actually involved in the request.

---

### General Principle: When in Doubt, Stop
If a request feels ambiguous, involves a sensitive client, or lacks proper documentation, **stop and consult Records or your manager**. It is always better to delay a request to verify compliance than to grant access that violates firm policy.

# On‑Boarding Playbook – iManage & Records Management Essentials  
*For the new IT Support Analyst joining the 60‑attorney firm*  

---

## 1. Create a Matter Folder **only after** Records confirms the conflict check is clear  

**What to do**  
- Do **not** provision a new iManage folder until you receive written confirmation from the Records team that the conflict‑of‑interest check has been approved.  

**Why it matters**  
- If you create the folder early and the conflict check later returns a positive (i.e., the matter is conflicted), every user who has already been granted access will have been exposed to confidential client data.  
- This exposure is an **ethical breach** – it can trigger disciplinary action, damage client trust, and force a costly remediation.  
- Waiting for the official clearance eliminates the risk of accidental “premature” access.

---

## 2. Check the **Restricted‑Client (Ethical Wall) List** before handling any request that touches a matter name  

**What to do**  
- When a request mentions a client, case, or matter name that *sounds* like it could be on the restricted list, **pause**.  
- Open the Restricted‑Client List in Records and verify the client’s status.  
- If there is any doubt, open a ticket with Records for clarification before proceeding.  

**Why it matters**  
- Certain clients are subject to **ethical walls** that prevent firm personnel from accessing their files without explicit authorization.  
- Guessing based on a matter name can lead to inadvertent exposure of privileged information and violate the firm’s conflict‑of‑interest policies.  
- A quick check protects both the client and the firm from ethical violations.

---

## 3. Grant iManage Access **only** according to the **Matter Team List** from the intake form  

**What to do**  
- When a new matter is opened, pull the “Matter Team” list that was entered during intake.  
- Add access for every person on that list **and only** those people.  
- Ignore any ad‑hoc emails, Slack messages, or “please add me” requests that do not match the intake list.  

**Why it matters**  
- The intake form is the authoritative source for who is authorized to work on the matter.  
- Unauthorized additions bypass security controls and can lead to data leakage or conflicts of interest.  
- Following the documented list ensures that access is auditable and defensible in case of an audit or incident review.

---

## 4. All **Partner Password Resets** must be routed through the **Partner’s Executive Assistant**  

**What to do**  
- When a partner requests a password reset, do **not** reset it directly.  
- Forward the request to the partner’s Executive Assistant (EA) for verification.  
- The EA must confirm the request came from the partner (e.g., via a known email signature or a phone call).  
- Once verified, proceed with the reset and document the approval.  

**Why it matters**  
- Partner accounts often have elevated privileges and may be subject to additional compliance checks.  
- Skipping the EA verification opens the door to **social‑engineering attacks** where an impostor pretends to be the partner.  
- Using the EA as the gatekeeper creates a clear audit trail and ensures that only legitimate partner‑initiated changes are processed.

---

## 5. Document Every Action in the Ticketing System  

**What to do**  
- For each of the steps above, create a ticket (or update the existing one) with:  
  - Date & time of the action  
  - Who performed the action  
  - What was done (e.g., “Records cleared conflict check – ID #12345”)  
  - Any verification notes (e.g., “EA Jane Doe confirmed request”)  

**Why it matters**  
- Accurate documentation enables **traceability** and supports internal audits.  
- It provides evidence that proper procedures were followed, protecting both the analyst and the firm if questions arise later.  
- It also helps teammates understand the context without having to hunt for emails or chat logs.

---

## 6. Escalate Unclear Situations Immediately  

**What to do**  
- If a request does not match any of the documented rules, **stop** and open a high‑priority escalation ticket to the Records Management team or the IT Security Lead.  
- Do not guess or apply a workaround until the proper channel validates the request.  

**Why it matters**  
- The firm’s legal‑ethics and data‑security posture rely on strict adherence to policy.  
- Early escalation prevents accidental breaches and ensures the correct authority makes the decision.

# IT Support Onboarding: Critical iManage & Access Protocols

**Purpose:** This document converts legacy tribal knowledge into standard operating procedure. These rules are non-negotiable. In a law firm, IT access controls directly intersect with attorney-client privilege, conflict-of-interest compliance, and professional responsibility. Treat every access decision as a legal risk, not just a technical task.

---

**1. Do not create iManage matter folders until Records confirms the conflicts check has cleared.**
- **What to Do:** Wait for written confirmation from the Records department that the new matter’s conflicts check is clear before provisioning any folder, permissions, or access in iManage. If you receive a folder-creation ticket without that clearance, hold it and follow up.
- **Why It Matters:** If the conflicts check later returns positive, you will have already granted file access to attorneys or staff who should not have it. Premature access creates a real ethics and privilege breach—not just a technical cleanup issue.

**2. Always verify restricted clients against the official Ethical Walls list before processing any access request.**
- **What to Do:** Before creating folders, granting permissions, or routing documents for any matter, cross-reference the client name with the current Ethical Walls/Restricted Client list managed by Records. If there is any ambiguity, pause and consult Records directly. Never infer restrictions from a matter name alone.
- **Why It Matters:** Matter names, file names, and client aliases rarely match the official restricted list exactly. Guessing based on a matter name can accidentally bypass an ethical wall, leading to unauthorized information sharing between opposing parties—a serious professional responsibility violation that can trigger disqualification or malpractice exposure.

**3. Grant iManage matter access strictly according to the matter team list on the intake form, not per individual email requests.**
- **What to Do:** When provisioning access for a new matter, use only the authorized team members listed on the signed intake form. Do not add users who email or call requesting access unless their name appears on that form. Direct any add-on requests to the matter’s managing attorney or intake coordinator for proper documentation.
- **Why It Matters:** Unauthorized additions create hidden access paths that violate firm data-security policies and privilege boundaries. The intake form is the firm’s official record of who is authorized to work the matter; treating it as the single source of truth prevents over-privileged accounts, audit discrepancies, and inadvertent disclosure.

**4. Route all partner password resets through the partner’s Executive Assistant; never process requests directly from the partner.**
- **What to Do:** When a partner needs a password reset, verify the request with their assigned Executive Assistant (EA) first. The EA confirms the partner’s identity and authorization before you proceed. If an EA is unavailable, escalate to the partner’s designated backup contact—never bypass this step.
- **Why It Matters:** Direct requests can be spoofed, misrouted, or made by staff without proper authorization. Using the EA as the verification step ensures the request is genuinely from the partner and protects the firm from unauthorized account access, social-engineering attacks, and compromised matter folders.
