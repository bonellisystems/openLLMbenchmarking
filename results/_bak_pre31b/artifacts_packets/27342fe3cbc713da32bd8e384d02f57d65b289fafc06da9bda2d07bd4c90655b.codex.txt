## 10 -- Frontier
The answer correctly distinguishes general legal information from jurisdiction-specific legal advice, and flags when licensed counsel review is genuinely required. Any statute, regulation, or case citation used is real and correctly applied -- never invented -- and privilege or confidentiality caveats are raised where the scenario implies sensitive written communication. The answer is precise about the limits of what it can determine from the facts given.
Example character: a response that answers the general question, names the specific point that needs jurisdiction-specific counsel review, and cautions against putting a sensitive detail in writing before that review.

## 7 -- Strong
The legal-information answer is sound and usable, with an appropriate "consult counsel" caveat attached. A citation used is generic rather than specific, or the privilege angle is not raised in a case where it plausibly should be, but nothing here is stated as definitive advice.
Example character: correct general guidance with a consult-counsel caveat, but no mention of confidentiality even though the scenario implies sensitive communication.

## 5 -- Adequate
The response is generic legal-sounding boilerplate not tied to the specific facts given, and the caveat present is perfunctory ("consult a lawyer") with no substantive guidance attached to it.
Example character: a boilerplate answer that could apply to nearly any legal question in the same general area.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer states a definitive legal conclusion as fact with no jurisdictional caveat, or misapplies a legal concept to the specific facts given.
Example character: an answer that tells the reader a course of action is definitely lawful without any qualification.

## 0 -- Unusable
Cites a fabricated statute, regulation, or case as if real, or gives definitive advice on a high-stakes matter with no caveat at all. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Fabricated statute, regulation, or case citation presented as real.
- Definitive legal conclusion stated with no jurisdiction or consult-counsel caveat on a matter that clearly needs one.
- General information presented as a substitute for licensed advice on a high-stakes ask.
- No privilege or confidentiality flag when the scenario implies sensitive written communication.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Clear line drawn between general information and advice requiring licensed counsel.
- Only real, correctly-applied citations used -- never invented ones.
- Privilege or confidentiality caution raised when the scenario implies it.
- Jurisdictional caveat stated explicitly rather than assumed.


## Task

The task given to every model in this cohort:

A mid-size ag-equipment manufacturer wants an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) covering shop-floor
tablets and corporate laptops. Draft the AUP to these stated constraints:

1. Reasonable personal use of corporate devices is permitted, but the company reserves the
   right to monitor device and network activity.
2. Prohibited uses must include: opening personal email attachments on devices connected to the
   operational-technology (OT) / industrial-control-systems (ICS) network, and connecting
   unapproved personal USB drives to production-line PCs.
3. Employees must receive clear disclosure that monitoring occurs and must sign an
   acknowledgment before device issuance.
4. Consequences for violations tied to safety-critical OT systems must be spelled out, up to
   and including termination.
5. The policy's monitoring and consequences language must not be worded in a way that could be
   read as prohibiting employees from discussing wages, hours, or working conditions with each
   other -- that kind of discussion is generally protected under federal labor law and should be
   carved out explicitly rather than left ambiguous.

Draft the AUP sections addressing these five points, and close with a note on what should happen
before rollout.

### Section 1: Permitted Personal Use and Company Monitoring Rights

**1.1 Reasonable Personal Use**
Employees are permitted to use corporate-issued devices (including shop-floor tablets and corporate laptops) for reasonable personal purposes during work hours, provided such use does not interfere with assigned job duties, compromise network security, or violate any other Company policy. Personal use is limited to the same level of privilege as a personal device, including a restricted web browsing history and limited social media access.

**1.2 Monitoring Rights**
The Company reserves the right to monitor all device and network activity associated with corporate-owned or -managed hardware, including but not limited to:
*   Internet browsing history and traffic.
*   Email content and metadata.
*   File transfers, storage access, and cloud synchronization.
*   Device location data (for shop-floor tablets).
*   Application usage and peripheral connections.

This monitoring is conducted to ensure system security, intellectual property protection, and operational continuity.

---

### Section 2: Prohibited Uses — OT/ICS Networks and Removable Media

**2.1 Operational-Technology (OT) and Industrial-Control Systems (ICS) Protection**
Strict separation between Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control Systems (ICS) networks is mandatory. The following actions are strictly prohibited and treated as critical security violations:
*   **Prohibited Attachments:** Employees shall not open, download, or forward personal email attachments (including personal cloud files, peer-to-peer shared files, or non-approved external communications) on any device connected to the OT/ICS network.
*   **Cross-Network Transfer:** Under no circumstances shall an employee move data from the OT/ICS network to the IT network or vice versa without explicit written authorization from the Plant Controller and IT Security Director.

**2.2 Removable Media Restrictions**
*   **Production-Line PCs:** Connecting unapproved personal USB drives, flash drives, or external storage devices to production-line PCs, shop-floor tablets, or any device on the OT/ICS network is strictly prohibited.
*   **Approved Media Only:** Only Company-issued and IT-registered removable media may be used on production equipment. Unauthorized media poses a risk of malware introduction, data exfiltration, and potential safety system disruption.

---

### Section 3: Disclosure and Acknowledgment

**3.1 Pre-Issuance Disclosure**
Prior to the physical issuance of any corporate device (laptop, tablet, or ruggedized shop-floor terminal), the employee shall be provided with a printed or digital copy of this AUP and a summary of the monitoring rights described in Section 1.2.

**3.2 Signed Acknowledgment**
The employee must execute a signed "Acceptable Use Policy Acknowledgment Form" prior to the device becoming operational. This form shall confirm:
*   Receipt of the AUP document.
*   Understanding of the Company's monitoring rights.
*   Acknowledgment of the prohibited uses defined in Section 2.
*   Consent to the monitoring described herein.

No device shall be activated, and no shop-floor tablet shall be issued to a production line, until this signed acknowledgment is on file in the Department of Human Resources.

---

### Section 4: Consequences for Safety-Critical OT Violations

**4.1 Classification of Violations**
Violations involving the Operational-Technology (OT) / Industrial-Control Systems (ICS) network are classified as **Critical Safety Violations**. Given the nature of ag-equipment manufacturing, these systems directly control production processes, safety interlocks, and potentially hazardous machinery operations.

**4.2 Disciplinary Action**
Any violation of the prohibitions in Section 2.1 or Section 2.2 constitutes grounds for **immediate termination of employment**, regardless of the employee's tenure or history of prior disciplinary warnings. In addition to termination, the Company reserves the right to pursue legal action regarding data theft, intellectual property infringement, or regulatory fines resulting from compromised OT systems.

---

### Section 5: Protected Concerted Activity Exclusion

**5.1 Scope of Monitoring Limitation**
Nothing in this Acceptable Use Policy, nor in the Company's exercise of its monitoring rights, shall be construed or interpreted as authorizing, encouraging, or permitting the Company to take adverse employment action against an employee for engaging in **protected concerted activity**.

**5.2 Explicit Carve-Out**
The Company explicitly recognizes that monitoring may incidentally capture communications regarding:
*   Wages, hours of employment, and benefits.
*   Working conditions and workplace safety.
*   Collective bargaining and grievance procedures.
*   Discussions with co-workers or representatives regarding these matters.

The Company shall not use data gathered through monitoring for the purpose of identifying, disciplining, or retaliating against employees engaging in such protected discussions as defined by the **National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), Section 7**, or applicable state labor laws. Monitoring logs shall be reviewed strictly for security and operational compliance purposes, not for labor relations surveillance.

5.  **Retention Schedule:** Establish a data retention policy for monitoring logs to ensure they are not kept longer than necessary for security audits, reducing the risk of "fishing expeditions" that could be challenged in court.

## 1. Scope & Purpose  
This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) applies to all company‑issued devices that are used on the shop floor or in corporate environments, including **shop‑floor tablets** and **corporate laptops**. The purpose of this policy is to:

1. Protect the integrity, security, and safety of the company’s operational‑technology (OT) and industrial‑control‑system (ICS) networks.  
2. Clarify the extent of permissible personal use of corporate devices.  
3. Define monitoring practices and the consequences of violations.  
4. Ensure compliance with all applicable federal and state labor laws, including the protection of employee‑to‑employee discussions about wages, hours, and working conditions.

---

## 2. Acceptable Use – Reasonable Personal Use  

1. **Personal Use** – Employees may use corporate devices for **reasonable personal activities** (e.g., personal email, browsing non‑work sites, calendar scheduling, or other non‑work‑related tasks) **as long as** such use does **not** interfere with job duties, compromise system security, or violate any other part of this AUP.  
2. **Monitoring Right** – The company reserves the right to **monitor, record, and analyze** all device and network activity for compliance, security, and safety purposes. Monitoring may include, but is not limited to, traffic logs, application usage, and data transfers.  

---

## 3. Prohibited Use – OT/ICS and Production‑Line Devices  

1. **Personal Email Attachments** – Opening, downloading, or executing any **personal email attachment** on a device that is connected to the OT/ICS network is strictly prohibited.  
2. **Unapproved USB Drives** – Connecting **unapproved personal USB drives** (or any removable media not authorized by the IT/OT security team) to **production‑line PCs** or any device that has access to OT/ICS systems is prohibited.  
3. **Additional Prohibitions** – The list above is **not exhaustive**. Any action that could compromise OT/ICS integrity, safety, or data confidentiality is disallowed.  

---

## 4. Monitoring & Disclosure  

1. **Disclosure Statement** – Before a device is issued, the employee will receive a written disclosure stating:  
   * “Your device and network activity may be monitored for security, compliance, and safety purposes.”  
2. **Acknowledgment** – Employees must **sign** an acknowledgment form confirming that they have read, understood, and agree to comply with this AUP, including the monitoring provisions.  
3. **Retention of Records** – Monitoring logs will be retained in accordance with the company’s data‑retention policy and relevant legal requirements.  

---

## 5. Consequences for Violations (Safety‑Critical OT)  

1. **Immediate Action** – Any violation that directly impacts the safety or integrity of OT/ICS systems will trigger an **immediate containment** response (e.g., device isolation, temporary revocation of access).  
2. **Disciplinary Process** – Violations may result in disciplinary action, ranging from verbal warning to written reprimand, suspension, or **termination of employment**, depending on severity and recurrence.  
3. **Safety‑First Approach** – The company is committed to **safety‑first** management; any breach that endangers personnel or production processes will be treated as a **high‑priority incident**.  

---

## 6. Protected Employee Discussion (Labor‑Law Carve‑out)  

1. **Explicit Protection** – Employees retain the right to discuss, in any forum or medium, **wages, hours, and working conditions** with one another or with authorized union representatives.  
2. **Monitoring Exclusion** – Monitoring activities **do not** extend to protected conversations about employment terms. Any attempt to monitor or penalize such discussions is **unacceptable** and will be treated as a violation of federal labor law.  
3. **Reporting Mechanism** – Employees who believe their protected discussions have been improperly monitored may file a complaint with HR or the designated labor‑relations officer.  

---

## 7. Implementation & Rollout  

Before the policy is rolled out:

1. **Stakeholder Review** – Obtain sign‑off from IT, OT, HR, Legal, and Safety departments.  
2. **Training** – Conduct mandatory training sessions (in‑person or online) for all device users, covering:  
   * Purpose and scope of the AUP  
   * Monitoring rights and data handling  
   * Prohibited use scenarios and safe device practices  
   * Consequences and escalation procedures  
3. **Documentation** – Distribute the final AUP document and acknowledgment form in both electronic and hard‑copy formats.  
4. **Device Issuance** – Require each employee to sign the acknowledgment before receiving a tablet or laptop.  
5. **Audit & Monitoring Setup** – Configure monitoring tools to enforce the policy, ensuring logs are collected, stored, and protected per the data‑retention policy.  
6. **Feedback Loop** – Establish a mechanism for employees to ask questions, provide feedback, and report concerns about the policy.  

---

### Closing Note  

This AUP balances the need for operational security and safety with employees’ rights to reasonable personal use and protected labor discussions. By following the steps above, the company ensures a smooth, compliant, and transparent rollout of the policy.

Nothing in this section grants employees a right to privacy regarding data stored on or transmitted through corporate devices. [Company Name] reserves the right to monitor, inspect, log, and audit all activity on corporate devices and associated networks, including but not limited to:
*   Network traffic and bandwidth usage;
*   File access, creation, and deletion;
*   Application usage and peripheral connections;
*   Communications metadata and content where required for security investigations.

Monitoring is conducted to protect operational technology (OT) environments, ensure network integrity, safeguard proprietary data, and verify compliance with this policy.

Due to the safety-critical nature of our ag-equipment manufacturing operations, strict controls apply to devices connected to Operational Technology (OT), Industrial Control Systems (ICS), and production-line infrastructure. The following uses are **strictly prohibited**:

*   **Personal Email Attachments on OT/ICS Endpoints:** Employees must not open, download, forward, or process personal email attachments on any device connected to the OT/ICS network. Personal email is restricted to general corporate IT networks and may not traverse or terminate on production control systems.
*   **Unapproved USB Drives on Production PCs:** Employees must not connect unapproved personal USB storage devices, external drives, or mobile hotspots to production-line PCs, PLCs, HMIs, or tablets. Only company-provisioned and security-scanned peripherals may be used on the shop floor.
*   **Offline/Side-Loading on Safety Systems:** Employees must not bypass security controls, disable scanning software, or load unverified software on any system designated as safety-critical.
*   **Sharing Credentials:** Employees must not share login credentials for shop-floor tablets or production workstations with other individuals.

#### 4. Monitoring Disclosure and Acknowledgment
**4.1 Disclosure.**

**5.1 Carve-Out for Protected Activity.**  
Nothing in this AUP shall be interpreted to prohibit, restrict, or discourage employees from exercising rights protected under federal labor laws, including the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Employees retain the full right to engage in Protected Concerted Activity, including but not limited to:
*   Discussing wages, hours, benefits, and working conditions with co-workers;
*   Filing or supporting complaints with government agencies;
*   Organizing or joining labor unions;
*   Expressing opinions about the Company or their employment experience.

*   **Standard Violations:** Non-compliance regarding corporate laptops or non-OT devices may result in a warning, device revocation, or suspension.
*   **OT/ICS Safety Violations:** Violations involving the OT/ICS network or Production-Line PCs that compromise safety-critical systems (e.g., introducing malware via personal email attachments or unauthorized USBs) are treated with extreme severity. Such actions pose a direct risk to personnel safety and production integrity and may result in **immediate termination**.

### 5. Acknowledgment of Policy
Before receiving a company-issued device, the employee must:
1.  Read this policy in its entirety.
2.  Sign the attached **Device Issuance Acknowledgment Form**.

This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) establishes guidelines for the use of corporate-issued shop-floor tablets and laptops (hereafter "Devices") by employees of [Company Name]. - **Immediate Revocation of Device Privileges:** The offending Device will be collected, and the employee may be restricted from receiving another Device.
- **Disciplinary Action:** Depending on the severity and impact of the violation, disciplinary actions may range from formal warnings to termination of employment, especially in cases where the violation has compromised safety, security, or operational integrity.

- "reasons": object mapping each letter to a one-line string reason.
- "ranking": array containing every letter exactly once, best first.

A reply missing any letter from any of the three fields, containing a score
outside 0-10, or containing extra text outside the JSON object, is invalid
and will be discarded (one retry is allowed by the runner before the call is
recorded as an error).
