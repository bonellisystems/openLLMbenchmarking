## Anchors

The following anchors define what a 0, 3, 5, 7, and 10 look like for this
business unit. Use them as the scoring scale for every answer.

# HR & People Ops -- scoring anchors (0-10)

Scale: integers 0-10. Anchors define 0/3/5/7/10; use 1-2/4/6/8-9 for answers between anchors.

## 10 -- Frontier
The recommended policy or action is consistent with generally-accepted employment-law caution -- at-will limits, documentation requirements, protected-class awareness -- without asserting a jurisdiction-specific statute as universal fact. Documentation or paper-trail steps are recommended where relevant (for example, performance history before termination), employment-counsel review is flagged for high-stakes actions, and the tone stays confidential and non-discriminatory throughout.
Example character: guidance that recommends documenting the performance issue first, names the point where legal review is needed, and never treats a protected characteristic as relevant to the decision.

## 7 -- Strong
The guidance is sound and usable, with the correct documentation instinct present. The counsel-review caveat is missing or generic in a scenario that is clearly higher-stakes (termination, accommodation, investigation), but nothing here is discriminatory or procedurally unsafe.
Example character: correct documentation-first guidance for a termination scenario with no explicit flag to loop in employment counsel.

## 5 -- Adequate
The advice is generic HR-fundamentals guidance ("be fair," "document everything") not tied to the specific personnel situation given, and any caveat present is perfunctory.
Example character: general fairness advice that does not engage the specific facts of the situation described.

## 3 -- Weak
The answer recommends an action that skips a required documentation or process step, treats a jurisdiction-specific rule as universally applicable without caveat, or carries a judgmental tone toward a protected characteristic implied in the prompt.
Example character: recommends termination without any reference to a documented performance record when the prompt implies none exists.

## 0 -- Unusable
Recommends an action that is facially discriminatory or clearly unlawful (for example, tying an adverse action to a stated protected characteristic) as if it were fine, or cites a fabricated employment-law statute. Empty or off-task.

## Unit-specific red flags (deduct hard)
- Adverse action tied to a protected characteristic, or recommended with no documented performance record when the scenario implies neither exists.
- Fabricated employment-statute or case citation.
- No counsel or legal-review flag on a high-stakes action (termination, accommodation, investigation).
- Treating one jurisdiction's rule as universally applicable without caveat.

## Unit-specific excellence markers
- Documentation or paper-trail step recommended before any adverse action.
- Protected-class and accommodation awareness woven into the reasoning, not bolted on.
- Explicit flag for employment-counsel review on high-stakes actions.
- Confidentiality maintained throughout the response.

## II. Specific, Measurable Goals (SMART)
Goals are calibrated to correct the specific behaviors cited in the prior notes. Metrics are binary or quantifiable to minimize subjectivity.

### Goal 1: Deadline Adherence
* **Tied to:** Week 3 Meridian Tower issue.
* **Target:** 100% on-time delivery of all assigned drawing packages and milestone deliverables.
* **Measure:** Submission date must equal or precede the date agreed upon in the project schedule.
    * *Metric:* **0 delays** permitted. Any deliverable submitted after the agreed deadline constitutes a failure of this goal, regardless of reason, unless an approved schedule change was documented *prior* to the deadline.
    * *Threshold:* To address the "4-day lag," the employee must demonstrate the ability to manage workflow buffers. Late submissions trigger immediate escalation.

### Goal 2: QA Error Rate & Pre-Submission Quality
* **Tied to:** Week 6 QA issue.
* **Target:** Zero critical errors and ≤1 non-critical correction per major deliverable.
* **Measure:**
    * **Critical Errors:** Defined as dimensional conflicts, structural calculation mismatches, code/standard violations, or missing layers/annotations that halt production. **Target: 0 Critical Errors.**
    * **Non-Critical Errors:** Typos, minor annotation adjustments, or plotting issues. **Target: ≤1 per 100-sheet package or per major deliverable.**
    * **Self-Check Compliance:** 100% of deliverables must include a signed QA checklist confirming internal review prior to submission. Submission of an un-checked package is an automatic failure.

### Goal 3: RFI Response Time
* **Tied to:** Week 9 Client complaint.
* **Target:** Rapid acknowledgment and resolution of RFIs to meet client SLA.
* **Measure:**
    * **Acknowledgment:** All RFIs received from clients/contractors must be acknowledged within **4 business hours**.
    * **Resolution:** Substantive responses (with drawings/notes attached or clear path to resolution) provided within **24 business hours**.
    * **Metric:** 100% of RFIs acknowledged within 4 hours; ≥90% resolved within 24 hours. Any RFI pending >24 hours without a status update to the PM constitutes a failure.

## V. Protected Leave & Accommodation Protocol
Performance improvement plans interact with protected leaves (FMLA, ADA, Workers' Comp, jury duty, military leave). Failure to manage this can expose the firm to discrimination claims.

* **Mechanism:** If the employee takes protected leave, the Day 30, 60, and 90 checkpoints shift forward by the number of days the employee is on leave.

* **Notification:** The employee must notify the PM/HR immediately upon the start of any protected leave. HR is responsible for calculating the extended timeline and issuing a revised schedule to the PM.

### 2. Reasonable Accommodation
* **Process:** If the employee requests an accommodation under ADA or similar law, the PIP process is paused pending the Interactive Process.
* **Adjustment:** If an accommodation is granted (e.g., modified hours, assistive technology), the PM must adjust the *method* of goal measurement if the accommodation legitimately alters how the work is performed.

* **Continuity:** The PIP remains active during intermittent protected leave; the employee must meet goals for the days they work.
* **Documentation:** The PM must continue to document performance on days worked. A "return to work" meeting should occur to review metrics upon the employee's return.

## 1. Purpose and Background
This Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) is issued following a documented pattern of performance deficiencies over a 90-day period preceding this document. These deficiencies have been previously communicated through informal written check-in notes, which serve as the evidentiary basis for this formal plan.

**Prior Documentation Reference:**
The goals established below address the specific performance gaps identified in the following records on file with Human Resources:

*   **Week 3 (Check-in Note [Ref #001]):** Missed the deadline for the Meridian Tower drawing package by 4 days.
*   **Week 6 (Check-in Note [Ref #002]):** QA flagged 4 drawing-revision errors in a single deliverable that should have been caught before submission.
*   **Week 9 (Check-in Note [Ref #003]):** A client contact emailed a complaint regarding slow response times on Requests for Information (RFIs).

The employee has been notified that these issues are the basis for this formal evaluation. Further informal coaching is suspended; the employee is expected to meet the specific metrics outlined in Section 3.

---

## 2. Performance Improvement Goals
The following goals are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART). They are tied directly to the three documented issues above.

### Goal 1: Deadline Adherence (Addressing Week 3 Issue)
*   **Metric:** 100% on-time delivery of all assigned drawing packages for the duration of the PIP period (90 days).
*   **Definition:** No deliverable shall be submitted after the agreed-upon project milestone date without prior written approval from the Project Manager. No extensions shall be granted during this period.
*   **Target:** 0 missed deadlines.

### Goal 2: QA Error Rate (Addressing Week 6 Issue)
*   **Metric:** Zero (0) errors flagged by Quality Assurance (QA) in any single deliverable submitted for approval.
*   **Definition:** A "QA Error" is defined as a deviation from the structural standards, drafting conventions, or project specifications identified by the QA review process.
*   **Target:** 0 errors per submission for the 90-day period.

### Goal 3: RFI Response Time (Addressing Week 9 Issue)
*   **Metric:** All RFIs assigned to the employee are acknowledged and a substantive response drafted within 24 business hours of assignment.
*   **Definition:** "Acknowledged" means an email reply confirming receipt and a timeline for the technical response. "Substantive response" means a technical answer or a delegation to the lead engineer, not a placeholder.
*   **Target:** 100% of assigned RFIs acknowledged within 24 business hours.

## 3. Support and Resources
To facilitate meeting these goals, the firm agrees to provide the following:
1.  **Weekly 1-on-1 Check-ins:** Occurring every Friday at [Time] with the Project Manager to review progress on Goals 1-3.
2.  **QA Pre-Submission Review:** The employee is permitted to submit drafts to the Project Manager for a technical pre-check prior to the QA formal submission.
3.  **Resource Allocation:** Confirmation that the employee has access to all necessary software, templates, and reference materials required to meet the drafting standards.

## 4. Checkpoint Evaluations
The PIP will be evaluated at three specific intervals. The Project Manager and Human Resources will jointly review the data.

### Checkpoint 1: Day 30 (End of Month 1)
*   **Objective:** Assess early progress and determine if corrective actions are yielding results.
*   **Criteria:**
    *   Has the employee met Goal 1 (Deadlines) for the first 30 days?
    *   Has the employee met Goal 2 (QA Errors) for the first 30 days?
    *   Has the employee met Goal 3 (RFI Response) for the first 30 days?
*   **Outcomes:**
    *   **Meeting Standards:** If all three goals are fully met for 30 consecutive days, the PIP is closed, and the employee returns to the standard performance review cycle.
    *   **Partial Progress:** If the employee is making good-faith efforts but has missed a goal once or twice, the PIP continues. A written coaching note is added to the file.
    *   **Non-Compliance:** If an employee fails to meet deadlines, produces errors, or ignores RFI timelines during this period, the Project Manager will issue a verbal warning regarding the seriousness of the plan and schedule a follow-up meeting at Day 45.

### Checkpoint 2: Day 60 (End of Month 2)
*   **Objective:** Assess sustained ability to meet the standard over a longer period.
*   **Criteria:** Same metrics as Day 30, applied to the 30-day window from Day 31–60.
*   **Outcomes:**
    *   **Meeting Standards:** If the employee has met all goals for the cumulative 60-day period, the PIP is closed.
    *   **Non-Compliance:** If the employee has failed to meet goals on more than one occasion during the Day 31–60 window, the PIP is extended to Day 90. An updated performance warning is issued.

### Checkpoint 3: Day 90 (End of Month 3)
*   **Objective:** Final determination of employment status.
*   **Criteria:** Review of the full 90-day cumulative record against Goals 1, 2, and 3.
*   **Outcomes:**
    *   **Successful Completion:** The employee has met all goals for the full 90-day period. The PIP is formally closed. The employee is placed back on the standard performance schedule.
    *   **Unsuccessful Completion:** The employee has failed to demonstrate sustained improvement in deadline adherence, QA accuracy, or RFI responsiveness.
    *   **Action:** Following a final meeting with the employee and HR, the firm will proceed with **termination of employment**, effective [Date]. A separation package will be prepared in accordance with company policy and applicable law.

## 5. Protected Leave and Accommodation
The firm recognizes that during the PIP period, the employee may be eligible for protected leave or reasonable accommodation under applicable laws (e.g., FMLA, ADA, State Family Leave Laws, Religious Accommodation).

### 5.1 Leave of Absence (FMLA / Medical)
*   If the employee is approved for protected leave (e.g., FMLA) during the PIP period, the PIP clock will be **paused**.
*   The PIP will resume on the date the employee returns to work.
*   Upon return, the employee will be expected to meet the cumulative goals as of the date of return. If the employee returns after Day 60, the remaining period will be shortened to Day 90.

### 5.2 Reasonable Accommodation (ADA / Other)
*   If the employee requests a reasonable accommodation (e.g., ergonomic equipment, modified schedule, assistive technology, or a temporary reassignment of duties due to a disability) under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), the firm will evaluate and implement the accommodation in good faith.
*   **Performance Standard:** The implementation of a reasonable accommodation does not lower the performance standard. The employee is expected to meet the goals of the PIP (Goals 1, 2, and 3) using the accommodated tools/environment.
*   If the accommodation itself prevents the employee from meeting the goals (e.g., the accommodation significantly reduces the speed of drafting without a corresponding increase in resources), the firm and employee will engage in an interactive process to adjust the plan. However, the firm is not required to provide an accommodation that fundamentally alters the nature of the job or imposes an undue hardship.

### 5.3 Documentation
All leave requests and accommodation requests submitted during the PIP period must be documented in writing and filed with HR. Any gaps in attendance or changes in workflow resulting from leave/accommodation will be noted in the weekly check-ins but will not be counted as missed deadlines or performance failures unless the absence exceeds approved leave limits.

### 7. Protected Leave or Accommodation Requests During the PIP  

1. **Legal Framework** – The firm is obligated to comply with the **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)**, **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)**, and any applicable state/local statutes.  
2. **Process**  
   - **Employee Request** – If the employee submits a request for protected leave (e.g., medical, family) or an accommodation (e.g., modified schedule, assistive technology), they must provide a written request to HR.  
   - **HR Review** – HR conducts an interactive assessment to determine whether the requested leave/accommodation is **reasonable** and **necessary** for the employee to perform essential job functions.  
   - **Impact on PIP** –  
     - **During the PIP period**, the **timeline of the PIP may be temporarily paused** only with mutual written agreement, to avoid penalizing the employee for exercising a protected right.  
     - The **performance standards themselves do not change**; however, the **method of measurement may be adjusted** (e.g., extending the deadline for a deliverable if the employee is on approved leave).  
     - Any **accommodation** (such as flexible hours) must be documented and implemented before the next checkpoint; failure to meet the adjusted schedule will be considered a **new performance issue**, not a PIP violation.  
   - **Documentation** – All correspondence regarding leave/accommodation, HR’s determination, and any modifications to the PIP schedule are filed separately but referenced in the PIP file to demonstrate that the firm acted in good faith and complied with legal obligations.  
3. **If a Protected Leave is Approved Mid‑PIP**  
   - The **next checkpoint date is shifted** by the length of the approved leave (e.g., if a 2‑week leave begins on Day 35, the 60‑day checkpoint is now scheduled for Day 71).  
   - The **goals remain unchanged**; only the **deadline for achieving them is extended** proportionally.  
   - The employee must still **meet the revised schedule**; failure to do so will be treated as a performance deficiency, not as a breach of the PIP.  
4. **If an Accommodation is Granted**  
   - The accommodation must be **specific, measurable, and documented** (e.g., “All RFI responses must be entered into the tracking sheet within 8 hours of receipt”).  
   - The employee and manager sign an **Accommodation Agreement** that is attached to the PIP file.  
   - The PIP’s **measurement criteria** are updated to reflect the accommodation (e.g., “RFI response time measured from the moment the request is received via the approved channel”).
