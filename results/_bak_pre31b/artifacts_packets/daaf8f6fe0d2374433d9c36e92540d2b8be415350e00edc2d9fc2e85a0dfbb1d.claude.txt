# IT Support Onboarding Document: Critical Operational Rules
**Role:** IT Support Specialist  
**Department:** Law Firm IT  
**Context:** The following procedures are mandatory for all iManage operations and user support requests. In a law firm environment, IT actions directly impact client privilege, ethical compliance, and firm liability. Adherence to these rules is non-negotiable.

---

### 1. Matter Folder Creation Requires Conflicts Clearance
**Rule:** Never create a new matter folder in iManage until you receive explicit confirmation from Records that the conflicts check has cleared.  
**Reason:** Premature folder creation grants file access to users before the firm has verified the engagement is ethically permissible. If a conflict is discovered after the folder exists, sensitive data may already have been exposed to the wrong personnel. This creates a severe ethics violation and potential malpractice risk, rather than a simple technical cleanup task.

### 2. Ethical Wall Verification is Mandatory
**Rule:** When processing requests involving clients on the restricted list or potential "ethical walls," you must consult Records for validation. Never rely on the matter name or your own assessment to determine restrictions.  
**Reason:** Ethical walls (Chinese walls) are strict legal safeguards designed to prevent information leakage between conflicting matters. Relying on guesswork or naming conventions risks breaching these walls, which can result in disqualification from cases, loss of client trust, and significant liability for the firm.

### 3. Access Assignment Follows the Intake Form Only
**Rule:** Configure iManage access permissions strictly according to the official matter team list provided in the intake form. Do not grant access based on ad-hoc email requests, verbal instructions, or who emails you asking to be added.  
**Reason:** This ensures that access rights are auditable, consistent, and aligned with the formally authorized matter team. Bypassing the intake form creates security gaps, introduces unauthorized users, and undermines the integrity of the matter's access control list.

### 4. Partner Password Resets Route Through Executive Assistants
**Rule:** All password reset requests for Partners must be routed to and verified through the Partner's designated Executive Assistant. Do not process password resets requested directly by the Partner via email, chat, or phone.  
**Reason:** This protocol prevents social engineering and impersonation attacks. Attackers often target high-value accounts by posing as senior leadership. Requiring verification through the EA ensures the request is legitimate and provides a layer of accountability that protects against unauthorized account compromise.

---

**General Guidance:**  
If a request is unclear, falls outside these rules, or involves sensitive client data, **pause and consult Records or your IT Manager** before taking action. In this firm, slowing down to verify compliance is always preferred over moving fast and risking an ethics breach.

## 1. Matter Creation & iManage Access

*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Receive a request to create a new matter folder in iManage.
    2.  **Do not** create the folder immediately.
    3.  Contact the **Records Department** and confirm that the conflicts check for this new matter has cleared.

### Rule 2: Access Follows the Intake Form, Not Email Requests
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  When granting access to an iManage matter, locate the official **Intake Form**.
    2.  Grant access strictly to the individuals listed on the "Matter Team" section of that form.
    3.  Ignore any ad-hoc emails requesting access additions. Direct the requester to submit a formal update to the Intake Form via Records.
*   **Why:** The Intake Form is the authoritative source of truth for authorized personnel. Email requests can be mistaken, unauthorized, or spoofed. Relying on the form prevents unauthorized users from accessing confidential client data.

## 2. Security & Ethical Walls

### Rule 3: Verify Restricted Clients with Records; Never Guess
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  If any matter name, client name, or request raises a suspicion that it relates to a client on the **Restricted List/Ethical Wall**, pause all action.
    2.  Consult the **Records Department** immediately to verify the status.
    3.  Do not attempt to deduce ethical walls based on matter names or intuition.
*   **Why:** Matter names can be misleading, generic, or shared across different clients. Ethical walls are legal requirements to prevent conflicts of interest. Guessing can lead to accidental data leakage or compliance breaches that could disqualify the firm from representing a client.

## 3. Authentication Protocols

### Rule 4: Partner Password Resets Require EA Verification

*   **Why:** Direct requests from Partners can be socially engineered or spoofed. The EA acts as a trusted proxy and verification point. This protocol protects the firm from unauthorized access attempts that may appear to originate from high-level partners.

Follow these steps exactly. If you are ever unsure, stop and consult the Records Department.

---

### 1. Gate Matter Creation on Conflicts Clearance
**Action:** Never create a new matter folder in iManage until the Records Department has confirmed the conflicts check has fully cleared. If you receive a request to create a folder before that confirmation, hold the request and wait.

**Why:** Creating a folder before clearance risks granting file access to individuals who may be flagged by a positive conflicts check. ### 2. Verify Restricted Clients Against the Ethical Wall List
**Action:** If a request involves a client that appears on the Restricted/Ethical Wall list, pause immediately and verify with Records before proceeding. Never assume a matter is restricted or unrestricted based solely on the matter name or a quick search.

**Why:** Ethical walls restrict access to specific clients regardless of how the matter is named. Guessing can result in granting access to sensitive restricted information. Trusting the name over the list is a common error that leads to unauthorized access violations.

**Action:** Grant iManage access for a new matter strictly according to the matter team list found on the official Intake Form. Do not add users based on ad-hoc emails, verbal requests, or calendar invites. If someone emails asking to be added but is not on the Intake Form team, deny the request and direct them to the matter team lead.

**Why:** The Intake Form is the source of truth for authorized matter membership. **Action:** Partner password resets must be authorized and processed through the partner's Executive Assistant (EA). Never perform a reset directly upon a request from the partner alone, even if the caller claims to be that partner.

### General Rule: Ethics Over Speed
**Action:** If a request feels urgent but conflicts with any of the rules above, **do not bypass the rule.** Contact the senior IT team or Records immediately.

# IT Support On‑boarding Checklist  
**For the new IT Support Analyst – 60‑Attorney Law Firm**  

> **Purpose:**  Follow these rules every time you handle a matter‑related request.  The “why” is included so you understand the risk behind each step and can explain it to colleagues if needed.  

---

## 1.  Conflict‑of‑Interest Checks Before Folder Creation  

**Rule:** *Never create a new iManage “Matter” folder until Records has confirmed that the conflict‑of‑interest (COI) check is clear.*  

**Why it matters:**  
- If a conflict surfaces **after** you’ve already created the folder, anyone who has already been granted access will have been exposed to privileged information.  
- That breach is an **ethics violation** (confidential client data disclosed) rather than just a technical “oops.”  
- Fixing it means re‑configuring permissions, re‑issuing access logs, and possibly notifying the firm’s ethics committee – a costly and time‑consuming clean‑up.  

**Action:**  
1. Check the COI dashboard in Records.  
2. Wait for the “Clear” status before proceeding with folder creation or permission assignments.  

---

## 2.  Ethical Wall – Restricted‑Client List  

**Rule:** *When a request references any client on the Restricted List, pause and consult Records before granting any access.*  

**Why it matters:**  
- Certain clients are subject to **ethical walls** that prevent the firm from representing them in related matters.  
- A matter name that *sounds* unrelated may still involve a restricted client (e.g., a matter titled “Acquisition of XYZ Corp.” could actually be about a client on the restricted list).  
- Ignoring the wall and granting access can expose the firm to **conflict‑of‑interest claims** and possible disciplinary action.  

**Action:**  
1. Search the client name in the Restricted List.  
2. If a match is found, route the request to Records for verification before any iManage actions are taken.  

---

## 3.  iManage Access Is Driven by the Intake Form, Not the Requestor  

**Rule:** *All iManage access for a new matter must be derived from the official “Matter Team List” on the intake form.  Do not add or remove users based on ad‑hoc emails or direct requests.*  

**Why it matters:**  
- The intake form is the **single source of truth** for who is authorized to work on a matter.  
- Emails can be forged, mis‑routed, or sent by non‑authorized staff; following them leads to “shadow teams” that bypass compliance controls.  
- Maintaining the official list ensures:  
  - Accurate audit trails.  
  - Consistent permission sets for reporting and security.  
  - No accidental inclusion of external counsel or vendors.  

**Action:**  
1. Open the intake form (or the latest version stored in the intake repository).  
2. Verify the “Matter Team” field matches the user’s name/role.  
3. Only then create or modify iManage folders/permissions.  

---

## 4.  Partner Password Resets – Follow the Executive Assistant Path  

**Rule:** *All password‑reset requests for Partners must be routed through the Partner’s Executive Assistant (EA), never directly to the Partner.*  

**Why it matters:**  
- EAs act as the **official gatekeeper** for senior‑level account changes.  
- Direct requests bypass verification of the requester’s identity, opening the door to social‑engineering attacks.  
- Some reset requests may be *mis‑addressed* (e.g., a junior associate trying to “reset” a Partner’s password). The EA can confirm the legitimacy before proceeding.  

**Action:**  
1. Receive the request from the EA (via ticket or approved email).  
2. Confirm the EA’s authority (e.g., signature, departmental approval).  
3. Initiate the password reset using the firm’s privileged‑access workflow.  
4. Document the reset in the audit log and notify the EA of completion.  

---

## 5.  General “Do‑Not‑Guess” Principle  

**Rule:** *If any part of a request feels uncertain—whether it’s a matter name, a client identifier, or a user’s authority—stop and verify with Records or the appropriate stakeholder before taking action.*  

**Why it matters:**  
- Guessing creates **unknown exposure** that can cascade into ethics violations, data leaks, or compliance failures.  
- Verification takes only a few minutes but prevents hours (or days) of remediation later.  

**Action:**  
- Log the uncertainty in the ticket.  
- Tag the ticket with “Pending Verification – Records.”  
- Await clearance before proceeding.
